\begin{abstract}
At rational \(b^2\) the poles of the residue recursions for Virasoro
conformal blocks collide, and individual residues have poles in \(b\).  For
the torus one-point block and the sphere four-point block, and for every
fixed \(b_\star\ne0\), we give the principal part of each block coefficient
at each Kac weight as a sum of constant terms of finite Laurent expansions in
\(b-b_\star\) of the generic residue data.  At an interior Kac weight
\(\lambda\) of a minimal model, the pole order of the level-\(N\)
coefficient at \(\lambda\) is at most the number of Jantzen layers of the
Verma module that have reached level \(N\).  When this number is positive,
the coefficient at that order is a sum of blocks at shifted weights,
weighted by matrix elements of singular vectors divided by their Jantzen
norms.  For indeterminate external weights the bound is attained at every
interior weight and every \(N\geq1\).  In the Ising model the first double
poles at the energy, spin and identity weights occur at levels 7, 10 and 11.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace Virasoro algebra; conformal blocks;
torus one-point block; sphere four-point block; Zamolodchikov recursion;
minimal models; Jantzen filtration; Ising model.

\section{Introduction}

The one-point function of a primary field on a torus with modular parameter
\(\tau\) must transform covariantly under \(\tau\mapsto-1/\tau\).  Together
with crossing symmetry of the sphere four-point function, this is one of the
basic consistency conditions of a conformal field theory on closed surfaces,
and recursions for the torus one-point block are used to test it
numerically~\cite[\S\S1 and~5]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}.  The one-point
function is a sum over the spectrum, or an integral for a continuous
spectrum, of a structure constant times a torus one-point block and its
antiholomorphic
counterpart~\cite[\S2]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}.\footnote{Equations (3)--(4) of~\cite{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010} and the display after (4).}  The torus one-point Virasoro block with internal weight \(\Delta\) and
external weight \(d\) is a power series in \(q\).  Its coefficient \(F_N\)
pairs the level-\(N\) subspace of the Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) with itself
through the inverse Shapovalov matrix and the matrix elements of a primary
field of weight \(d\).  Multiplying the series by
\(\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)\) gives the eta-reduced coefficients \(H_N\).  Write
\(c=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2\).  For generic \(b\), each \(H_N\) has only simple poles
in \(\Delta\), at the Kac weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with \(rs\leq N\), and the
residues satisfy Poghossian's recursion~\cite[\S5]{Poghossian2009}, derived
in~\cite[\S3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010};\footnote{The recursion is (5.3)--(5.7) of~\cite{Poghossian2009} and (7)--(9) of~\cite{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}.} see
\cite[\S2.2]{PoghosyanPoghosyan2026} for the light limit \(b\to0\) at fixed
weights.  The recursion computes \(H_N\) from lower coefficients without
inverting the Shapovalov matrix, whose size at level \(N\) is the number of
partitions of \(N\).  Zamolodchikov's recursion does the same for the sphere
four-point block~\cite{Zamolodchikov1987}: the block is an explicit elliptic
prefactor times a series \(\sum_NH_N(16q)^N\) in the nome, each \(H_N\) has
simple poles at the Kac weights with residues given by lower coefficients,
and the large-weight limit \(H_N\to\delta_{N,0}\) fixes the remaining
polynomial part~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{PolzerLargeWeight2026}.  Numerical
bootstrap computations use Zamolodchikov's recursions to compute sphere
blocks to high order~\cite[\S4.3]{Ribault2026}.

The recursions break down at rational \(b^2\).  There two things go wrong:
different Kac labels give the same weight, and individual residues have poles
in \(b\).  Every minimal-model charge, \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), is of this kind.
The block coefficients are still well defined there.  Ribault calls a
recursion at rational \(b^2\) an open problem whose simplest case is the
torus one-point block~\cite[\S4.3.3]{Ribault2026}.  By \emph{confluence} we
mean passing to the limit \(b\to b_\star\) in the generic recursion after
grouping the terms whose poles collide.

At every fixed \(b_\star\neq0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} gives the
coefficients of the torus block at internal weight \(\Delta\), for \(N\geq1\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 &=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},\\
 A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 &=\CT_t
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 R_a(t)\,S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\,
 \bigl(h_a(t)-\lambda\bigr)^{k-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) consists of the Kac weights with
\(rs\leq N\), and \({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) contains the labels
attaining \(\lambda\).  For \(a=(r,s)\), set \(t=b-b_\star\),
\(h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\) and \(\ell_a=rs\).  \(R_a\) is the generic
residue, \(S_{a,M}\) the shifted Laurent block, \(\CT_t\) the constant term,
and only finitely many terms are nonzero.  In the generic recursion each
label \(a\) contributes a simple pole at \(h_a(b)\), and as \(b\to b_\star\)
the poles of the labels in \({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) converge to
\(\lambda\).  Expanding \(1/(\Delta-h_a(b))\) in powers of
\(h_a(b)-\lambda\) shows that colliding poles can yield terms of higher order
at \(\lambda\); the coefficient of \((\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\) is the moment
in~\eqref{eq:intro-main}, weighted by \((h_a(t)-\lambda)^{k-1}\).  A single
product \(R_aS_{a,N-\ell_a}\) can have poles in \(t\).  Since \(H_N\) is
regular in \(t\) at \(t=0\) over the field of rational functions in \(\Delta\)
and \(d\), uniqueness of the principal parts in \(\Delta\) makes the negative
powers of \(t\) cancel in each full moment, and the constant term is the value
of that moment at \(t=0\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:confluence} gives the same
formula for the sphere coefficients.  The example after
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} works this out at \(c=0\) and level
five: two residue terms each have a simple pole in \(t\), the poles cancel in
the first moment, and the second moment, which gives the double pole in
\(\Delta\), is the coefficient of \(t^{-1}\) in one of them times the
\(t\)-derivative of its Kac weight.

At a minimal-model charge and an interior Kac weight \(\lambda\), the
Feigin--Fuchs embedding diagram describes the Jantzen filtration
\(J^\bullet\) of the specialized Verma module
(Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} and Figure~\ref{fig:braided}).  Its dimensions
determine the multiplicities of the local Smith exponents \(e_j\) of the
Shapovalov matrix: \(\dim J^i_N=\#\{j:e_j\geq i\}\).  Thus the pole order of
\(H_N\) at \(\lambda\) is at most the Jantzen depth \(j_N(\lambda)\), the
number of layers that have reached level \(N\).  A pole of order \(m\) at
\(\lambda\) allows principal-part terms from \((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\) through
\((\Delta-\lambda)^{-m}\); at generic \(b\) all poles are simple.  Let
\(w^\pm_k\) be the \(k\)-th layer's singular vectors, at levels
\(\ell^\pm_k\), and put \(\ell_k=\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\).  For \(k\geq1\)
and \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\), Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-top}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^kH_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}
 \frac{\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}\bigl(c_\star,\lambda+\ell^\pm_k,d\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(\gamma^\pm_k\), nonzero for each sign in the sum, is the \(k\)-th
Jantzen norm of \(w^\pm_k\).  This is a fixed-\(c\) analogue of
Zamolodchikov's residue formula for the coefficient at the Jantzen bound.
For the sphere block, Theorems~\ref{thm:jantzen-bound}
and~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph} give the same bound and the same formula, with
the torus matrix element of \(w^\pm_k\) replaced by the product of its two
three-point factors.

For indeterminate external weights the bound \(j_N(\lambda)\) is attained at
every interior weight and every \(N\geq1\): Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp}
proves this for the torus block, and Theorems~\ref{thm:sharp-B}
and~\ref{thm:sharp-H} for the sphere block.  At specialized external weights
the pole can disappear: for the torus block at \(d=0\), \(H_N=0\) for
\(N\geq1\).  In the Ising model the first double poles at the energy, spin and
identity weights occur at levels \(7\), \(10\) and \(11\), in both blocks
(Propositions~\ref{prop:ising-double-poles} and~\ref{prop:ising-example}).

Zamolodchikov introduced residue recursions for sphere four-point
blocks~\cite{Zamolodchikov1984,Zamolodchikov1987}.  Cho, Collier and Yin give
central-charge recursions in the plumbing frame at arbitrary genus and
internal-weight recursions in the sphere linear and torus necklace
channels~\cite{ChoCollierYin2017}.  At rational \(b^2\), Javerzat,
Santachiara and Foda regularize sphere
examples~\cite[\S\S3.4--3.6]{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018},\footnote{Equations (32)--(34) of~\cite{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.}
and analyze the cancellation of the colliding poles of the torus block in
examples and for the identity
insertion~\cite[\S\S4--6]{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.\footnote{The limits of the resonant terms are (60)--(61) of~\cite{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.}
For \(d=0\) they also obtain the minimal-model characters by a
three-parameter limit of the generic torus recursion, and check the
coefficient identity following their (83) numerically up to
\(N\approx300\)~\cite[\S5]{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.
Stocco gives central-charge-pole-free expressions through fourth order,
conjectures their all-order form and proposes a way to compute
them~\cite[\S\S II.B--II.C and Appendix~A]{Stocco2022}.  In our comparison
(see the code availability section) his expressions reproduce \(H_2\) and
\(H_3\) identically and \(H_4\) at sample values of \(b\).  Ribault finds
cancellations and double poles of sphere blocks for generic external momenta
and then treats degenerate representations~\cite[\S\S3.2--3.3]{Ribault2019}.
He asks for a recursion at rational central charge that is manifestly finite
and exhibits the higher-order poles~\cite[end of \S3.2]{Ribault2019}.  The
confluence theorems compute the principal parts at each level by a finite
calculation in the generic residue data, higher-order poles included, and
the Jantzen theorems give their exact orders and top coefficients at
interior minimal-model weights.  The
irreducible minimal-model traces with a primary insertion are treated in a
separate paper~\cite{PolzerTraces2026}.

Part~I (Sections~\ref{sec:blocks}--\ref{sec:ising}) treats the torus block
and Part~II (Sections~\ref{sec:setup-sph}--\ref{sec:examples-sph}) the sphere
block.  Section~\ref{sec:blocks} fixes notation; Table~\ref{tab:notation}
lists the main symbols.  Section~\ref{sec:resonance} describes which Kac
labels give the same weight at fixed \(b_\star\), and
Section~\ref{sec:partial-fractions} proves the finite partial-fraction
expansion.  Section~\ref{sec:smith} relates the local Smith exponents of the
Shapovalov matrix to Jantzen layers and singular vectors,
Section~\ref{sec:generic-recursion} collects the generic residue data and the
generic recursion, and Section~\ref{sec:all-order} proves the confluence
formula.  Section~\ref{sec:mm-layers} gives the pole-order bound and its top
coefficient at interior minimal-model weights, Section~\ref{sec:sharpness}
proves that the bound is attained, and Section~\ref{sec:ising} treats the
Ising model.  Part~II follows the same order for the sphere block and uses
the results of Part~I on the Verma module.  Section~\ref{sec:scope} lists
what the paper leaves open.  Appendix~\ref{app:h13} contains the integrality
and level-polynomiality proofs used in Section~\ref{sec:sharpness}.

\section{Blocks and the reduced series}\label{sec:blocks}

\begin{definition}[Virasoro Verma module]\label{def:verma-module-B}
The Virasoro algebra has generators \(L_n\) (\(n\in\mathbb Z\)) and central
element \(C\), with
\([L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{C}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}\).
The Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) is generated by \(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) with
\(L_0\lvert\Delta\rangle=\Delta\lvert\Delta\rangle\),
\(L_n\lvert\Delta\rangle=0\) (\(n>0\)), on which \(C\) acts by the central
charge \(c\in\mathbb C\).
For a partition \(Y=(y_1\geq\cdots\geq y_\ell)\), set
\(L_{-Y}=L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_\ell}\), \(|Y|=\sum_i y_i\), and
\(\ell(Y)=\ell\).  Write \(\langle\Delta|\) for the linear functional on
\(M(c,\Delta)\) that maps \(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) to \(1\) and vanishes on
all positive levels, and \(\omega\) for the anti-involution of the enveloping
algebra with \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  The \emph{Shapovalov form} is the
symmetric bilinear form
\(\langle x\lvert\Delta\rangle,y\lvert\Delta\rangle\rangle
=\langle\Delta|\omega(x)y|\Delta\rangle\) on \(M(c,\Delta)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shapovalov and torus matrices]\label{def:matrices}
At level \(N\), for partitions \(|Y|=|Y'|=N\) and \(L_Y=L_{y_\ell}\cdots L_{y_1}\), let
\[
 G_N(c,\Delta)_{Y,Y'}=\langle\Delta|L_YL_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle,
 \qquad
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}=\langle\Delta|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle,
\]
where \(V_d(z)\) is primary with \(\langle\Delta|V_d(z)|\Delta\rangle=z^{-d}\).
Thus \(G_N\) is the Gram matrix of the Shapovalov form at level \(N\).  The
matrix \(\rho_N\) is computed from the primary Ward identity, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}; we call the
underlying bilinear form the \emph{torus form}.  Whenever \(G_N\) is invertible, put
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=\sum_{|Y|=|Y'|=N}(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1})_{Y,Y'}\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced block]\label{def:eta-reduced}
The torus one-point block is
\(q^{\Delta-c/24}\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)\), where
\(\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)=\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N\).
Set \(\phi(q)=\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)=\sum_{j\geq0}\eta_jq^j\) and define the
eta-reduced series
\(H_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)=\phi(q)\,\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)\).
The coefficient of \(q^N\) in \(H_\Delta^{(c)}\) is \(H_N=\sum_{j=0}^N\eta_jF_{N-j}\),
with \(\eta_0=1\) and \(\eta_1=\eta_2=-1\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial matrix entries]\label{lem:polynomial-entries}
For fixed \(N\), every entry of \(G_N(c,\Delta)\) belongs to
\(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta]\), and every entry of
\(\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,d]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Commute positive modes to the ket, where they annihilate
\(|\Delta\rangle\). The surviving \(L_0\) and central terms act by
\(\Delta\) and \(c\), giving polynomial Gram entries. For the torus entries,
also commute through the insertion using
\([L_n,V_d(z)]=z^n(z\partial_z+(n+1)d)V_d(z)\), and move the remaining
negative modes to the bra. Each Ward commutator removes a mode, and the
remaining derivatives of \(z^{-d}\) at \(z=1\) are polynomial in \(d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terminology and notation]\label{rem:notation}
We call \(\Delta\) the \emph{internal weight} and \(d\) the \emph{external
weight}.  The \(L_0\)-offset \(N\) of a graded component is called its
\emph{level}.  For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), we write
\(\ell_a=rs\) for the level of its singular vector.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and
Cramer's rule, each \(F_N\) and \(H_N\) is a rational function of
\((c,\Delta)\) and a polynomial in \(d\), and \(H_0=1\).  For a rational
function \(f\) of \(\Delta\) and a point \(\lambda\),
\(\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) denotes the principal part of \(f\) at \(\lambda\),
the sum of the negative-power terms of its Laurent expansion.
\(\Res_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) denotes the coefficient of
\((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\).
Unless \(d\) is specialized, it is an indeterminate and pole
orders in \(\Delta\) are understood over \(\mathbb Q(c,d)\), or over
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\) after fixing \(b=b_\star\).  We write \(\Vir_-\) for the span
of the \(L_{-n}\), \(n\geq1\), call \(\{L_{-Y}\lvert\Delta\rangle\}\) the PBW
(Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt) basis, and \(\ord_x\) is the order at \(x=0\).
\end{remark}

\section{Fixed specializations and collision classes}
\label{sec:resonance}

\begin{definition}[Kac weights and fixed specializations]
\label{def:fixed-specialization}
For \(b\in\mathbb C^\times\), set
\(c(b)=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2\) and
\(h_{r,s}(b)=\frac14((b+b^{-1})^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2)\),
which depend on \(b\) only through \(b^2\).  Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\),
put \(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and
\(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)=\{h_{r,s}(b_\star):r,s>0,\ rs\leq N\}\), and for
\(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) define the finite collision class
\[
 {\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2:
      rs\leq N,\ h_{r,s}(b_\star)=\lambda\}.
\]
As \(b\to b_\star\), the poles \(h_{r,s}(b)\) of the generic recursion at the
labels in \({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) converge to \(\lambda\); these are
the poles that collide.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal-model collision classes]
\label{prop:minimal-collisions}
Let \(p,p'\) be coprime positive integers and choose
\(b_\star^2=-p'/p\).  Then
\[
 c_\star=1-\frac{6(p'-p)^2}{pp'},\qquad
 h_{r,s}(b_\star)
 =\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}.
\]
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\), their specialized weights
are equal if and only if, for some \(n\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 (r',s')=(r+pn,s+p'n)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (r',s')=(pn-r,p'n-s).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formulas follow by substituting \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) in
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.  Equality of the two weights is
equivalent to \(p'r'-ps'=\pm(p'r-ps)\).  For the plus sign, coprimality gives
\((r'-r,s'-s)=(pn,p'n)\); for the minus sign it gives
\((r'+r,s'+s)=(pn,p'n)\).  Conversely, both operations preserve
\((p'r-ps)^2\) and hence the weight.
\end{proof}


\section{Coefficientwise partial fractions}\label{sec:partial-fractions}

\begin{lemma}[Large internal weight]\label{lem:large-weight}
For fixed \(c,d,N\), \(F_N(c,\Delta,d)\to p(N)\), the number of partitions
of \(N\), and \(H_N(c,\Delta,d)\to\delta_{N,0}\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rescale \(L_{-Y}|\Delta\rangle\) by \(\Delta^{-\ell(Y)/2}\). Each factor \(\Delta\) in a Gram entry comes from a
commutator producing \(L_0\) and uses at least one original mode on each
side, and each original mode is used once. The degree is at most \(\min(\ell(Y),\ell(Y'))\), so a rescaled entry
vanishes in the limit if \(\ell(Y)\ne\ell(Y')\). At equal lengths, a term of top
degree pairs equal positive and negative indices via \(2jL_0\) in
\([L_j,L_{-j}]\). Thus it occurs only on the diagonal, where the leading
coefficient is \(\prod_j m_j(Y)!\,(2j)^{m_j(Y)}\), with \(m_j(Y)\) the
multiplicity of \(j\) in \(Y\).

For \(\rho_N\), the terms with no Ward commutator reproduce the Gram
terms since \(\langle\Delta|V_d(1)|\Delta\rangle=1\). Each Ward
commutator spends a mode without producing a factor \(\Delta\), lowering
the degree below the top degree at equal lengths. The two rescaled matrices
therefore tend to the same invertible diagonal matrix \(D_N\).
Then \(F_N\to\Tr(D_N^{-1}D_N)=p(N)\), and
\((\sum_j\eta_jq^j)(\sum_kp(k)q^k)=1\) gives the limit of \(H_N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite pole expansion at a fixed specialization]
\label{thm:general-partial-fractions}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\).  Then \(H_0=1\),
and for every \(N\geq1\) there are unique coefficients
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\)
(\(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\), \(k\geq1\)), only finitely many nonzero,
such that
\[
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}\ \sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\]
The largest \(k\) with \(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\neq0\) (or \(0\) if all
vanish) is the pole multiplicity \(m^\star_{\lambda,N}\) of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda\).
Consequently
\(H_\Delta^{(c_\star)}(d\mid q)=1+\sum_{\lambda,k}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)
(\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\) with
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)=\sum_{N\geq1}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)q^N\),
locally finite coefficientwise in \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries},
\(F_N=\Tr(G_N^{-1}\rho_N^{\mathsf T})\) is rational in
\(\Delta\), and its poles are contained in the zero locus of
\(\det G_N\).  The Kac determinant formula
\cite{Kac1979}, \cite[\S3.2, Theorem~3.1]{FeiginFuchs1982}, in the form of
\cite[Theorem~4.2, (4.16) and (4.21)]{IoharaKoga2011}, gives
\(\det G_N(c(b),\Delta)=C_N\prod_{rs\leq N}(\Delta-h_{r,s}(b))^{p(N-rs)}\)
with \(C_N\neq0\) independent of \(\Delta\).  At \(c=c_\star\), the determinant
roots lie in \(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\), and the finite sum defining \(H_N\)
introduces no other poles.  For \(N\geq1\), Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} rules
out a polynomial part, so the partial fractions are unique.

The field \(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)\) contains \(c_\star\) and every pole
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\).  The matrix entries belong to
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[\Delta,d]\), and \(\det G_N\) does not involve
\(d\).  Laurent expansion at each pole therefore gives
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\).
Coefficientwise local finiteness follows because \(rs\leq N\) leaves
only finitely many labels.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} in Section~\ref{sec:all-order}
computes the coefficients \(A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\) as finite constant-term
expressions in the generic residue data.


\section{Smith exponents, Jantzen layers, and singular vectors}\label{sec:smith}

This section holds at every central charge.  It relates the local Smith
exponents of the Shapovalov matrix to the Jantzen filtration in the internal
weight, and computes the torus form and the first Jantzen form on a singular
submodule.  Section~\ref{sec:generic-recursion} uses it at generic charge
and Section~\ref{sec:mm-layers} at minimal-model charges.


\begin{lemma}[Pole order from local invariant factors]\label{lem:smith}
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[x]]\), and let \(G(x)\in M_m(R)\) have nonzero determinant
and Smith exponents \(0\leq e_1\leq\cdots\leq e_m\).  Then
\(\ord_x\det G=\sum_{i=1}^m e_i\), \(\dim\ker G(0)=\#\{i:e_i>0\}\), and
\(G(x)^{-1}\) has pole order \(e_m\).  In particular, if
\(\ord_x\det G=\dim\ker G(0)\), every positive Smith exponent equals \(1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(R\) is a discrete valuation ring, there are
\(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R)\) with
\(UGV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_m})\).  Taking determinants,
reducing modulo \(x\), and inverting give the three assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Jantzen layers in the internal weight]
\label{def:jantzen-layers}
Fix \((c,h)\) and identify \(M(c,h+x)_N\) with \(\mathbb C^{p(N)}\) in the PBW
basis.  For \(i\geq0\), let \(J^i_N(c,h)\) be the set of \(v\in M(c,h)_N\)
admitting a polynomial family \(v(x)\) with \(v(0)=v\) and
\(G_N(c,h+x)\,v(x)\in x^i\,\mathbb C[x]^{p(N)}\), and put
\(J^i(c,h)=\bigoplus_{N\geq0}J^i_N(c,h)\).
\end{definition}

For \(c\neq1,25\), this fixed-charge filtration agrees with the Jantzen
filtration of~\cite[\S5.5, (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011}, and it is a Jantzen
filtration along a curve in the sense of~\cite[\S4 and \S5.2]{Astashkevich1997}.

\begin{lemma}[Smith exponents and the Jantzen filtration]
\label{lem:jantzen-smith}
Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), with local Smith exponents
\(e_1,\ldots,e_{p(N)}\) at \(x=0\).  Then
\(\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\#\{j:e_j\geq i\}\) (\(i\geq1\)) and
\(\sum_{i\geq1}\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\ord_x\det G_N(c,h+x)\).
Each \(J^i(c,h)\) is a submodule of \(M(c,h)\), and \(J^1(c,h)\) is the
radical of the Shapovalov form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(UG_NV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_j})\) over \(\mathbb C[[x]]\).
With \(v(x)=V(x)w(x)\), the layer condition is
\(w_j\in x^{\max(0,i-e_j)}\mathbb C[[x]]\). Truncation modulo \(x^i\)
gives polynomial families, so \(J^i_N=V(0)\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_j:e_j\geq i\}\)
for the coordinate vectors \(\epsilon_j\). Counting and summing gives
both dimension identities by Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}.

If \(v(x)\) is a family for \(v\), then \(L_nv(x)\) specializes to
\(L_nv\). It is a polynomial family, and for every PBW vector \(y\),
\(\langle y,L_nv(x)\rangle_{h+x}=\langle L_{-n}y,v(x)\rangle_{h+x}\in
x^i\mathbb C[x]\). Thus the layers are submodules. At \(i=1\) the
condition is \(G_N(c,h)v=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{example*}[Level two at \(c=0\)]
In the basis \(L_{-1}^2|\Delta\rangle\), \(L_{-2}|\Delta\rangle\),
\[
 G_2(0,\Delta)=\begin{pmatrix}4\Delta(2\Delta+1)&6\Delta\\6\Delta&4\Delta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det G_2(0,\Delta)=4\Delta^2(8\Delta-5).
\]
At \(c=0\) the labels \((1,1)\) and \((1,2)\) both give the weight \(0\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions} with \((p,p')=(2,3)\)), and the
determinant vanishes to order two there.  Since \(G_2(0,0)=0\), the kernel is
two-dimensional, so \(J^1_2(0,0)=M(0,0)_2\) and both local Smith exponents
equal \(1\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}; indeed
\[
 G_2(0,\Delta)^{-1}
 =\frac{1}{\Delta(8\Delta-5)}
  \begin{pmatrix}1&-\tfrac32\\-\tfrac32&2\Delta+1\end{pmatrix}
\]
has a simple pole at \(\Delta=0\).  The order of vanishing of the determinant
is two, and the pole order is one; the Jantzen layers measure the second.  At
\(\lambda=0\) the second Jantzen layer starts at level five
(Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}), and for indeterminate \(d\) the first
double pole of \(H_N\) at \(\Delta=0\) occurs at \(N=5\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp}).
\end{example*}


\begin{lemma}[Jantzen forms and the top coefficient]
\label{lem:top-coefficient}
Fix \((c,h)\).  For \(i\geq1\) and \(u,v\in J^i_N(c,h)\), choose families
\(u(x),v(x)\) as in Definition~\ref{def:jantzen-layers} and put
\(B^i(u,v)=[x^i]\,v(x)^{\mathsf T}G_N(c,h+x)\,u(x)\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(B^i\) is a well-defined symmetric form, and
\(B^i(L_{-n}u,v)=B^i(u,L_nv)\) for \(n>0\), \(u\in J^i_{N-n}(c,h)\), and
\(v\in J^i_N(c,h)\).
\item[(ii)] Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), and let \(k\geq1\) be
the largest local Smith exponent at \(x=0\).  Then \(B^k\) is
nondegenerate on \(J^k_N(c,h)\), and
\([x^{-k}]\,G_N(c,h+x)^{-1}=\iota\,(B^k)^{-1}\iota^*\),
where \(\iota\colon J^k_N(c,h)\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^{p(N)}\) is the
inclusion.
\item[(iii)] Write \(G_N(c,h+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3)\).  Then
\(B^1(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}G_1u\) for \(u,v\in J^1_N(c,h)\), and
\(B^2(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}(G_2u+G_1u_1)\) for \(u,v\in J^2_N(c,h)\) and every
\(u_1\) with \(G_0u_1=-G_1u\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Changing \(u(x)\) by \(xf(x)\) changes the pairing by
\(x(G_Nv(x))^{\mathsf T}f(x)\in x^{i+1}\mathbb C[x]\), since \(G_N\) is symmetric;
by symmetry the same holds for \(v(x)\). As in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}, applying \(L_{-n}\) or \(L_n\) gives families
for the descendants. Contravariance gives the stated identity at order \(x^i\).

(ii) In the Smith bases, \(G_N^{-1}=V\operatorname{diag}(x^{-e_j})U\).
Its coefficient \(S=[x^{-k}]G_N^{-1}=V(0)EU(0)\), with \(E\) projecting to
the indices \(e_j=k\), is symmetric with column space \(J_N^k(c,h)\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}.
Thus \(S=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}u_au_b^{\mathsf T}\) for a basis \((u_a)\) of
\(J_N^k\). For a family \(u(x)\) write \(G_Nu(x)=x^kg(x)\).
At \(x=0\), \(u=Sg(0)\) and \(B^k(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}g(0)\).
For \(u=u_c\) this gives \(u_c=\sum_{a,b}u_aC_{ab}B^k(u_c,u_b)\), so
\(C\) is the inverse matrix of \(B^k\).

(iii) For \(J^1_N\) use constant families. For \(J^2_N\) use
\(u+xu_1\) and \(v+xv_1\) with \(G_0u_1=-G_1u\), \(G_0v_1=-G_1v\).
The \(x^2\) coefficient is
\(v^{\mathsf T}(G_2u+G_1u_1)+v_1^{\mathsf T}(G_1u+G_0u_1)\), with the
last term zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Torus form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-factorization}
Let \(\chi\in M(c,h)\) be a singular vector of level \(\ell\), normalized
by a fixed PBW coefficient, and put \(P_\chi(d)=\langle\chi|V_d(1)|\chi\rangle\).
Then for all partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\(\langle\chi|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\chi\rangle=P_\chi(d)\,\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\chi\) is singular of weight \(h+\ell\), the map
\(L_{-Y}|h+\ell\rangle\mapsto L_{-Y}\chi\) is a module homomorphism.
The torus form pulled back along it in both arguments obeys the Ward
recursion of \(\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)\), which determines every entry from the
highest-weight value: \(P_\chi(d)\) for the pullback and \(1\) for \(\rho_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First crossing form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-crossing}
Fix \(c\) and let \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\in M(c,h_0)\) be singular of level
\(\ell\geq1\), with \(P\in U(\Vir_-)\) held fixed as \(h\) varies.  Set
\(\beta_\chi=\left.\frac{d}{dh}\langle h|\omega(P)P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}\),
where \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  Then for all partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\(\left.\frac{d}{dh}\langle h|\omega(P)L_YL_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}
=\beta_\chi\,G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The vectors \(u=L_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\) and \(v=L_{-Y}P|h\rangle\) have
constant PBW coordinates and specialize at \(h_0\) into the submodule
generated by \(\chi\), hence into the radical.
Using them as constant families identifies the derivative with \(B^1(u,v)\)
and gives \(\beta_\chi=B^1(\chi,\chi)\). By contravariance of \(B^1\) and
singularity of \(\chi\),
\[
 B^1(L_{-Y'}\chi,L_{-Y}\chi)=B^1(\chi,L_YL_{-Y'}\chi)
 =G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}\,\beta_\chi .
\]
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Torus norm of a singular vector]\label{lem:singular-degree}
Let \(w\in M(c,h)\) be a nonzero singular vector of level \(\ell\geq1\).
Then the coefficient \(a_w\) of \(L_{-1}^\ell\lvert h\rangle\) in \(w\) is
nonzero, and \(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle\) is a polynomial in \(d\) of
degree exactly \(2\ell\) with leading coefficient \(a_w^2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Call \(\ell(Y)\) the length of the PBW vector \(L_{-Y}\lvert h\rangle\).
The commutator of two modes is a single mode or a scalar, so a product of
\(K\) negative modes applied to \(\lvert h\rangle\) equals its ordered PBW
monomial plus vectors of length less than \(K\), and a positive mode does
not increase the length.  Let \(K\) be the largest length occurring in
\(w\), and encode its length-\(K\) part
\(\sum_{\ell(Y)=K}a_YL_{-Y}\lvert h\rangle\) as the nonzero polynomial
\(\sigma=\sum_{\ell(Y)=K}a_Yx_{y_1}\cdots x_{y_K}\) in commuting variables
\(x_1,x_2,\ldots\).  Let \(j\geq1\).  Moving \(L_j\) to the right through
\(L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_K}\lvert h\rangle\), a factor \(L_{-m}\) with \(m>j\)
is replaced by \((m+j)L_{-(m-j)}\); for \(m=j\) the commutator acts by a
scalar, and for \(m<j\) it is the positive mode \(L_{j-m}\); both lower the
length.  Hence the length-\(K\) part of \(L_jw\) is encoded by
\(D_j\sigma\), where
\(D_j=\sum_{m>j}(m+j)\,x_{m-j}\,\partial/\partial x_m\), and singularity of
\(w\) gives \(D_j\sigma=0\) for all \(j\geq1\).  If a variable \(x_m\) with
\(m\geq2\) occurs in \(\sigma\), let \(m\) be the largest one and
\(j=m-1\); then \(D_j\sigma=(2m-1)\,x_1\,\partial\sigma/\partial x_m\neq0\),
a contradiction.  So \(\sigma\) is a multiple of \(x_1^K\), and comparing
levels gives \(K=\ell\) and \(a_w\neq0\).

For the second assertion, evaluate \(\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\) by the Ward
recursion in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}.  When a mode
\(L_{\pm n}\), \(n>0\), is commuted through \(V_d(z)\) inside a matrix
element between vectors of levels \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), the commutator
\([L_{\pm n},V_d(z)]=z^{\pm n}(z\partial_z+(1\pm n)d)V_d(z)\) acts on
\(z^{m_1-m_2-d}\) and contributes, with the sign of the commutation, the
factor \(nd\pm(m_1-m_2)\), of degree one in \(d\) with leading coefficient
\(n\).  Every other step (a commutator of two modes, the action of \(L_0\)
or of the central element, or an annihilation at \(\lvert h\rangle\) or
\(\langle h|\)) removes at least one mode without producing a factor
\(d\).  Since \(\langle h|V_d(1)|h\rangle=1\), the entry
\(\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\) has degree at most \(\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')\) in
\(d\), and the coefficient of \(d^{\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')}\) is
\(\prod_iy_i\prod_jy'_j\).  In
\(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle=\sum_{Y,Y'}a_Ya_{Y'}\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\)
the only pair with \(\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')=2\ell\) is \(Y=Y'=(1^\ell)\).
\end{proof}

\section{Generic residue data and the generic recursion}\label{sec:generic-recursion}

\begin{definition}[Generic residue data]
\label{def:generic-residue}
With \(c(b)\) and \(h_{r,s}(b)\) from Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}
and \(\Lambda^2=(b+b^{-1})^2-4d\), define
\[
 {\mathcal N}_{r,s}(b,d)
 =\prod_{\substack{1\leq k\leq2r-1\\k\ {\rm odd}}}
  \prod_{\substack{1\leq l\leq2s-1\\l\ {\rm odd}}}
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}\,
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4},
\]
and \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b){\mathcal N}_{r,s}(b,d)\),
where
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)=\frac12\prod_{(a,b')\in[1-r,r]\times[1-s,s]\setminus\{(0,0),(r,s)\}}(ab+b'b^{-1})^{-1}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Crossing scalar and torus norm of singular vectors]
\label{prop:imported-normalizations}
For positive \(r,s\) and generic \(b\), let \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) be the
singular vector of level \(rs\) in \(M(c(b),h_{r,s}(b))\) whose
\(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient is \(1\); it exists and is unique by
\cite[Propositions~5.1, 5.2 and Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}
(with \(t=-b^2\) in their (5.4)).  Let
\(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\) be its crossing scalar from
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.  Then
\begin{align}
 \beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}&={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)^{-1},\label{eq:crossing-normalization}\\
 \langle\chi_{r,s}(b)|V_d(1)|\chi_{r,s}(b)\rangle&={\mathcal N}_{r,s}(b,d).\label{eq:fusion-normalization}
\end{align}
The identities specialize wherever the normalized singular vector is regular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation (7) of~\cite[\S3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010} uses the reciprocal
norm derivative at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}\) with the PBW coefficients fixed,
which is \(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}^{-1}\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.
The product following their (7), for \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\), is
term for term \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\), which proves~\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization}.

With \(d=((b+b^{-1})^2-\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2)/4\), we have
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2=\Lambda^2\). For each odd \(k,l\) in
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the four factors
\((\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}\pm(kb\pm lb^{-1}))/2\) of their (8) multiply to
\(\frac14(\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2)\cdot\frac14(\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2)\); the
product over \(k,l\) proves~\eqref{eq:fusion-normalization}.  Their (9) is the
recursion of Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} below.
Both identities are rational in \(b\) since the normalized singular-vector
coefficients solve a finite linear system over \(\mathbb Q(b)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Distinct weights and no shifted self-poles away from
rational \(b^2\)]
\label{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
For positive labels \((r,s)\) and \((u,v)\), \(h_{r,s}(b)+rs-h_{u,v}(b)\)
is not the zero rational function of \(b\), nor is
\(h_{r,s}(b)-h_{u,v}(b)\) when \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\).  If
\(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), then
neither function vanishes at \(b_\star\), and every
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular and nonzero at \(b_\star\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Kac formula gives \(h_{r,s}(b)+rs=h_{r,-s}(b)\), which equals
\(h_{u,v}(b)\) exactly when \((rb-sb^{-1})^2=(ub+vb^{-1})^2\), that is,
\(rb-sb^{-1}=\pm(ub+vb^{-1})\), since \(\mathbb C(b)\) is an integral
domain.  Equivalently
\((r\mp u)b^2=s\pm v\).  As an identity in \(b\) this contradicts
\(r,s,u,v>0\), and at a point it forces \(b^2=(s\pm v)/(r\mp u)\in\mathbb Q\);
here \(r\mp u\neq0\), because \(r=u\) would force \(s+v=0\) and
\(r+u>0\).  Similarly
\(h_{r,s}=h_{u,v}\) means \((r\mp u)b^2=\pm v-s\); as an identity this
forces \((r,s)=(u,v)\), and at a point with \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\) it forces
\(b^2\in\mathbb Q\).  Each factor \(ab+b'b^{-1}\) of
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is finite at \(b_\star\neq0\), and it vanishes only
if \(a\neq0\) and \(b_\star^2=-b'/a\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic eta-reduced recursion]
\label{prop:generic-recursion}
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For all \(b\) outside a finite set of algebraic values,
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{a=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\ \ell_a=rs\leq N}}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)}{\Delta-h_{r,s}(b)}\,
 H_{N-\ell_a}\bigl(c(b),h_{r,s}(b)+rs,d\bigr)
\]
as an identity of rational functions of \(\Delta\), polynomial in
\(d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exclude \(b=0\).  For all labels of level at most \(N\), exclude also the
zeros and poles in \(\mathbb Q(b)\) of the nonzero differences \(h_a-h_g\)
and \(z_a-h_g\), where \(z_a=h_a+\ell_a\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}), of the nonzero
coefficients of \({\mathcal R}_a\) and \({\mathcal A}_a\), and of the
nonzero normalized singular-vector coefficients.  These are finitely many
algebraic values.  For the remaining \(b\) the weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with
\(rs\leq N\) are pairwise distinct, and no shifted weight
\(z_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b)+rs\) is a degenerate weight of level at most \(N\).

Fix such a \(b\), write \(c=c(b)\), and take \(a=(r,s)\) with \(rs\leq N\).
By distinctness, the Kac determinant has valuation \(p(N-rs)\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)\).
The level-\(N\) image of the singular embedding \(j_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\) is a
\(p(N-rs)\)-dimensional subspace of the specialized Gram kernel by
injectivity~\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} the nullity is at most the valuation, so the kernel
is that image and every positive local Smith exponent equals \(1\).
With \(k=1\), Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient} gives the inverse Gram residue
\(\iota(B^1)^{-1}\iota^*\) on this kernel, and by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(iii) and~\ref{lem:singular-crossing},
\(B^1=\beta_{\chi}\,G_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s})\) in the basis
\(L_{-Z}\chi_{r,s}(b)\), where
\(\beta_\chi^{-1}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\) by
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization}.  Taking the trace of the residue of \(G_N^{-1}\) against \(\rho_N\) and
factoring the pulled-back torus form by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization}
and \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives
\(\Res_{\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)}F_N(c,\Delta,d)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)\,F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d)\).
The difference between \(F_N\) and the sum of these principal parts has no
finite pole and tends to \(p(N)\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight}), so
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=p(N)+\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}({\mathcal R}_a(b,d)/(\Delta-h_a(b)))F_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d)\).
Convolving with \(\eta_j\) gives the stated recursion because
\(\sum_j\eta_jp(N-j)=\delta_{N,0}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing at \(d=0\) and the first \(d\)-derivative]
\label{lem:d-derivatives}
For \(N\geq1\), \(H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)\) vanishes at \(d=0\), and, as
rational functions of \((b,\Delta)\),
\[
 \partial_dH_N\big|_{d=0}
 =-\sum_{rs=N}\frac{rb^2+s}{2(b^2+1)(\Delta-h_{r,s}(b))}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x,y)=xb+yb^{-1}\) and \(B=[1-r,r]\times[1-s,s]\).  Since
\(\Lambda^2=\varphi(1,1)^2-4d\), at \(d=0\) the factors of
\({\mathcal N}_{r,s}\) in Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} are
\(\frac14(\varphi(1,1)^2-\varphi(k,\pm l)^2)
=\frac14\varphi(1-k,1\mp l)\varphi(1+k,1\pm l)\), and over the odd \(k,l\) and
both signs these products run once over \(\varphi(2a,2b')\),
\((a,b')\in B\).  The factor with \((k,\pm l)=(1,1)\) equals \(-d\).  Hence
every \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\) vanishes at \(d=0\), and comparison with
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\), whose product runs over
\(B\setminus\{(0,0),(r,s)\}\), gives
\(\partial_d{\mathcal R}_{r,s}|_{d=0}=-\varphi(r,s)/(2\varphi(1,1))\), the
displayed coefficient.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} and
induction on \(N\), \(H_N\) vanishes at \(d=0\) for \(N\geq1\), and
differentiating the recursion there leaves only the labels with \(rs=N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Regular normalized singular vectors]
\label{lem:regular-everywhere}
For every positive label \((r,s)\) and every \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\),
the normalized singular vector \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} is regular at \(b_\star\),
and its value is a singular vector of level \(rs\) in
\(M(c_\star,h_{r,s}(b_\star))\) with \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\).
Hence \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} holds at \(b_\star\), and so does
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization} when \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular
there.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e\geq0\) be minimal such that \((b-b_\star)^e\chi_{r,s}(b)\) is
regular at \(b_\star\), with value \(w\neq0\).  The conditions
\(L_1v=L_2v=0\) are linear with coefficients Laurent-polynomial in \(b\), and
\(L_1,L_2\) generate the positive modes, so \(w\) is singular in
\(M(c_\star,h_{r,s}(b_\star))\).  The \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient of \(w\) is
\((b-b_\star)^e\) evaluated at \(b_\star\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree} it
cannot vanish; hence \(e=0\).  The rational identities of
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} then specialize as stated.
\end{proof}


\section{All-order confluent recursion}\label{sec:all-order}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}: at a fixed
parameter \(b_\star\) (central charge \(c_\star\)), the principal parts of \(H_N\) are obtained by expanding the
generic recursion in \(t=b-b_\star\), grouping the labels whose poles tend to
the same weight \(\lambda\), and taking the coefficient of \(t^0\) in the
moments of each group.  Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} writes the
shifted coefficients that enter the recursion as Laurent series in \(t\).
Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments} defines the moments.  The proof of
the theorem uses uniqueness of partial fractions and
Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}.  The example after the proof works out
the case \(c=0\), \(N=5\).  Section~\ref{sec:truncation} at the end of the
section bounds the poles in \(t\), gives an a priori bound on the pole order
in \(\Delta\), and gives the order in \(t\) to which each Laurent series has
to be expanded.

\begin{definition}[Shifted Laurent recursion]\label{def:shifted-laurent}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), put \(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and
\(t=b-b_\star\), and work in \(K_\star((t))\) with
\(K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\), \(d\) indeterminate.  (The working field
contains \(b_\star\) because \(t=b-b_\star\); the final coefficients lie in
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}.)
For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), write \(\ell_a=rs\),
\(h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\), \(R_a(t)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b_\star+t,d)\), and
\(z_a(t)=h_a(t)+\ell_a\).
Define the shifted Laurent blocks recursively by \(S_{a,0}(t)=1\) and, for
\(M\geq1\),
\[
 S_{a,M}(t)
 =\sum_{\substack{g=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_g=uv\leq M}}
 \frac{R_g(t)}
 {z_a(t)-h_g(t)}S_{g,M-\ell_g}(t).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole} ensures each denominator is nonzero.
\end{definition}

The series \(S_{a,M}(t)\) is the coefficient \(H_M\) evaluated at the shifted
weight \(z_a(t)\) and expanded in \(t\) (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}).  A single summand of the recursion can
have a pole at \(t=0\), for example from a pole of \(R_g\), from a denominator
\(z_a(t)-h_g(t)\) that vanishes at \(t=0\), or from the nested block
\(S_{g,M-\ell_g}\).  The full block \(S_{a,M}\) is
regular at \(t=0\) when \(z_a(0)\) differs from every \(h_g(0)\) with
\(\ell_g\leq M\), since then \(\det G_M(c_\star,z_a(0))\neq0\); when
\(z_a(0)=h_g(0)\) for such a \(g\), it can have a pole, although cancellations
may remove it.

\begin{definition}[Collision moments]\label{def:collision-moments}
For \(N\geq1\), \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\), and
\(a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), put
\(B_{a,N}(t)=R_a(t)S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\) and
\(\delta_a(t)=h_a(t)-\lambda\in tK_\star[[t]]\).  For \(k\geq1\), the
\emph{collision moments} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-moments}
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 =\CT_{t}
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1},
\end{equation}
where \(\CT_t\) extracts the coefficient of \(t^0\).
\end{definition}

The \(k\)-th collision moment collects what the labels whose poles converge
to \(\lambda\) contribute to the coefficient of \((\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\): the
expansion \(1/(\Delta-\lambda-\delta_a)=\sum_{j\geq0}\delta_a^j(\Delta-\lambda)^{-j-1}\)
puts \(B_{a,N}\delta_a^{k-1}\) at that order.

We can now state the main result of Part~I.

\begin{theorem}[Finite algebraic confluence at fixed \(b_\star\)]
\label{thm:general-confluence}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(N\geq1\).  For every
\(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) and \(k\geq1\), the
partial-fraction coefficients of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}
are the collision moments~\eqref{eq:collision-moments}:
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\).  Hence only
finitely many collision moments are nonzero, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k\geq1}
   \frac{\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\end{equation}
If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), every collision class is a singleton,
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=0\) for \(k\geq2\), and
\eqref{eq:general-pole-free-recursion} is
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} evaluated at \(b=b_\star\).
\end{theorem}

The computation of the right side is finite: the recursion of
Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} lowers the level at each step, and
Section~\ref{sec:truncation} gives a truncation order for every Laurent germ
that occurs and a bound on the \(k\) with
\(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\neq0\).  The proof uses one elementary fact about such
germs.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise pole support of a collision germ]
\label{lem:collision-pole-support}
Let \(B(t)\in K_\star((t))\) and
\(\delta(t)\in tK_\star[[t]]\).  Every coefficient of
\(B(t)/(x-\delta(t))=\sum_{j\geq0}B(t)\delta(t)^jx^{-j-1}\), as a Laurent
series in \(t\), is a finite \(K_\star\)-linear combination of
\(x^{-1},x^{-2},\ldots\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\ord_tB\geq-v\), the term \(B\delta^j\) has valuation at least
\(j-v\), so only \(j\leq m+v\) contribute to the coefficient of \(t^m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}]
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For generic \(b\), Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}
and induction on \(M\) give \(S_{a,M}(t)=H_M(c(b),z_a(t),d)\) as rational
functions of \(b\), and grouping by limiting weights yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-grouped}
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}{\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N},\qquad
 {\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-h_a(t)},
\end{equation}
as rational functions of \((b,\Delta,d)\). Work in
\(K_\star(\Delta)((t))\) and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}
with \(x=\Delta-\lambda\).

By Cramer's rule the denominator of each \(F_m\), \(m\leq N\), divides
\(\det G_m\). The Kac exponents \(p(m-rs)\) are nondecreasing in \(m\), so
\(\det G_N\) is a common denominator for the left side of
\eqref{eq:generic-grouped}. It is nonzero in \(K_\star(\Delta)\) at
\(t=0\), so the left side is regular there with constant term
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\). On the right, by Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support} and
Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments}, each group has constant term
\(\sum_{k\geq1}\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)(\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\),
a finite sum.  By uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}
these are its principal parts.  This gives \(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}
=A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\).

If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
shows that every class is a singleton, every \(R_g\) is regular at \(t=0\), and
no shifted denominator vanishes there.  By induction on \(M\), every
\(S_{a,M}\) is then regular at \(t=0\), with
\(S_{a,M}(0)=H_M(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\).  So \(B_{a,N}\) is regular, the
moments with \(k\geq2\) vanish because \(\delta_a\in tK_\star[[t]]\), and the
sole moment is \(R_a(0)H_{N-\ell_a}(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{example*}[Two poles in \(b\) that make a double pole in \(\Delta\)]
Let \(b_\star^2=-3/2\), so that \(c_\star=0\) and
\(h_{r,s}(b_\star)=((3r-2s)^2-1)/24\), take \(N=5\) and \(\lambda=0\), and keep
\(d\) indeterminate.  The labels with \(rs\leq5\) and weight \(0\) are \((1,1)\)
and \((1,2)\), and every \(R_g\) with \(\ell_g\leq5\) is regular at \(t=0\).
Since \(S_{a,M}\) is \(H_M\) at the shifted weight \(z_a\),
\[
 B_{(1,1),5}=R_{(1,1)}(t)\,H_4\bigl(c(b),1,d\bigr),\qquad
 B_{(1,2),5}=R_{(1,2)}(t)\,H_3\bigl(c(b),h_{1,2}(b)+2,d\bigr),
\]
with \(b=b_\star+t\).  The coefficient \(H_4\) has a simple pole at
\(\Delta=h_{1,4}(b)\) with residue \(R_{(1,4)}(t)\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}), and \(h_{1,4}(b_\star)=1\).  The
coefficient \(H_3\) has a simple pole at \(\Delta=h_{3,1}(b)\) with residue
\(R_{(3,1)}(t)\), and
\(h_{3,1}(b_\star)=2=h_{1,2}(b_\star)+2\).  No other shifted denominator in the
two recursions vanishes at \(t=0\).  Write \(h'_{r,s}\) for the derivative of
\(h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\) at \(t=0\) and \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\) for
\(R_{(r,s)}(0)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b_\star,d)\).  Then \(h'_{1,4}=5h'_{1,2}\) and
\(h'_{3,1}=-6h'_{1,2}\), so
\[
 B_{(1,1),5}(t)=-\frac{{\mathcal R}_{1,1}{\mathcal R}_{1,4}}{5h'_{1,2}\,t}+O(1),\qquad
 B_{(1,2),5}(t)=\frac{{\mathcal R}_{1,2}{\mathcal R}_{3,1}}{7h'_{1,2}\,t}+O(1).
\]
Neither term has a value at \(b_\star\).  The first moment
\(B_{(1,1),5}+B_{(1,2),5}\) is regular at \(t=0\) (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}), so the two poles cancel and
\({\mathcal R}_{1,1}{\mathcal R}_{1,4}/5={\mathcal R}_{1,2}{\mathcal R}_{3,1}/7\), an
identity between polynomials of degree ten in \(d\).  In the second moment the
label \((1,1)\) drops out, since \(h_{1,1}\equiv0\), and
\(\delta_{(1,2)}(t)=h'_{1,2}t+O(t^2)\) turns the pole of \(B_{(1,2),5}\) into a
constant.  Since the pole of \(H_5\) at \(\Delta=0\) has order at most
\(j_5(0)=2\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}),
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to0}\Delta^2H_5(0,\Delta,d)
 =\widetilde A^\star_{0,2,5}(d)
 =\frac{{\mathcal R}_{1,2}{\mathcal R}_{3,1}}{7}
 =\frac{{\mathcal R}_{1,1}{\mathcal R}_{1,4}}{5}
 =-\tfrac{3}{11200}\,{\mathcal N}_{1,1}(b_\star,d)\,{\mathcal N}_{1,4}(b_\star,d),
\]
because \({\mathcal A}_{1,1}=\tfrac12\) and
\({\mathcal A}_{1,4}(b_\star)=-\tfrac3{1120}\).  So, for indeterminate \(d\), the
double pole of \(H_5\) at \(c=0\) comes from two simple poles in \(b\) that
cancel in the first moment.  At \(d=0\) the coefficient vanishes, as it must by
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives the same coefficient as
\(\mathcal N^-_2/\gamma^-_2\): here \(\ell^-_2=5<\ell^+_2=7\), the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria} with \(j=1\) gives
\(\mathcal N^-_2={\mathcal N}_{1,1}{\mathcal N}_{1,4}\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients}, with \(c_2=-1\) and
\(\varrho=-\tfrac12\), gives \(\gamma^-_2=-[d^2]\mathcal N^-_2/\varrho^2=-11200/3\).
A script listed under Code availability checks each step in exact arithmetic.
Ribault found the double pole of the sphere block at this level from the same
two pairs of labels, \((1,1)\) with \((1,4)\) and \((1,2)\) with
\((3,1)\)~\cite[\S3.2, (3.19) and (3.24)]{Ribault2019}.
\end{example*}

\subsection{Valuation bounds and truncation orders}\label{sec:truncation}

The quantities in this subsection bound the computation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}; the theorem itself does not use them.

\begin{lemma}[A priori shifted valuation bound]
\label{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}
For positive labels \(a,g\), set \(\pi_g=\max\{0,-\ord_tR_g(t)\}\) and
\(\sigma_{a,g}=\max\{0,\ord_t(z_a(t)-h_g(t))\}\).  Define nonnegative integers
\(W_{a,M}\) recursively by \(W_{a,0}=0\) and, for \(M\geq1\),
\(W_{a,M}=\max(\{0\}\cup\{\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}+W_{g,M-\ell_g}:\ell_g\leq M\})\).
Then \(\ord_t S_{a,M}(t)\geq-W_{a,M}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The claim holds at \(M=0\). By induction the \(g\)-summand has valuation at
least \(-\pi_g-\sigma_{a,g}-W_{g,M-\ell_g}\geq-W_{a,M}\), as does their sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A priori pole bound]\label{cor:pole-bound}
For \(N\geq1\), \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) and
\(a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), put
\(\varepsilon_a=\ord_t\delta_a\geq1\) (\(\varepsilon_a=\infty\) if
\(\delta_a\equiv0\)) and \(\widehat v_{a,N}=\pi_a+W_{a,N-\ell_a}\), and
define \(\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}=\max_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
(1+\lfloor\widehat v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor)\) (with \(1\) when
\(\varepsilon_a=\infty\)).  Then \(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=0\) for
\(k>\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), and
\(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound},
\(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\).  For
\(k>\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) one has
\((k-1)\varepsilon_a>\widehat v_{a,N}\), so every summand
of~\eqref{eq:collision-moments} has positive valuation and
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=0\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}
gives \(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Laurent precision for the recursion]
\label{lem:precision-budget}
Suppose \(S_{a,M}\) is needed modulo \(t^{U+1}\).  For its \(g\)-summand it
suffices to know
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-propagation}
 \frac{R_g}{z_a-h_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+W_{g,M-\ell_g}},
 \qquad
 S_{g,M-\ell_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}}.
\end{equation}
The level decreases at each request. For the collision moments it suffices to compute
\(R_a\) through \(t^{W_{a,N-\ell_a}}\), \(S_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through \(t^{\pi_a}\),
and \(\delta_a^{\,k-1}\) through \(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For factors of valuations at least \(-\alpha\) and \(-\beta\), the product
through \(t^U\) uses the first factor through \(t^{U+\beta}\) and the second
through \(t^{U+\alpha}\).  With \(\alpha=\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}\) and
\(\beta=W_{g,M-\ell_g}\) this gives~\eqref{eq:precision-propagation}; applied to
\(B_{a,N}=R_aS_{a,N-\ell_a}\) with \(U=0\), \(\alpha=\pi_a\) and
\(\beta=W_{a,N-\ell_a}\), it gives the stated bounds.  Since
\(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\), only terms of \(\delta_a^{k-1}\) through
\(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\) can contribute to the constant term.
\end{proof}

\section{Jantzen layers at minimal-model charges and the top pole}\label{sec:mm-layers}

Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be
coprime, let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) with
\(0<r<p\) and \(0<s<p'\).  The labels \((r,s)\) and \((p-r,p'-s)\) give the
same weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions}); we choose the one with
\(n_1:=p'r+ps<pp'\), and put \(n_0:=p'r-ps\).  Define the layer levels
\begin{gather*}
 \ell^\pm_{2j}=j(jpp'\pm n_0)\quad(j\geq1),\\
 \ell^+_{2j+1}=(r+jp)(s+jp'),\qquad

\begin{theorem}[Smith layers at minimal-model charges]
\label{thm:jantzen-smith}
In this setting, \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) has, for every \(k\geq1\), singular
vectors \(w^\pm_k\) of levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), and with
\(W^\pm_k=U(\Vir_-)w^\pm_k\), one has
\(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)=W^+_i+W^-_i\) for \(i\geq1\).  Consequently, at every
level \(N\), the number of local Smith exponents of \(G_N(c_\star,\lambda+x)\)
that are at least \(i\) is \(\dim(W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\).  The inverse Shapovalov
matrix \(G_N(c_\star,\Delta)^{-1}\) has a pole of order exactly
\(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\), and \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) have poles of order at most \(j_N(\lambda)\)
there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the notation of~\cite[\S5.1.4]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(c_\star=c_{p,p'}\), and
\(p'r+ps<pp'\) puts \((r,s)\) in the interior set, so \((c_\star,\lambda)\) is in
Class~\(R^+\), Case~\(1+\).  Since \(c_\star\neq1,25\),
\cite[Theorem~6.3(i), (6.3)]{IoharaKoga2011} identifies \(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)\)
with the sum of the two Verma submodules of highest weights \(\xi_{\pm i}\) in
the braided diagram of~\cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011}.
By \cite[(5.4) and (5.22)]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(\xi_0=\lambda\)
and, for \(k\geq1\), the weights \(\xi_{\pm k}\) have squared Coulomb-gas
momenta \((2jpp'\mp n_0)^2\) when \(k=2j\), and \((2(j+1)pp'-n_1)^2\) and
\((2jpp'+n_1)^2\) when \(k=2j+1\).  Since
\(h_{u,v}(b_\star)=((p'u-pv)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), their levels above
\(\lambda\) are \(\ell^\mp_k\); note that the sign of \(\xi_{\pm k}\) is
opposite to that of the level.

The same layers follow from \cite[Theorem~B(e), case \(\mathrm{III}_-\)]{Astashkevich1997},
which applies because the line \(c=c_\star\) is transverse to every Kac
curve through \((\lambda,c_\star)\) (as \(c'(b_\star)\neq0\)).

By Lemmas~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} and~\ref{lem:smith}, the pole order of
\(G_N^{-1}\) is the largest \(i\geq1\) with \((W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\neq0\), or zero if
there is none, and this space is
nonzero exactly when \(\min(\ell^+_i,\ell^-_i)\leq N\). The trace \(F_N\) has at most
this order, and so does \(H_N=\sum_j\eta_jF_{N-j}\) since \(j_{N-j}\leq j_N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Top Jantzen layer]\label{thm:top-layer}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, put
\(\mu^\pm_k=\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\),
\(\gamma^\pm_k=B^k(w^\pm_k,w^\pm_k)\), and
\(\mathcal N^\pm_k(d)=\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle\).
Let \(k\geq1\) and \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\).  Then \(j_N(\lambda)=k\);
for each sign with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N\), one has \(\gamma^\pm_k\neq0\),
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\neq0\) for \(M\leq N-\ell^\pm_k\),
\(\deg_d\mathcal N^\pm_k=2\ell^\pm_k\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-layer}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^kH_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}\frac{\mathcal N^\pm_k(d)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\ell^\pm_0=0\).  For \(j\geq0\), expanding the layer levels gives
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
  & \text{upper sign} & \text{lower sign}\\[2pt]
  \ell^+_{2j+1}-\ell^\pm_{2j} & s(r+2jp) & r(s+2jp')\\
  \ell^-_{2j+1}-\ell^\pm_{2j} & (p-r)((2j+1)p'-s) & (p'-s)((2j+1)p-r)\\
  \ell^\pm_{2j+2}-\ell^+_{2j+1} & (p'-s)((2j+1)p+r) & (p-r)((2j+1)p'+s)\\
  \ell^\pm_{2j+2}-\ell^-_{2j+1} & r(2(j+1)p'-s) & s(2(j+1)p-r)
 \end{array}
\]
and all these differences are positive.  Hence \(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<\ell_{k+1}\), so
\(j_N(\lambda)=k\) and \(J^{k+1}_N(c_\star,\lambda)=0\).  Moreover,
for \(j\geq1\), \(\ell^+_{2j}-\ell^-_{2j}=2jn_0\neq0\) (since \(p\nmid r\)), and
for \(j\geq0\), \(\ell^+_{2j+1}-\ell^-_{2j+1}=(2j+1)(n_1-pp')\neq0\).

Fix \(N'\) with \(\ell_k\leq N'\leq N\).  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, \(k\) is the largest local Smith exponent
of \(G_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x)\), and
\(J^k_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda)=(W^+_k)_{N'}+(W^-_k)_{N'}\).  For each sign with
\(\ell^\pm_k\leq N'\), the vectors \(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k\), with \(Z\) a partition
of \(N'-\ell^\pm_k\), form a basis of \((W^\pm_k)_{N'}\), because the
embedding \(M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\to M(c_\star,\lambda)\) that sends the
highest-weight vector to \(w^\pm_k\) is injective
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(i) and singularity of \(w^\pm_k\),
\[
 B^k(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k,L_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)=B^k(w^\pm_k,L_ZL_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)
 =\gamma^\pm_k\,G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)_{Z,Z'} .
\]
If both signs occur, let \(w\) and \(w'\) be the singular vectors of the
lower and the higher level.  Then \(L_ZL_{-Z'}w\) is a combination of
vectors \(L_{-Y}w\) with \(|Y|\) equal to the level of \(w'\) minus the level
of \(w\), which is positive, and
\(B^k(w',L_{-Y}w)=B^k(L_Yw',w)=0\); so the two summands are
\(B^k\)-orthogonal.  As \(B^k\) is nondegenerate on \(J^k_{N'}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii), the sum is direct and
\(\gamma^\pm_kG_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\) is invertible.  Letting
\(N'\) range from \(\ell^\pm_k\) to \(N\) proves the nonvanishing
assertions.

Since \(k\) is the pole order of \(G_{N'}^{-1}\) at \(\lambda\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}), Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii) in the basis
above and Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [x^{-k}]F_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x,d)
 &=\sum_{\pm}\frac1{\gamma^\pm_k}\sum_{Z,Z'}
 \bigl(G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)^{-1}\bigr)_{Z,Z'}
 \langle w^\pm_k|L_ZV_d(1)L_{-Z'}|w^\pm_k\rangle\\
 &=\sum_{\pm}\frac{\mathcal N^\pm_k(d)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 F_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the sums run over signs with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N'\).  For
\(N'<\ell_k\), \(F_{N'}\) has a pole of order at most
\(j_{N'}(\lambda)<k\) at \(\lambda\).  Multiplying by the Euler
coefficients and summing as in Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced} gives
\eqref{eq:top-layer}.  The degree of \(\mathcal N^\pm_k\) is
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness of the Jantzen bound}\label{sec:sharpness}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, the pole order of \(H_N\) at an interior
weight equals \(j_N(\lambda)\) as soon as the right side
of~\eqref{eq:top-layer} is a nonzero polynomial in \(d\).  On a level where
only one layer vector contributes, this follows from the nonvanishing of the
shifted block (Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}), and at
\(N=\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\) from the orders of vanishing at \(d=0\) (proof
of Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp}).  On a strict two-vector level,
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\), the right side is the sum of the two
products
\(\mathcal N^\pm_kH_{M^\pm}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d)/\gamma^\pm_k\) with
\(M^\pm=N-\ell^\pm_k\geq1\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} does not exclude
that they cancel.

To show that they do not, it suffices to find one value of \(d\) and one
Taylor coefficient there at which the sum is nonzero.  The first candidate is
\(d=0\).  Both products vanish there to order at least \(k+1\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} with Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}
gives the coefficient of \(d^{k+1}\) in closed form: up to a nonzero factor it
is \(f(M^+,m^+_k)+(-1)^kf(M^-,m^-_k)\), where
\(f(M,m)=\sum_{uv=M}x_{u,v}/(m^2-x_{u,v}^2)\), \(x_{u,v}=p'u-pv\), and
\(m^\pm_k\) is the Coulomb momentum of \(\mu^\pm_k\), so that
\(\mu^\pm_k=((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\).  The two sums take both
signs, and we do not have a proof that they never cancel.  They do not cancel
on any strict two-vector level with \(p,p'\leq13\) and \(k\leq3\) (see the code
availability section).

The proof below uses the external weights \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b_\star)\) and
\(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b_\star)\) instead, where the size of each shifted block at an
odd prime \(\varpi\) dividing \(p'\) is known (after \(b\mapsto b^{-1}\), which
exchanges \(p\) and \(p'\), if \(p'\) has no odd prime factor).  At these
weights the generic
recursion keeps only the labels \((u,1)\).  After its denominators are
cleared, \(H_M\) becomes a polynomial in \(t=b^2\) and \(Y=\Delta t^2\) with
coefficients in \(\mathbb Z[\frac12]\) and constant term \(4^M\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(i)).  At \(b=b_\star\) and
\(\Delta=\mu\) both \(t\) and \(Y\) are divisible by \(\varpi\), so this
polynomial takes a \(\varpi\)-adic unit value, and the \(\varpi\)-adic
valuation of \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\) is minus that of the cleared
denominator, given by~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}.  When the last sum in~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}
vanishes, this valuation is \(-v_\varpi(M!)-Mv_\varpi(p')\), which decreases strictly
with \(M\).  The torus norms are products of linear forms in \(d\)
(Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}), so the valuations of their Taylor
coefficients at \(d_1\), and through Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients}
those of the Jantzen norms, can be counted factor by factor.

Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria} splits into three cases by \(s\).  In
Case~2, the main one, the count shows that
\([(d-d_1)^k]\mathcal N^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k\) has the same valuation for both
signs, and the last sum in~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation} vanishes.  Both products
vanish to order \(k\) at \(d_1\), and their Taylor coefficients of degree \(k\)
there then have valuations differing by
\(-v_\varpi(M^+!)+v_\varpi(M^-!)-(M^+-M^-)v_\varpi(p')\neq0\), and two rational numbers
of different valuation cannot cancel.  Case~1 is simpler: the two torus norms
vanish to different orders at \(d_1\).  In Case~3 the last sum
in~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation} can make the two valuations equal.  The proof then
compares the ratio of the two leading Taylor coefficients at \(d_1\) with the
ratio at \(d_2\), using Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iv) to control
how the ratio of shifted blocks changes with the level.

In the Lee--Yang model, \((p,p')=(2,5)\), at
\(\lambda=h_{1,2}(b_\star)=-\frac15\), the first strict two-vector level
falls under Case~2 and shows it in numbers.  The first
layer has singular vectors at levels \(2\) and \(3\), so
\(\mu^+_1=\frac95\) and \(\mu^-_1=\frac{14}5\), and the second layer starts at
level \(9\).  At \(N=4\) one has \(M^+=2\), \(M^-=1\), \(\varpi=5\) and
\(s=2\), and \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b_\star)=-\frac15\) is the weight of the Lee--Yang
field itself.  Both products vanish to first order at \(d_1\), and their first
Taylor coefficients there are
\begin{align*}
 \frac{[(d-d_1)]\mathcal N^+_1}{\gamma^+_1}\,H_2\bigl(c_\star,\tfrac95,d_1\bigr)
 &=\frac3{14}\cdot\frac{349}{425}=\frac{1047}{5950},\\
 \frac{[(d-d_1)]\mathcal N^-_1}{\gamma^-_1}\,H_1\bigl(c_\star,\tfrac{14}5,d_1\bigr)
 &=-\frac13\cdot\frac3{70}=-\frac1{70}.
\end{align*}
The norm ratios \(\frac3{14}\) and \(-\frac13\) are \(5\)-adic units.  The
shifted blocks have \(5\)-adic valuations \(-2\) and \(-1\),
as~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation} gives with \(a=1\), so the sum
\(\frac{481}{2975}\) is nonzero.  (Here \(\gamma^+_1=-\frac{84}{25}\),
\(\gamma^-_1=\frac{4536}{625}\) and \(H_1(c,\Delta,d)=d(d-1)/(2\Delta)\).)

\begin{proposition}[Layer coefficients at \(d=0\)]
\label{prop:layer-coefficients}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, let \(m^\pm_k\) be the
Coulomb momentum of \(\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\) (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}), \(\varrho=-n_0/(2(p'-p))\), and
\(c_k=(-1)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-1}{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\).  Then
\(\mathcal N^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k=c_k(\pm\varrho)^kd^k+O(d^{k+1})\); in particular
\(\mathcal N^\pm_k\) vanishes to order exactly \(k\) at \(d=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We read off \(\mathcal N^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k\) from the generic recursion by letting
\(b^2\), \(\Delta\) and \(d\) tend to \((b_\star^2,\lambda,0)\) at the same
rate.  Along such a curve only the chains of labels whose poles collide at
every step survive (Steps~1 and~2), and the limit is the lowest homogeneous
part of \(H_N\), whose behaviour at large slope \(\zeta\) gives
\([d^k](\mathcal N^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k)\) (Step~3).  Steps~4 and~5 list the colliding
chains and evaluate the resulting moments.

Put \(\nu=b^2\), \(\nu_\star=-p'/p\), \(N_0=2pp'\); dots denote \(d/d\nu\) at
\(\nu_\star\).  As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, write
\(\varphi(x,y)=xb+yb^{-1}\), which vanishes at \(\nu_\star\) iff
\((x,y)\in\mathbb Z(p,p')\), and put \(B_g=[1-u,u]\times[1-v,v]\) for a label
\(g=(u,v)\).

\emph{Step 1: the multipliers along a blow-up.}  By the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, the factors of \({\mathcal N}_g(b,d)\) vanishing
at \((\nu,d)=(\nu_\star,0)\) are \(-d\) and the factors whose restrictions to
\(d=0\) are \(\frac14\varphi(2jp,2jp')\varphi(2-2jp,2-2jp')\) with \(j\neq0\)
and \((jp,jp')\in B_g\).  At \(d=0\) their \(\nu\)-derivatives are
\(j\kappa_0\), where \(\kappa_0=p(p'-p)/p'\).  The pole of \({\mathcal A}_g\)
has order at most their number minus one, with equality when
\(\varrho_g:=\partial_d{\mathcal R}_g|_{d=0}=-(p'u-pv)/(2(p'-p))\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}) is nonzero.  So along the curve
\[
 (\nu,\Delta,d)=(\nu_\star+\varepsilon,\ \lambda+\varepsilon\zeta,\ \varepsilon y)
 \qquad(\varepsilon\to0)
\]
with \(\zeta\) and \(y\) fixed,
\[
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_g}{\varepsilon}\to\varrho_g\,y\prod_j\Bigl(1-\frac{y}{j\kappa_0}\Bigr).
\]

\emph{Step 2: the limit along the curve.}  Expand \(H_N\) by
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} into chains \(g_1,\dots,g_n\) with
denominators \(z_{i-1}-h_{g_i}\), where \(z_0=\Delta\) and
\(z_i=h_{g_i}+\ell_{g_i}\).  For generic \(\zeta,y\), such a denominator is of
order \(\varepsilon\) if \(h_{g_i}(\nu_\star)=z_{i-1}(\nu_\star)\), and a unit
otherwise; consecutive slopes differ, as Step~4 shows.  Only the chains whose
denominators are all of order \(\varepsilon\) contribute to the limit, and \(H_N\)
tends to
\[
 T_N(\zeta,y)=\sum_{\text{colliding chains}}\ \prod_i
 \frac{\varrho_{g_i}\,y\prod_j(1-y/(j\kappa_0))}{\zeta_{i-1}-\dot h_{g_i}},
 \qquad \zeta_0=\zeta,\quad\zeta_i=\dot h_{g_i}.
\]

\emph{Step 3: reduction to a limit in \(\zeta\).}  The denominator of
\(H_N\) is a product of powers of \(\Delta-h_g\), whose leading forms are
units or \(\Delta-\lambda-\dot h_g(\nu-\nu_\star)\).  So
\(T_N(\zeta,y)=\Phi(1,\zeta,y)\) for the lowest homogeneous part \(\Phi\) of
\(H_N\) in \((\nu-\nu_\star,\Delta-\lambda,d)\), which has degree \(0\) and is
regular at \((0,1,\cdot)\).  Hence \([y^k]T_N=O(\zeta^{-k})\) as
\(\zeta\to\infty\), and
\(H_N(c_\star,\lambda+\varepsilon,\varepsilon y)\to\Phi(0,1,y)\) as
\(\varepsilon\to0\).  With Theorems~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}
and~\ref{thm:top-layer} and Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives} this gives
\[
 [d^k]\frac{\mathcal N^\pm_k}{\gamma^\pm_k}
 =\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\zeta^k[y^k]T_{\ell^\pm_k}.
\]

\emph{Step 4: the colliding chains.}  By integrality of \(u,v\), the colliding
chains from \(\lambda\) are the sequences \(n_0=x_0,x_1,\dots\) with
\(|x_i|\) increasing and \(x_i\) alternately in \(n_1+N_0\mathbb Z\) and
\(n_0+N_0\mathbb Z\), via
\[
 g_i=\Bigl(\frac{\epsilon_i(x_{i-1}+x_i)}{2p'},\
          \frac{\epsilon_i(x_i-x_{i-1})}{2p}\Bigr),
 \qquad \epsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}x_i.
\]
Then
\[
 \varrho_{g_i}=-\frac{\epsilon_ix_{i-1}}{2(p'-p)},\qquad
 4p'^2\dot h_{g_i}=p'^2-p^2-x_{i-1}x_i,
\]
so \(4p'^2(\dot h_{g_{i-1}}-\dot h_{g_i})=x_{i-1}(x_i-x_{i-2})\neq0\), and
\(|x_i|\) is the momentum reached.  The endpoints \(\pm m^\pm_k\) are
\(n_1+jN_0\) and \(n_1-(j+1)N_0\) for \(k=2j+1\), and \(n_0\pm jN_0\) for
\(k=2j\).  The first steps of the chains ending there are \(x_1=n_1+iN_0\)
with \(-j\leq i\leq j\), \(-j-1\leq i\leq j-1\) and \(-j\leq i\leq j-1\) in
these three cases; these are \(k\) distinct slopes.

\emph{Step 5: the moments.}  As
\([y^k]T_N=\sum_\theta C_\theta/(\zeta-\dot h_\theta)\) is
\(O(\zeta^{-k})\), its moments of order \(\leq k-2\) vanish, and for each
\(\theta_0\)
\[
 \lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\zeta^k[y^k]T_N
 =C_{\theta_0}\prod_{\theta\neq\theta_0}\frac{n_0(x_\theta-x_{\theta_0})}{4p'^2}.
\]
For \(k=2j+1\) take \(\theta_0\) the endpoint, \((r+jp,s+jp')\) or
\(((j+1)p-r,(j+1)p'-s)\), whose box meets \(\mathbb Z(p,p')\) for
\(|j'|\leq j\) (as \(0<r<p\), \(0<s<p'\)).  Then
\(C_{\theta_0}=\pm\varrho(-1)^j/(j!^2\kappa_0^{2j})\), the product is
\((n_0N_0/4p'^2)^{2j}(2j)!\), and \(n_0N_0/(4p'^2\kappa_0)=-\varrho\).  For
\(k=2j\) take \(\theta_0=n_1+(j-1)N_0\) and then the step \(g\) to the endpoint,
\((r+(2j-1)p,p'-s)\) or \((p-r,s+(2j-1)p')\), whose box meets
\(\mathbb Z(p,p')\) only at \(0\).  Then
\(\varrho_g/(\dot h_{\theta_0}-\dot h_g)=-2p'^2/((p'-p)jN_0)\), and the product is
\(-(n_0N_0/4p'^2)^{2j-1}(2j-1)!\).  Both cases give \(c_k(\pm\varrho)^k\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integrality, valuation, and level polynomiality at \(d=h_{1,\pm3}(b)\)]
\label{prop:h13-nonvanishing}
Put \(t=b^2\), \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b)=-1-2/t\), \(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b)=2-2/t\),
\(P_0^{(\nu)}=1\), and for \(M\geq1\) and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\),
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y):=M!\,t^{2M}\prod_{u=1}^M(2\Delta-2h_{u,1}(b))\,
H_M(c(b),\Delta,d_\nu)|_{\Delta=Y/t^2}\).
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{3pt}
\item[(i)] For \(M\geq1\) and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\),
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\),
\(P_M^{(2)}(0,Y)=P_M^{(1)}(0,Y)\), and \(P_M^{(\nu)}(0,0)=4^M\).
\item[(ii)] For every \(i,j\geq0\) and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\),
\(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M):=[t^iY^j](P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)/4^M)\) is a polynomial in
\(M\) of degree at most \(2i+2j\), valid for all integers \(M\geq0\).
\item[(iii)] Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime, \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let
\(\varpi\mid p'\) be an odd prime with \(a=v_\varpi(p')\geq1\).  If
\(M\geq1\) and \(\mu=(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\) (\(m\in\mathbb Z\)) satisfy
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\), then \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\) (\(\nu=1,2\))
is regular and nonzero with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h13-valuation}
 v_\varpi\bigl(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\bigr)
 =-v_\varpi(M!)-Ma-\sum_{u=1}^M v_\varpi\bigl(m^2-(p'u-p)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, for every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\) (using the symmetry
\(p\leftrightarrow p'\) when \(p'\) has no odd prime factor), every \(M\geq1\)
and every integer momentum \(m\) with
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\), one has \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\) in
\(\mathbb Q[d]\).
\item[(iv)] In the setting of~(iii), if \(t_0\in\varpi^a\mathbb Z_\varpi\),
\(Y_1,Y_2\in\varpi^{a+1}\mathbb Z_\varpi\), and integers \(M_1,M_2\geq1\),
\(e\geq0\) satisfy \(v_\varpi(M_1-M_2)\geq e\) and \(v_\varpi(Y_1-Y_2)\geq e+a\),
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-screening-lip}
 v_\varpi\!\left(
 \frac{P_{M_1}^{(1)}(t_0,Y_1)\,P_{M_2}^{(2)}(t_0,Y_2)}
      {P_{M_1}^{(2)}(t_0,Y_1)\,P_{M_2}^{(1)}(t_0,Y_2)}-1\right)
 \geq e+a.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Parts (i) and (ii) are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:h13}.

(iii) Specialize to \(t_\star=-p'/p\) and \(\Delta=\mu\).  By the Kac
determinant formula, \(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) gives
\(\det G_{M'}(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) (\(M'\leq M\)) and
\(\mu\neq h_{u,1}(b_\star)\) (\(1\leq u\leq M\)), so
\(H_M(c(b),\Delta,d_\nu)\) is regular at \((b_\star,\mu)\).  Both \(t_\star\)
and \(Y_\star=\mu t_\star^2=p'(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4p^3)\) lie in
\(\varpi\mathbb Z_\varpi\), so~(i) gives
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t_\star,Y_\star)\equiv4^M\not\equiv0\pmod{\varpi\mathbb Z_\varpi}\),
whence \(v_\varpi(P_M^{(\nu)}(t_\star,Y_\star))=0\) and
\(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\neq0\); since
\(2\mu-2h_{u,1}(b_\star)=(m^2-(p'u-p)^2)/(2pp')\), taking \(v_\varpi\) gives
\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}.  Every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\) has an odd prime
factor, so \(b\mapsto b^{-1}\) gives \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\) in
general.

(iv) Fix \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\).  By~(i)
and~(ii), each \(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M)\) has degree \(D\leq2i+2j\) and lies in
\(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]\) for all \(M\geq0\), so
\(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M)=\sum_{u=0}^D\beta_u\binom{M}{u}\)
(\(\beta_u\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]\)).  For \(D=0\),
\(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_1)=c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_2)\).  For \(i+j\geq1\) and
\(1\leq u\leq D\), \((M_1-M_2)\mid u!(\binom{M_1}{u}-\binom{M_2}{u})\), so
\(v_\varpi(\binom{M_1}{u}-\binom{M_2}{u})\geq e-v_\varpi(D!)\); since \(\varpi\geq3\),
Legendre's formula gives \(v_\varpi(D!)\leq(D-1)/(\varpi-1)\leq(D-1)/2\), hence
\(v_\varpi(D!)\leq i+j-1\).  Since \(v_\varpi(t_0)\geq a\) and
\(v_\varpi(Y_1)\geq a+1\), each term with \(i+j\geq1\) in
\(P_{M_1}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_1}-P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_2}
=\sum_{i+j\geq1}(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_1)-c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_2))t_0^iY_1^j\)
has valuation at least \(e-(i+j-1)+ia+j(a+1)=e+(i+j)(a-1)+j+1\geq e+a\).
As \(P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y)/4^{M_2}\in\mathbb Z_\varpi[Y]\),
\(v_\varpi(P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_2}-P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_2)/4^{M_2})
\geq v_\varpi(Y_1-Y_2)\geq e+a\).  Combining gives
\(v_\varpi(P_{M_1}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_1}-P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_2)/4^{M_2})\geq e+a\)
(\(\nu=1,2\)), and since all four values are \(\varpi\)-adic units,
\eqref{eq:second-screening-lip} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Nonvanishing on all two-vector levels]
\label{prop:two-term-criteria}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, let
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\), and let \(P_N(d)\) denote the
right side of \eqref{eq:top-layer}.  Then \(P_N(d)\not\equiv0\) in
\(\mathbb Q[d]\), and \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) has a pole of order exactly
\(k\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both terms of \(P_N(d)\) are present, and we show that they cannot cancel by
looking at them near \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b_\star)\) and, if needed,
\(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b_\star)\), where Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}
controls the shifted blocks.  In Case~1 the two terms vanish to different
orders at \(d_1\).  In Case~2 they vanish to the same order, and their leading
coefficients at \(d_1\) have different \(\varpi\)-adic valuations.  In Case~3 the
valuations can agree, and we show instead that the ratio of the two leading
coefficients at \(d_1\) differs from the ratio at \(d_2\).

By \(b\mapsto b^{-1}\) (swapping \(p\leftrightarrow p'\),
\(r\leftrightarrow s\)), assume an odd prime \(\varpi\mid p'\) with
\(a=v_\varpi(p')\geq1\).  Write \(M^\pm=N-\ell^\pm_k\geq1\) and
\[
 d_x=\frac{x^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'},\qquad x_0=|p'-p|,\quad x_1=|p'-3p|,\quad
 x_2=p'+3p,
\]
so that \(d_{x_0}=0\), \(d_{x_1}=d_1=h_{1,3}(b_\star)\) and
\(d_{x_2}=d_2=h_{1,-3}(b_\star)\).  Normalize each \(w^\pm_k\) to
\(L_{-1}^{\ell^\pm_k}\)-coefficient \(1\), and write
\(\mathcal N^\pm_k(d)=\prod_x(d-d_x)^{o^\pm_k(x)}\), so that
\(o^\pm_k(x)=\ord_{d=d_x}\mathcal N^\pm_k\).  By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere} and~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} and
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}, at \(b=b_\star\),
\[
 \mathcal N^+_{2j+1}={\mathcal N}_{r+jp,\,s+jp'},\qquad
 \mathcal N^-_{2j+1}={\mathcal N}_{(j+1)p-r,\,(j+1)p'-s},
\]
and for \(k=2j\),
\begin{gather*}
 \mathcal N^+_{2j}={\mathcal N}_{r+(j-1)p,\,s+(j-1)p'}\,{\mathcal N}_{(2j-1)p+r,\,p'-s},\\
 \mathcal N^-_{2j}={\mathcal N}_{r+(j-1)p,\,s+(j-1)p'}\,{\mathcal N}_{p-r,\,(2j-1)p'+s},
\end{gather*}
where, by Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue},
\[
 {\mathcal N}_{R,S}(b_\star,d)=\prod_{a',l}(d-d_{p'a'-pl})(d-d_{p'a'+pl})
 \qquad(\text{odd }1\leq a'<2R,\ \text{odd }1\leq l<2S).
\]
Moreover \(M^+-M^-=\ell^-_k-\ell^+_k\) equals \(k(pp'-n_1)>0\) for odd \(k\)
and \(-kn_0\neq0\) for even \(k\).  Put \(e_\pm:=o^\pm_k(x_1)\), the
multiplicity of \(d_1\) in \(\mathcal N^\pm_k\).

\emph{Case 1: \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\).}  Solve \(|p'a'\pm pl|=|p'-3p|\) in odd
positive integers \((a',l)\).  The plus sign gives \(p'(a'+1)=p(3-l)\), which
forces \(l=1\) and \(p'\mid2\), impossible as \(\varpi\mid p'\), or
\(p'(a'-1)=-p(l+3)\), which is impossible.  The minus sign gives
\(p'(a'-1)=p(l-3)\) or \(p'(a'+1)=p(l+3)\), with solutions
\((1+2ip,3+2ip')\) (\(i\geq0\)) and \((2ip-1,2ip'-3)\) (\(i\geq1\)).  Thus the
multiplicity of \(d_1\) in \({\mathcal N}_{R,S}\) is
\[
 \#\{i\geq0:ip\leq R-1,\ ip'\leq S-2\}+\#\{i\geq1:ip\leq R,\ ip'\leq S+1\}.
\]
Evaluating on \(\mathcal N^\pm_k\) gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
  & e_+ & e_-\\\hline
  k=2j+1 & 2j+\mathbf 1_{s\geq2} & 2j+\mathbf 1_{s\leq p'-2}\\
  k=2j & 2j-1+\mathbf 1_{s\leq p'-2} & 2j-\mathbf 1_{s=1}
 \end{array}
\]
Hence \(e_+\neq e_-\) if and only if \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\), and \(e_+=e_-=k\)
otherwise.  When \(e_+\neq e_-\), extracting
\([(d-d_1)^{\min(e_+,e_-)}]P_N(d)\) removes the branch of higher order and
leaves a nonzero multiple of \(H_{M^{\min}}(c_\star,\mu^{\min}_k,d_1)\), which
is nonzero by Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iii).

\emph{Case 2: \(2\leq s\leq p'-2\) and \(s\not\equiv\pm1\pmod\varpi\).}  Now
\(e_+=e_-=k\).  By Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients},
\(\gamma^\pm_k=[d^k]\mathcal N^\pm_k/(c_k(\pm\varrho)^k)\).  We have
\begin{align*}
 [(d-d_1)^k]\mathcal N^\pm_k&=(4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}\prod_{x\neq x_1}(x_1^2-x^2)^{o^\pm_k(x)},\\
 [d^k]\mathcal N^\pm_k&=(4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}\prod_{x\neq x_0}(x_0^2-x^2)^{o^\pm_k(x)}.
\end{align*}
Combined with~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}, this shows that the two summands of
\([(d-d_1)^k]P_N(d)\) have \(\varpi\)-adic valuations differing by
\(V_\varpi^+(N)-V_\varpi^-(N)\), where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_\varpi^\pm(N):={}&\sum_{x\neq x_1}o^\pm_k(x)\,v_\varpi(x_1^2-x^2)
 -\sum_{x\neq x_0}o^\pm_k(x)\,v_\varpi(x_0^2-x^2)\\
 &-v_\varpi(M^\pm!)-M^\pm a-\sum_{u=1}^{M^\pm}v_\varpi\bigl((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'u-p)^2\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
We show that the first two sums give the same total for both signs.  In
every factor \({\mathcal N}_{R,S}\) of \(\mathcal N^\pm_k\), one has
\(S\equiv\pm s\not\equiv\pm1\pmod\varpi\).

If \(\varpi\geq5\), the odd indices \(1\leq l<2S\) with
\(l\equiv\pm3\pmod\varpi\) pair bijectively with those with
\(l\equiv\pm1\pmod\varpi\) via \((2i\varpi+3\epsilon,\,2i\varpi+\epsilon)\), with
\(\epsilon=+1\), \(i\geq0\) and with \(\epsilon=-1\), \(i\geq1\).  For
\(\beta\in\{1,3\}\), \(\sigma\in\{\pm1\}\) and odd \(a'\), put
\(A=p'-\beta p\) and \(B=p'a'+\sigma p(2i\varpi+\beta\epsilon)\).  Then
\[
 A^2-B^2=(A+\epsilon\sigma B)(A-\epsilon\sigma B),\qquad
 A+\epsilon\sigma B=p'(1+\epsilon\sigma a')+2\epsilon i\varpi p,
\]
where \(A+\epsilon\sigma B\) does not depend on \(\beta\), and
\(A-\epsilon\sigma B\equiv-2\beta p\not\equiv0\pmod\varpi\) (as \(\varpi\mid p'\)).
So the paired factors either both vanish or have the same \(\varpi\)-adic
valuation.

If \(\varpi=3\), then \(3\mid s\) and \(3\mid S\), so the odd indices
\(1\leq l<2S\) partition into triples \(\{6i+1,6i+3,6i+5\}\)
(\(0\leq i<S/3\)).  Fix an odd \(a'\) and a triple.  At \(l=6i+3\) one has
\(v_3(x_0^2-x^2)=0\), while \((x_1^2-(p'a'+pl)^2)(x_1^2-(p'a'-pl)^2)\) has the
\(3\)-divisible factors
\[
 p'(1+a')+6ip,\quad p'(1-a')-6(i+1)p,\quad p'(1+a')-6(i+1)p,\quad p'(1-a')+6ip.
\]
At \(l=6i+1\) and \(l=6i+5\) one has \(v_3(x_1^2-x^2)=0\), and the
\(3\)-divisible factors of \((x_0^2-(p'a'+pl)^2)(x_0^2-(p'a'-pl)^2)\) are the
first and fourth of these (at \(6i+1\)) and the second and third (at
\(6i+5\)).  Whenever one of them vanishes (at \(x=x_1\)), the \(x_1\)-sum skips
both it and its partner \(2(p'-3p)\), whereas the \(x_0\)-sum skips the
matching zero, whose partner \(\pm2(p'-p)\) is a \(3\)-adic unit, and keeps
\(2(p'-3p)\).  Thus the first two sums in \(V_3^\pm(N)\) total
\(-e_\pm v_3(p'-3p)=-kv_3(p'-3p)\), independent of the sign.

In both cases these sums cancel in \(V_\varpi^+(N)-V_\varpi^-(N)\).  Since
\(m^\pm_k\equiv\pm ps\pmod{p'}\) gives
\((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'u-p)^2\equiv p^2(s^2-1)\not\equiv0\pmod\varpi\), the last sum
also vanishes, leaving
\[
 V_\varpi^+(N)-V_\varpi^-(N)=-\bigl(v_\varpi(M^+!)-v_\varpi(M^-!)\bigr)-(M^+-M^-)a,
\]
which is nonzero because \(M^+\neq M^-\), \(a\geq1\) and \(v_\varpi(M!)\) does not
decrease with \(M\).  Two numbers of different \(\varpi\)-adic valuation do not
cancel, so \([(d-d_1)^k]P_N(d)\neq0\).

\emph{Case 3: \(2\leq s\leq p'-2\) and \(s\equiv\pm1\pmod\varpi\).}  Solving
\(|p'a'\pm pl|=p'+3p\) in odd positive \((a',l)\) yields \((1,3)\),
\((1+2ip,2ip'-3)\) and \((2ip-1,2ip'+3)\) (\(i\geq1\)); counting on
\(\mathcal N^\pm_k\) as in Case~1 gives
\(\ord_{d=d_2}\mathcal N^+_k=\ord_{d=d_2}\mathcal N^-_k=k\).  Put
\(x_1(\varepsilon)=p'-3p+\varepsilon\) and
\(x_2(\varepsilon)=p'+3p-\varepsilon\).  As \(a'\) runs over odd
\(1\leq a'<2R\), \(A\in\{1-a',1+a'\}\) runs once over
\(\{0,\pm2,\ldots,\pm2(R-1),2R\}\), so
\[
 \frac{{\mathcal N}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_1(\varepsilon)})}
      {{\mathcal N}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_2(\varepsilon)})}
 =\prod_{A}\prod_{\text{odd }1\leq l<2S}
 \frac{(p'A-p(3+l)+\varepsilon)(p'A-p(3-l)+\varepsilon)}
      {(p'A+p(3+l)-\varepsilon)(p'A+p(3-l)-\varepsilon)}.
\]
Sending \(A\mapsto-A\) in the denominator cancels every
\(A\in\{0,\pm2,\ldots,\pm2(R-1)\}\) (with \((-1)^{2S}=+1\)), leaving
\(A=2R\).  For each factor \({\mathcal N}_{R,S}\) of \(\mathcal N^\pm_k\) that is not
common to both signs, \(p\nmid R\), so no factor \(2Rp'\pm2ip\) vanishes at
\(\varepsilon=0\).  The values \(3\pm l\) form \(\{4-2S,6-2S,\dots,2S+2\}\);
cancelling \(\{4-2S,\dots,2S-4\}\) with its negation leaves
\(\{2S-2,2S,2S+2\}\), so
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \frac{{\mathcal N}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_1(\varepsilon)})}
      {{\mathcal N}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_2(\varepsilon)})}
 =\prod_{\epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}}\frac{Rp'-Sp-\epsilon p}{Rp'+Sp+\epsilon p}.
\]
Since
\[
 d_{x_1(\varepsilon)}-d_1=\tfrac{p'-3p}{2pp'}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 d_{x_2(\varepsilon)}-d_2=-\tfrac{p'+3p}{2pp'}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)
\]
(with \(p'\neq3p\) as \(\gcd(p,p')=1\) and \(p\geq2\)), and
\(\ord_{d_1}\mathcal N^\pm_k=\ord_{d_2}\mathcal N^\pm_k=k\), the prefactors
\((-(p'-3p)/(p'+3p))^k\) cancel in the ratio of the two branches.  For both
parities of \(k\), the factors \((R^\pm,S^\pm)\) not common to both signs
satisfy \(R^+p'-S^+p=-(R^-p'-S^-p)\notin\{0,\pm p\}\) (since \(p\nmid r\),
hence \(p\nmid(R^+p'-S^+p)\)) and \(R^\pm p'+S^\pm p=m^\pm_k\).  So the
numerator triple has ratio \((-1)^3=-1\), and
\[
 T_k:=\frac{[(d-d_1)^k]\mathcal N^+_k\big/[(d-d_1)^k]\mathcal N^-_k}
          {[(d-d_2)^k]\mathcal N^+_k\big/[(d-d_2)^k]\mathcal N^-_k}
 =-\frac{m^-_k\bigl((m^-_k)^2-p^2\bigr)}{m^+_k\bigl((m^+_k)^2-p^2\bigr)}.
\]
Using \(m^+_k+m^-_k=2kpp'\) and factoring \((m^+_k)^3+(m^-_k)^3\) gives
\[
 T_k-1=\frac{-2kpp'\bigl(3(m^+_k)^2-p^2-6kpp'm^+_k+4k^2p^2p'^2\bigr)}
            {m^+_k\bigl((m^+_k)^2-p^2\bigr)}.
\]
Because \(m^+_k\equiv\pm ps\equiv\pm p\pmod\varpi\) and \(\varpi\nmid2p\), both
\(m^+_k\) and the parenthesized factor (\(\equiv2p^2\pmod\varpi\)) are
\(\varpi\)-adic units, while \(v_\varpi((m^+_k)^2-p^2)\geq1\).  Hence either
\(v_\varpi(T_k)\neq0\) or
\[
 v_\varpi(T_k-1)=v_\varpi(k)+a-v_\varpi\bigl((m^+_k)^2-p^2\bigr)\leq v_\varpi(k)+a-1.
\]
Put \(t_0=-p'/p\) and \(Y^\pm=p'((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4p^3)\).  Then
\(v_\varpi(t_0)=a\), \(v_\varpi(Y^\pm)\geq a+1\) (as
\((m^\pm_k)^2\equiv p^2\pmod\varpi\)), \(v_\varpi(M^+-M^-)\geq v_\varpi(k)\), and
\(Y^+-Y^-=p'(2kpp')(m^+_k-m^-_k)/(4p^3)\) has
\(v_\varpi(Y^+-Y^-)\geq v_\varpi(k)+2a\).  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iii),
\(H_{M^\pm}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d_\nu)\) is regular and nonzero for \(\nu=1,2\).
Put
\[
 H_{\mathrm{rat}}:=\frac{H_{M^+}(d_1)/H_{M^+}(d_2)}{H_{M^-}(d_1)/H_{M^-}(d_2)};
\]
the common denominators \(\prod_u(2\mu^\pm_k-2h_{u,1})\) cancel in this ratio.
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iii) gives
\(v_\varpi(H_{\mathrm{rat}})=0\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iv) with \(e=v_\varpi(k)\) gives
\(v_\varpi(H_{\mathrm{rat}}-1)\geq v_\varpi(k)+a\).  If \(v_\varpi(T_k)\neq0\), then
\(v_\varpi(T_kH_{\mathrm{rat}})\neq0\); otherwise
\(T_kH_{\mathrm{rat}}-1=(T_k-1)H_{\mathrm{rat}}+(H_{\mathrm{rat}}-1)\) has
valuation \(v_\varpi(T_k-1)\leq v_\varpi(k)+a-1\).  So \(T_kH_{\mathrm{rat}}\neq1\).
If \([(d-d_1)^k]P_N(d)\) and \([(d-d_2)^k]P_N(d)\) both vanished, then at
\(d_\nu\) (\(\nu=1,2\)) the leading coefficients
\([(d-d_\nu)^k]\mathcal N^\pm_k\,H_{M^\pm}(d_\nu)/\gamma^\pm_k\) of the two
summands of \(P_N\) would be opposite, and dividing their ratio at \(d_1\) by
their ratio at \(d_2\) removes \(\gamma^\pm_k\) and gives
\(T_kH_{\mathrm{rat}}=1\).  Hence \(P_N\not\equiv0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp pole order for \(H_N\)]
\label{cor:top-layer-sharp}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, for every coprime pair
\(p,p'\geq2\), every interior Kac weight \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\), and
every level \(N\geq1\), the coefficient \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\), viewed as
a rational function of \(\Delta\) over \(\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\) with \(d\)
indeterminate, has pole order exactly \(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\),
where order zero means regularity.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(k=j_N(\lambda)\).  If \(k=0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} gives
regularity.  Suppose \(k\geq1\), so \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\).  On a single-vector level
\(\ell_k\leq N<\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), only the sign with
\(\ell^\pm_k=\ell_k\) contributes to \eqref{eq:top-layer}, giving
\((\mathcal N/\gamma)H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\) with \(M=N-\ell_k\),
\(\mu=\lambda+\ell_k\), and \(\mathcal N\neq0\).  For \(M=0\) one has \(H_0=1\);
for \(M\geq1\), Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) and
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing} gives \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\).
At \(N=\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), \eqref{eq:top-layer} reads
\((\mathcal N/\gamma)H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)+\mathcal N'/\gamma'\) with \(M\geq1\); by
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,0)=0\), so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} the first term vanishes at \(d=0\) to
order at least \(k+1\) and the second to order exactly \(k\), whence the sum is
nonzero.  Every two-vector level
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\) is settled by
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}.
\end{proof}

\section{The Ising model}\label{sec:ising}

At \(c=1/2\) one has \(b_\star^2=-4/3\), \((p,p')=(3,4)\), and
\(h_{r,s}(b_\star)=((4r-3s)^2-1)/48\).  The interior weights are the energy
weight \(\tfrac12\), the spin weight \(\tfrac1{16}\) and the identity weight
\(0\), with labels \((r,s)=(2,1),(1,2),(1,1)\) and \(n_0=5,-2,1\).  Their
layer levels (Section~\ref{sec:mm-layers}) and the labels with \(rs\leq6\) that
give the same weight are
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\lambda&\text{labels with }rs\leq6&(\ell^+_1,\ell^-_1)&
 (\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)&(\ell^+_3,\ell^-_3)\\ \hline
1/2&(2,1),(1,3)&(2,3)&(17,7)&(25,28)\\
1/16&(1,2),(2,2)&(2,4)&(10,14)&(24,30)\\
0&(1,1),(2,3)&(1,6)&(13,11)&(20,35)
\end{array}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Ising double poles]\label{prop:ising-double-poles}
For \(N<7\) every pole of \(H_N(\tfrac12,\Delta,d)\) in \(\Delta\) is
simple.  Over \(\mathbb Q(d)\), the first double pole at each interior weight
\(\lambda\) occurs at the level \(N\) shown in the table, and its leading
Laurent coefficient is
\(\lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^2H_N(\tfrac12,\Delta,d)=\mathcal N(d)/\gamma\):
\[
\begin{array}{cclr}
\lambda&N&\mathcal N(d)&\gamma\\ \hline
1/2&7&{\mathcal N}_{2,1}{\mathcal N}_{1,5}&-700700\\
1/16&10&{\mathcal N}_{1,2}{\mathcal N}_{4,2}&-1121229484375/192\\
0&11&{\mathcal N}_{1,1}{\mathcal N}_{2,5}&-2477917160468750/6561
\end{array}
\]
Here \({\mathcal N}_{R,S}={\mathcal N}_{R,S}(b_\star,d)\) is the polynomial of
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, monic of degree \(2RS\) in \(d\), and
\(\mathcal N\) and \(\gamma\) are the torus and second Jantzen norms of the singular
vector that starts the second Jantzen layer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(rs\leq6\), the only repeated values of \(|4r-3s|\) are \(1,2,5\), giving
the three classes in the table. Consider a
weight with a single label \(a\) of level \(\ell_a\leq N\).  The injective
singular embedding \cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}
puts \(p(N-\ell_a)\) independent vectors in the radical.  This number is the
valuation of the Kac determinant, so all positive Smith exponents are \(1\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}).  At the three interior weights,
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} gives \(j_N(\lambda)\leq1\) below the
second-layer levels \(7\), \(10\) and \(11\).  At those levels~\eqref{eq:top-layer} has the single term \(\mathcal N/\gamma\),
with \(M=0\). For the factors of \(\mathcal N\), use the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria} with \(j=1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} with \(c_2=-1\) gives
\(\gamma=-[d^2]\mathcal N/\varrho^2\), where \(\varrho=-n_0/2\).
\end{proof}

The ancillary scripts compare the norms \(\gamma\) at \(N=7\) and \(N=10\)
with direct PBW evaluations of Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(iii), and
reconstruct the double-pole coefficient by Shapovalov inversion at \(N=7\)
and, in the deep suite, at \(N=10\) (see the code availability section).

\section{The sphere four-point block}\label{sec:setup-sph}

Part~II uses the Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\), its PBW basis and the
Shapovalov Gram matrices \(G_N(c,\Delta)\) of Section~\ref{sec:blocks}, and
writes \(v_\Delta=\lvert\Delta\rangle\) for the highest-weight vector and
\(\langle u,v\rangle\) for the Shapovalov form.  In Part~II, \(H_N\) denotes
Zamolodchikov's elliptic coefficient of the sphere block
(Definition~\ref{def:H-series}), not the eta-reduced torus coefficient of
Part~I.

\begin{definition}[Three-point pairing and sphere block]\label{def:sphere-block}
For indeterminate weights \(\Delta,\Delta_a,\Delta_b\) and a partition
\(Y=(y_1\geq\dots\geq y_r\geq1)\), define the three-point polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-Y}
 \Gamma_Y(\Delta;\Delta_a,\Delta_b)
 :=\prod_{j=1}^r\Bigl(\Delta+\sum_{i=j+1}^r y_i+y_j\Delta_b-\Delta_a\Bigr)
 \in\mathbb Z[\Delta,\Delta_a,\Delta_b],
\end{equation}
with \(\Gamma_\emptyset=1\).  Let \(\langle u\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle\)
denote the unique linear functional on \(M(c,\Delta)\) with
\(\langle v_\Delta\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle=1\) and
\(\langle L_{-n}u\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle
=(h(u)+n\Delta_b-\Delta_a)\langle u\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle\) for
homogeneous \(u\) of \(L_0\)-weight \(h(u)\) and \(n\geq1\).  Induction on
\(r\) gives \(\langle L_{-Y}v_\Delta\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle
=\Gamma_Y(\Delta;\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\).

The \(s\)-channel Verma sphere four-point block is
\[
 \mathcal F(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;z)
 =z^{\Delta-\Delta_1-\Delta_2}\mathcal B(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;z),
 \qquad
 \mathcal B(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;z)
 =\sum_{N=0}^\infty\mathcal B_N(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)\,z^N,
\]
with \(\mathcal B_0=1\) and, for \(N\geq1\),
\begin{multline}\label{eq:B-def}
 \mathcal B_N(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 :=\sum_{|Y|=|Z|=N}\Gamma_Y(\Delta;\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,
   \bigl(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1}\bigr)_{Y,Z}\,\Gamma_Z(\Delta;\Delta_1,\Delta_2)\\
 \in\mathbb Q(c,\Delta)[\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4].
\end{multline}
\end{definition}

The order in~\eqref{eq:B-def} places the external primaries of weights
\((\Delta_4,\Delta_3,\Delta_2,\Delta_1)\) at \((\infty,1,z,0)\), so \(\mathcal B_1=(\Delta+\Delta_3-\Delta_4)(\Delta+\Delta_2-\Delta_1)/(2\Delta)\).

\begin{definition}[Elliptic nome and Zamolodchikov \(H\)-series]\label{def:H-series}
Define the Jacobi theta series
\[
 \theta_3(q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n^2},\qquad
 \theta_4(q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^n q^{n^2},\qquad
 \theta_2(q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{(n+1/2)^2}.
\]
The cross-ratio \(z(q):=\theta_2(q)^4/\theta_3(q)^4\) lies in
\(16q(1+q\mathbb Z[[q]])\), and its compositional inverse \(q(z)\) lies in
\(\frac{z}{16}(1+z\mathbb Q[[z]])\).  The series \(q(z)\) is the elliptic nome
\(\exp(-\pi K(1-z)/K(z))\), where
\(K(z)=\frac\pi2{}_2F_1(\tfrac12,\tfrac12;1;z)\).  Define Zamolodchikov's prefactor~\cite[\S3, the formula after (18)]{Zamolodchikov1987}
\begin{multline}\label{eq:Phi-def}
 \Phi(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)
 :=(16q)^{\Delta-\frac{c-1}{24}}\,
   z(q)^{\frac{c-1}{24}-\Delta_1-\Delta_2}\,
   \bigl(1-z(q)\bigr)^{\frac{c-1}{24}-\Delta_2-\Delta_3}\\
   \times\theta_3(q)^{\frac{c-1}{2}-4(\Delta_1+\Delta_2+\Delta_3+\Delta_4)},
\end{multline}
and the elliptic \(H\)-series
\(H(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)=\sum_{N=0}^\infty H_N(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)(16q)^N\)
(\(H_0=1\)) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-def}
 H(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)
 :=\widetilde\Phi(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)^{-1}
   \sum_{m=0}^\infty\mathcal B_m(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)\,z(q)^m,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde\Phi(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)
 &:=z(q)^{-\Delta+\Delta_1+\Delta_2}\Phi(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)\\
 &=\Bigl(\frac{16q}{z(q)}\Bigr)^{\Delta-\frac{c-1}{24}}
  \bigl(1-z(q)\bigr)^{\frac{c-1}{24}-\Delta_2-\Delta_3}
  \theta_3(q)^{\frac{c-1}{2}-4\sum_{j=1}^4\Delta_j}
\end{align*}
lies in \(1+q\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4][[q]]\).  Thus
\(\mathcal B=\widetilde\Phi H\) and \(\mathcal F=\Phi H\): the prefactor
\(\Phi\) relates \(H\) to the block, and \(\widetilde\Phi\) relates it to the
cross-ratio series.
\end{definition}

Let \(\psi_r(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) be the coefficient of \(z^r\) in
\(\widetilde\Phi(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q(z))\).  Since
\((16q(z))^m=z^m(16q(z)/z)^m\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-from-H}
 \mathcal B_N(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 =\sum_{m=0}^N\psi_{N-m}(c,\Delta+m,\Delta_i)\,H_m(c,\Delta,\Delta_i),
\end{equation}
where \(\psi_r(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)\in\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4]\).
Since \(16q(z)/z=1+\tfrac12 z+O(z^2)\), the coefficient \(\psi_r\) has degree
\(r\) in \(\Delta\) with leading term \(\frac{1}{2^r r!}\Delta^r\).
The large-weight limit
\(\lim_{\Delta\to\infty}H_m(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)=\delta_{m,0}\), for every
\(m\geq0\) and every \((c,\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4)\in\mathbb
C^5\)~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{PolzerLargeWeight2026}, then gives
\(\mathcal B_N\sim\frac{1}{2^N N!}\Delta^N\), so the polynomial part of
\(\mathcal B_N\) has degree \(N\).  Since \(\widetilde\Phi^{-1}\) has
coefficients polynomial in \((c,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) and \(\det G_m\) divides
\(\det G_N\) for \(m\leq N\) (Kac determinant formula~\cite[Theorem~4.2,
(4.16) and (4.21)]{IoharaKoga2011}), \(\det G_N(c,\Delta)\) is a common
denominator of \(H_N\) that does not depend on the external weights.
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion-sph} and Theorem~\ref{thm:confluence}
give the recursion for \(H_N\) at generic \(b\) and at fixed \(b_\star\).

\section{Generic recursion and confluence for the sphere block}\label{sec:confluence}

Throughout, write \(c(b)=1+6Q(b)^2\) with \(Q(b)=b+b^{-1}\),
\(h_{r,s}(b)=\frac14(Q(b)^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2)\) for \(r,s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\),
and parameterize external weights by \(\Lambda_j^2=Q(b)^2-4\Delta_j\).  The
sign of \(\Lambda_j\) does not matter: the fusion polynomial below is even in
each of \(\Lambda_a\) and \(\Lambda_b\).

\begin{definition}[Sphere generic residue data]\label{def:sphere-residues}
For \(r,s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and indeterminate \(\Delta_a,\Delta_b\), define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A_{r,s}(b)
 &:=\frac12\prod_{\substack{1-r\leq m\leq r,\ 1-s\leq n\leq s\\(m,n)\neq(0,0),(r,s)}}(mb+nb^{-1})^{-1},
 \label{eq:Ars-def}\\
 P_{r,s}(b,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)
 &:=\prod_{\substack{j=1-r\\j\equiv r-1\ (2)}}^{r-1}
    \prod_{\substack{l=1-s\\l\equiv s-1\ (2)}}^{s-1}
    \frac{\Lambda_a+\Lambda_b+jb+lb^{-1}}{2}\,
    \frac{\Lambda_a-\Lambda_b+jb+lb^{-1}}{2},
 \label{eq:Prs-def}
\end{align}
and set
\(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{sph}}_{r,s}(b,\Delta_i)
:=\mathcal A_{r,s}(b)\,P_{r,s}(b,\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,P_{r,s}(b,\Delta_1,\Delta_2)\).
Pairing \((j,l)\) with \((-j,-l)\) shows that \(P_{r,s}\) is even in each of
\(\Lambda_a\) and \(\Lambda_b\), so that
\(P_{r,s}(b,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\in\mathbb Q[b^2,b^{-2},\Delta_a,\Delta_b]\);
it has total degree \(rs\) in \((\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Three-point factorization]\label{lem:3pt-factor}
For any homogeneous vector \(w\in M(c,h)_\ell\) and any partition \(Z\),
\[
 \langle L_{-Z}w\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle
 =\Gamma_Z(h+\ell;\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\,\langle w\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the Ward recursion of Definition~\ref{def:sphere-block} successively to
the parts of \(Z\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Singular-vector norm and three-point factor]\label{prop:imported}
For positive integers \(r,s\) and generic \(b\), let
\(\chi_{r,s}(b)\in M(c(b),h_{r,s}(b))_{rs}\) be the unique singular vector
with \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\)~\cite[Propositions~5.1--5.2 and Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011},
\cite[Theorem~3.1 and Corollary~3.2]{Astashkevich1997}.
Its Shapovalov crossing scalar \(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\), the
\(\Delta\)-derivative of its norm at fixed \(c(b)\) and fixed PBW coefficients,
and its sphere three-point scalar are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:imported-ids}
 \beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}=\mathcal A_{r,s}(b)^{-1},\qquad
 \langle\chi_{r,s}(b)\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle=P_{r,s}(b,\Delta_a,\Delta_b).
\end{equation}
The norm identity is used only at generic \(b\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere} the vector \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) is regular at
every \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), so the three-point identity specializes
there.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is Zamolodchikov's norm formula, the product
of~\cite[\S3, (17)]{Zamolodchikov1987}.  It also follows from the
\(\alpha\)-derivative of the singular-vector norm defined in~\cite[(6.1)]{Zamolodchikov2004}
and evaluated in~\cite[(7.5)]{Zamolodchikov2004}: with \((m,n)=(s,r)\), changing
from \(\alpha\) to \(\Delta\) divides by \(d\Delta/d\alpha|_{\alpha_{r,s}}=rb+s/b\) and
gives \(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}=\mathcal A_{r,s}(b)^{-1}\).  The formula after (7)
in~\cite[\S3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010} gives \(\mathcal A_{r,s}\) as
the reciprocal \(\Delta\)-derivative of the norm, as
in~Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations}.  The second identity is Zamolodchikov's
fusion polynomial~\cite[\S3, (17)]{Zamolodchikov1987}, proved
in~\cite[\S5, (5.9)--(5.12)]{Zamolodchikov2004}.  Its sign in our convention is \(+1\),
because the leading coefficient in \(\Delta_b-\Delta_a\) of both sides is
\(+1\) by~\eqref{eq:gamma-Y} and~\eqref{eq:Prs-def}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic Zamolodchikov recursion]\label{prop:generic-recursion-sph}
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For every \(b\in\mathbb C^\times\) outside a finite set of
algebraic values,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-rec}
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,\Delta_i)
 =\sum_{\substack{a=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_a=rs\leq N}}
  \frac{\mathcal R^{\mathrm{sph}}_{r,s}(b,\Delta_i)}{\Delta-h_{r,s}(b)}\,
  H_{N-\ell_a}\bigl(c(b),h_{r,s}(b)+rs,\Delta_i\bigr)
\end{equation}
as an identity of rational functions of \(\Delta\), polynomial in
\((\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exclude \(b=0\) and the finitely many algebraic values, for
\(\ell_a,\ell_g\leq N\), where some \(h_a(b)-h_g(b)\) (\(a\neq g\)) or
\(h_a(b)+\ell_a-h_g(b)\) vanishes (Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}) or where
\(\mathcal A_a(b)\) has a pole.  Let \(a=(r,s)\) with \(\ell_a\leq N\).  Since
the Kac root \(h_a(b)\) is distinct from the others and the embedding of
\(M(c(b),h_a(b)+\ell_a)\) is injective (as in the proof
of~Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}), for \(\ell_a\leq m\leq N\) the
kernel of \(G_m(c(b),h_a(b))\) is the \(p(m-\ell_a)\)-dimensional level-\(m\)
part of \(U(\Vir_-)\chi_a(b)\), and \(\det G_m\) vanishes to that order at
\(\Delta=h_a(b)\).  So every positive local Smith exponent there is \(1\).  For
\(m<\ell_a\) there is no pole at \(h_a(b)\).  By Smith-form residue extraction
(Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii)), Lemma~\ref{lem:3pt-factor} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported},
\[
 \Res_{\Delta=h_a}\mathcal B_m(c,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 =\mathcal R^{\mathrm{sph}}_a(b,\Delta_i)\,\mathcal B_{m-\ell_a}(c,h_a+\ell_a,\Delta_i)
 \qquad(\ell_a\leq m\leq N).
\]
The prefactor \(\widetilde\Phi(c,\Delta,\Delta_i;q)\) is regular in \(\Delta\) and
satisfies
\[
 \frac{\widetilde\Phi(c,h_a+\ell_a,\Delta_i;q)}{\widetilde\Phi(c,h_a,\Delta_i;q)}
 =\Bigl(\frac{16q}{z(q)}\Bigr)^{\ell_a}.
\]
The factor \(z(q)^{\ell_a}\) turns the right side into \((16q)^{\ell_a}\). Thus
\[
 \Res_{\Delta=h_a}H_N
 =\mathcal R^{\mathrm{sph}}_a(b,\Delta_i)H_{N-\ell_a}(c,h_a+\ell_a,\Delta_i).
\]
These residues exhaust the poles of \(H_N\), which are simple and lie at
\(\{h_a(b):\ell_a\leq N\}\).  The large-weight limit~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{PolzerLargeWeight2026} gives zero
polynomial part, so their partial fractions sum to~\eqref{eq:generic-rec}.
\end{proof}

Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and put
\(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and \(t=b-b_\star\).  We work in \(K_\star((t))\), where
\(K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)\).  For a label
\(a=(r,s)\) with \(\ell_a=rs\), write \(h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\),
\(z_a(t)=h_a(t)+\ell_a\) and
\(R_a(t)=\mathcal R^{\mathrm{sph}}_{r,s}(b_\star+t,\Delta_i)\).  Let
\(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star):=\{h_a(0):\ell_a\leq N\}\) and
\(\mathcal C^\star_{\lambda,N}:=\{a:\ell_a\leq N,\ h_a(0)=\lambda\}\).

\begin{definition}[Shifted Laurent recursion and collision moments]
\label{def:confluence}
Define \(S_{a,0}(t):=1\) and, recursively for \(M\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shifted-rec}
 S_{a,M}(t)
 :=\sum_{\substack{g=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_g=uv\leq M}}
  \frac{R_g(t)}{z_a(t)-h_g(t)}\,S_{g,M-\ell_g}(t)\in K_\star((t)).
\end{equation}
Every denominator \(z_a(t)-h_g(t)\) is nonzero in \(K_\star[[t]]\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}.  For \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) and
\(a\in\mathcal C^\star_{\lambda,N}\), put \(B_{a,N}(t):=R_a(t)S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\)
and \(\delta_a(t):=h_a(t)-\lambda\in tK_\star[[t]]\), and define the
\emph{collision moments}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-moments-sph}
 \widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4)
 :=\CT_t\sum_{a\in\mathcal C^\star_{\lambda,N}}B_{a,N}(t)\,\delta_a(t)^{k-1}
 \qquad(k\geq1).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[All-order confluent sphere recursion at fixed \(b_\star\)]
\label{thm:confluence}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(N\geq1\).
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{3pt}
\item[(i)] Every collision moment \(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\) lies
  in \(\mathbb Q(b_\star)[\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4]\) and only
  finitely many are nonzero.
\item[(ii)] As a rational function of \(\Delta\) with coefficients in
  \(\mathbb Q(b_\star)[\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4]\),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:confluence-H}
   H_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)
   =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}
    \sum_{k\geq1}
    \frac{\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
  \end{equation}
\item[(iii)] Substituting~\eqref{eq:confluence-H} into~\eqref{eq:B-from-H}
  yields the exact finite expansion of the cross-ratio coefficient
  \(\mathcal B_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) as a degree-\(N\) polynomial in
  \(\Delta\) plus a finite partial-fraction sum over \(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion-sph} and induction on \(M\),
\(S_{a,M}(t)=H_M(c(b_\star+t),z_a(t),\Delta_i)\) in \(K_\star((t))\).  Setting
\(C_{\lambda,k}(t):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal C^\star_{\lambda,N}}B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1}\in K_\star((t))\),
we expand in \(K_\star(\Delta)((t))\):
\begin{align*}
 H_N(c(b_\star+t),\Delta,\Delta_i)
 &=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}\sum_{a\in\mathcal C^\star_{\lambda,N}}
  \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-\lambda-\delta_a(t)}
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}\sum_{k=1}^\infty
  \frac{C_{\lambda,k}(t)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\end{align*}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}, applied over \(K_\star\), only
finitely many \(k\) contribute to each power of \(t\).  Since the
Gram denominator \(\det G_N(c(b_\star+t),\Delta)\) is nonzero at \(t=0\) as a
polynomial in \(\Delta\), the left side lies in \(K_\star(\Delta)[[t]]\) and
specializes to \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)\).  For each \(m<0\), uniqueness of
partial fractions at the distinct \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) forces
\([t^m]C_{\lambda,k}=0\) for all \(k\), so every \(C_{\lambda,k}\) lies in
\(K_\star[[t]]\).  Taking \(t=0\) now
gives~\eqref{eq:confluence-H}, a finite sum, with
\(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}=C_{\lambda,k}(0)\).  By Section~\ref{sec:setup-sph}, the denominator \(\det G_N(c_\star,\Delta)\) of
\(H_N\) does not depend on the external weights, so every principal-part
coefficient is polynomial in them over \(\mathbb Q(b_\star)\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:B-from-H} gives part~(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Truncation orders]\label{rem:sph-truncation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}, Corollary~\ref{cor:pole-bound} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-budget} hold for the shifted Laurent
recursion~\eqref{eq:shifted-rec}, with the same proofs, when \(R_g\) is the
sphere residue and \(K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4)\).
So each collision moment is computed in finitely many algebraic operations,
and the pole order of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda\) is at most
\(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Computing in \(\nu=b^2\)]\label{rem:nu-parameter}
All ingredients \(h_{r,s}\), \(\mathcal A_{r,s}\), and \(P_{r,s}\) are rational
functions of \(\nu=b^2\) over \(\mathbb Q\): one has
\(\mathcal A_{r,s}=\frac12\nu^{2rs-1}\prod(m\nu+n)^{-1}\), and
Definition~\ref{def:sphere-residues} gives \(P_{r,s}\in\mathbb Q[b^2,b^{-2},\Delta_a,\Delta_b]\).
Under the invertible coordinate change \(u=\nu-b_\star^2=2b_\star t+t^2\)
(\(du/dt|_{t=0}=2b_\star\neq0\)), the constant term of a general Laurent series
is not preserved: \(t^{-1}=2b_\star u^{-1}+(2b_\star)^{-1}+O(u)\).  By the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:confluence}, each grouped numerator
\(C_{\lambda,k}\) is regular at the collision, so either coordinate gives its
value there:
\(\CT_u C_{\lambda,k}=C_{\lambda,k}(0)=\CT_t C_{\lambda,k}=\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\).
If \(b_\star^2\in\mathbb Q\), computing in \(u\) takes place over
\(\mathbb Q[\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_4]\).
\end{remark}

\begin{example*}[Two poles in \(b\) that make a double pole in \(\Delta\)]
Let \(b_\star^2=-3/2\), so that \(c_\star=0\) and
\(h_{r,s}(b_\star)=((3r-2s)^2-1)/24\), take \(N=5\) and \(\lambda=0\), and keep
the external weights indeterminate.  The
labels with \(rs\leq5\) and weight \(0\) are \((1,1)\) and \((1,2)\), and every
\(\mathcal A_{r,s}\) with \(rs\leq5\) is regular at \(b_\star\).  By the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:confluence},
\[
 B_{(1,1),5}=R_{(1,1)}(t)\,H_4\bigl(c(b),1,\Delta_i\bigr),\qquad
 B_{(1,2),5}=R_{(1,2)}(t)\,H_3\bigl(c(b),h_{1,2}(b)+2,\Delta_i\bigr),
\]
with \(b=b_\star+t\) and \(R_a(t)\) as defined before
Definition~\ref{def:confluence}.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion-sph},
\(H_4\) has a simple pole at \(h_{1,4}(b)\), which tends to \(1\), with residue
\(R_{(1,4)}(t)\), and \(H_3\) has one at \(h_{3,1}(b)\), which tends to
\(2=h_{1,2}(b_\star)+2\), with residue \(R_{(3,1)}(t)\).  No other shifted denominator in the two recursions vanishes at
\(t=0\).  Write \(h'_{r,s}\) for the derivative of \(h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\) at
\(t=0\) and \(\mathcal R_{r,s}\) for
\(R_{(r,s)}(0)=\mathcal R^{\mathrm{sph}}_{r,s}(b_\star,\Delta_i)\).  Then
\(h'_{1,4}=5h'_{1,2}\) and \(h'_{3,1}=-6h'_{1,2}\), so the two terms have the
poles \(-\mathcal R_{1,1}\mathcal R_{1,4}/(5h'_{1,2}t)\) and
\(\mathcal R_{1,2}\mathcal R_{3,1}/(7h'_{1,2}t)\) in \(t\); neither has a value at
\(b_\star\), and the poles cancel in the first moment.  In the second moment the
label \((1,1)\) drops out, since \(h_{1,1}\equiv0\), and
\(\delta_{(1,2)}(t)=h'_{1,2}t+O(t^2)\) turns the pole of \(B_{(1,2),5}\) into a
constant.  The pole of \(H_5\) at \(\Delta=0\) has order at most \(j_5(0)=2\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-bound}), so
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to0}\Delta^2H_5(0,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 =\frac{\mathcal R_{1,2}\mathcal R_{3,1}}{7}
 =\frac{\mathcal R_{1,1}\mathcal R_{1,4}}{5}
 =-\frac{3}{11200}\,\varphi(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,\varphi(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)
\]
with \(\varphi=P_{1,1}P_{1,4}\), because \(\mathcal A_{1,1}=\tfrac12\) and
\(\mathcal A_{1,4}(b_\star)=-\tfrac3{1120}\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:c0-example}
obtains the same coefficient from the top Jantzen layer.  A script listed
under Code availability checks each step in exact arithmetic and compares both
moments with a direct Gram-matrix computation of \(H_5\) at sample external
weights.
\end{example*}

\section{Minimal-model pole orders for the sphere block}\label{sec:jantzen}

Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime integers.  Exchanging \(p\) with \(p'\) and \(r\)
with \(s\) leaves the charge and the Kac weight unchanged, so we take
\(p'>p\) and set
\[
 b_\star=i\sqrt{\frac{p'}{p}},\qquad
 c_\star=1-\frac{6(p'-p)^2}{pp'},\qquad
 Q_\star=i\frac{p'-p}{\sqrt{pp'}}.
\]
Let \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)=\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}\) be an
interior Kac weight, so \(1\leq r\leq p-1\) and \(1\leq s\leq p'-1\).  Of the
two labels \((r,s)\) and \((p-r,p'-s)\) of \(\lambda\), choose the one with
\(p'r+ps<pp'\); equality is impossible since \(\gcd(p,p')=1\).  Put
\[
 n_1:=p'r+ps<pp',\qquad n_0:=p'r-ps\neq0.
\]

We use the layer levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), \(\ell_k=\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\)
and the Jantzen depth \(j_N(\lambda)\) of Section~\ref{sec:mm-layers}, and put
\(\mu^\pm_k:=\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\).
The shifted weights \(\mu^\pm_k=((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\) have
positive integer Coulomb momenta
\(m^+_{2j+1}=2jpp'+n_1<2(j+1)pp'-n_1=m^-_{2j+1}\) and
\(m^\pm_{2j}=2jpp'\pm n_0\).  In both parities \(m^+_k\neq m^-_k\) and
\(m^+_k+m^-_k=2kpp'\).  Write \(m^{\mathrm{small}}_k<m^{\mathrm{big}}_k\) for
the two momenta, and use the same labels for the corresponding levels
\(\ell^{\mathrm{small}}_k<\ell^{\mathrm{big}}_k\), shifted weights
\(\mu^{\mathrm{small}}_k<\mu^{\mathrm{big}}_k\) and singular vectors
\(w^{\mathrm{small}}_k,w^{\mathrm{big}}_k\).  Since \(n_1<pp'\) and
\(n_0\neq0\), \(m^{\mathrm{big}}_k\geq kpp'+1\).

This section uses the results of Part~I on the Verma module
\(M(c_\star,\lambda)\), which concern the Shapovalov matrix alone:
Lemmas~\ref{lem:smith}, \ref{lem:jantzen-smith} and~\ref{lem:top-coefficient},
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} with its
proof.  We write \(W^\pm_k:=U(\Vir_-)w^\pm_k\) for the submodules generated by
the singular vectors \(w^\pm_k\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}.

\begin{example*}[\((p,p')=(2,3)\), \(\lambda=0\)]
Here \(c_\star=0\), \(r=s=1\), \(n_1=5\) and \(n_0=1\), so \(\ell^+_1=1\),
\(\ell^-_1=2\), \(\ell^-_2=5\), \(\ell^+_2=7\) and \(\ell_3=12\).  Thus
\(j_N(0)=1\) for \(1\leq N\leq4\) and \(j_N(0)=2\) for \(5\leq N\leq11\).  The
example after Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} shows the level-two Gram matrix.
For indeterminate external weights, the first double pole at \(\Delta=0\)
appears at level five (Proposition~\ref{prop:c0-example}).
\end{example*}

\begin{theorem}[Jantzen pole-order bound]\label{thm:jantzen-bound}
For every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\), every interior Kac weight
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\), and every \(N\geq0\), both
\(\mathcal B_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) have poles of order at most \(j_N(\lambda)\)
at \(\Delta=\lambda\); order zero means that they are regular there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, \(G_N(c_\star,\Delta)^{-1}\) has a pole of order exactly
\(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).  Since
\(\Gamma_Y(\Delta;\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\) is polynomial in \(\Delta\),
\(\mathcal B_N\) has pole order at most \(j_N(\lambda)\).  Since
\(\widetilde\Phi^{-1}\) in~\eqref{eq:H-def} has coefficients polynomial in
\(\Delta\) and \(j_m(\lambda)\leq j_N(\lambda)\) for \(m\leq N\), so does
\(H_N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit singular three-point polynomials \(\varphi^\pm_k\)]
\label{lem:pi-factorization}
For \(k\geq1\), let \(w^\pm_k\in M(c_\star,\lambda)_{\ell^\pm_k}\) be the
unique singular vector with \(L_{-1}^{\ell^\pm_k}\)-coefficient \(1\), and
set \(\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_a,\Delta_b):=\langle w^\pm_k\mid V_{\Delta_b}(1)\mid\Delta_a\rangle\).
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{3pt}
\item[(i)] For odd \(k=2j+1\) (\(j\geq0\)), with
  \((R^+_k,S^+_k)=(r+jp,s+jp')\) and
  \((R^-_k,S^-_k)=((j+1)p-r,(j+1)p'-s)\),
  \(\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_a,\Delta_b)=P_{R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k}(b_\star,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\).
\item[(ii)] For even \(k=2j\) (\(j\geq1\)), with
  \((R^+_k,S^+_k)=(r+jp,jp'-s)\) and \((R^-_k,S^-_k)=(jp-r,jp'+s)\),
  \(\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_a,\Delta_b)=P_{r,s}(b_\star,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\,P_{R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k}(b_\star,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\).
\item[(iii)] Write \(\Lambda_a=ix_a/\sqrt{pp'}\) and
  \(\Lambda_b=ix_b/\sqrt{pp'}\), so that
  \(\Delta_a=(x_a^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), and likewise for \(\Delta_b\).  In
  the \((x_a,x_b)\)-plane the zero locus of \(\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\) is
  a union of lines \(x_a\pm x_b=e\) with \(|e|\leq m^\pm_k-p'-p\), and it
  contains both lines \(x_a-x_b=\pm(m^\pm_k-p'-p)\).  In particular, if
  \(|x_a+x_b|\) and \(|x_a-x_b|\) both exceed \(m^\pm_k-p'-p\), then
  \(\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\neq0\).  These statements are unchanged
  under \((x_a,x_b)\mapsto(-x_a,-x_b)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:imported}, every \(\chi_{R,S}(b_\star)\) is regular
with \(L_{-1}^{RS}\)-coefficient \(1\) and three-point scalar
\(P_{R,S}(b_\star,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\).  The embeddings
of~\cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011} give a nonzero
singular vector at each weight \(\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\) in \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\),
and~\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011} bounds each singular space by one
dimension; see also~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Astashkevich1997}.

For odd \(k=2j+1\), \(h_{R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k}(b_\star)=\lambda\) and
\(R^\pm_kS^\pm_k=\ell^\pm_k\), so \(w^\pm_k=\chi_{R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k}(b_\star)\).

For even \(k=2j\), \(R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k\geq1\),
\(h_{R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k}(b_\star)=\mu^+_1=\lambda+rs\) and
\(R^\pm_kS^\pm_k=\ell^\pm_k-rs\).  The injective Verma map
\(M(c_\star,\mu^+_1)\hookrightarrow M(c_\star,\lambda)\),
\(v_{\mu^+_1}\mapsto\chi_{r,s}(b_\star)\), takes
\(\chi_{R^\pm_k,S^\pm_k}(b_\star)\) to \(w^\pm_k\); apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:3pt-factor} to this image.

For (iii), \(b_\star=ip'/\sqrt{pp'}\) and \(b_\star^{-1}=-ip/\sqrt{pp'}\), so
the roots of \(P_{R,S}(b_\star,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\) are
\(x_a\pm x_b=-(jp'-lp)\), with \(j\) and \(l\) in the ranges
of~\eqref{eq:Prs-def}.  Their maximal magnitude is
\((R-1)p'+(S-1)p=Rp'+Sp-p'-p\), attained at \((j,l)=(-(R-1),S-1)\).  For both
parities \(R^\pm_kp'+S^\pm_kp=m^\pm_k\).  For even \(k\), the roots of the
factor \(P_{r,s}\) have magnitude at most \(n_1-p'-p\), and
\(n_1-p'-p<pp'-p'-p<m^\pm_k-p'-p\).
\end{proof}

For even \(k\), the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria} factors
the torus matrix element of \(w^\pm_k\) through the singular vector of layer
\(2j-1\), whereas
Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(ii) factors \(w^\pm_k\) through
\(\chi_{r,s}(b_\star)\).  Both describe the same vector, since a singular
vector of \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) of a given level is unique up to a scalar.

\begin{theorem}[Top Jantzen layer for \(\mathcal B_N\) and \(H_N\)]
\label{thm:top-layer-sph}
Let \(k\geq1\) and \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\).  Let \(B^k\) be the \(k\)-th
Jantzen form of \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}), and let
\(\gamma^\pm_k:=B^k(w^\pm_k,w^\pm_k)\) be the \(k\)-th Jantzen norms of
\(w^\pm_k\), as in Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}.

Then \(j_N(\lambda)=k\).  For each sign with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N\) one has
\(\gamma^\pm_k\neq0\), and \(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\neq0\) for every
\(M\) with \(0\leq M\leq N-\ell^\pm_k\).  Moreover
\begin{align}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^k\mathcal B_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 &=\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}
   \frac{\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
   \mathcal B_{N-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,\Delta_i),
 \label{eq:top-B}\\
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^k H_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 &=\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}
   \frac{\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
   H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,\Delta_i).
 \label{eq:top-H}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(\ell_k\leq N'<\ell_{k+1}\), Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} and its proof give maximal local Smith exponent
\(k\) for \(G_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x)\), the \(B^k\)-orthogonal direct sum
\(J^k_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\pm:\ell^\pm_k\leq N'}(W^\pm_k)_{N'}\),
the form
\(B^k(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k,L_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)=\gamma^\pm_k G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)_{Z,Z'}\)
on each summand, and the stated nonvanishing of \(\gamma^\pm_k\) and of
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\) for \(M\leq N'-\ell^\pm_k\).  Applying the
formula for \([x^{-k}]\,G_{N'}^{-1}\) in Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii) to the bilinear
form~\eqref{eq:B-def} and factoring via
Lemma~\ref{lem:3pt-factor} gives~\eqref{eq:top-B} at every such level
\(N'\).

For~\eqref{eq:top-H}, apply~\eqref{eq:H-def} at \(\Delta=\lambda+x\).  The
reciprocal prefactor is regular at \(x=0\).  Since \(\mathcal B_m\) has pole
order at most \(k\) for \(m<\ell_{k+1}\), only the constant term of the
prefactor contributes to \([x^{-k}]\), and levels \(m<\ell_k\) contribute zero.
The prefactors at \(\lambda\) and at \(\mu^\pm_k\) are related by
\[
 \widetilde\Phi(c_\star,\lambda,\Delta_i;q)^{-1}z(q)^{\ell^\pm_k}
 =(16q)^{\ell^\pm_k}\widetilde\Phi(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,\Delta_i;q)^{-1}.
\]
Extracting \([x^{-k}]\) and using~\eqref{eq:top-B} at each level of the layer
gives
\begin{multline*}
 \sum_{N'=\ell_k}^{\ell_{k+1}-1}[x^{-k}]H_{N'}(16q)^{N'}\\
 \equiv\sum_{\pm}\frac{\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\varphi^\pm_k(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)}{\gamma^\pm_k}
   (16q)^{\ell^\pm_k}\sum_{r=0}^{\ell_{k+1}-1-\ell^\pm_k}H_r(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,\Delta_i)(16q)^r
\end{multline*}
modulo \(q^{\ell_{k+1}}\).  Extracting \([(16q)^N]\) gives~\eqref{eq:top-H}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp pole order for \(\mathcal B_N\)]
\label{thm:sharp-B}
For every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\), every interior Kac weight
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\), and every level \(N\geq1\), the coefficient
\(\mathcal B_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)\),
viewed as a rational function of \(\Delta\) over
\(\mathbb Q(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)\), has pole order exactly
\(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(k=j_N(\lambda)=0\) there is no pole.  For \(k\geq1\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph} shows that the pole order is exactly \(k\) when
the right side of~\eqref{eq:top-B}, a polynomial in the external weights, is
not zero, and it suffices to find one point where it does not vanish.  We
choose the external weights so that the term of \(w^{\mathrm{big}}_k\)
vanishes when it is present, the three-point factors of
\(w^{\mathrm{small}}_k\) do not, and the remaining coefficient
\(\mathcal B_M\) is a Taylor coefficient of a solution of a BPZ equation, which
can be evaluated.

Fix \(k=j_N(\lambda)\).  For \(k=0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-bound} gives
regularity.  For \(k\geq1\), so that \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\), set
\(M:=N-\ell^{\mathrm{small}}_k\geq0\), \(\mu:=\mu^{\mathrm{small}}_k\) and
\(\Lambda_\mu:=im^{\mathrm{small}}_k/\sqrt{pp'}\).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph}, \(\det G_m(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) for
\(0\leq m\leq M\).  Specialize the external weights to
\(\Delta_j=(x_j^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), where
\[
 x_1=m^{\mathrm{small}}_k+p',\quad
 x_2=2p'-p,\quad
 x_3=kpp'-p,\quad
 x_4=\frac{m^{\mathrm{small}}_k-m^{\mathrm{big}}_k+2p'}{2},
\]
and choose \(\Lambda_j=ix_j/\sqrt{pp'}\) for \(j=1,2\) and
\(\Lambda_j=-ix_j/\sqrt{pp'}\) for \(j=3,4\).  Since \(\Delta_j\) depends only
on \(\Lambda_j^2\), Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(iii) applies to these
values (its statements are unchanged under a common sign change).

\emph{(a) Vanishing of the higher branch.}  Since
\(x_4-x_3=-(m^{\mathrm{big}}_k-p'-p)\), Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(iii)
gives \(\varphi^{\mathrm{big}}_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)=0\).

\emph{(b) Nonvanishing of the lower-branch factors.}  The threshold
\(m^{\mathrm{small}}_k-p'-p\) is nonnegative: for \(k=1\),
\(m^{\mathrm{small}}_1=p'r+ps\geq p'+p\), and for \(k\geq2\),
\(m^{\mathrm{small}}_k>pp'>p'+p\).  Each of the four quantities
\begin{align*}
 x_1+x_2&=m^{\mathrm{small}}_k+3p'-p,&
 x_1-x_2&=m^{\mathrm{small}}_k-p'+p,\\
 x_3-x_4&=m^{\mathrm{big}}_k-p'-p,&
 x_3+x_4&=m^{\mathrm{small}}_k+p'-p
\end{align*}
is larger than this threshold.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(iii),
\(\varphi^{\mathrm{small}}_k(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)\neq0\) and
\(\varphi^{\mathrm{small}}_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\neq0\).

\emph{(c) Exact evaluation of \(\mathcal B_M\).}  Set \(\Delta_2=h_{2,1}(b)\)
and \(\Lambda_1=\Lambda_\mu+b\), with \((b,\Lambda_\mu,\Lambda_3,\Lambda_4)\)
generic.  The fusion factor \(P_{2,1}(b,\Delta_1,\mu)\) vanishes, so the
level-\(2\) null vector \((L_{-2}+b^{-2}L_{-1}^2)v_{h_{2,1}}\) and its
descendants decouple, and \(\mathcal F(z)=z^{\mu-\Delta_1-\Delta_2}\mathcal B(z)\) solves the
hypergeometric BPZ equation~\cite[(5.2), (5.17), (5.19)]{BPZ1984}
\[
 \left(b^{-2}\partial_z^2-\Bigl(\frac1z+\frac{1}{z-1}\Bigr)\partial_z
 +\frac{\Delta_1}{z^2}+\frac{\Delta_3}{(z-1)^2}
 +\frac{\Delta_1+\Delta_2+\Delta_3-\Delta_4}{z(1-z)}\right)\mathcal F(z)=0.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal B_m=[z^m]\bigl((1-z)^\kappa\,{}_2F_1(A,B;C;z)\bigr)\) as an
identity of rational functions of \((b,\Lambda_\mu,\Lambda_3,\Lambda_4)\)
for every \(m\geq0\), with
\begin{align*}
 C&=1+b^2+b\Lambda_\mu,&
 \kappa&=\frac{1+b^2+b\Lambda_3}{2},\\
 A&=\frac{1+b^2+b(\Lambda_\mu+\Lambda_3-\Lambda_4)}{2},&
 B&=\frac{1+b^2+b(\Lambda_\mu+\Lambda_3+\Lambda_4)}{2}.
\end{align*}
At our specialization \(\Delta_2=h_{2,1}(b_\star)\),
\(\Lambda_1=\Lambda_\mu+b_\star\) and
\[
 b_\star^2=-\frac{p'}{p},\qquad
 b_\star\Lambda_\mu=-\frac{m^{\mathrm{small}}_k}{p},\qquad
 b_\star\Lambda_3=\frac{x_3}{p},\qquad
 b_\star\Lambda_4=\frac{x_4}{p}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \(C=1-(p'+m^{\mathrm{small}}_k)/p\notin\{0,-1,\dots,-(M-1)\}\): if
  \(C=1-u\) for an integer \(1\leq u\leq M\), then
  \(m^{\mathrm{small}}_k=up-p'\), so
  \(\Lambda_\mu=-(b_\star+ub_\star^{-1})\) and \(\mu=h_{1,u}(b_\star)\),
  forcing \(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)=0\), contradicting
  Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph}.  Both sides of the identity for \(\mathcal B_m\)
  specialize through degree \(M\), since \(\det G_m(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) and
  \((C)_m\neq0\) for \(m\leq M\).
\item Substitution gives
  \(B=\frac12(1-\frac{p'+m^{\mathrm{small}}_k}{p}+\frac{m^{\mathrm{small}}_k+p'-p}{p})=0\),
  so the hypergeometric factor is \(1\) through degree \(M\).
\item \(\kappa=\frac12(1-\frac{p'}{p}+\frac{kpp'-p}{p})=\frac{(kp-1)p'}{2p}\).
  Since \(\gcd(p,(kp-1)p')=1\) and \(p\geq2\), \(\kappa\notin\mathbb Z\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore \(\mathcal B_M=(-1)^M\binom{\kappa}{M}\neq0\), so the
right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:top-B} is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp pole order for \(H_N\)]
\label{thm:sharp-H}
For every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\), every interior Kac weight
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\), and every level \(N\geq1\), the elliptic
coefficient \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)\),
viewed as a rational function of \(\Delta\) over
\(\mathbb Q(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_4)\), has pole order exactly
\(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The plan is the one of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-B}, with a different
specialization.  Setting \((\Delta_1,\Delta_2)=(\mu,0)\) inserts the
identity, so that \(\mathcal B_m=\delta_{m,0}\) through the level needed and
\(H_M\) comes from the prefactor \(\widetilde\Phi\) alone.  On the line
\(\Lambda_3-\Lambda_4=i\alpha\), \(\Lambda_3+\Lambda_4=iv\) in the momenta,
with \(\alpha\) fixed below, the top coefficient \([v^M]H_M\)
is then a value of a Hermite polynomial, and its nonvanishing is a question
about the roots of Hermite polynomials.

Use \(k\), \(M\) and \(\mu\) as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-B}; the
case \(k=0\) is the same.  For \(k\geq1\) specialize
\((\Delta_1,\Delta_2):=(\mu,0)\); in the coordinates of
Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(iii) this is \(x_1=m^{\mathrm{small}}_k\) and
\(x_2=p'-p\).  Put \(e_k:=m^{\mathrm{big}}_k-p'-p\) and
\(\alpha:=e_k/\sqrt{pp'}\), and restrict the momenta \((\Lambda_3,\Lambda_4)\) to the affine
line
\[
 \Lambda_3-\Lambda_4=i\alpha,\qquad \Lambda_3+\Lambda_4=iv,
\]
with \(v\) an indeterminate.
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \emph{Vanishing of the higher branch.}  By
  Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(iii), \(\varphi^{\mathrm{big}}_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)=0\)
  identically in \(v\).
\item \emph{Nonvanishing of the lower-branch factors.}  Both
  \(x_1\pm x_2=m^{\mathrm{small}}_k\pm(p'-p)\) exceed the nonnegative
  threshold \(m^{\mathrm{small}}_k-p'-p\), so
  \(\varphi^{\mathrm{small}}_k(\mu,0)\neq0\).  On the affine line, every linear
  factor of \(\varphi^{\mathrm{small}}_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\) is either nonconstant
  in \(v\) or a constant \(\alpha\pm c\) with
  \(|c|\leq(m^{\mathrm{small}}_k-p'-p)/\sqrt{pp'}<\alpha\), so
  \(\varphi^{\mathrm{small}}_k(\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(v\).
\item \emph{Identity insertion and the Hermite coefficient.}
  At \((\Delta_1,\Delta_2)=(\mu,0)\), the three-point polynomial
  \(\Gamma_Z(\mu;\mu,0)\) of~\eqref{eq:gamma-Y} vanishes for every nonempty
  partition \(Z=(z_1,\dots,z_r)\), because its last factor, \(j=r\), is
  \(\mu+0+0-\mu=0\).  Since \(\det G_m(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) for
  \(0\leq m\leq M\), it follows that
  \(\mathcal B_m(c_\star,\mu,\mu,0,\Delta_3(v),\Delta_4(v))=\delta_{m,0}\)
  for \(0\leq m\leq M\).  By~\eqref{eq:H-def}, modulo \(q^{M+1}\),
  \begin{multline*}
   H(c_\star,\mu,\mu,0,\Delta_3(v),\Delta_4(v);q)\\
   \equiv\left(\frac{16q}{z}\right)^{\frac{c_\star-1}{24}-\mu}
         (1-z)^{\Delta_3(v)-\frac{c_\star-1}{24}}
         \theta_3^{4(\mu+\Delta_3(v)+\Delta_4(v))-\frac{c_\star-1}{2}}.
  \end{multline*}
  Since \(\Delta_3(v)=\frac{Q_\star^2}{4}+\frac{(v+\alpha)^2}{16}\),
  \(\Delta_4(v)=\frac{Q_\star^2}{4}+\frac{(v-\alpha)^2}{16}\), and
  \(1-z=(\theta_4/\theta_3)^4\), the \(v\)-dependent factor is
  \[
   \bigl(\theta_3(q)\theta_4(q)\bigr)^{v^2/4}
   \left(\frac{\theta_4(q)}{\theta_3(q)}\right)^{\alpha v/2}
   =\exp\bigl(-2\alpha vq-v^2q^2+vO(q^3)+v^2O(q^4)\bigr).
  \]
  In the logarithm of the right-hand side, the monomials \(-2\alpha vq\) and
  \(-v^2q^2\) have equal degree in \(v\) and in \(q\).  The remaining
  \(v\)-independent factor is \(1+O(q)\).  Every other monomial \(v^aq^b\) has
  \(a<b\).  Thus
  \(f(v):=H_M(c_\star,\mu,\mu,0,\Delta_3(v),\Delta_4(v))\) is a polynomial in
  \(v\) of degree at most \(M\).  Let \(\mathrm{Herm}_M(x)\) denote the \(M\)-th
  physicist's Hermite polynomial, so that
  \(\exp(2x\xi-\xi^2)=\sum_{n\geq0}\mathrm{Herm}_n(x)\xi^n/n!\).  The \([v^M]\) coefficient of \(f\)
  is
  \[
   [v^M]f(v)
   =16^{-M}[\xi^M]\exp(-2\alpha\xi-\xi^2)
   =\frac{(-1)^M}{16^M M!}\mathrm{Herm}_M(\alpha).
  \]
\item \emph{Nonvanishing of \(\mathrm{Herm}_M(\alpha)\).}  Because
  \(m^{\mathrm{big}}_k\geq pp'+1\), we have
  \(e_k\geq(p-1)(p'-1)\geq2\) and
  \(\alpha^2=\frac{e_k^2}{pp'}\geq\frac{(p-1)^2}{p}\cdot\frac{(p'-1)^2}{p'}\).
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \(M=0\): \(\mathrm{Herm}_0(\alpha)=1\neq0\).
  \item \(M=1\): \(\mathrm{Herm}_1(\alpha)=2\alpha>0\).
  \item \(M=2\): \(\mathrm{Herm}_2(\alpha)=4\alpha^2-2>0\) since
    \(\alpha^2\geq\frac{1^2\cdot 2^2}{2\cdot 3}=\frac23>\frac12\).
  \item \(M=3\): \(\mathrm{Herm}_3(\alpha)=4\alpha(2\alpha^2-3)\).  The function
    \((x-1)^2/x\) increases strictly on \(x\geq1\).  For coprime \(p'>p\geq2\)
    with \((p,p')\neq(2,3)\), either \(p=2\) and \(p'\geq5\), and then
    \(\alpha^2\geq\frac12\cdot\frac{16}{5}=\frac85\), or \(p\geq3\) and
    \(p'\geq4\), and then \(\alpha^2\geq\frac43\cdot\frac94=3\).  In both cases
    \(\alpha^2\geq\frac85>\frac32\).  At
    \((p,p')=(2,3)\) the only interior weight is \(\lambda=h_{1,1}=0\), with
    \(r=s=1\).  For \(k=1\), \(m^{\mathrm{big}}_1=7\), so \(e_1=2\) and
    \(\alpha^2=\frac23\).  For \(k\geq2\), \(e_k\geq13-5=8\) and
    \(\alpha^2\geq\frac{32}{3}\).  In every case \(\alpha^2\neq\frac32\).
  \item \(M\geq4\): Write \(M=2m+\varepsilon\) with \(m\geq2\) and
    \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\).  The monic Hermite polynomials are
    \begin{gather*}
     \mathrm{He}_n(x)=2^{-n/2}\mathrm{Herm}_n(x/\sqrt2),\\
     \mathrm{He}_0=1,\quad \mathrm{He}_1=x,\quad
     \mathrm{He}_{n+1}=x\mathrm{He}_n-n\mathrm{He}_{n-1}.
    \end{gather*}
    Define \(K^{(0)}_m,K^{(1)}_m\in\mathbb Q[t]\), both of degree \(m\), by
    \(\mathrm{He}_{2m}(x)=K^{(0)}_m(x^2)\) and
    \(\mathrm{He}_{2m+1}(x)=xK^{(1)}_m(x^2)\).  By Schur~\cite{Schur1929}
    (stated as Hilfssatz~D in~\cite[\S1, p.~54]{Schur1931}, where \(H_n\)
    denotes \(\mathrm{He}_n\)), \(\mathrm{He}_{2m+\varepsilon}(x)/x^\varepsilon\) is
    irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for \(m\geq2\).  A rational root \(s\) of
    \(K^{(\varepsilon)}_m\) would give the proper factor \(x^2-s\) of this
    quotient of degree \(2m\geq4\); hence \(K^{(\varepsilon)}_m\) has no rational
    root.  From
    \(\mathrm{Herm}_n(x)=2^{n/2}\mathrm{He}_n(\sqrt2\,x)\),
    \[
     \mathrm{Herm}_{2m}(x)=2^mK^{(0)}_m(2x^2),\qquad \mathrm{Herm}_{2m+1}(x)=2^{m+1}xK^{(1)}_m(2x^2).
    \]
    Since \(\alpha\neq0\) and \(2\alpha^2=2e_k^2/(pp')\in\mathbb Q\), we get
    \(\mathrm{Herm}_M(\alpha)\neq0\).
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
Hence \([v^M]f\neq0\), and by items 1--2 the right-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:top-H} is a nonzero polynomial in \(v\).
\end{proof}

\section{Minimal-model examples for the sphere block}\label{sec:examples-sph}

\begin{proposition}[First double pole at \((2,3)\), \(c=0\), \(\lambda=0\)]
\label{prop:c0-example}
Let \((p,p')=(2,3)\), so that \(c_\star=0\) and \(b_\star^2=-\tfrac32\), and
let \(\lambda=h_{1,1}(b_\star)=0\).  The first two Jantzen layers have
\(\ell^+_1=1\), \(\ell^-_1=2\), \(\ell^-_2=5\) and \(\ell^+_2=7\).  Hence, for
indeterminate external weights, the first double pole of \(H_N(0,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) and
\(\mathcal B_N(0,\Delta,\Delta_i)\) at \(\Delta=0\) occurs at \(N=5\),
where Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph} gives
\begin{align*}
 \lim_{\Delta\to0}\Delta^2 H_5(0,\Delta,\Delta_i)
 &=\lim_{\Delta\to0}\Delta^2 \mathcal B_5(0,\Delta,\Delta_i)\\
 &=\frac{P_{1,1}(b_\star,\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,P_{1,4}(b_\star,\Delta_4,\Delta_3)\,
         P_{1,1}(b_\star,\Delta_1,\Delta_2)\,P_{1,4}(b_\star,\Delta_1,\Delta_2)}{\gamma^-_2}
\end{align*}
with the second Jantzen norm \(\gamma^-_2=-11200/3\) of \(w^-_2\).  The
\(H_5\) coefficient matches~\cite[\S3.2, (3.24)]{Ribault2019} after setting
\(b=i\beta\), writing Ribault's momenta as \(P^{\mathrm R}_j=i\Lambda_j/2\),
and exchanging \(\Delta_3\) and \(\Delta_4\); the exchange
multiplies \(P_{1,1}P_{1,4}\) by \((-1)^5=-1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[First double poles at the Ising charge \(c=\tfrac12\)]
\label{prop:ising-example}
Let \((p,p')=(3,4)\), so that \(c_\star=\tfrac12\) and

\[
 \begin{array}{ccl r}
  \lambda & N & \varphi(\Delta_a,\Delta_b) & \gamma \\ \hline
  1/2 & 7 & P_{2,1}P_{1,5} & -700700 \\
  1/16 & 10 & P_{1,2}P_{4,2} & -1121229484375/192 \\
  0 & 11 & P_{1,1}P_{2,5} & -2477917160468750/6561
 \end{array}
\]
In the third column all factors are evaluated at \((b_\star,\Delta_a,\Delta_b)\).
The second Jantzen norms are those in~Proposition~\ref{prop:ising-double-poles}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Propositions~\ref{prop:c0-example} and~\ref{prop:ising-example}]
In each case the level formulas give the listed \(N=\ell_2\).  At this level
Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph} has the single term \(M=0\), where
\(H_0=\mathcal B_0=1\); Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(ii) at \(j=1\) gives
\(\varphi\), and the norm is
\(\gamma=B^2(w^{\mathrm{small}}_2,w^{\mathrm{small}}_2)\).

For \((p,p')=(2,3)\) and \(\lambda=0\), the norm \(\gamma^-_2=-11200/3\) is a
property of the Verma module alone; the example after
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} computes it.  The Ising norms are those of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ising-double-poles}.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and outlook}\label{sec:scope}

The confluence theorems (Theorems~\ref{thm:general-confluence}
and~\ref{thm:confluence}) hold at every \(b_\star\neq0\).  The pole-order
bounds, the top coefficients and their sharpness
(Sections~\ref{sec:mm-layers}--\ref{sec:sharpness} and~\ref{sec:jantzen})
concern interior minimal-model weights.  The top coefficients are nonzero
over the rational function field of the external weights and can vanish at a
specialization (Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives},
Lemma~\ref{lem:pi-factorization}(iii)).

Several questions remain open.  At the boundary weights of Class \(R^+\),
whose Jantzen filtrations are given
by~\cite[Theorem~6.3(ii)--(iv) and (6.4)]{IoharaKoga2011}, and at the charges
\(c_\star=1,25\), the confluence formulas still give every coefficient, but
the pole order and the top coefficient are not determined here.  The
coefficients below the top one, \(A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\) with
\(k<m^\star_{\lambda,N}\), are computed here only as collision moments; a
formula for them in terms of the specialized module, like
Theorems~\ref{thm:top-layer} and~\ref{thm:top-layer-sph} for the top one, is
not known.  The confluence formulas expand the generic recursion in
\(b-b_\star\) and use generic-\(b\) jets.  Ribault asks for a recursion at
rational \(b^2\), with the torus one-point block as the simplest
case~\cite[\S4.3.3]{Ribault2026}; a recursion of that kind in data at
\(c=c_\star\) alone is not given here.  Irreducible minimal-model sphere
blocks are not treated here; for the torus, the irreducible traces are
treated in~\cite{PolzerTraces2026}.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(i)--(ii)}\label{app:h13}

We keep the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}; in
particular \(t=b^2\), \(\Delta=Y/t^2\), and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\) indexes the two
external weights \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b)\) and \(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b)\).  Partitions are
denoted \(Z,Z'\), and \(\delta i\), \(\delta j\) denote increments of the
exponents \(i\) and \(j\) in \([t^iY^j]\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(i)--(ii)]
Both parts rest on a count of modes.  After the rescaling by
\(t^{\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')}\) and \(\Delta=Y/t^2\), the Gram matrix at level \(M\)
has entries in \(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\) and is diagonal at \(t=0\), and,
with \(d=-2/t+\zeta\), the torus matrix has entries in
\(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y,\zeta]\) and does not depend on \(\zeta\) at
\(t=0\).  Part~(i) follows from this
structure and the Kac determinant formula.  For part~(ii) we
expand the inverse Gram matrix as a series in \(t\) around its diagonal part,
write each partition of \(M\) as \(1^a\) together with a partition with parts
at least \(2\), and show that only finitely many configurations contribute to
a given coefficient \([t^iY^j]\), each by a polynomial in \(M\) of degree at
most \(2i+2j\).

(i) At \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b)\) and \(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b)\) one has
\(\Lambda^2=(b\pm3b^{-1})^2\), so the factor \((k,l)=(1,3)\) in
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} vanishes for every \(v\geq2\) and
\({\mathcal R}_{u,v}(b,d_\nu)=0\) (\(v\geq2\)).  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} and induction on \(M\),
\(H_M(c(b),\Delta,d_\nu)\) has at most simple poles in \(\Delta\), all at
\(h_{u,1}(b)\) (\(1\leq u\leq M\)), so \(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in\mathbb Q(t)[Y]\)
has \(Y\)-degree at most \(M-1\).
Write \(c(b)=6/t+13+6t\), \(\Delta=Y/t^2\), and \(d=-2/t+\zeta\).  Since
\(6\mid(m^3-m)\) and \(c(b)/2\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1}]\), the entries of
\(G_M\) and \(\rho_M\) lie in \(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1},\Delta]\) and
\(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1},\Delta,\zeta]\).  For \(Z,Z'\vdash M\),
put \(\widetilde G_{Z,Z'}=t^{\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')}G_{M,Z,Z'}\)
and \(\widetilde\rho_{Z,Z'}=t^{\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')}\rho_{M,Z,Z'}\).
In \(G_{M,Z,Z'}\), every \(\Delta\) or \(c\) consumes at least two
modes, so \(2(d_\Delta+d_c)\leq\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')\); since
\(\Delta=Y/t^2\) and \(c=O(t^{-1})\), a \(t^0\)-term requires
\(2d_\Delta+d_c=\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')\), forcing \(d_c=0\) and direct
opposite-mode contractions only, hence \(Z=Z'\).  Thus
\(\widetilde G(t,Y)=D_0(Y)+tG_1(t,Y)\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y])\)
with \(D_0(Y)=\operatorname{diag}(K_Z Y^{\ell(Z)})\),
\(K_Z=\prod_{n\geq1}m_n!(2n)^{m_n}\) for \(Z=(1^{m_1}2^{m_2}\cdots)\).
In \(\rho_{M,Z,Z'}\), each \(d\) consumes at least one mode and each
\(\Delta\) or \(c\) at least two, so
\(d_d+2d_\Delta+d_c\leq\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')\), giving
\(\widetilde\rho\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y,\zeta])\)
with \(\widetilde\rho(0,Y;\zeta)\) independent of \(\zeta\).  Thus
\(F_M=\Tr(\widetilde G^{-1}\widetilde\rho)\) and
\(H_M=\sum_s\eta_sF_{M-s}\) lie in \(\mathbb Q(Y)[[t]]\) with \(t=0\) value
independent of \(\zeta\).  Since
\(t^{2M}\prod_{u=1}^M(2\Delta-2h_{u,1})=\prod_{u=1}^M(2Y+(u-1)t^2+\tfrac{u^2-1}{2}t^3)\),
\(P_M^{(\nu)}\) is regular at \(t=0\), \(P_M^{(2)}(0,Y)=P_M^{(1)}(0,Y)\), and
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(0,Y)=M!(2Y)^M\sum_{s=0}^M\eta_s\sum_{Z\vdash M-s}
(K_Z Y^{\ell(Z)})^{-1}\widetilde\rho^{(M-s)}(0,Y)_{Z,Z}\).
Only \((s,Z)=(0,(1^M))\) has \(M-\ell(Z)=0\), where
\(M!2^M/K_{(1^M)}=1\) and \(\widetilde\rho^{(M)}(0,0)_{(1^M),(1^M)}=(-2)^{2M}=4^M\)
(proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}), so \(P_M^{(\nu)}(0,0)=4^M\).
Also, \(\det G_M=K_M\prod_{uv\leq M}(\Delta-h_{u,v})^{p(M-uv)}\)
(\(K_M=\prod_{Z\vdash M}K_Z\)); Cramer's rule and monic division
by \(\Delta-h_{u,v}\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1}][\Delta]\) give
\(K_MP_M^{(\nu)}\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1},Y]\), hence
\(P_M^{(\nu)}\in\mathbb Q[t,Y]\).  To clear odd primes, adjoin \(q_0\) with
\(q_0^2=-(b+b^{-1})^2/2\) (\(c(b)=1-12q_0^2\)) and \(\alpha\) with
\(\Delta=\frac12\alpha(\alpha-2q_0)\).  On the Fock module
\({\mathcal H}(\alpha)\) (\([a_m,a_n]=m\delta_{m+n,0}\),
\(a_0|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle\)),
\(L_n=\frac12\sum_k{:}a_ka_{n-k}{:}-q_0(n+1)a_n\) gives a Virasoro action of
charge \(c(b)\) and weight \(\Delta\).  Under the contravariant pairing between
\({\mathcal H}(2q_0-\alpha)\) and \({\mathcal H}(\alpha)\)
(\(a_n^\dagger=-a_{-n}\) for \(n\neq0\), \(a_0^\dagger=2q_0-a_0\)),
\(L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}\) and
\(\langle2q_0-\alpha|(a_{-Z})^\dagger a_{-Z'}|\alpha\rangle
=\delta_{Z,Z'}(-1)^{\ell(Z)}\prod_n m_n!n^{m_n}\).  Hence
\(G_M=B_M(q_0,2q_0-\alpha)^TD_MB_M(q_0,\alpha)\), where
\(B_M(q_0,\alpha)\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][q_0,\alpha])\)
expresses \(L_{-Z}|\alpha\rangle\) in \(a_{-Z'}|\alpha\rangle\) and
\(D_M=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^{\ell(Z)}\prod_n m_n!n^{m_n})\).  Since
\(|\det D_M|=2^{-\sum_Z\ell(Z)}K_M\), taking determinants gives
\(\det B_M(q_0,2q_0-\alpha)\det B_M(q_0,\alpha)
=\pm2^{\sum\ell(Z)}\prod_{uv\leq M}(\Delta-h_{u,v})^{p(M-uv)}\), whose
leading \(\alpha\)-coefficient is a power of \(2\).  Fix an odd prime \(\varpi\),
put \(R=\mathbb Z_{(\varpi)}[b,b^{-1},\alpha]_{(\varpi)}\) (a DVR where
\(b+b^{-1}\) is a unit), and let \(\mathfrak p\) be a prime above \((\varpi)\) in
\({\mathcal S}=R[q_0]\) (finite \'etale over \(R\) as \(u=q_0/(b+b^{-1})\)
satisfies \(u^2=-\tfrac12\)).  Then \({\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}\) is a DVR
with uniformizer \(\varpi\) in which \(\det B_M(q_0,\alpha)\) and
\(\det B_M(q_0,2q_0-\alpha)\) are units.  By Lagrange's theorem for the
centralizer of cycle type \(Z\) in \(S_M\),
\(\prod_n m_n!n^{m_n}\mid M!\), so
\(M!D_M^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z)\) and
\(M!G_{M-s}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M-s)}({\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p})\)
for all \(0\leq s\leq M\) (as \((M-s)!\mid M!\)).  Hence
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in{\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}\cap\mathbb Q[t,Y]
=\mathbb Z_{(\varpi)}[t,Y]\) (since \(t=b^2\) and
\(Y=\frac12b^4\alpha(\alpha-2q_0)\) are algebraically independent over
\(\mathbb F_\varpi\) in \({\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p{\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}\));
as \(\varpi\) was arbitrary, \(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\).

(ii) Factor
\(\prod_{u=1}^M(2Y+(u-1)t^2+\tfrac{u^2-1}{2}t^3)
=(2Y)^M\prod_{u=1}^M(1+\tfrac{u-1}{2}\tfrac{t^2}{Y}+\tfrac{u^2-1}{4}\tfrac{t^3}{Y})\).
Each \([t^aY^{-b}]\) of the normalized product is a polynomial in \(M\) of
degree at most \(2a-2b\) for all \(M\geq0\) (since \(\sum_{u=1}^M(u-1)\) and
\(\sum_{u=1}^M(u^2-1)\) have degrees \(2\) and \(3\leq4\) and vanish at
\(M=0\)).

Put
\[
 R_M(t,Y):=(M!/2^M)Y^MF_M(c(b),Y/t^2,-2/t+\zeta)\qquad(\zeta\in\{-1,2\}).
\]
Then
\((M!/2^M)Y^MH_M=\sum_{s\geq0}\eta_s\,M(M-1)\cdots(M-s+1)(Y/2)^sR_{M-s}\),
where \(M(M-1)\cdots(M-s+1)\) has degree \(s\leq2s\) and vanishes for
\(0\leq M<s\), so it suffices to show that each \([t^iY^j]R_M\) (\(i\geq0\),
\(j\in\mathbb Z\)) is a polynomial in \(M\) of degree at most \(2i+2j\) for all
\(M\geq0\).

Expand
\[
 R_M=(M!/2^M)Y^M\sum_{r\geq0}(-t)^r\Tr\bigl(D_0^{-1}(G_1D_0^{-1})^r\widetilde\rho\bigr);
\]
only \(0\leq r\leq i\) contribute to \([t^iY^j]\).  Write each
\(Z_m\vdash M\) (\(0\leq m\leq r\)) as \(Z_m=(1^{a_m},\bar Z_m)\)
with all parts of \(\bar Z_m\) at least \(2\), \(w_m=|\bar Z_m|\geq2\ell(\bar Z_m)\),
and \(a_m=M-w_m\), so
\(D_0(Z_m)=a_m!\,2^{a_m}K_{\bar Z_m}Y^{a_m+\ell(\bar Z_m)}\).  In the
\(m\)-th factor
\((tG_1D_0^{-1})_{Z_m,Z_{m+1}}=\widetilde G_{Z_m,Z_{m+1}}/D_0(Z_{m+1})-\delta_{Z_m,Z_{m+1}}\)
(\(0\leq m<r\)), the subtracted diagonal (\(Z_m=Z_{m+1}\),
\(p_m=q_m=0\)) has degree \(0\); for the \(\widetilde G\)-term, applying
\(L_1^{a_m}X=\sum_{p_m}\binom{a_m}{p_m}(\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_m}X)L_1^{a_m-p_m}\)
and \(L_1^kL_{-1}^b|\Delta\rangle=(b!/q!)(\prod_{s=q}^{b-1}(2\Delta+s))L_{-1}^q|\Delta\rangle\)
(\(k=a_m-p_m\), \(q=b-k\)) yields a sum over \(p_m,q_m\geq0\) with
\(p_m-q_m=w_{m+1}-w_m\) of
\begin{multline*}
 \frac{\binom{a_m}{p_m}}{q_m!\,2^{q_m}K_{\bar Z_{m+1}}}
 \Bigl(\prod_{s=q_m}^{a_{m+1}-1}\bigl(1+\tfrac{s\,t^2}{2Y}\bigr)\Bigr)
 \frac{t^{p_m+q_m+\ell(\bar Z_m)+\ell(\bar Z_{m+1})}}{Y^{q_m+\ell(\bar Z_{m+1})}}\\
 \times\langle\Delta|\bigl(\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_m}(L_{\bar Z_m}L_{-\bar Z_{m+1}})\bigr)L_{-1}^{q_m}|\Delta\rangle.
\end{multline*}
Since every part of \(\bar Z_{m+1}\) is at least \(2\) and \([L_1,L_n]\)
introduces no central charge, each term of
\(\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_m}(L_{\bar Z_m}L_{-\bar Z_{m+1}})L_{-1}^{q_m}\) has at
most \(\ell(\bar Z_m)+\lfloor p_m/2\rfloor\) non-negative modes; moving the
rightmost non-negative mode to the right shows by induction that every
monomial in the bracket satisfies
\(d_\Delta^{(m)}+d_c^{(m)}\leq\ell(\bar Z_m)+p_m/2\).  In
\(\widetilde\rho_{Z_r,Z_0}\), commute \(L_1^{a_r}\) past
\(L_{\bar Z_r}\) (\(p_1\) times) and \(L_{-1}^{a_0}\) past \(L_{-\bar Z_0}\)
(\(q_1\) times), and apply the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) identities below, which
are proved by induction on the exponent; the contravariant adjoint of the
first one moves \(L_{-1}^{b'-k_r}\) past \(V_d(1)\):
\begin{align*}
 \langle v|L_1^{a'}V_d(1)|W\rangle
 &=\sum_{m_L=0}^{a'}\tbinom{a'}{m_L}\!\Bigl(\prod_{u=0}^{m_L-1}(d+h_v-h_W+a'-1-u)\Bigr)
   \langle v|V_d(1)L_1^{a'-m_L}|W\rangle,\\
 L_1^{\tilde r}L_{-1}^{b'}
 &=\sum_{k_r=0}^{\min(\tilde r,b')}\!\tbinom{\tilde r}{k_r}\tfrac{b'!}{(b'-k_r)!}\,
   L_{-1}^{b'-k_r}\!\Bigl(\prod_{u=0}^{k_r-1}(2L_0+b'+\tilde r-k_r-1-u)\Bigr)L_1^{\tilde r-k_r}.
\end{align*}
This yields a sum over \(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2,k_r\geq0\).  Put
\(p_r^*=p_1+p_2\), \(q_r^*=q_1+q_2\), \(s_L=a_r-k_r-p_r^*\) and
\(s_R=a_0-k_r-q_r^*\).  Each summand is the product of
\[
 \frac{M!}{s_R!}\frac{a_r!}{s_L!}\frac{1}{k_r!\,p_1!\,p_2!\,q_1!\,q_2!},\quad
 \prod_{u=0}^{k_r-1}(2Y+(M+c_u)t^2),\quad
 (-2)^{s_L+s_R}\prod_{u=0}^{s_L-1}(1+c'_ut)\prod_{u=0}^{s_R-1}(1+c''_ut)
\]
and the local bracket
\(\langle\Delta|M_{p_1,q_2}V_d(1)N_{p_2,q_1}|\Delta\rangle\), where
\[
 M_{p_1,q_2}=(-\operatorname{ad}(L_{-1}))^{q_2}\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_1}(L_{\bar Z_r}),\qquad
 N_{p_2,q_1}=\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_2}(-\operatorname{ad}(L_{-1}))^{q_1}(L_{-\bar Z_0}).
\]
The coefficients \(c'_u,c''_u\) are affine-linear in \(M\) and \(u\), and
\((-1)^{s_L+s_R}\) and \(2^{s_L+s_R}/(2^M2^{a_0})\) are independent of \(M\).
The bracket has \(\ell(\bar Z_r)+\ell(\bar Z_0)\) modes and hence satisfies
\(d_d^{(r)}+d_\Delta^{(r)}+d_c^{(r)}\leq\ell(\bar Z_r)+\ell(\bar Z_0)\).
Expanding \(k\) correction factors, each a factor \(t^2/Y\), a factor \(t\)
from the \(s_L\) or \(s_R\) product, or a factor \((M+c_u)t^2\) from the
\(k_r\) product, increases \(i+j\) by \(k\) and \(2i+2j\) by \(2k\).  The
result is an elementary symmetric polynomial in \(M\), of degree at most
\(2k\) by Faulhaber's formula and Newton's identities.

On the remaining base exponents, for each \(0\leq m<r\) one has
\(\delta i_m+\delta j_m\geq p_m+\ell(\bar Z_m)-(d_\Delta^{(m)}+d_c^{(m)})\geq p_m/2\),
whence \(2\delta i_m+2\delta j_m-p_m\geq0\); on the \(\widetilde\rho\) factor,
\(w_0\geq2\ell(\bar Z_0)\) and
\(d_d^{(r)}+d_\Delta^{(r)}+d_c^{(r)}\leq\ell(\bar Z_r)+\ell(\bar Z_0)\) give
\(\delta i_r+\delta j_r\geq w_0/2+k_r+p_r^*+q_r^*\) and
\(2\delta i_r+2\delta j_r-(w_0+p_r^*+q_r^*+2k_r)
\geq w_0+2\ell(\bar Z_r)+p_r^*+q_r^*-2(d_d^{(r)}+d_\Delta^{(r)}+d_c^{(r)})\geq0\).
Summing yields
\(i+j\geq w_0/2+k_r+p_r^*+q_r^*+\frac12\sum_{m=0}^{r-1}p_m\) and
\(\deg_M\leq2i+2j\).  Since \(0\leq r\leq i\) and \(w_{m+1}-w_m=p_m-q_m\),
\(w_0\leq2(i+j)\), \(w_m\leq4(i+j)\), \(p_m\leq2(i+j)\),
\(q_m\leq6(i+j)\) and \(k_r,p_r^*,q_r^*\leq i+j\).  Hence only finitely many
tuples contribute to \((i,j)\).  Each such tuple yields a polynomial in \(M\)
that vanishes on
\[
 [0,w_0+q_r^*+k_r-1]\cup[w_r,w_r+p_r^*+k_r-1]\cup\bigcup_{m=0}^{r-1}[w_m,w_m+p_m-1].
\]
Since \(w_m+p_m=w_{m+1}+q_m\geq w_{m+1}\), induction on \(m\) shows that this
union contains every level
\(0\leq M<\max(w_m+p_m,w_r+p_r^*+k_r,w_0+q_r^*+k_r)\) at which the tuple is
absent.  So the finite sum is a single polynomial in \(M\) for all
\(M\geq0\).
\end{proof}

\section*{Code availability}

The repository \href{https://github.com/skypher/virasoro}%
{github.com/skypher/virasoro} contains the source and audit scripts.  The
proofs use no computer calculation.  The ancillary scripts for Part~I
(thirteen audit scripts and two shared libraries) reproduce finite
computations from the paper in exact rational arithmetic;
\path{AUDIT_MANIFEST.md} lists commands, dependencies and hashes.  The
comparison with Stocco's expressions in the introduction is
\path{tools/audit_stocco_comparison.py}.  The Ising norms \(\gamma\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ising-double-poles} at \(N=7\) and \(N=10\) are compared
with direct PBW evaluations; the double-pole coefficient is reconstructed by
Shapovalov inversion at \(N=7\), and at \(N=10\) in the deep suite
(\texttt{make audit-deep}), but not at \(N=11\).  The repository script
\path{tools/check_c0_level5_example.py}, which is not part of the archive,
checks the examples after Theorems~\ref{thm:general-confluence}
and~\ref{thm:confluence}: the two poles in \(t\), their cancellation and both
collision moments.  With \texttt{--torus-direct} it compares both torus
moments with Shapovalov inversion at \(N=5\), and it compares both sphere
moments with \texttt{exact\_\allowbreak sphere\_\allowbreak block.py} at
three choices of rational external weights.  The repository script
\path{tools/check_leeyang_level4_example.py} recomputes the Lee--Yang example in
Section~\ref{sec:sharpness} by Shapovalov inversion up to level four, and the
C++ program \path{tools/check_d0_coefficient.cpp} evaluates the coefficient of
\(d^{k+1}\) at \(d=0\) described there on all strict two-vector levels with
\(p,p'\leq13\) and \(k\leq3\) and finds no zero.  Neither is part of the
archive.

The scripts in \texttt{sphere/tools/} check the sphere confluence of Part~II
in exact rational and symbolic arithmetic, through level seven for
\((2,3),(2,5),(3,4)\) and level three at \(b_\star^2=\sqrt2\).
\texttt{exact\_\allowbreak sphere\_\allowbreak block.py} reconstructs selected
sphere blocks through level seven and checks the \(c=0\) level-five
coefficient~\cite[(3.24)]{Ribault2019} and the Ising coefficient with
\(\gamma=-700700\).
\texttt{audit\_\allowbreak sphere\_\allowbreak top\_\allowbreak layer.py}
extracts that norm and checks selected sharpness witnesses through shifted
degree four.  The torus audit checks the Ising norms at levels ten and eleven
from the layer formula.
