\begin{abstract}
Fix a nonzero complex Coulomb-gas parameter \(b_\star\).  We prove an
exact, finite confluence formula for every coefficient of the
eta-reduced torus one-point Virasoro block at
\(c_\star=1+6(b_\star+b_\star^{-1})^2\).  Generic Kac-recursion terms
are grouped by their common limiting internal weight; such collisions,
and poles of the generic residues, occur only when \(b_\star^2\) is
rational.  Each coefficient of the fixed-central-charge principal part
is a collision moment: the constant term of a finite sum of Laurent
germs in \(t=b-b_\star\), computed within an a priori finite jet budget.
Uniqueness of partial fractions identifies the construction
coefficientwise in \(q\) with the inverse-Shapovalov definition of the
meromorphic Verma block.  The formal germ is an exact algebraic device,
not a numerical regularization.  At minimal-model charges the local
Smith exponents of the Shapovalov matrix in the internal weight are the
dimensions of the Jantzen layers of the Feigin--Fuchs embedding diagram,
which bounds the pole order of every block coefficient.  We show that the
top Jantzen layer determines the leading Laurent coefficient: it is a sum
of shifted block coefficients weighted by the torus norms of the singular
vectors divided by their Jantzen norms.  Through a one-screening closed form at
the degenerate external weight \(d=h_{1,3}(b)\), we prove that the bound
is attained at every single-vector level of every layer, at the start of every
two-vector range, and at every level of every minimal model with \(p=3\) or
\(p'=3\), including the Ising model.  At the Ising charge one two-branch
closure formula gives the complete principal parts at the first double poles,
at levels seven and ten.  Ancillary scripts replay the finite computations in
exact rational arithmetic.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace Virasoro algebra; conformal blocks; torus
one-point block; Zamolodchikov recursion; minimal models; Jantzen
filtration; Ising model.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Residue recursions for Virasoro blocks originate with Zamolodchikov's
recursive representations of the sphere four-point block
\cite{Zamolodchikov1984,Zamolodchikov1987}; see
\cite{ChoCollierYin2017} for a modern general treatment.  For generic
central charge, the eta-reduced torus one-point block obeys the residue
recursion conjectured by Poghossian \cite{Poghossian2009} and derived in
\cite[Section~3, equations~(7)--(9)]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}; its
light limit \(b\to0\) was studied recently in
\cite{PoghosyanPoghosyan2026}.  At rational values of \(b^2\), distinct
Kac labels have the same degenerate weight, and individual summands of the
generic recursion develop poles in the deformation parameter.  Javerzat,
Santachiara, and Foda analyzed the cancellation of these non-physical poles
in examples and in the identity-insertion character problem
\cite[Sections~4--6, especially equations~(60)--(61)]%
{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.  Stocco constructed
central-charge-pole-free torus expressions through fourth order and
proposed an all-order Laurent-expansion procedure, whose cancellation and
algebraic form were left conjectural
\cite[Sections~II.2--II.3 and Appendix~A]{Stocco2022}; his numerators agree
with \(H_2,H_3\) identically and with \(H_4\) at sample values.  Ribault identifies
a recursion at rational \(b^2\) as an open problem, with torus one-point
blocks as its simplest incarnation \cite[Section~4.3.3]{Ribault2026};
higher-order internal-weight poles and rational limits of sphere
four-point blocks were analyzed in \cite[Section~3]{Ribault2019}.

The first main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}, is an
all-order coefficientwise construction at every fixed nonzero
\(b_\star\), for the meromorphic Verma-module block.  At every level we
partition the generic Kac summands by their common limiting weight at
\(b=b_\star\); for every \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 &=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},\\
 A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 &=\operatorname{CT}_t
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 R_a(t)\,S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\,
 \bigl(h_a(t)-\lambda\bigr)^{k-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(H_N\) is the \(N\)-th eta-reduced block coefficient,
\({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is the finite class of labels
\(a=(r,s)\) with \(rs\leq N\) that collide at \(\lambda\),
\(t=b-b_\star\) is the exact Coulomb-gas germ, \(R_a\) is the generic
residue, \(S_{a,M}\) is the shifted Laurent block of
Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}, \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) extracts the
constant term, and \(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is an a priori pole
bound.  Every ingredient is a finite jet of an explicit rational function
of \(b\), and the moments are exactly the partial-fraction coefficients of
the inverse-Shapovalov block.  Collisions occur only when \(b_\star^2\) is
rational.  The formula is exact at fixed \(c_\star\), with no numerical
\(\varepsilon\)-limit, but it combines generic summands through the
formal germ \(b=b_\star+t\), so it is not an intrinsic recursion in the
specialized representation category.

The second main result concerns the pole orders, which are governed by the
specialized Verma module.  At a minimal-model charge and an interior Kac
weight \(\lambda\), the local Smith exponents of the Shapovalov matrix are
the dimensions of the Jantzen layers of the Feigin--Fuchs embedding diagram
(Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}), so the pole order of \(H_N\) at
\(\lambda\) is at most the number \(j_N(\lambda)\) of layers that have
reached level \(N\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} computes the coefficient
at this bound.  If \(w^\pm_k\) are the singular vectors of the \(k\)-th
layer, at levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), then for \(N\) from the first level of the
\(k\)-th layer up to the first level of the next one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-top}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^kH_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}
 \frac{\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}\bigl(c_\star,\lambda+\ell^\pm_k,d\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(\gamma^\pm_k\neq0\) is the \(k\)-th Jantzen norm of \(w^\pm_k\).
This is a fixed-\(c\) analogue of Zamolodchikov's residue formula for the
deepest pole, and every quantity in it is intrinsic to the specialized
module.  The torus norm \(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle\) of a singular vector
of level \(\ell\) has exact degree \(2\ell\) in \(d\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}).  At the degenerate external weight
\(d=h_{1,3}(b)\) the generic recursion collapses to labels \((u,1)\) and
admits an algebraic one-screening closed form (Lemma~\ref{lem:one-screening});
clearing denominators gives a polynomial with constant term \(4^M\), so
\(\ell\)-adic reduction at any odd prime dividing \(pp'\) establishes
attainment of \(j_N(\lambda)\) at every single-vector level of every layer
(Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp}).  Combined with the \(k\)-th Taylor
coefficients of \(\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle/\gamma^\pm_k\) at
\(d=0\) and \(d=1\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients}), this also
yields order, \(\ell\)-adic valuation, and degree criteria on two-vector
levels (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}), closing every level of
every minimal model with \(p=3\) or \(p'=3\) (including the Ising model).

At the Ising charge the first double poles occur at levels \(7\), \(10\),
and \(11\), at the energy, spin, and identity weights.  For the first two,
one two-branch closure formula (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-branch}) gives
the complete principal part, and comparison with \eqref{eq:intro-top}
determines the second Jantzen norms \(\gamma_7=-700700\) and
\(\gamma_{10}=-1121229484375/192\), which direct Gram-matrix computations
confirm.

Sections~\ref{sec:blocks}--\ref{sec:partial-fractions} state the
confluence theorem, Section~\ref{sec:smith} proves the top-layer theorem,
Sections~\ref{sec:generic-data}--\ref{sec:all-order} prove the generic
identities and the confluence theorem, and Section~\ref{sec:ising} treats the
Ising diamonds.  All general theorems are proved without computer calculation;
the explicit Ising evaluations are computer-assisted finite calculations in
exact rational arithmetic, all replayed by the ancillary scripts (see Code
availability).

\section{Blocks and the reduced series}\label{sec:blocks}

\begin{definition}[Virasoro Verma module]
The Virasoro algebra has generators \(L_n\) (\(n\in\mathbb Z\)) and central
element \(c\), with
\([L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}\).
The Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) is generated by \(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) with
\(L_0\lvert\Delta\rangle=\Delta\lvert\Delta\rangle\),
\(L_n\lvert\Delta\rangle=0\) (\(n>0\)), and central charge \(c\).
For a partition \(Y=(y_1\geq\cdots\geq y_\ell)\), set
\(L_{-Y}=L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_\ell}\), \(|Y|=\sum_i y_i\), and
\(\ell(Y)=\ell\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shapovalov and torus matrices]\label{def:matrices}
At level \(N\), for partitions \(|Y|=|Y'|=N\) and \(L_Y=L_{y_\ell}\cdots L_{y_1}\), let
\[
 G_N(c,\Delta)_{Y,Y'}=\langle\Delta|L_YL_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle,
 \qquad
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}=\langle\Delta|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle,
\]
where \(V_d(z)\) is primary with \(\langle\Delta|V_d(z)|\Delta\rangle=z^{-d}\).
Whenever \(G_N\) is invertible, put
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=\sum_{|Y|=|Y'|=N}(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1})_{Y,Y'}\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced block]\label{def:eta-reduced}
Write \(\mathcal F_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)=q^{\Delta-c/24}\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N\)
and define
\(H_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)=\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N=\sum_{N\geq0}H_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N\).
With \(\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)=\sum_{j\geq0}\eta_jq^j\) (\(\eta_0=1\),
\(\eta_1=\eta_2=-1\), \(\eta_3=\eta_4=0\)), one has \(H_N=\sum_{j=0}^N\eta_jF_{N-j}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial matrix entries]\label{lem:polynomial-entries}
For fixed \(N\), every entry of \(G_N(c,\Delta)\) belongs to
\(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta]\), and every entry of
\(\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,d]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Repeated use of the Virasoro commutator moves every positive mode in
\(L_Y\) to the right.  Positive modes annihilate
\(\lvert\Delta\rangle\), while each \(L_0\) and the central element act by
\(\Delta\) and \(c\), respectively.  This gives the first assertion.

For the second assertion use, in addition,
\([L_n,V_d(z)]=z^n(z\partial_z+(n+1)d)V_d(z)\).  Move positive modes through \(V_d(z)\), then move the remaining negative
modes through \(V_d(z)\) toward the bra.  The process terminates because
each commutation removes one mode from one side of the insertion.
The remaining derivatives act on \(z^{-d}\); after setting \(z=1\), their
values are polynomials in \(d\).  The other commutators contribute only
polynomials in \(c\) and \(\Delta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terminology and notation]\label{rem:notation}
The \(L_0\)-offset \(N\) of a graded component is called its
\emph{level}.  For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), we write
\(\ell_a=rs\) for the level of its singular vector.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and
Cramer's rule, each \(F_N\) and \(H_N\) is a rational function of
\((c,\Delta)\) and a polynomial in \(d\); note \(H_0=1\).  For a rational
function \(f\) of \(\Delta\) and a point \(\lambda\), we write
\(\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) for the principal part of \(f\) at \(\lambda\),
that is, the sum of the negative-power terms of its Laurent expansion,
and \(\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) for the coefficient of
\((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\).
Unless \(d\) is explicitly specialized, it is an indeterminate and pole
orders in \(\Delta\) are understood over \(\mathbb Q(c,d)\), or over
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\) after a fixed algebraic specialization.
\end{remark}

\section{Algebraic specializations and Ising resonance classes}
\label{sec:resonance}

\begin{definition}[Kac weights and fixed algebraic specializations]
\label{def:fixed-specialization}
For \(b\in\mathbb C^\times\), set
\(c(b)=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2\) and
\(h_{r,s}(b)=\frac14((b+b^{-1})^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2)\),
which depend on \(b\) only through \(b^2\).  Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\),
put \(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)=\{h_{r,s}(b_\star):r,s>0,\ rs\leq N\}\), and for
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) define the finite collision class
\[
 {\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2:
      rs\leq N,\ h_{r,s}(b_\star)=\lambda\}.
\]
For the Ising specialization, put \(b_0=2i/\sqrt3\) (\(c(b_0)=\tfrac12\)),
\(\Delta_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b_0)=((4r-3s)^2-1)/48\), and
\(\lambda_n=(n^2-1)/48\) (\(n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\)).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal-model collision classes]
\label{prop:minimal-collisions}
Let \(p,p'\) be coprime positive integers and choose
\(b_\star^2=-p'/p\).  Then
\[
 c_\star=1-\frac{6(p'-p)^2}{pp'},\qquad
 h_{r,s}(b_\star)
 =\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}.
\]
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\), their specialized weights
are equal if and only if, for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 (r',s')=(r+p\ell,s+p'\ell)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (r',s')=(p\ell-r,p'\ell-s).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formulas follow by substituting \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) in
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.  Equality of the two weights is
equivalent to \(p'r'-ps'=\pm(p'r-ps)\).  For the plus sign, coprimality gives
\((r'-r,s'-s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\); for the minus sign it gives
\((r'+r,s'+s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\).  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ising collision classes]\label{cor:ising-classes}
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\),
\(\Delta_{r,s}=\Delta_{r',s'}\) if and only if
\((r',s')=(r+3\ell,s+4\ell)\) or \((3\ell-r,4\ell-s)\) for some
\(\ell\in\mathbb Z\).  Moreover \(\Delta_{r,s}=\lambda_{|4r-3s|}\), the set of distinct
positive-label degenerate weights at \(c=1/2\) is
\(\{\lambda_n:n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\}\), every collision class contains
infinitely many positive pairs, and for each fixed level \(N\) only
finitely many classes contain a pair with \(rs\leq N\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since \(b_0^2=-4/3\), the first assertion is
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions} with \((p,p')=(3,4)\), and
\(\Delta_{r,s}=\lambda_{|4r-3s|}\) is its weight formula.  For \(n\geq0\)
and large \(r\equiv n\pmod3\), \(s=(4r-n)/3\) gives \(4r-3s=n\), and
\((r+3\ell,s+4\ell)\), \(\ell\geq0\), stay in the class; \(rs\leq N\) forces
\(r,s\leq N\).
\end{proof}

\section{Coefficientwise partial fractions}\label{sec:partial-fractions}

\begin{lemma}[Large internal weight]\label{lem:large-weight}
For fixed \(c,d,N\), \(F_N(c,\Delta,d)\to p(N)\), the number of partitions
of \(N\), and \(H_N(c,\Delta,d)\to\delta_{N,0}\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a partition \(Y\) put \(a_Y=\ell(Y)\), and rescale
\(L_{-Y}\lvert\Delta\rangle\) by \(\Delta^{-a_Y/2}\).  In a fully commuted
matrix element of \(G_N\), every factor \(\Delta\) arises from a
contraction of a positive mode of \(L_Y\) with a negative mode of
\(L_{-Y'}\) of the same index, through the \(2jL_0\) term of
\([L_j,L_{-j}]\).  Each contraction consumes one mode on each side, so
\(\deg_\Delta G_N(Y,Y')\leq\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\); for \(a_Y=a_{Y'}\) equality
forces \(Y=Y'\), and the top term of \(G_N(Y,Y)\) is
\(\prod_{j}m_j(Y)!\,(2j)^{m_j(Y)}\Delta^{a_Y}\), where \(m_j(Y)\) is the
multiplicity of \(j\) in \(Y\).  With one \(V_d\) insertion, a term using
no Ward commutator reproduces the corresponding Shapovalov term, because
\(\langle\Delta|V_d(1)|\Delta\rangle=1\).  A term using a Ward commutator
has fewer than \(\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\) contractions: attaining the minimum
would match every mode of the shorter side with an equal-index mode of the
other side, and these indices already sum to \(N\), leaving no positive
mode for a Ward commutator.  So after rescaling, \(G_N\) and \(\rho_N\)
converge to the same invertible diagonal matrix \(D_N\), and
\(F_N\to\operatorname{tr}(D_N^{-1}D_N)=p(N)\).  The identity
\((\sum_j\eta_jq^j)(\sum_kp(k)q^k)=1\) gives the second limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite pole expansion at a fixed specialization]
\label{thm:general-partial-fractions}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\).  Then \(H_0=1\),
and for every \(N\geq1\) there are unique coefficients
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\)
(\(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\), \(k\geq1\)), only finitely many nonzero,
such that
\[
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}\ \sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\]
The largest \(k\) with \(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\neq0\) (or \(0\)) is the
pole multiplicity \(m^\star_{\lambda,N}\) of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda\).
Consequently
\(H_\Delta^{(c_\star)}(d\mid q)=1+\sum_{\lambda,k}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)
(\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\) with
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)=\sum_{N\geq1}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)q^N\),
locally finite coefficientwise in \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries},
\(F_N=\operatorname{tr}(G_N^{-1}\rho_N^{\mathsf T})\) is rational in
\(\Delta\), and its poles are contained in the zero locus of
\(\det G_N\).  The Kac determinant formula
\cite{Kac1979,FeiginFuchs1982}, in the form of
\cite[Theorem~4.2 and equations (4.16)--(4.21)]{IoharaKoga2011}, gives
\(\det G_N(c,\Delta)=C_N\prod_{rs\leq N}(\Delta-\Delta_{r,s}(c))^{p(N-rs)}\)
with \(C_N\neq0\) independent of \(\Delta\).  At \(c=c_\star\), the distinct zeros belong to the finite set
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).  Since \(H_N\) is a finite integer linear
combination of \(F_0,\ldots,F_N\), it has the same pole containment.
Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} shows that \(H_N\) tends to zero at infinity
for \(N\geq1\), so its partial-fraction expansion has no polynomial part
and is unique.

The field \(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)\) contains \(c_\star\) and every pole
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\).  The matrix entries belong to
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[\Delta,d]\), and \(\det G_N\) does not involve
\(d\).  Laurent expansion at each pole therefore gives
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\).
Coefficientwise local finiteness follows because \(rs\leq N\) leaves
only finitely many labels.
\end{proof}

The conceptual result can now be stated independently of the Ising
calculations.  Its collision moments and finite precision budget are defined
in Section~\ref{sec:all-order}, after the generic residue data; the proof is
given there as well.

\begin{theorem}[Finite algebraic confluence at fixed \(b_\star\)]
\label{thm:general-confluence}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\).  For every \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},
 \qquad
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d],
\end{equation}
and \(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\).
The sums and constant-term calculations are finite, the level-decreasing
recursion of Definitions~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}--\ref{def:collision-moments}
terminates, and its backward precision rule supplies a finite truncation order
for every intermediate Laurent germ.  The resulting locally finite series
agrees coefficientwise in \(q\) with the inverse-Shapovalov meromorphic
Verma-module block.  If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), every collision class is
a singleton, every \(\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) equals \(1\), and
\eqref{eq:general-pole-free-recursion} is
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} evaluated at \(b=b_\star\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Ising specialization]\label{cor:ising-confluence}
At \(b_\star=b_0\) (\(c_\star=1/2\)), write \({\mathcal C}_{n,N}\),
\(\widehat M_{n,N}\), \(m_{n,N}\), \(A_{n,k,N}\), and \(\widetilde A_{n,k,N}\)
for the objects of Theorems~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}
and~\ref{thm:general-confluence} at \(\lambda=\lambda_n\), where
\({\mathcal C}_{n,N}=\{(r,s):|4r-3s|=n,\ rs\leq N\}\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:ising-classes}).  Then for every \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{n\geq0\\{\mathcal C}_{n,N}\neq\varnothing}}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M_{n,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A_{n,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k},
 \qquad
 \widetilde A_{n,k,N}=A_{n,k,N}\in\mathbb Q[d],
\end{equation}
and \(m_{n,N}\leq\widehat M_{n,N}\) is the exact pole multiplicity at \(\lambda_n\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} at \(b_\star=b_0\), where \(\mathbb Q(b_0^2)=\mathbb Q\).
\end{proof}

\section{Smith exponents, Jantzen layers, and the top pole}\label{sec:smith}

\begin{lemma}[Pole order from local invariant factors]\label{lem:smith}
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[x]]\), and let \(G(x)\in M_m(R)\) have nonzero determinant
and Smith exponents \(0\leq e_1\leq\cdots\leq e_m\).  Then
\(\ord_x\det G=\sum_{i=1}^m e_i\), \(\dim\ker G(0)=\#\{i:e_i>0\}\), and
\(G(x)^{-1}\) has pole order \(e_m\).  In particular, if
\(\ord_x\det G=\dim\ker G(0)\), every positive Smith exponent equals \(1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(R\) is a discrete valuation ring, there are
\(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R)\) with
\(UGV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_m})\).  Taking determinants,
reducing modulo \(x\), and inverting give the three assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Jantzen layers in the internal weight]
\label{def:jantzen-layers}
Fix \((c,h)\) and identify \(M(c,h+x)_N\) with \(\mathbb C^{p(N)}\) in the PBW
basis.  For \(i\geq0\), let \(J^i_N(c,h)\) be the set of \(v\in M(c,h)_N\)
admitting a polynomial family \(v(x)\) with \(v(0)=v\) and
\(G_N(c,h+x)\,v(x)\in x^i\,\mathbb C[x]^{p(N)}\), and put
\(J^i(c,h)=\bigoplus_{N\geq0}J^i_N(c,h)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Smith exponents are Jantzen dimensions]
\label{lem:jantzen-smith}
Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), with local Smith exponents
\(e_1,\ldots,e_{p(N)}\) at \(x=0\).  Then
\(\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\#\{j:e_j\geq i\}\) (\(i\geq1\)) and
\(\sum_{i\geq1}\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\ord_x\det G_N(c,h+x)\).
Each \(J^i(c,h)\) is a submodule of \(M(c,h)\), and \(J^1(c,h)\) is the
radical of the Shapovalov form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_{p(N)}(\mathbb C[[x]])\) with
\(UG_NV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_{p(N)}})\).  Writing
\(v(x)=V(x)w(x)\), the condition \(G_Nv\in x^i\) becomes
\(w_j\in x^{\max(0,i-e_j)}\mathbb C[[x]]\) for every \(j\); truncation
modulo \(x^i\) shows that power series and polynomial families define the
same space.  Hence \(J^i_N=V(0)\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_j:e_j\geq i\}\),
where \(\epsilon_j\) are the standard basis vectors, which proves the
dimension formula.  Summing over \(i\) and using Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}
gives the second identity.  If \(v(x)\) is a family for \(v\in J^i_N\)
and \(n\in\mathbb Z\), then \(L_nv(x)\), computed in \(M(c,h+x)\), is a
polynomial family with value \(L_nv\) at \(x=0\), and contravariance gives
\(\langle y,L_nv(x)\rangle_{h+x}=\langle L_{-n}y,v(x)\rangle_{h+x}\in
x^i\mathbb C[x]\) for every PBW vector \(y\).  Thus \(J^i(c,h)\) is a
submodule.  For \(i=1\) the defining condition is \(G_N(c,h)v=0\).
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Jantzen forms and the top coefficient]
\label{lem:top-coefficient}
Fix \((c,h)\).  For \(i\geq1\) and \(u,v\in J^i_N(c,h)\), choose families
\(u(x),v(x)\) as in Definition~\ref{def:jantzen-layers} and put
\(B^i(u,v)=[x^i]\,v(x)^{\mathsf T}G_N(c,h+x)\,u(x)\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(B^i\) is a well-defined symmetric form, and
\(B^i(L_{-n}u,v)=B^i(u,L_nv)\) for \(n>0\), \(u\in J^i_{N-n}(c,h)\), and
\(v\in J^i_N(c,h)\).
\item[(ii)] Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), and let \(k\geq1\) be
the largest local Smith exponent at \(x=0\).  Then \(B^k\) is
nondegenerate on \(J^k_N(c,h)\), and
\([x^{-k}]\,G_N(c,h+x)^{-1}=\iota\,(B^k)^{-1}\iota^*\),
where \(\iota\colon J^k_N(c,h)\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^{p(N)}\) is the
inclusion.
\item[(iii)] Write \(G_N(c,h+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3)\).  Then
\(B^1(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}G_1u\), and
\(B^2(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}(G_2u+G_1u_1)\) for every \(u_1\) with
\(G_0u_1=-G_1u\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Two families for \(u\) differ by \(xf(x)\) with \(f\) polynomial, and
\(v(x)^{\mathsf T}G_N\,xf(x)=x\,(G_Nv(x))^{\mathsf T}f(x)\in
x^{i+1}\mathbb C[x]\), because \(G_N\) is symmetric.  Hence \(B^i\) does not
depend on the family of \(u\), by symmetry neither on that of \(v\), and it
is symmetric.  The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} shows that
\(L_{-n}u(x)\) and \(L_nv(x)\), computed in \(M(c,h+x)\), are families for
\(L_{-n}u\) and \(L_nv\).  Contravariance of the Shapovalov form of
\(M(c,h+x)\) and extraction of the coefficient of \(x^i\) give the last
identity.

(ii) With \(UG_NV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_{p(N)}})\) as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}, \(G_N^{-1}=V\operatorname{diag}(x^{-e_j})U\), so
\(S=[x^{-k}]G_N^{-1}=V(0)EU(0)\), where \(E\) is the diagonal projection
onto the indices with \(e_j=k\).  By the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}, the column space of \(S\) is
\(V(0)\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_j:e_j=k\}=J^k_N(c,h)\).  Since \(S\) is
symmetric, \(S=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}u_au_b^{\mathsf T}\) for a basis
\(u_1,\ldots,u_m\) of \(J^k_N(c,h)\) and a symmetric matrix \(C\).  For
\(u\in J^k_N(c,h)\) with family \(u(x)\), write
\(G_N(c,h+x)u(x)=x^kg(x)\).  Since \(x^kG_N^{-1}=S+O(x)\), evaluation at
\(x=0\) gives \(u=Sg(0)\), while \(B^k(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}g(0)\) for
\(v\in J^k_N(c,h)\).  For \(u=u_c\) this reads
\(u_c=\sum_au_a\sum_bC_{ab}B^k(u_c,u_b)\), so \(C\) is inverse to the Gram
matrix of \(B^k\).

(iii) A vector \(u\in J^1_N\) is its own family.  If \(u,v\in J^2_N\) and
\(G_0u_1=-G_1u\), \(G_0v_1=-G_1v\), then \(u+xu_1\) and \(v+xv_1\) are
families, and the coefficient of \(x^2\) in
\((v+xv_1)^{\mathsf T}G_N(u+xu_1)\) is
\(v^{\mathsf T}(G_2u+G_1u_1)+v_1^{\mathsf T}(G_1u+G_0u_1)\), whose last
term vanishes.
\end{proof}

The case \(k=1\) of (ii) is the residue formula behind the generic
recursion of Section~\ref{sec:generic-data}, and \(B^2\) is the second
crossing form evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:ising}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}, the multiplicity of a Kac factor in \(\det G_N\) is
the sum of the positive Smith exponents, whereas the pole order of
\(G_N^{-1}\) is their maximum.

\begin{lemma}[Torus form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-factorization}
Let \(\chi\in M(c,h)\) be a singular vector of level \(\ell\), normalized
by a fixed PBW coefficient, and put \(P_\chi(d)=\langle\chi|V_d(1)|\chi\rangle\).
Then for all partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\(\langle\chi|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\chi\rangle=P_\chi(d)\,\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map \(L_{-Y}|h+\ell\rangle\mapsto L_{-Y}\chi\) is a homomorphism
\(M(c,h+\ell)\to M(c,h)\), because \(\chi\) is singular of weight
\(h+\ell\).  The torus form pulled back along it in both arguments obeys the
Ward recursion of \(\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)\), which determines every matrix
element from the highest-weight value; that value is \(P_\chi(d)\) for the
pullback and \(1\) for \(\rho_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First crossing form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-crossing}
Fix \(c\) and let \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\in M(c,h_0)\) be singular of level
\(\ell\geq1\), with \(P\in U(\Vir_-)\) held fixed as \(h\) varies.  Set
\(\beta_\chi=\left.\frac{d}{dh}\langle h|\omega(P)P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}\),
where \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  Then for all partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\(\left.\frac{d}{dh}\langle h|\omega(P)L_YL_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}
=\beta_\chi\,G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The vectors \(u=L_{-Y'}P\lvert h\rangle\) and \(v=L_{-Y}P\lvert h\rangle\)
have PBW coordinates independent of \(h\), because \(L_{-Y'}P\) and
\(L_{-Y}P\) lie in \(U(\Vir_-)\).  At \(h=h_0\) they lie in the proper
submodule generated by \(\chi\), hence in the radical
\(J^1_{m+\ell}(c,h_0)\), so they are their own families.  The left side is
therefore \(B^1(u,v)\) in the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient},
and \(\beta_\chi=B^1(\chi,\chi)\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(i)
and singularity of \(\chi\),
\[
 B^1(L_{-Y'}\chi,L_{-Y}\chi)=B^1(\chi,L_YL_{-Y'}\chi)
 =G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}\,\beta_\chi .
\]
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Torus norm of a singular vector]\label{lem:singular-degree}
Let \(w\in M(c,h)\) be a nonzero singular vector of level \(\ell\geq1\).
Then the coefficient \(a_w\) of \(L_{-1}^\ell\lvert h\rangle\) in \(w\) is
nonzero, and \(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle\) is a polynomial in \(d\) of
degree exactly \(2\ell\) with leading coefficient \(a_w^2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Call \(\ell(Y)\) the length of the PBW vector \(L_{-Y}\lvert h\rangle\).
The commutator of two modes is a single mode or a scalar, so a product of
\(K\) negative modes applied to \(\lvert h\rangle\) equals its ordered PBW
monomial plus vectors of length less than \(K\), and a positive mode does
not increase the length.  Let \(K\) be the largest length occurring in
\(w\), and encode its length-\(K\) part
\(\sum_{\ell(Y)=K}a_YL_{-Y}\lvert h\rangle\) as the nonzero polynomial
\(\sigma=\sum_{\ell(Y)=K}a_Yx_{y_1}\cdots x_{y_K}\) in commuting variables
\(x_1,x_2,\ldots\).  Let \(j\geq1\).  Moving \(L_j\) to the right through
\(L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_K}\lvert h\rangle\), a factor \(L_{-m}\) with \(m>j\)
is replaced by \((m+j)L_{-(m-j)}\); for \(m=j\) the commutator acts by a
scalar, and for \(m<j\) it is the positive mode \(L_{j-m}\); both lower the
length.  Hence the length-\(K\) part of \(L_jw\) is encoded by
\(D_j\sigma\), where
\(D_j=\sum_{m>j}(m+j)\,x_{m-j}\,\partial/\partial x_m\), and singularity of
\(w\) gives \(D_j\sigma=0\) for all \(j\geq1\).  If a variable \(x_m\) with
\(m\geq2\) occurs in \(\sigma\), let \(m\) be the largest one and
\(j=m-1\); then \(D_j\sigma=(2m-1)\,x_1\,\partial\sigma/\partial x_m\neq0\),
a contradiction.  So \(\sigma\) is a multiple of \(x_1^K\), and comparing
levels gives \(K=\ell\) and \(a_w\neq0\).

For the second assertion, evaluate \(\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\) by the Ward
recursion in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}.  When a mode
\(L_{\pm n}\), \(n>0\), is commuted through \(V_d(z)\) inside a matrix
element between vectors of levels \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), the commutator
\([L_{\pm n},V_d(z)]=z^{\pm n}(z\partial_z+(1\pm n)d)V_d(z)\) acts on
\(z^{m_1-m_2-d}\) and contributes, with the sign of the commutation, the
factor \(nd\pm(m_1-m_2)\), of degree one in \(d\) with leading coefficient
\(n\).  Every other step (a commutator of two modes, the action of \(L_0\)
or of the central element, or an annihilation at \(\lvert h\rangle\) or
\(\langle h|\)) removes at least one mode without producing a factor
\(d\).  Since \(\langle h|V_d(1)|h\rangle=1\), the entry
\(\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\) has degree at most \(\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')\) in
\(d\), and the coefficient of \(d^{\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')}\) is
\(\prod_iy_i\prod_jy'_j\).  In
\(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle=\sum_{Y,Y'}a_Ya_{Y'}\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\)
the only pair with \(\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')=2\ell\) is \(Y=Y'=(1^\ell)\).
\end{proof}

The filtration \(J^\bullet(c,h)\) of Definition~\ref{def:jantzen-layers} is the Jantzen filtration of \(M(c,h)\) along the
deformation \(h\mapsto h+x\) at fixed central charge.  For
\(c\neq1,25\) this is the filtration studied in
\cite[\S5.5 and equation (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011}, and it is a Jantzen
filtration along a curve in the sense of
\cite[\S4 and \S5.2]{Astashkevich1997}.  The Feigin--Fuchs structure
theorem describes it completely; at minimal-model charges it reads as
follows.

\begin{theorem}[Smith layers at minimal-model charges]
\label{thm:jantzen-smith}
Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime, let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) with \(0<r<p\) and \(0<s<p'\), labelled so that
\(n_1:=p'r+ps<pp'\).  Put \(n_0=p'r-ps\),
\(\ell^\pm_{2j}=j(jpp'\pm n_0)\) (\(j\geq1\)),
\(\ell^+_{2j+1}=(r+jp)(s+jp')\), and
\(\ell^-_{2j+1}=((j+1)p-r)((j+1)p'-s)\) (\(j\geq0\)).
Then \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) has, for every \(k\geq1\), singular vectors
\(w^\pm_k\) of levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), and with
\(W^\pm_k=U(\Vir_-)w^\pm_k\), one has
\(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)=W^+_i+W^-_i\) for \(i\geq1\).  Consequently, at every level \(N\), the number of local Smith exponents of
\(G_N(c_\star,\lambda+x)\) that are at least \(i\) is
\(\dim(W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\).  The inverse Shapovalov matrix
\(G_N(c_\star,\Delta)^{-1}\) has a pole of order exactly
\(j_N(\lambda)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{k\geq1:\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\leq N\})\)
at \(\Delta=\lambda\), and \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) have poles of order at most \(j_N(\lambda)\)
there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the notation of \cite[\S5.1.4]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(c_\star=c_{p,p'}\)
and \((r,s)\) lies in the interior set \((K^+_{p,p'})^\circ\), so
\((c_\star,\lambda)\) belongs to Class~\(R^+\), Case~\(1+\).  Since
\(c_\star\neq1,25\), the deformation used there is
\(h\mapsto h+x\) at fixed \(c\) \cite[equation (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011},
so \(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)\) is the Jantzen filtration of
\cite[Theorem~6.3]{IoharaKoga2011}.  Part~(i) of that theorem, with
equation~(6.3) there, identifies the \(i\)-th layer with the sum of the
two Verma submodules of highest weights \(\xi_{\pm i}\) in the braided
embedding diagram \cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011}.
By \cite[equations (5.4) and (5.22)]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(\xi_0=\lambda\)
and, for \(k\geq1\), the weights \(\xi_{\pm k}\) correspond to the
Coulomb-gas momenta \(2jpp'\mp n_0\) when \(k=2j\), and
\(2(j+1)pp'-n_1\) and \(2jpp'+n_1\) when \(k=2j+1\).  Since
\(h_{u,v}(b_\star)=((p'u-pv)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), their levels above
\(\lambda\) are \(\ell^\mp_k\).

Independently, \cite[Theorem~B(e)]{Astashkevich1997} gives the same
layers, in its case \(\mathrm{III}_-\) (the integral points of the line
\(p'k-pl=n_0\) with \(kl>0\) are, up to sign, the labels
\((r+jp,s+jp')\) and \(((j+1)p-r,(j+1)p'-s)\)), along every smooth curve
through \((\lambda,c_\star)\) that is not tangent to a Kac curve.  The line
\(c=c_\star\) is such a curve: near \((\lambda,c_\star)\) every Kac curve is
a graph \(\Delta=h_{u,v}(b(c))\) over the \(c\)-axis, because
\(c'(b_\star)=12(b_\star+b_\star^{-1})(1-b_\star^{-2})\neq0\).

Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} converts the layers into Smith exponents.
The space \((W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\) is nonzero exactly when
\(\min(\ell^+_i,\ell^-_i)\leq N\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} gives the
pole order \(j_N(\lambda)\) of \(G_N^{-1}\).  The trace \(F_N\) cannot have
a higher pole.  Finally, \(j_{N-j}(\lambda)\leq j_N(\lambda)\), and
\(H_N=\sum_j\eta_jF_{N-j}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Other weights]\label{rem:jantzen-other}
The other weights of \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) at a minimal-model charge,
such as \(h_{1,5}(b_0)=5/2\), are weights \(\xi_i\), \(i\neq0\), of
Case~\(1+\) or of boundary type; \cite[Theorem~6.3]{IoharaKoga2011}
describes their Jantzen filtration as well.  At \(c_\star=1,25\) the
fixed-\(c\) line is tangent to some Kac curves and needs a separate
analysis.  The confluence theorem uses none of these results.
\end{remark}


\begin{theorem}[Top Jantzen layer]\label{thm:top-layer}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, put
\(\ell_k=\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), \(\mu^\pm_k=\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\),
\(\gamma^\pm_k=B^k(w^\pm_k,w^\pm_k)\), and
\(\tau^\pm_k(d)=\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle\).
Let \(k\geq1\) and \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\).  Then \(j_N(\lambda)=k\);
for each sign with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N\), one has \(\gamma^\pm_k\neq0\),
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\neq0\) for \(M\leq N-\ell^\pm_k\),
\(\deg_d\tau^\pm_k=2\ell^\pm_k\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-layer}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^kH_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}\frac{\tau^\pm_k(d)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\ell^\pm_0=0\).  Direct expansion of Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}
for \(j\geq0\) gives, for the upper and lower signs respectively,
\(\ell^+_{2j+1}-\ell^\pm_{2j}=s(r+2jp),\,r(s+2jp')\),
\(\ell^-_{2j+1}-\ell^\pm_{2j}=(p-r)((2j+1)p'-s),\,(p'-s)((2j+1)p-r)\),
\(\ell^\pm_{2j+2}-\ell^+_{2j+1}=(p'-s)((2j+1)p+r),\,(p-r)((2j+1)p'+s)\), and
\(\ell^\pm_{2j+2}-\ell^-_{2j+1}=r(2(j+1)p'-s),\,s(2(j+1)p-r)\),
all positive.  Hence \(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<\ell_{k+1}\), so
\(j_N(\lambda)=k\) and \(J^{k+1}_N(c_\star,\lambda)=0\).  Moreover
\(\ell^+_{2j}-\ell^-_{2j}=2jn_0\neq0\) (since \(p\nmid r\)) and
\(\ell^+_{2j+1}-\ell^-_{2j+1}=(2j+1)(n_1-pp')\neq0\).

Fix \(N'\) with \(\ell_k\leq N'\leq N\).  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, \(k\) is the largest local Smith exponent
of \(G_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x)\), and
\(J^k_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda)=(W^+_k)_{N'}+(W^-_k)_{N'}\).  For each sign with
\(\ell^\pm_k\leq N'\), the vectors \(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k\), with \(Z\) a partition
of \(N'-\ell^\pm_k\), form a basis of \((W^\pm_k)_{N'}\), because the
embedding \(j_{w^\pm_k}\) is injective
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(i) and singularity of \(w^\pm_k\),
\[
 B^k(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k,L_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)=B^k(w^\pm_k,L_ZL_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)
 =\gamma^\pm_k\,G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)_{Z,Z'} .
\]
If both signs occur, let \(w\) and \(w'\) be the singular vectors of the
lower and the higher level.  Then \(L_ZL_{-Z'}w\) is a combination of
vectors \(L_{-Y}w\) with \(|Y|=\ell(w')-\ell(w)>0\), and
\(B^k(w',L_{-Y}w)=B^k(L_Yw',w)=0\); so the two summands are
\(B^k\)-orthogonal.  As \(B^k\) is nondegenerate on \(J^k_{N'}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii), the sum is direct and
\(\gamma^\pm_kG_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\) is invertible.  Letting
\(N'\) range from \(\ell^\pm_k\) to \(N\) proves the nonvanishing
assertions.

Since \(k\) is the pole order of \(G_{N'}^{-1}\) at \(\lambda\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}), Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii) in the basis
above and Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [x^{-k}]F_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x,d)
 &=\sum_{\pm}\frac1{\gamma^\pm_k}\sum_{Z,Z'}
 \bigl(G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)^{-1}\bigr)_{Z,Z'}
 \langle w^\pm_k|L_ZV_d(1)L_{-Z'}|w^\pm_k\rangle\\
 &=\sum_{\pm}\frac{\tau^\pm_k(d)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 F_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d),
 \end{aligned}
\]
with the sums over the signs with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N'\).  For
\(N'<\ell_k\), \(F_{N'}\) has a pole of order at most
\(j_{N'}(\lambda)<k\) at \(\lambda\).  Multiplying by the Euler
coefficients and summing as in Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced} gives
\eqref{eq:top-layer}.  The degree of \(\tau^\pm_k\) is
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharpness of the Jantzen bound]
\label{cor:top-layer-sharp}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) has
a pole of order exactly \(j_N(\lambda)=k\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\) at every
level \(N\) in the following ranges:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] every single-vector level \(\ell_k\leq N<\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\),
with leading coefficient \(\tau/\gamma\) at \(N=\ell_k\);
\item[(ii)] \(N=\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), and every two-vector level
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\) with
\(\max(p,p')M^\pm<m^\pm_k+\min(p,p')\) for both signs, where
\(M^\pm=N-\ell^\pm_k\) and \((m^\pm_k)^2=4pp'(\lambda+\ell^\pm_k)+(p'-p)^2\);
\item[(iii)] every level \(N\geq1\) whenever \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\) and \(p'\)
has an odd prime divisor, or \(r\in\{1,p-1\}\) and \(p\) has an odd prime
divisor; in particular, at every minimal model with \(p=3\) or \(p'=3\)
(including the Ising charge \(c=1/2\) and \(c=0\)), the bound \(j_N(\lambda)\)
is attained at every interior Kac weight \(\lambda\) and every level \(N\geq1\);
\item[(iv)] every two-vector level satisfying the \(\ell\)-adic valuation or
coprime-factor degree criterion of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(i) Only the sign with \(\ell^\pm_k=\ell_k\) contributes to
\eqref{eq:top-layer}, so the leading coefficient is
\(\tau(d)H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)/\gamma\) with \(M=N-\ell_k\), \(\mu=\lambda+\ell_k\),
and \(\tau\neq0\).  For \(M=0\) one has \(H_0=1\).  For \(M\geq1\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives \(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\), and
\(\mu=(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\) for the Coulomb momentum \(m\) of \(\mu\); hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing} gives \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\).

(ii) At \(N=\max(\ell^\pm_k)\), the leading coefficient is
\((\tau/\gamma)H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)+\tau'/\gamma'\), with \(\tau',\gamma'\) the
data of the other singular vector and \(M=N-\ell_k\geq1\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,0)=0\), so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} the first term vanishes to order at
least \(k+1\) at \(d=0\) and the second to order exactly \(k\).  Above it, put
\(f(u,v)=p'u-pv\) for \(uv=M^\pm\); by hypothesis
\(|f(u,v)|\leq\max(p'M^\pm-p,pM^\pm-p')<m^\pm_k\).  For \(u>v\), pairing
\((u,v)\) with \((v,u)\) gives \(|f(v,u)|<f(u,v)\) if \(p'>p\) (and
\(|f(u,v)|<-f(v,u)\) if \(p'<p\)); if \(M^\pm\) is a square, the unpaired term
\(u=v\) has \(f(u,u)=(p'-p)u\), of the same sign as \(p'-p\).  Since
\(x/((m^\pm_k)^2-x^2)\) is odd and increasing on \((-m^\pm_k,m^\pm_k)\), the
derivatives \(K^\pm=-\frac{2pp'}{p'-p}\sum_{uv=M^\pm}f/((m^\pm_k)^2-f^2)\) at
\(d=0\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}) are negative for both signs.  For even
\(k\) the coefficient of \(d^{k+1}\) in \eqref{eq:top-layer} is
\(c_k\rho^k(K^++K^-)\neq0\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients}).  For
odd \(k\) the coefficient of \((d-1)^{k+1}\) is
\(c_k\sigma^{k-1}(m^+_kL^++m^-_kL^-)/(2(p+p'))\neq0\), where
\(L^\pm=\frac{2pp'}{p+p'}\sum_{uv=M^\pm}(p'u+pv)/((m^\pm_k)^2-f^2)>0\).

(iii)--(iv) Both follow directly from Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}.
When \(p=3\) every interior weight has \(r\in\{1,2\}=\{1,p-1\}\), and when
\(p'=3\) every interior weight has \(s\in\{1,2\}=\{1,p'-1\}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The remaining two-vector levels]\label{rem:top-layer-beyond}
All single-vector levels are settled by Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp}(i).
Open are only the two-vector levels \(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\)
not covered by parts~(ii)--(iv) of Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp}; in
particular \(2\leq s\leq p'-2\) (when \(p'\) has an odd prime factor) and
\(2\leq r\leq p-2\) (when \(p\) has an odd prime factor).  At \(d=0,1\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives} and Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients}
show that attainment fails only if
\(K^++(-1)^kK^-=0=m^+_kL^+-(-1)^km^-_kL^-\), where
\(L^\pm=\frac{2pp'}{p+p'}\sum_{uv=M^\pm}(p'u+pv)/(C_N-(p'u+pv)^2)\) share the
common modulus \(C_N=n_0^2+4pp'N=(m^\pm_k)^2+4pp'M^\pm\).  Across all
\(565\,072\) two-vector levels with \(pp'\leq120\) and \(k\leq4\), the
\(\ell\)-adic criterion of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria} closes all
but \(8\) levels, all at \((p,p')=(8,9)\), \((r,s)=(3,5)\), \(k=1\)
(\(N\in\{23,29,32,38,41,47,50,56\}\)); among these, \(N=23\) is closed by the
degree bound, and direct exact evaluation of \([(d-h_{1,3})^1]P_N\) via
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-screening} is nonzero on all eight.  Direct reconstruction
at \(d=11/13\) confirms the pole orders for \(N\leq10\) at the interior weights
of all models with \(6<pp'\leq21\) and for \(N\leq11\) at \((2,3)\).
\end{remark}

\section{Generic residue data}\label{sec:generic-data}

\begin{definition}[Generic residue data used for algebraic confluence]
\label{def:generic-residue}
With \(c(b)\) and \(h_{r,s}(b)\) from Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}
and \(\Lambda^2=(b+b^{-1})^2-4d\), define
\[
 {\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)
 =\prod_{\substack{1\leq k\leq2r-1\\k\ {\rm odd}}}
  \prod_{\substack{1\leq l\leq2s-1\\l\ {\rm odd}}}
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}\,
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4},
\]
and \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b){\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)\),
where
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)=\frac12\prod_{(p,q)\in[1-r,r]\times[1-s,s]\setminus\{(0,0),(r,s)\}}(pb+qb^{-1})^{-1}\).
For generic \(b\), \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\) is the residue coefficient in the
eta-reduced torus recursion (Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Imported generic normalization identities]
\label{prop:imported-normalizations}
For positive \(r,s\) and generic \(b\), let \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) be the
singular vector whose \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient is \(1\), and let
\(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\) be its crossing scalar from
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.  Then
\begin{align}
 \beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}&={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)^{-1},\label{eq:crossing-normalization}\\
 \langle\chi_{r,s}(b)|V_d(1)|\chi_{r,s}(b)\rangle&={\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d).\label{eq:fusion-normalization}
\end{align}
Both identities are identities of rational functions in \(b\), and hence
continue to every specialization at which the normalized singular vector
is regular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The recursion was conjectured in \cite{Poghossian2009}.  In
\cite[Section~3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}, equation~(7) expresses the
residue through the inverse norm derivative \(A_{r,s}(c)\), the reciprocal
of \(\partial_\Delta\langle\chi_{r,s}^{\Delta}\mid\chi_{r,s}^{\Delta}\rangle\)
at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}\) with the coefficients of the normalized vector held
fixed; by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing} this derivative is
\(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\).  The product following equation~(7), for the
normalization with \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\), is term-for-term
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\), proving \eqref{eq:crossing-normalization}.
The source writes \(d=((b+b^{-1})^2-\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2)/4\), so
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2=\Lambda^2\).  For odd \(k,l\) in the ranges of
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the four factors
\((\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}\pm(kb+lb^{-1}))/2\) and
\((\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}\pm(kb-lb^{-1}))/2\) of equation~(8) multiply to
\(\frac14(\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2)\cdot\frac14(\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2)\);
taking the product proves \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization}, with no sign,
power of two, or choice of square root.  Equation~(9) is the eta-reduced
recursion.  The normalized singular-vector coefficients solve a finite
linear system over \(\mathbb Q(b)\), so both left-hand sides are rational
in \(b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Distinct weights and no shifted self-poles away from
rational \(b^2\)]
\label{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
For positive labels \((r,s)\) and \((u,v)\), \(h_{r,s}(b)+rs-h_{u,v}(b)\)
is not the zero rational function of \(b\), nor is
\(h_{r,s}(b)-h_{u,v}(b)\) when \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\).  If
\(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), then
neither function vanishes at \(b_\star\), and every
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular and nonzero at \(b_\star\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Kac formula gives \(h_{r,s}(b)+rs=h_{r,-s}(b)\), which equals
\(h_{u,v}(b)\) exactly when \((rb-sb^{-1})^2=(ub+vb^{-1})^2\), that is,
\(rb-sb^{-1}=\pm(ub+vb^{-1})\), since \(\mathbb C(b)\) is an integral
domain.  Equivalently
\((r\mp u)b^2=s\pm v\).  As an identity in \(b\) this contradicts
\(r,s,u,v>0\), and at a point it forces \(b^2=(s\pm v)/(r\mp u)\in\mathbb Q\);
here \(r\mp u\neq0\), because \(r=u\) would force \(s+v=0\) and
\(r+u>0\).  Similarly
\(h_{r,s}=h_{u,v}\) means \((r\mp u)b^2=\pm v-s\); as an identity this
forces \((r,s)=(u,v)\), and at a point with \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\) it forces
\(b^2\in\mathbb Q\).  Finally, each factor \(pb+qb^{-1}\) of
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is finite at \(b_\star\neq0\), and it vanishes only
if \(p\neq0\) and \(b_\star^2=-q/p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic eta-reduced recursion]
\label{prop:generic-recursion}
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For all \(b\) outside a finite set of algebraic values,
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{a=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\ \ell_a=rs\leq N}}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)}{\Delta-h_{r,s}(b)}\,
 H_{N-\ell_a}\bigl(c(b),h_{r,s}(b)+rs,d\bigr)
\]
as an identity of rational functions of \(\Delta\), polynomial in
\(d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exclude \(b=0\) and the finitely many zeros and poles in \(\mathbb Q(b)\)
of the nonzero differences \(h_a-h_g\) and \(z_a-h_g\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}), of the coefficients of
\({\mathcal R}_a\) and \({\mathcal A}_a\), and of the normalized
singular-vector coefficients, for all labels of level at most \(N\); every
excluded value is algebraic.  Then the weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with
\(rs\leq N\) are pairwise distinct, and no shifted weight
\(z_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b)+rs\) is a degenerate weight of level at most \(N\).

Fix such a \(b\), write \(c=c(b)\), and fix a label \(a=(r,s)\) with
\(rs\leq N\).  By distinctness, \(h_{r,s}(b)\) lies on no other Kac
divisor of level at most \(N\), so the level-\(N\) Kac determinant has
valuation exactly \(p(N-rs)\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)\).  The
level-\(N\) image of the injective singular embedding
\(j_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\), whose injectivity is the result stated in
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}, is a
\(p(N-rs)\)-dimensional subspace of the
kernel of the specialized Gram matrix.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} the
nullity is at most the valuation, so the kernel is exactly that image,
and every positive local Smith exponent equals \(1\).  Hence
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}, with \(k=1\), gives the residue of
\(G_N^{-1}\) as \(\iota(B^1)^{-1}\iota^*\) on this kernel, and by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(iii) and~\ref{lem:singular-crossing},
\(B^1=\beta_{\chi}\,G_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s})\) in the basis
\(L_{-Z}\chi_{r,s}(b)\), where
\(\beta_\chi^{-1}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\) by
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization}.  Taking the trace of the residue of \(G_N^{-1}\) against \(\rho_N\) and
factoring the pulled-back torus form by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization}
and \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives
\(\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)}F_N(c,\Delta,d)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)\,F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d)\).
The difference between \(F_N\) and the sum of these principal parts has no
finite pole and tends to \(p(N)\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight}), so
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=p(N)+\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}({\mathcal R}_a(b,d)/(\Delta-h_a(b)))F_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d)\).
Multiplying by the Euler coefficients \(\eta_j\)
(Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced}) and using \(\sum_j\eta_jp(N-j)=\delta_{N,0}\)
gives \(H_N(c,\Delta,d)=\delta_{N,0}+\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}({\mathcal R}_a(b,d)/(\Delta-h_a(b)))H_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d)\),
which for \(N\geq1\) is the displayed recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The external weights \(d=0\) and \(d=1\)]
\label{lem:d-derivatives}
For \(N\geq1\), \(H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)\) vanishes at \(d=0\)
and at \(d=1\), and, as rational functions of \((b,\Delta)\),
\[
 \partial_dH_N\big|_{d=0}
 =-\sum_{rs=N}\frac{rb^2+s}{2(b^2+1)(\Delta-h_{r,s}(b))},\quad
 \partial_dH_N\big|_{d=1}
 =\sum_{rs=N}\frac{rb^2-s}{2(b^2-1)(\Delta-h_{r,s}(b))}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x,y)=xb+yb^{-1}\) and \(B=[1-r,r]\times[1-s,s]\).  Since
\(\Lambda^2=\varphi(1,1)^2-4d\), at \(d=0\) the factors of
\({\mathcal P}_{r,s}\) in Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} are
\(\frac14(\varphi(1,1)^2-\varphi(k,\pm l)^2)
=\frac14\varphi(1-k,1\mp l)\varphi(1+k,1\pm l)\), and over the odd \(k,l\) and
both signs these products run once over \(\varphi(2p,2q)\),
\((p,q)\in B\).  The factor with \((k,\pm l)=(1,1)\) equals \(-d\).  At
\(d=1\), \(\Lambda^2=\varphi(1,-1)^2\), the factors run once over
\(\varphi(2p,2q)\) with \((p,q)\in B-(0,1)\), and the factor with
\((k,\pm l)=(1,-1)\) equals \(1-d\).  Hence every \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\)
vanishes at \(d=0,1\), and comparison with \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\), whose
product runs over \(B\setminus\{(0,0),(r,s)\}\), gives
\(\partial_d{\mathcal R}_{r,s}|_{d=0}=-\varphi(r,s)/(2\varphi(1,1))\) and
\(\partial_d{\mathcal R}_{r,s}|_{d=1}=-\varphi(-r,s)/(2\varphi(1,-1))\),
using \(\prod_{p}\varphi(p,-s)/\varphi(p,s)=\varphi(-r,s)/\varphi(r,s)\)
over \(p\in[1-r,r]\).  These are the displayed coefficients.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} and induction on \(N\),
\(H_N\) vanishes at \(d=0,1\) for \(N\geq1\), and differentiating the
recursion there leaves only the labels with \(rs=N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Regular normalized singular vectors]
\label{lem:regular-everywhere}
For every positive label \((r,s)\) and every \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\),
the normalized singular vector \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} is regular at \(b_\star\),
and its value is a singular vector of level \(rs\) in
\(M(c_\star,h_{r,s}(b_\star))\) with \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\).
Hence \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} holds at \(b_\star\), and so does
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization} when \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular
there.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e\geq0\) be minimal such that \((b-b_\star)^e\chi_{r,s}(b)\) is
regular at \(b_\star\), with value \(w\neq0\).  The conditions
\(L_1v=L_2v=0\) are linear with coefficients polynomial in \(b\), and
\(L_1,L_2\) generate the positive modes, so \(w\) is singular in
\(M(c_\star,h_{r,s}(b_\star))\).  Its \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient is the value
of \((b-b_\star)^e\), which is nonzero by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree};
so \(e=0\).  The identities of Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations}
are identities of rational functions of \(b\) and specialize.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Layer coefficients at \(d=0\) and \(d=1\)]
\label{prop:layer-coefficients}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, let \(m^\pm_k\) be the
Coulomb momentum of \(\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\) (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}), \(\rho=-n_0/(2(p'-p))\),
\(\sigma=n_0/(2(p+p'))\) and
\(c_k=(-1)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-1}{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\).  Then
\(\tau^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k\) equals \(c_k(\pm\rho)^kd^k\) and
\((\pm1)^{k+1}c_k\sigma^{k-1}m^\pm_k(d-1)^k/(2(p+p'))\) up to higher
powers of \(d\) and of \(d-1\), respectively; in particular
\(\tau^\pm_k\) vanishes to order exactly \(k\) at \(d=0\) and at \(d=1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\nu=b^2\), \(\nu_\star=-p'/p\), \(N_0=2pp'\); dots denote \(d/d\nu\) at
\(\nu_\star\).  By the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, the factors of
\({\mathcal P}_g(b,0)\) vanishing at \(\nu_\star\) (where \(\varphi(x,y)=0\) iff
\((x,y)\in\mathbb Z(p,p')\)) are \(-d\) and
\(\frac14\varphi(2jp,2jp')\varphi(2-2jp,2-2jp')\), \(0\neq j\),
\((jp,jp')\in B_g\), of derivative \(j\kappa_0\), \(\kappa_0=p(p'-p)/p'\);
\({\mathcal A}_g\) has a pole of order at most their number minus one, equal
to it when \(\rho_g:=\partial_d{\mathcal R}_g|_{d=0}=-(p'u-pv)/(2(p'-p))\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}) is nonzero; so along
\((\nu,\Delta,d)=(\nu_\star+\tau,\lambda+\tau w,\tau y)\),
\({\mathcal R}_g/\tau\to\rho_gy\prod_j(1-y/(j\kappa_0))\).  Expanding \(H_N\)
by Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} into chains \(g_1,\dots,g_n\)
with denominators \(z_{i-1}-h_{g_i}\) (\(z_0=\Delta\),
\(z_i=h_{g_i}+\ell_{g_i}\)), each of order \(\tau\) if
\(h_{g_i}(\nu_\star)=z_{i-1}(\nu_\star)\) (consecutive slopes differ, see
below) and a unit otherwise (generic \(w,y\)), \(H_N\) tends to \(T_N(w,y)\), the sum over such colliding chains
of \(\prod_i\rho_{g_i}y\prod_j(1-y/(j\kappa_0))/(\zeta_{i-1}-\dot h_{g_i})\),
\(\zeta_0=w\), \(\zeta_i=\dot h_{g_i}\).  The denominator of \(H_N\) is a
product of powers of \(\Delta-h_g\), with leading forms units or
\(\Delta-\lambda-\dot h_g(\nu-\nu_\star)\); so \(T_N(w,y)=\Phi(1,w,y)\) for the
lowest homogeneous part \(\Phi\) of \(H_N\) in
\((\nu-\nu_\star,\Delta-\lambda,d)\), of degree \(0\) and regular at
\((0,1,\cdot)\).  Hence \([y^k]T_N=O(w^{-k})\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\lambda+\varepsilon,\varepsilon y)\to\Phi(0,1,y)\); with
Theorems~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, \ref{thm:top-layer} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives} this gives
\([d^k](\tau^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k)=\lim_ww^k[y^k]T_{\ell^\pm_k}\).

By integrality of \(u,v\), the colliding chains from \(\lambda\) are the
sequences \(n_0=x_0,x_1,\dots\), \(|x_i|\) increasing, alternately in
\(n_1+N_0\mathbb Z\) and \(n_0+N_0\mathbb Z\), via
\(g_i=(\epsilon_i(x_{i-1}+x_i)/(2p'),\epsilon_i(x_i-x_{i-1})/(2p))\),
\(\epsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}x_i\); then
\(\rho_{g_i}=-\epsilon_ix_{i-1}/(2(p'-p))\),
\(4p'^2\dot h_{g_i}=p'^2-p^2-x_{i-1}x_i\) (so \(4p'^2(\dot h_{g_{i-1}}-\dot h_{g_i})=x_{i-1}(x_i-x_{i-2})\neq0\)),
and \(|x_i|\) is the momentum reached.  The endpoints \(\pm m^\pm_k\) are \(n_1+jN_0\), \(n_1-(j+1)N_0\)
for \(k=2j+1\) and \(n_0\pm jN_0\) for \(k=2j\); the first steps of chains
ending there are \(x_1=n_1+iN_0\) with \(-j\leq i\leq j\),
\(-j-1\leq i\leq j-1\), \(-j\leq i\leq j-1\): \(k\) distinct slopes.  As
\([y^k]T_N=\sum_\theta C_\theta/(w-\dot h_\theta)\) is \(O(w^{-k})\), its
moments of order \(\leq k-2\) vanish, and
\(\lim_ww^k[y^k]T_N=C_\kappa\prod_{\theta\neq\kappa}n_0(x_\theta-x_\kappa)/(4p'^2)\)
for each \(\kappa\).  For \(k=2j+1\) take \(\kappa\) the endpoint, \((r+jp,s+jp')\) or
\(((j+1)p-r,(j+1)p'-s)\), whose box meets \(\mathbb Z(p,p')\) for \(|j'|\leq j\)
(as \(0<r<p\), \(0<s<p'\)): \(C_\kappa=\pm\rho(-1)^j/(j!^2\kappa_0^{2j})\),
the product is \((n_0N_0/4p'^2)^{2j}(2j)!\), and
\(n_0N_0/(4p'^2\kappa_0)=-\rho\).  For \(k=2j\) take
\(\kappa=n_1+(j-1)N_0\), then the step \(g\) to the endpoint, \((r+(2j-1)p,p'-s)\) or
\((p-r,s+(2j-1)p')\), whose box meets \(\mathbb Z(p,p')\) only at \(0\),
\(\rho_g/(\dot h_\kappa-\dot h_g)=-2p'^2/((p'-p)jN_0)\); the product is
\(-(n_0N_0/4p'^2)^{2j-1}(2j-1)!\).  Both give \(c_k(\pm\rho)^k\).  At \(d=1\),
with \(y=(d-1)/\tau\), the vanishing factors of \({\mathcal P}_g(b,1)\) are
\(1-d\) and \(\frac14\varphi(2jp,2jp')\varphi(2-2jp,-2-2jp')\),
\((jp,jp'+1)\in B_g\) (for the labels \(\kappa,g\) above, the same \(j\) as before), of derivative \(j\kappa_1\), \(\kappa_1=p(p+p')/p'\);
\(\partial_d{\mathcal R}_g|_{d=1}=(p'u+pv)/(2(p+p'))=|x_i|/(2(p+p'))\),
\(n_0N_0/(4p'^2\kappa_1)=\sigma\), and for \(k=2j\) the ratio becomes
\(2p'^2x_{\rm end}/((p+p')x_\kappa jN_0)\); the same steps give the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-screening formula at \(d=h_{1,3}(b)\)]
\label{lem:one-screening}
Write \(t=b^2\), so \(h_{u,1}(b)=\frac14((1-u^2)t+2(1-u))\) and
\(h_{1,3}(b)=-1-2/t\).  Define \(c_{M,k}(t)\in\mathbb Q(t)\) (\(0\leq k\leq M\))
by the formal expansion in \(\mathbb Q(t)[W][[q]]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-screening-gen}
 \prod_{n\geq1}\left(1-\frac{q^n}{(1-q^n)^2}W\right)^{2/t}
 =\sum_{M\geq0}\sum_{k=0}^M c_{M,k}(t)\,W^kq^M.
\end{equation}
Then for every \(M\geq0\), as an identity in \(\mathbb Q(t,\Delta)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-screening}
 H_M\bigl(c(b),\Delta,h_{1,3}(b)\bigr)
 =\sum_{k=0}^M c_{M,k}(t)\,
 \frac{(-1)^k\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(jt+2)}
      {2^kt^k\prod_{j=1}^k\bigl(2\Delta-2h_{j,1}(b)\bigr)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At \(d=h_{1,3}(b)\) one has \(\Lambda^2=(b+3b^{-1})^2\).  If \(v\geq2\), the
factor \((k,l)=(1,3)\) in Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} vanishes, so
\({\mathcal R}_{u,v}(b,h_{1,3}(b))=0\).  For \(v=1\), only \(l=1\) occurs, and
factoring each difference of squares in \({\mathcal P}_{u,1}\) gives
\({\mathcal P}_{u,1}(b,h_{1,3}(b))=\prod_{x=1-u}^u(xb+b^{-1})(xb+2b^{-1})\).
Dividing by \(2\prod_{(p,q)\neq(0,0),(u,1)}(pb+qb^{-1})\) cancels
\(pb+b^{-1}\) for \(1-u\leq p\leq u-1\), leaving, with \(\gamma=2/t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-u1-h13}
 {\mathcal R}_{u,1}\bigl(b,h_{1,3}(b)\bigr)
 =\frac{(-1)^{u-1}(ut+1)\prod_{x=1-u}^u(xt+2)}{2(u-1)!u!\,t^{2u}}
 =\frac{(-1)^{u-1}(\gamma+2u)}{2\gamma(u-1)!u!}\prod_{x=1-u}^u(\gamma+x).
\end{equation}

For indeterminates \(\nu,\gamma\), define the \(\mathbb Q(\nu,\gamma)\)-linear
functional \(\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}\) on \(\mathbb Q(\nu,\gamma)[z,z^{-1}]\) by
\(\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}(z^m)=(\nu)_m/(\nu+\gamma+1)_m\), where
\((a)_m=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(a+j)\) for \(m\geq0\) and
\((a)_m=\prod_{j=1}^{-m}(a-j)^{-1}\) for \(m<0\).  Since
\((a)_{m+1}=(a+m)(a)_m\) for all \(m\in\mathbb Z\), induction on \(r\geq0\)
gives
\(\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}((1-z)^rz^m)=(\gamma+1)_r(\nu)_m/(\nu+\gamma+1)_{r+m}\):
the step \(r\to r+1\) is the subtraction
\((\nu+\gamma+1+r+m)-(\nu+m)=\gamma+1+r\).  Taking \(r=2k\), \(m=-k\) yields,
for \(W=(1-z)^2/z\),
\[
 \Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}(W^k)
 =\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(\gamma+j)}{\prod_{j=1}^k(\nu-j)(\nu+\gamma+j)}.
\]
Relating \(\Delta\) to \(\nu\) by
\(\Delta(\nu)=(1+\gamma-\nu(\nu+\gamma))/(2\gamma)\) (with \(\gamma=2/t\)),
one has \(2\Delta(\nu)-2h_{j,1}(b)=-(\nu-j)(\nu+\gamma+j)/\gamma\), so
\(\Delta(u)=h_{u,1}(b)\), \(\Delta(u-\gamma)=h_{u,1}(b)+u\),
\(\Delta'(u)=-(\gamma+2u)/(2\gamma)\), and \(\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}(W^k)\) is
the factor multiplying \(c_{M,k}(t)\) in \eqref{eq:one-screening}.

Let \(\widetilde H_M(t,\Delta)\) denote the right side of
\eqref{eq:one-screening}, and put \(G_M(z)=\sum_{k=0}^Mc_{M,k}(2/\gamma)W^k\),
so \(\widetilde H_M(2/\gamma,\Delta(\nu))=\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}(G_M(z))\).
Setting \(W=0\) in \eqref{eq:one-screening-gen} gives
\(c_{M,0}(t)=\delta_{M,0}\), so \(\widetilde H_0=1=H_0\), and for \(M\geq1\),
\(\widetilde H_M\to0\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\).  For generic \(t\) the weights
\(h_{1,1},\ldots,h_{M,1}\) are distinct (Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}),
so \(\widetilde H_M\) has at most simple poles in \(\Delta\), all at
\(h_{u,1}(b)\) (\(1\leq u\leq M\)).  By Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion},
induction on \(M\), and uniqueness of partial fractions, it suffices to prove
for every \(M\geq1\) and \(1\leq u\leq M\) that
\(\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_{u,1}(b)}\widetilde H_M(2/\gamma,\Delta)
={\mathcal R}_{u,1}(b,h_{1,3}(b))\widetilde H_{M-u}(2/\gamma,h_{u,1}(b)+u)\)
in \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).  Both sides are rational functions of \(\gamma\)
(regular at every integer \(\gamma>u\), since
\(2(h_{u,1}+u)-2h_{j,1}=-(u-\gamma-j)(u+j)/\gamma\neq0\) for \(j\geq1\)), so
it suffices to check the identity at every integer \(\gamma>u\).

Fix an integer \(\gamma>u\), and set
\(F(z;q)=(1-z)^\gamma\sum_{M\geq0}G_M(z)q^M=\sum_{M\geq0}F_M(z)q^M\).  Since
\(1-q^nW/(1-q^n)^2=(1-q^nz)(1-q^nz^{-1})/(1-q^n)^2\), we have
\[
 F(z;q)=(1-z)^\gamma\prod_{n\geq1}
 \frac{(1-q^nz)^\gamma(1-q^nz^{-1})^\gamma}{(1-q^n)^{2\gamma}}.
\]
Because \(\gamma\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), the factor \((1-qz^{-1})^\gamma\)
contributes monomials \(z^{-r}q^r\) with \(0\leq r\leq\gamma\), while every
other factor contributes monomials \(z^aq^M\) with \(M\leq2(M+a)\); hence every
monomial \(z^aq^M\) in \(F(z;q)\) satisfies \(M+a\geq0\) and
\(M\leq2(M+a)+\gamma\).  Substituting \(z=qw\) is therefore well-defined in
\(\mathbb Q[w,w^{-1}][[q]]\) and reindexing the product gives
\(F(qw;q)=(1-w^{-1})^\gamma F(w;q)/(1-w)^\gamma=(-1)^\gamma w^{-\gamma}F(w;q)\).
Extracting \([q^{M-u}w^{-u}]\) from both sides yields
\([z^{-u}]F_M(z)=(-1)^\gamma[w^{\gamma-u}]F_{M-u}(w)\).

For \(\gamma\in\mathbb Z_{>u}\), \(\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}(z^m)\) is regular at
\(\nu=u\) for \(m\geq0\), while for \(m=-(u+s)\) (\(s\geq0\)) it has a simple
pole at \(\nu=u\) with residue
\[
 \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{u+s}(u+\gamma+1-j)}{(u-1)!(-1)^ss!}
 =\frac{\prod_{i=1}^u(\gamma+i)}{(u-1)!}\,(-1)^s\binom{\gamma}{s}
\]
(which vanishes for \(s>\gamma\)); summing against \(G_M(z)\) gives
\(\operatorname*{Res}_{\nu=u}\Lambda_{\nu,\gamma}(G_M(z))
=(\prod_{i=1}^u(\gamma+i)/(u-1)!)[z^{-u}]F_M(z)\).  At \(\nu=u-\gamma\), every
denominator in \(\Lambda_{u-\gamma,\gamma}(w^m)\) is nonzero (since
\(u-\gamma<0\) and \(u+1>0\)), and direct evaluation gives
\(\binom{\gamma}{u}\Lambda_{u-\gamma,\gamma}(w^m)=(-1)^m\binom{\gamma}{u+m}
=(-1)^{\gamma-u}[w^{\gamma-u-m}](1-w)^\gamma\) for all \(m\in\mathbb Z\)
(vanishing outside \(-u\leq m\leq\gamma-u\)), so
\(\binom{\gamma}{u}\Lambda_{u-\gamma,\gamma}(G_{M-u}(w))
=(-1)^{\gamma-u}[w^{\gamma-u}]F_{M-u}(w)\).  Combining this with the shift
identity and multiplying by \(\Delta'(u)=-(\gamma+2u)/(2\gamma)\) reproduces
\eqref{eq:R-u1-h13} times \(\widetilde H_{M-u}(2/\gamma,h_{u,1}(b)+u)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integrality and \(\ell\)-adic valuation at \(d=h_{1,3}(b_\star)\)]
\label{prop:h13-nonvanishing}
For every \(M\geq1\), the cleared numerator
\[
 P_M(t,Y):=M!\,t^{2M}\Bigl(\prod_{u=1}^M\bigl(2\Delta-2h_{u,1}(b)\bigr)\Bigr)
 H_M\bigl(c(b),\Delta,h_{1,3}(b)\bigr)\Big|_{\Delta=Y/t^2}
\]
belongs to \(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\) and satisfies \(P_M(0,0)=4^M\).
Consequently, let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime, \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let
\(\ell\) be an odd prime dividing \(p'\).  If \(M\geq1\) and
\(\mu=(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\) (\(m\in\mathbb Z\)) satisfy
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\), then \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,h_{1,3}(b_\star))\) is
regular and nonzero, with exact \(\ell\)-adic valuation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h13-valuation}
 v_\ell\bigl(H_M(c_\star,\mu,h_{1,3}(b_\star))\bigr)
 =-v_\ell(M!)-Mv_\ell(p')-\sum_{u=1}^M v_\ell\bigl(m^2-(p'u-p)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\) in \(\mathbb Q[d]\) for every
coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substituting \(\Delta=Y/t^2\) into Lemma~\ref{lem:one-screening} and splitting
\(t^{2M-k}=t^k\cdot t^{2(M-k)}\) and
\(\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(jt+2)/2^k=\prod_{i=1}^k(it+1)((2i-1)t+2)\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_M(t,Y)=\sum_{k=1}^M\frac{M!}{k!}
 &\Bigl((-1)^kk!\,t^kc_{M,k}(t)\Bigr)
 \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^k(it+1)\bigl((2i-1)t+2\bigr)\Bigr)\\
 &\times\prod_{u=k+1}^M\Bigl(2Y+(u-1)t^2+\frac{u^2-1}{2}t^3\Bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Expanding \eqref{eq:one-screening-gen} by the binomial series shows that
\((-1)^kk!\,t^kc_{M,k}(t)\) is a \(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\)-linear combination of
products \(\binom{k}{r_1,r_2,\ldots}\prod_n\prod_{j=0}^{r_n-1}(2-jt)\) with
\(\sum r_n=k\), hence lies in \(\mathbb Z[t]\).  Thus
\(P_M(t,Y)\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\).  Every \(k<M\) summand vanishes at
\((t,Y)=(0,0)\).  For \(k=M\), \(\sum nr_n\leq M=\sum r_n\) forces \(r_1=M\)
and \(r_n=0\) (\(n\geq2\)), so
\((-1)^MM!\,t^Mc_{M,M}(t)=\prod_{j=0}^{M-1}(2-jt)\), which equals \(2^M\) at
\(t=0\); the \(i\)-product also equals \(2^M\) at \(t=0\), giving
\(P_M(0,0)=4^M\).

Now specialize to \(t_\star=-p'/p\) and \(\Delta=\mu\).  By the Kac determinant
formula, \(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) implies \(\det G_{M'}(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\)
for all \(M'\leq M\) and \(\mu\neq h_{u,1}(b_\star)\) for all \(1\leq u\leq M\),
so \(H_M(c(b),\Delta,h_{1,3}(b))\) is regular at \((b_\star,\mu)\).  Since
\(\ell\mid p'\) is odd and \(\ell\nmid p\), both \(t_\star=-p'/p\) and
\(Y_\star=\mu t_\star^2=p'(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4p^3)\) lie in \(\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\).
Hence \(P_M(t_\star,Y_\star)\equiv 4^M\not\equiv0\pmod{\ell\mathbb Z_\ell}\),
so \(v_\ell(P_M(t_\star,Y_\star))=0\) and \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,h_{1,3}(b_\star))\neq0\).
Since \(2\mu-2h_{u,1}(b_\star)=(m^2-(p'u-p)^2)/(2pp')\) has valuation
\(v_\ell(m^2-(p'u-p)^2)-v_\ell(p')\), taking \(v_\ell\) of the definition of
\(P_M\) gives \eqref{eq:h13-valuation}.  Finally, for any coprime
\(p,p'\geq2\), at least one of \(p,p'\) has an odd prime divisor; if \(p'\) is
a power of \(2\), the symmetry \(b\mapsto b^{-1}\) (swapping \(p\leftrightarrow p'\)
and \(h_{1,3}\leftrightarrow h_{3,1}\)) yields
\(H_M(c_\star,\mu,h_{3,1}(b_\star))\neq0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Order, valuation, and degree criteria on two-vector levels]
\label{prop:two-term-criteria}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, let
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\), let \(P_N(d)\) denote the right
side of \eqref{eq:top-layer}, and put \(M^\pm=N-\ell^\pm_k\),
\(d_x=(x^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), and \(x_0=|p'-3p|\) (so \(d_{x_0}=h_{1,3}(b_\star)\)).
Normalize each \(w^\pm_k\) to have \(L_{-1}^{\ell^\pm_k}\)-coefficient \(1\),
and write \(\tau^\pm_k(d)=\prod_x(d-d_x)^{m^\pm_k(x)}\) and
\(\tau^\pm_k=g_k\widetilde\tau^\pm_k\) with \(g_k=\gcd(\tau^+_k,\tau^-_k)\) in
\(\mathbb Q[d]\).  Let \(\ell\) be an odd prime dividing \(p'\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] One has \(\ord_{d=h_{1,3}(b_\star)}\tau^+_k\neq
\ord_{d=h_{1,3}(b_\star)}\tau^-_k\) if and only if \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\);
whenever this holds, \(P_N\not\equiv0\) and \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) has pole
order \(k\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
\item[(ii)] If \(2\leq s\leq p'-2\), then both vanishing orders equal \(k\).
Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_\ell^\pm(N):={}&\sum_{x\neq x_0}m^\pm_k(x)v_\ell(x_0^2-x^2)
 -\sum_{x\neq|p'-p|}m^\pm_k(x)v_\ell\bigl((p'-p)^2-x^2\bigr)\\
 &-v_\ell(M^\pm!)-M^\pm v_\ell(p')
 -\sum_{u=1}^{M^\pm}v_\ell\bigl((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'u-p)^2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(V_\ell^+(N)\neq V_\ell^-(N)\), then \([(d-h_{1,3}(b_\star))^k]P_N(d)\neq0\)
and \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) has pole order \(k\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
The dual statements of (i)--(ii) hold with \(p\leftrightarrow p'\),
\(r\leftrightarrow s\), and \(h_{1,3}\leftrightarrow h_{3,1}\) for every odd
prime \(\ell\mid p\).
\item[(iii)] If \(2+\deg\widetilde\tau^+_k>2M^-\) or
\(2+\deg\widetilde\tau^-_k>2M^+\), then \(P_N\not\equiv0\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) has pole order \(k\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere}, Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization},
and uniqueness of singular vectors of a given weight in a Verma module
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}, at \(b=b_\star\),
\(\tau^+_{2j+1}={\mathcal P}_{r+jp,\,s+jp'}\),
\(\tau^-_{2j+1}={\mathcal P}_{(j+1)p-r,\,(j+1)p'-s}\), and
\[
 \tau^+_{2j}={\mathcal P}_{r+(j-1)p,\,s+(j-1)p'}{\mathcal P}_{(2j-1)p+r,\,p'-s},\qquad
 \tau^-_{2j}={\mathcal P}_{r+(j-1)p,\,s+(j-1)p'}{\mathcal P}_{p-r,\,(2j-1)p'+s},
\]
where each \({\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d)
=\prod_{k,l}(d-d_{p'k-pl})(d-d_{p'k+pl})\) over odd \(1\leq k<2R\) and odd
\(1\leq l<2S\) is monic of degree \(2RS\).

(i) For \(p\geq2\), \(p'\geq3\) coprime, solving \(|p'k\pm pl|=|p'-3p|\) in
odd positive integers \((k,l)\): the plus sign gives \(p'(k+1)=p(3-l)\) (forcing
\(l=1\) and \(p'\mid2\), impossible) or \(p'(k-1)=-p(l+3)\) (impossible); the
minus sign gives \(p'(k-1)=p(l-3)\) or \(p'(k+1)=p(l+3)\).  Since \(k\pm1\) and
\(l\pm3\) are even and at least one of \(p,p'\) is odd, the solutions are
\((1+2mp,3+2mp')\) (\(m\geq0\)) and \((2mp-1,2mp'-3)\) (\(m\geq1\)).  Hence
the multiplicity of \(d_{x_0}\) in \({\mathcal P}_{R,S}\) is
\(\#\{m\geq0:mp\leq R-1,\,mp'\leq S-2\}+\#\{m\geq1:mp\leq R,\,mp'\leq S+1\}\).
Evaluating on \(\tau^\pm_k\) gives: for \(k=2j+1\), \(e_+=2j+\mathbf 1_{s\geq2}\)
and \(e_-=2j+\mathbf 1_{s\leq p'-2}\); for \(k=2j\), the common factor
contributes \(2j-1\) to both (or \(2j-2\) if \(s=1\)), while the second factors
contribute \(\mathbf 1_{s\leq p'-2}+\mathbf 1_{s=1}\) to \(e_+\) and \(1\) to
\(e_-\).  In both parities \(e_+\neq e_-\) iff \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\), and
\(e_+=e_-=k\) otherwise.  When \(e_+\neq e_-\), extracting
\([(d-h_{1,3})^{\min(e_+,e_-)}]\) in \eqref{eq:top-layer} kills the higher-order
branch and leaves a nonzero multiple of
\(H_{M^{\min}}(c_\star,\mu^{\min}_k,h_{1,3}(b_\star))\), which is nonzero by
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}.

(ii) When \(e_+=e_-=k\), Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} gives
\(\gamma^\pm_k=[d^k]\tau^\pm_k/(c_k(\pm\rho)^k)\).  Writing
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [(d-d_{x_0})^k]\tau^\pm_k&=(4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}\prod_{x\neq x_0}(x_0^2-x^2)^{m^\pm_k(x)},\\
 [d^k]\tau^\pm_k&=(-1)^{2\ell^\pm_k-k}(4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}
 \prod_{x\neq|p'-p|}\bigl((p'-p)^2-x^2\bigr)^{m^\pm_k(x)},
 \end{aligned}
\]
the factors \((4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}\) cancel in the ratio
\([(d-d_{x_0})^k](\tau^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k)\), and \(c_k(\pm\rho)^k\) has the
same \(\ell\)-adic valuation for both signs.  Combining with
\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}, the two summands of \([(d-h_{1,3})^k]P_N(d)\) have
\(\ell\)-adic valuations differing by \(V_\ell^+(N)-V_\ell^-(N)\); if this is
nonzero their sum cannot vanish.

(iii) By Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients}, \(\ord_{d=0}\tau^\pm_k
=\ord_{d=1}\tau^\pm_k=k\), so \(d(d-1)\) divides \(g_k\) and is coprime to
\(\widetilde\tau^\pm_k\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, \(d(d-1)\) divides
\(H_{M^\pm}\).  Thus \(P_N\equiv0\) implies
\(d(d-1)\widetilde\tau^+_k\mid H_{M^-}\) and
\(d(d-1)\widetilde\tau^-_k\mid H_{M^+}\).  In the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}, \(\deg_d\rho_M(Y,Y')\leq\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')\leq2M\),
so \(\deg_d H_M\leq2M\); since \(H_{M^\pm}\not\equiv0\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}), \(2+\deg\widetilde\tau^+_k>2M^-\) or
\(2+\deg\widetilde\tau^-_k>2M^+\) gives a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section{All-order confluent recursion}\label{sec:all-order}

\begin{definition}[Shifted Laurent recursion]\label{def:shifted-laurent}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), put \(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and
\(t=b-b_\star\), and work in \(K_\star((t))\) with
\(K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\), \(d\) indeterminate.
For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), write \(\ell_a=rs\),
\(h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\), \(R_a(t)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b_\star+t,d)\), and
\(z_a(t)=h_a(t)+\ell_a\).
Define shifted Laurent coefficients recursively by \(S_{a,0}(t)=1\) and, for
\(M\geq1\),
\[
 S_{a,M}(t)
 =\sum_{\substack{g=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_g=uv\leq M}}
 \frac{R_g(t)}
 {z_a(t)-h_g(t)}S_{g,M-\ell_g}(t).
\]
Every denominator is a nonzero rational function of \(b\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}, so each summand has a well-defined
Laurent expansion, and the recursion terminates because its second index
strictly decreases.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A priori shifted valuation bound]
\label{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}
For positive labels \(a,g\), set \(\pi_g=\max\{0,-\ord_tR_g(t)\}\) and
\(\sigma_{a,g}=\max\{0,\ord_t(z_a(t)-h_g(t))\}\).  Define nonnegative integers
\(W_{a,M}\) recursively by \(W_{a,0}=0\) and, for \(M\geq1\),
\(W_{a,M}=\max(\{0\}\cup\{\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}+W_{g,M-\ell_g}:\ell_g\leq M\})\).
Then \(\ord_t S_{a,M}(t)\geq-W_{a,M}\).  Thus \(W_{a,M}\) is an a priori pole
bound computed solely from the finite recursion tree and the valuations of its
explicit residue and denominator factors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The assertion is clear for \(M=0\).  If it holds at lower levels, the
\(g\)-summand in Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} has valuation at least
\(-\pi_g-\sigma_{a,g}-W_{g,M-\ell_g}\).
The valuation of a finite sum is at least the minimum of the summand
valuations.  All sets involved are finite because \(\ell_g\leq M\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Collision moments]\label{def:collision-moments}
For \(N\geq1\), \(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\), and
\(a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), put
\(B_{a,N}(t)=R_a(t)S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\),
\(\delta_a(t)=h_a(t)-\lambda\in tK_\star[[t]]\),
\(\varepsilon_a=\ord_t\delta_a\geq1\) (\(\varepsilon_a=\infty\) if
\(\delta_a\equiv0\)), and \(\widehat v_{a,N}=\pi_a+W_{a,N-\ell_a}\), so that
\(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}.  Define the a priori pole bound
\(\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}=\max_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
(1+\lfloor\widehat v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor)\) (with \(1\) when
\(\varepsilon_a=\infty\)) and, for \(k\geq1\), the \emph{collision moments}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-moments}
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 =\operatorname{CT}_{t}
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1},
\end{equation}
where \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) extracts the coefficient of \(t^0\).  For
\(k>\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) every summand has positive valuation,
since \((k-1)\varepsilon_a>\widehat v_{a,N}\), so
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Backward Laurent-precision budget]
\label{lem:precision-budget}
Suppose \(S_{a,M}\) is required through \(t^U\).  For its \(g\)-summand it
suffices to know
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-propagation}
 \frac{R_g}{z_a-h_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+W_{g,M-\ell_g}},
 \qquad
 S_{g,M-\ell_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}}.
\end{equation}
Iterating these requests down the strictly level-decreasing recursion tree
produces a finite a priori upper precision at every node.  In particular,
\(R_a\) through \(t^{W_{a,N-\ell_a}}\), \(S_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through \(t^{\pi_a}\),
and \(\delta_a^{\,k-1}\) through \(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\) suffice for every
collision moment at level \(N\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first factor has valuation at least \(-\pi_g-\sigma_{a,g}\), and the
second at least \(-W_{g,M-\ell_g}\).  In a product coefficient of degree
at most \(U\), the first bound means that no coefficient of the second
factor above degree \(U+\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}\) can occur; the second means
that no coefficient of the first factor above degree
\(U+W_{g,M-\ell_g}\) can occur.  This proves
\eqref{eq:precision-propagation}.  The recursion tree is finite because the
remaining level decreases along every edge.
For \(B_{a,N}=R_aS_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through degree zero, the same argument
gives the stated precisions, and \(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\)
shows that only coefficients of \(\delta_a^{k-1}\) through degree
\(\widehat v_{a,N}\) can enter a constant term.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise pole support of a collision germ]
\label{lem:collision-pole-support}
Let \(B(t)\in K_\star((t))\) and
\(\delta(t)\in tK_\star[[t]]\).  Every coefficient of
\(B(t)/(x-\delta(t))=\sum_{j\geq0}B(t)\delta(t)^jx^{-j-1}\), as a Laurent
series in \(t\), is a finite \(K_\star\)-linear combination of
\(x^{-1},x^{-2},\ldots\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\ord_tB\geq-v\), the term \(B\delta^j\) has valuation at least
\(j-v\), so only \(j\leq m+v\) contribute to the coefficient of \(t^m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}]
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For generic \(b\), Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}
and induction on \(M\) give \(S_{a,M}(t)=H_M(c(b),z_a(t),d)\) as rational
functions of \(b\), and grouping by limiting weights yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-grouped}
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}{\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N},\qquad
 {\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-h_a(t)},
\end{equation}
as rational functions of \((b,\Delta,d)\).  Expand both sides in
\(K_\star(\Delta)((t))\).  For each \(\lambda\), \(x=\Delta-\lambda\) is
nonzero in \(K_\star(\Delta)\), and \(1/(\Delta-h_a(t))=1/(x-\delta_a(t))\)
expands by Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and Cramer's rule, the left side of
\eqref{eq:generic-grouped} has denominator \(\det G_N(c(b_\star+t),\Delta)\),
whose value at \(t=0\) is \(\det G_N(c_\star,\Delta)\neq0\); hence it lies in
\(K_\star(\Delta)[[t]]\) with constant term \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support} and Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments},
the constant term of \({\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is
\(g_\lambda(\Delta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)(\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\), so
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}g_\lambda(\Delta)\).
Since the points \(\lambda\) are distinct, uniqueness of partial fractions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions} gives
\(g_\lambda=\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\), i.e.\
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\) for all
\(k\geq1\), \(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), and
\(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\).  The finite-jet
statements are Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-budget}, and local finiteness follows
from finiteness of \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).

If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
shows that every class is a singleton, every \(R_g\) is regular at \(t=0\), and
no shifted denominator vanishes there; hence \(W_{a,M}=0\),
\(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}=1\), \(S_{a,M}(0)=H_M(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\),
and the sole moment is \(R_a(0)H_{N-\ell_a}(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Classwise regularity]\label{cor:class-regular}
Every \({\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) lies in \(K_\star(\Delta)[[t]]\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(m<0\), the coefficient of \(t^m\) on the left of
\eqref{eq:generic-grouped} vanishes.  On the right it is a sum over
\(\lambda\) of polynomials in \((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\) without constant
term (Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}).  Uniqueness of partial
fractions forces each of them to vanish.
\end{proof}

The two embedding diamonds of Section~\ref{sec:ising} are instances of
the following closure formula.  Dots denote \(d/dt\) at \(t=0\), that is, \(d/db\) at
\(b_\star\).

\begin{proposition}[Closure of a two-branch collision]
\label{prop:two-branch}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), \(N\geq1\), and
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) with
\({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}=\{\kappa_1,\kappa_2\}\).  For \(i=1,2\) put
\(M_i=N-\ell_{\kappa_i}\) and \(z_i=z_{\kappa_i}(0)\), and suppose that
there is a label \(\tau_i\) with \(\ell_{\tau_i}=M_i\) such that:
(i)~\(\tau_i\) is the only label \(g\) with \(\ell_g\leq M_i\) and
\(h_g(0)=z_i\); (ii)~\(R_{\kappa_i}\) and \(R_{\tau_i}\) are regular at
\(t=0\); (iii)~\(\zeta_i(t):=z_{\kappa_i}(t)-h_{\tau_i}(t)\) has a simple zero
at \(t=0\).  Define
\(Q_i(d)=\partial_z\{(z-z_i)H_{M_i}(c_\star,z,d)\}|_{z=z_i}\) and
\(T_i(d)=(\dot R_{\kappa_i}R_{\tau_i}+R_{\kappa_i}\dot R_{\tau_i})/\dot\zeta_i
-R_{\kappa_i}R_{\tau_i}\ddot\zeta_i/(2\dot\zeta_i^2)\), evaluated at \(t=0\).
Then
\begin{gather}
 \frac{R_{\kappa_1}R_{\tau_1}}{\dot\zeta_1}
 +\frac{R_{\kappa_2}R_{\tau_2}}{\dot\zeta_2}=0,\label{eq:two-branch-cancel}\\
 \PP_{\Delta=\lambda}H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\frac{(\dot h_{\kappa_1}-\dot h_{\kappa_2})R_{\kappa_1}R_{\tau_1}}
       {\dot\zeta_1\,(\Delta-\lambda)^2}
 +\frac{R_{\kappa_1}Q_1+R_{\kappa_2}Q_2+T_1+T_2}{\Delta-\lambda}.\label{eq:two-branch-pp}
\end{gather}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(i\).  By (i), near \((b,\Delta)=(b_\star,z_i)\) the only factor of
\(\det G_{M_i}(c(b),\Delta)\) that vanishes is
\(\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b)\), with exponent \(p(M_i-\ell_{\tau_i})=1\).
Hence \(U(b,\Delta)=(\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b))H_{M_i}(c(b),\Delta,d)\) is a
rational function regular at \((b_\star,z_i)\).  For generic \(b\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} at level \(M_i\) gives
\(U(b,h_{\tau_i}(b))=R_{\tau_i}(b)\).  Therefore
\[
 V(b,\Delta)=H_{M_i}(c(b),\Delta,d)-\frac{R_{\tau_i}(b)}{\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b)}
 =\frac{U(b,\Delta)-U(b,h_{\tau_i}(b))}{\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b)}
\]
is regular at \((b_\star,z_i)\), and \(V(b_\star,z_i)=Q_i\) because
\((z-z_i)H_{M_i}(c_\star,z,d)=R_{\tau_i}(0)+(z-z_i)V(b_\star,z)\).
Restricting to \(\Delta=z_{\kappa_i}(t)\) gives
\(S_{\kappa_i,M_i}(t)=R_{\tau_i}(t)/\zeta_i(t)+Q_i+O(t)\).  Expanding \(f_i=R_{\kappa_i}R_{\tau_i}=f_i(0)+\dot f_it+O(t^2)\) and
\(\zeta_i=\dot\zeta_it+\tfrac12\ddot\zeta_it^2+O(t^3)\), one finds
\(B_{\kappa_i,N}(t)=f_i(0)/(\dot\zeta_i\,t)+R_{\kappa_i}(0)Q_i+T_i+O(t)\).
The coefficient of \(t^{-1}(\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\) in
\({\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is \(\sum_if_i(0)/\dot\zeta_i\), because
\(B_{\kappa_i,N}\delta_{\kappa_i}^j\) has nonnegative valuation for
\(j\geq1\); Corollary~\ref{cor:class-regular} gives
\eqref{eq:two-branch-cancel}.  Since
\(\delta_{\kappa_i}=\dot h_{\kappa_i}t+O(t^2)\), the moments are
\[
 \widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,1,N}=\sum_i\bigl(R_{\kappa_i}(0)Q_i+T_i\bigr),
 \qquad
 \widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,2,N}
 =\sum_i\frac{\dot h_{\kappa_i}f_i(0)}{\dot\zeta_i}
 =\frac{(\dot h_{\kappa_1}-\dot h_{\kappa_2})f_1(0)}{\dot\zeta_1},
\]
the last step by \eqref{eq:two-branch-cancel}, and
\(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}=0\) for \(k\geq3\), because
\(B_{\kappa_i,N}\delta_{\kappa_i}^{k-1}\) then has positive valuation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} identifies these moments with the
principal part.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Second Jantzen norms]\label{prop:ising-jantzen-norms}
The second Jantzen layers at \(\tfrac12\) and \(\tfrac1{16}\) begin with the

\begin{proof}
The compositions are nonzero singular vectors of levels \(7\) and \(10\)
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011} with
\(L_{-1}^\ell\)-coefficient \(1\), hence equal
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}, and they span \(J^2\) there
(Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} and~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere}
give the torus norms, and Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:level-seven-principal-part}
and~\ref{thm:level-ten-principal-part} gives the norms \(\gamma\).  An exact
evaluation of Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(iii) in the PBW bases gives
the same values.
\end{proof}




\section{Scope and outlook}\label{sec:scope}

The Verma block at a degenerate weight is not an irreducible
minimal-model trace (it equals \(1\) at \(d=0\)).  Open directions include
attainment of \(j_N(\lambda)\) at every level
(Remark~\ref{rem:top-layer-beyond}), the weights of
Remark~\ref{rem:jantzen-other} and \(c_\star=1,25\), intrinsic formulas for
the lower Laurent coefficients, and irreducible minimal-model traces.
