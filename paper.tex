\begin{abstract}
Fix a nonzero complex Coulomb-gas parameter \(b_\star\).  We prove an
exact, finite confluence formula for every coefficient of the
eta-reduced torus one-point Virasoro block at
\(c_\star=1+6(b_\star+b_\star^{-1})^2\).  Generic Kac-recursion terms
are grouped by their common limiting internal weight; such collisions,
and poles of the generic residues, occur only when \(b_\star^2\) is
rational.  Each coefficient of the resulting fixed-central-charge
principal part is a collision moment: the constant term of a finite sum
of Laurent germs in \(t=b-b_\star\).  A valuation majorant and a
backward precision lemma give an a priori finite jet budget, and
uniqueness of partial fractions identifies the construction
coefficientwise in \(q\) with the inverse-Shapovalov definition of the
meromorphic Verma block; every collision class is then individually
regular.  The formal germ is an exact algebraic device, not a numerical
regularization or an intrinsic recursion in the specialized
representation category.  For \(c_\star\neq1,25\) the local Smith
exponents of the Shapovalov matrix in the internal weight are the
dimensions of the Jantzen layers of the Feigin--Fuchs embedding diagram;
at minimal-model charges this bounds the pole order of every block
coefficient explicitly.  At the Ising charge we classify the collision
classes and obtain the first two embedding diamonds from one two-branch
closure formula, including the first double pole at level seven and the
level-ten spin example.  Ancillary scripts replay the finite
computations in exact rational arithmetic.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace Virasoro algebra; conformal blocks; torus
one-point block; Zamolodchikov recursion; minimal models; Ising model.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{MSC2020 Classification.}\enspace
17B68; 17B10; 81T40.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Residue recursions for Virasoro blocks originate with Zamolodchikov's
recursive representations of the sphere four-point block
\cite{Zamolodchikov1984,Zamolodchikov1987}; see
\cite{ChoCollierYin2017} for a modern general treatment.  For generic
central charge, the eta-reduced torus one-point Virasoro block obeys the
residue recursion conjectured by Poghossian
\cite{Poghossian2009} and derived in
\cite[Section~3, equations~(7)--(9)]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}.
Poghosyan and Poghosyan recently reviewed the Liouville and
\(\mathcal W_3\) torus one-point recursions and derived their light-limit
forms \cite{PoghosyanPoghosyan2026}.  Their regime is \(b\to0\), hence
\(c\to\infty\), with fixed light dimensions; it is complementary to the
present problem of exact specialization at a fixed \(b_\star\), which is
nontrivial exactly when \(b_\star^2\) is rational.
At rational values of \(b^2\), distinct Kac labels have
the same degenerate weight and the individual generic-recursion summands
develop parameter poles.  Javerzat, Santachiara, and Foda analyzed the
cancellation of these non-physical poles in examples and in the
identity-insertion character problem
\cite[Sections~4--6, especially equations~(60)--(61)]%
{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.  Stocco subsequently constructed
central-charge-pole-free torus expressions through fourth order and proposed
an all-order Laurent-expansion procedure, whose cancellation and algebraic
form were left conjectural
\cite[Sections~II.2--II.3 and Appendix~A]{Stocco2022}.  Ribault identifies
a recursion when the squared Coulomb-gas parameter \(b^2\) is rational as
an open problem and singles out
torus one-point Virasoro blocks as its simplest incarnation
\cite[Section~4.3.3]{Ribault2026}.
Higher-order internal-weight poles and rational limits of sphere four-point
blocks were also analyzed by Ribault \cite[Section~3]{Ribault2019}; those
results provide important precedent for the confluence mechanism, although
they do not give a finite torus one-point recursion.

To the author's knowledge, this is the first all-order coefficientwise
construction at every fixed nonzero value \(b_\star\) of the
Coulomb-gas parameter.  The object
is the meromorphic Verma-module block, and the conclusion is coefficientwise
in \(q\).  At every fixed level we partition the generic Kac summands by their
common limiting weight at \(b=b_\star\).  Every coefficient of the
internal-weight principal part of a collision class is a finite constant
term of finite Laurent sums, and each class has a regular sum.  The
shifted recursion decreases the remaining level, while a recursive
valuation majorant supplies an a priori finite jet budget.  Collisions
and poles of the generic residues occur only when \(b_\star^2\) is
rational (Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}); for every other
\(b_\star\) the construction reduces to the generic recursion evaluated
at \(b_\star\).

The construction removes the divergent, non-physical poles in the
deformation parameter; the genuine Laurent poles in the internal weight
\(\Delta\) remain.  It uses the formal germ \(b=b_\star+t\) to combine
generic summands before taking a constant term.  Thus it gives an exact
formula at fixed \(c_\star\), with no numerical \(\varepsilon\)-limit, but it
is not an intrinsic recursion formulated solely in the specialized
representation category.  Nor does it by itself construct irreducible
minimal-model traces at degenerate internal weights.

In the notation of
Sections~\ref{sec:partial-fractions}--\ref{sec:all-order}, the main
result reads: for every level \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 &=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},\\
 A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 &=\operatorname{CT}_t
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 R_a(t)\,S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\,
 \bigl(h_a(t)-\lambda\bigr)^{k-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(H_N\) is the \(N\)-th eta-reduced block coefficient,
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) is the finite set of limiting Kac weights,
\({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is the finite class of labels
\(a=(r,s)\) with \(rs\leq N\) that collide at \(\lambda\),
\(t=b-b_\star\) is the exact Coulomb-gas germ, and \(R_a\) is the generic
residue coefficient of the label \(a\),
\(S_{a,M}\) is the shifted Laurent block of
Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}, \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) extracts
the constant term of a Laurent germ, and \(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is
the a priori pole bound of Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments}.  Every
ingredient is a finite jet of an explicit rational function of \(b\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} proves this identity: the
constant-term moments on the right are exactly the partial-fraction
coefficients of the inverse-Shapovalov block, at every power of
\(q\).  The coefficients lie in \(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\), hence in
\(\mathbb Q[d]\) whenever \(b_\star^2\) is rational.  At minimal-model
values the collision classes are the elementary Diophantine classes of
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:ising-confluence} records the Ising specialization.

The pole orders themselves are governed by the representation theory of
the specialized Verma module.  For \(c_\star\neq1,25\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} identifies the local Smith exponents of
the Shapovalov matrix in the internal weight with the dimensions of the
Jantzen layers, which the Feigin--Fuchs structure theorem describes
\cite{IoharaKoga2011,Astashkevich1997}.  At a minimal-model charge and an
interior Kac weight this gives an explicit bound \(j_N(\lambda)\) on the
pole order of every block coefficient: the number of embedding-diagram
layers that have reached level \(N\).

The local calculations exhibit how the algebraic confluence records
Virasoro embedding geometry.  The earliest collision is the transverse
energy-module collision at level three; its two branches first meet at level
seven and create the first double pole.  The spin-module collision at level
four closes its diamond at level ten.  Both closures are instances of one
two-branch formula (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-branch}), which expresses the
full principal part through two lower finite parts and an explicit
derivative term.  The fixed-\(c\) formulas are
Theorems~\ref{thm:first-recursive-residue},
\ref{thm:level-seven-principal-part},
\ref{thm:spin-recursive-residue}, and
\ref{thm:level-ten-principal-part}.  Finally,
Theorem~\ref{thm:ising-characters} distinguishes the Verma identity
specialization from the irreducible quotient
(Proposition~\ref{prop:identity-verma}) and records the three standard
Ising characters as a separate BGG consistency check.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:blocks} defines the block and its eta reduction and
collects notation in Remark~\ref{rem:notation}.
Section~\ref{sec:resonance} classifies the minimal-model collision classes
and records the Ising specialization.
Section~\ref{sec:partial-fractions} proves the finite coefficientwise
partial-fraction expansion and states the main theorem and its Ising
corollary before the examples.
Section~\ref{sec:smith} develops the
local Smith and crossing-form toolkit, identifies the Smith layers with
the Jantzen filtration, and computes the transverse
collisions at levels three and four.
Section~\ref{sec:generic-data} recalls the generic residue data and
proves the generic torus recursion.
Section~\ref{sec:all-order} proves the general finite confluence theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}) and the two-branch closure
formula.
Sections~\ref{sec:energy-diamond} and~\ref{sec:spin-diamond} work out
the two embedding diamonds---the energy diamond with its first double
pole at level seven, and the spin diamond at level ten;
Table~\ref{tab:local-summary} summarizes them.
Section~\ref{sec:low-level-output} summarizes the output through level four
and compares it with the expressions of Stocco; the expanded polynomials
are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:expanded-data}.
Section~\ref{sec:irreducible} treats the irreducible specialization and
the Ising characters; and Section~\ref{sec:scope} discusses scope and
open directions.

The general confluence theorem is proved independently of computer
calculation.  The explicit level-seven and level-ten Gram, crossing-form,
and polynomial evaluations are computer-assisted finite calculations in
exact rational arithmetic; no calculation uses floating-point arithmetic.
Remark~\ref{rem:audit-scope} records the scope and independence of the
ancillary replays.

\section{Blocks and the reduced series}\label{sec:blocks}

\begin{definition}[Virasoro Verma module]
The Virasoro algebra has generators \(L_n\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), and central
element \(c\), with
\[
 [L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}
 +\frac{c}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}.
\]
The Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) is generated by a vector
\(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) such that
\[
 L_0\lvert\Delta\rangle=\Delta\lvert\Delta\rangle,\qquad
 L_n\lvert\Delta\rangle=0\quad(n>0).
\]
The central element acts on \(M(c,\Delta)\) as multiplication by the scalar
\(c\).
For a partition \(Y=(y_1\geq\cdots\geq y_\ell)\), set
\(L_{-Y}=L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_\ell}\), \(|Y|=\sum_i y_i\), and
\(\ell(Y)=\ell\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shapovalov and torus matrices]\label{def:matrices}
At level \(N\), let
\[
 G_N(c,\Delta)_{Y,Y'}
 =\langle\Delta|L_YL_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle ,
 \qquad |Y|=|Y'|=N,
\]
where \(L_Y=L_{y_\ell}\cdots L_{y_1}\).
For a primary field \(V_d(z)\) normalized by
\(\langle\Delta|V_d(z)|\Delta\rangle=z^{-d}\), let
\[
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}
 =\langle\Delta|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle .
\]
Whenever \(G_N\) is invertible, define
\[
 F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{|Y|=|Y'|=N}
 \bigl(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1}\bigr)_{Y,Y'}
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'} .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced block]\label{def:eta-reduced}
Write
\[
 \mathcal F_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)
 =q^{\Delta-c/24}\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N
\]
and define
\[
 H_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)
 =\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)
   \sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N
 =\sum_{N\geq0}H_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N .
\]
Writing \(\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)=\sum_{j\geq0}\eta_jq^j\), so that
\(\eta_0=1\), \(\eta_1=\eta_2=-1\), and \(\eta_3=\eta_4=0\), one has
\(H_N=\sum_{j=0}^N\eta_jF_{N-j}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial matrix entries]\label{lem:polynomial-entries}
For fixed \(N\), every entry of \(G_N(c,\Delta)\) belongs to
\(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta]\), and every entry of
\(\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,d]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Repeated use of the Virasoro commutator moves every positive mode in
\(L_Y\) to the right.  Positive modes annihilate
\(\lvert\Delta\rangle\), while each \(L_0\) and the central element act by
\(\Delta\) and \(c\), respectively.  This gives the first assertion.

For the second assertion use, in addition,
\[
 [L_n,V_d(z)]
 =z^n\bigl(z\partial_z+(n+1)d\bigr)V_d(z).
\]
Move positive modes through \(V_d(z)\), then move the remaining negative
modes through \(V_d(z)\) toward the bra.  The process terminates because
each commutation removes one mode from one side of the insertion.
The remaining derivatives act on \(z^{-d}\); after setting \(z=1\), their
values are polynomials in \(d\).  The other commutators contribute only
polynomials in \(c\) and \(\Delta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terminology and notation]\label{rem:notation}
The \(L_0\)-offset \(N\) of a graded component is called its
\emph{level}.  For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), we write
\(\ell_a=rs\) for the level of its singular vector.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and
Cramer's rule, each \(F_N\) and \(H_N\) is a rational function of
\((c,\Delta)\) and a polynomial in \(d\); note \(H_0=1\).  For a rational
function \(f\) of \(\Delta\) and a point \(\lambda\), we write
\(\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) for the principal part of \(f\) at \(\lambda\),
that is, the sum of the negative-power terms of its Laurent expansion,
and \(\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) for the coefficient of
\((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\).
Unless \(d\) is explicitly specialized, it is an indeterminate and pole
orders in \(\Delta\) are understood over \(\mathbb Q(c,d)\), or over
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\) after a fixed algebraic specialization.
Specializing \(d\) can annihilate a principal-part coefficient and thereby
lower the pole order or make a candidate pole removable.
\end{remark}

\section{Algebraic specializations and Ising resonance classes}
\label{sec:resonance}

\begin{definition}[Kac weights and fixed algebraic specializations]
\label{def:fixed-specialization}
For \(b\in\mathbb C^\times\), set
\[
 c(b)=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2,
 \qquad
 h_{r,s}(b)
 =\frac{(b+b^{-1})^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2}{4}.
\]
Thus \(h_{r,s}(b)=\Delta_{r,s}(c(b))\) is the standard Kac
degenerate weight; both \(c(b)\) and \(h_{r,s}(b)\) depend on \(b\) only
through \(b^2\).  Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), and put
\[
 c_\star=c(b_\star),\qquad
 \Lambda_N(b_\star)
 =\{h_{r,s}(b_\star):r,s>0,\ rs\leq N\}.
\]
For \(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\), define the finite collision class
\[
 {\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2:
      rs\leq N,\ h_{r,s}(b_\star)=\lambda\}.
\]

For the Ising specialization, write
\[
 b_0=\frac{2i}{\sqrt3},\qquad c(b_0)=\frac12.
\]
For \(r,s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), define
\[
 \Delta_{r,s}
 =\frac{(4r-3s)^2-1}{48}.
\]
Thus \(\Delta_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b_0)\).  Set
\[
 \lambda_n=\frac{n^2-1}{48}\qquad(n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal-model collision classes]
\label{prop:minimal-collisions}
Let \(p,p'\) be coprime positive integers and choose
\(b_\star^2=-p'/p\).  Then
\[
 c_\star=1-\frac{6(p'-p)^2}{pp'},\qquad
 h_{r,s}(b_\star)
 =\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}.
\]
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\), their specialized weights
are equal if and only if, for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 (r',s')=(r+p\ell,s+p'\ell)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (r',s')=(p\ell-r,p'\ell-s).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formulas follow by substituting \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) in
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.  Equality of the two weights is
equivalent to
\[
 p'r'-ps'=\pm(p'r-ps).
\]
For the plus sign, coprimality gives
\((r'-r,s'-s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\); for the minus sign it gives
\((r'+r,s'+s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\).  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ising collision classes]\label{cor:ising-classes}
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\),
\(\Delta_{r,s}=\Delta_{r',s'}\) if and only if, for some
\(\ell\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 (r',s')=(r+3\ell,s+4\ell)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (r',s')=(3\ell-r,4\ell-s).
\]
Moreover \(\Delta_{r,s}=\lambda_{|4r-3s|}\), the set of distinct
positive-label degenerate weights at \(c=1/2\) is
\(\{\lambda_n:n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\}\), every collision class contains
infinitely many positive pairs, and for each fixed level \(N\) only
finitely many classes contain a pair with \(rs\leq N\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since \(b_0^2=-4/3\), the first assertion is
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions} with \((p,p')=(3,4)\), and
\(\Delta_{r,s}=\lambda_{|4r-3s|}\) is its weight formula.  Given
\(n\geq0\), choose a sufficiently large positive \(r\equiv n\pmod 3\);
then \(s=(4r-n)/3\) is a positive integer and \(4r-3s=n\).  The pairs
\((r+3\ell,s+4\ell)\), \(\ell\geq0\), stay positive and in the same class.
Finally, \(rs\leq N\) implies \(r,s\leq N\).
\end{proof}

\section{Coefficientwise partial fractions}\label{sec:partial-fractions}

\begin{lemma}[Large internal weight]\label{lem:large-weight}
For fixed \(c,d,N\),
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to\infty}F_N(c,\Delta,d)=p(N),
\]
where \(p(N)\) is the number of partitions of \(N\).  Consequently,
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to\infty}H_N(c,\Delta,d)=\delta_{N,0}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a partition \(Y\), put \(a_Y=\ell(Y)\), and rescale the PBW vector
\(L_{-Y}\lvert\Delta\rangle\) by \(\Delta^{-a_Y/2}\).
Commuting positive modes to the right shows that
\[
 \deg_\Delta G_N(Y,Y')\leq\min(a_Y,a_{Y'}).
\]
If \(a_Y=a_{Y'}\) and \(Y\neq Y'\), the degree is at most \(a_Y-1\).
For \(Y=Y'\), the leading term is
\[
 \left(\prod_{j\geq1}m_j(Y)!(2j)^{m_j(Y)}\right)
 \Delta^{a_Y},
\]
where \(m_j(Y)\) is the multiplicity of \(j\) in \(Y\).
Indeed, in a fully commuted matrix element every factor of \(\Delta\)
arises from an occurrence of \(L_0\), that is, from a contraction of a
positive mode of \(L_Y\) against a negative mode of \(L_{-Y'}\) of the
same index through the \(2jL_0\) term of \([L_j,L_{-j}]\).  Each
contraction consumes one mode on each side, so a single term carries at
most \(\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\) factors of \(\Delta\); this proves the degree
bound.  If \(a_Y=a_{Y'}\) and the degree is attained, every mode of
\(Y\) is contracted against a mode of \(Y'\) of equal index, which
forces \(Y=Y'\); hence off-diagonal entries of equal length have degree
at most \(a_Y-1\).  For \(Y=Y'\), the top-degree terms are the perfect
matchings of equal indices: the \(m_j(Y)!\) matchings of the modes of
index \(j\) each contribute \((2j\Delta)^{m_j(Y)}\) to leading order,
giving the displayed leading term.

Apply the same counting with one \(V_d\) insertion.  The number of
\(\Delta\)-factors of a term is again the number of positive--negative
contractions producing \(L_0\).  A term that uses no Ward commutator
\([L_n,V_d(z)]\) reproduces the corresponding Shapovalov term exactly,
because \(\langle\Delta|V_d(1)|\Delta\rangle=1\); a term that uses at
least one Ward commutator has consumed a mode without producing
\(L_0\), so its degree is strictly below \(\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\).
Indeed, since \(Y\) and \(Y'\) have the same total level, attaining
\(\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\) contractions would match every mode on the shorter
side with an equal-index mode on the other side.  Those matched indices
already sum to the full level, leaving no positive-index mode available
for a Ward commutator.
It follows that, after the above rescaling, both \(G_N\) and \(\rho_N\)
converge to the same invertible diagonal matrix \(D_N\).
Therefore
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to\infty}F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =\operatorname{tr}(D_N^{-1}D_N)=p(N).
\]
Since \(H_N=\sum_{j=0}^N\eta_jF_{N-j}\), the generating-function
identity
\[
 \left(\sum_{j\geq0}\eta_jq^j\right)
 \left(\sum_{k\geq0}p(k)q^k\right)=1
\]
gives the second limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite pole expansion at a fixed specialization]
\label{thm:general-partial-fractions}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\).  Then \(H_0=1\),
and for every \(N\geq1\) there are unique coefficients
\[
 A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d],
 \qquad \lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star),\quad k\geq1,
\]
only finitely many of them nonzero, such that
\[
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}\ \sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\]
If at least one \(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\) is nonzero, the largest such
\(k\) is the multiplicity \(m^\star_{\lambda,N}\) of the pole of \(H_N\)
at \(\lambda\); if they all vanish, \(\lambda\) is a removable candidate
pole and we put \(m^\star_{\lambda,N}=0\).  Consequently, with
\[
 A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)
 =\sum_{N\geq1}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)q^N,
\]
one has the coefficientwise locally finite expansion
\[
 H_\Delta^{(c_\star)}(d\mid q)
 =1+\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},
\]
where at each power of \(q\) the first sum is over the corresponding
finite set \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries},
\(F_N=\operatorname{tr}(G_N^{-1}\rho_N^{\mathsf T})\) is rational in
\(\Delta\), and its poles are contained in the zero locus of
\(\det G_N\).  The Kac determinant formula
\cite{Kac1979,FeiginFuchs1982}, in the form of
\cite[Theorem~4.2 and equations (4.16)--(4.21)]{IoharaKoga2011}, gives,
up to a nonzero factor independent of \(\Delta\),
\[
 \det G_N(c,\Delta)
 =C_N\prod_{\substack{r,s>0\\rs\leq N}}
   \bigl(\Delta-\Delta_{r,s}(c)\bigr)^{p(N-rs)}.
\]
At \(c=c_\star\), the distinct zeros belong to the finite set
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).  Since \(H_N\) is a finite integer linear
combination of \(F_0,\ldots,F_N\), it has the same pole containment.
Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} shows that \(H_N\) tends to zero at infinity
for \(N\geq1\), so its partial-fraction expansion has no polynomial part
and is unique.

The field \(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)\) contains \(c_\star\) and every pole
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\).  The matrix entries belong to
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[\Delta,d]\), and \(\det G_N\) does not involve
\(d\).  Laurent expansion at each pole therefore gives
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\).
Coefficientwise local finiteness follows because \(rs\leq N\) leaves
only finitely many labels.
\end{proof}

The conceptual result can now be stated independently of the Ising
calculations.  Its collision moments and finite precision budget are defined
in Section~\ref{sec:all-order}, after the generic residue data; the proof is
given there as well.

\begin{theorem}[Finite algebraic confluence at fixed \(b_\star\)]
\label{thm:general-confluence}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\).  For every
\(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}
        {(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\end{equation}
The sums and every constant-term calculation are finite,
and the level-decreasing recursion of
Definitions~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}--\ref{def:collision-moments}
terminates.  Its backward precision rule supplies a finite truncation order
for every intermediate Laurent germ.  Moreover, for every \(k\geq1\),
\[
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}
 =A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d],\qquad
 m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}.
\]
Consequently the resulting locally finite series agrees coefficient by
coefficient in \(q\) with the inverse-Shapovalov definition of the
meromorphic Verma-module torus one-point block.  If
\(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), every collision class consists of a single
label, every \(\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) equals \(1\), and
\eqref{eq:general-pole-free-recursion} is the generic recursion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} evaluated at \(b=b_\star\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Ising specialization]\label{cor:ising-confluence}
Let \(b_\star=b_0\), so \(c_\star=1/2\), and write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 {\mathcal C}_{n,N}={\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},\quad
 \widehat M_{n,N}=\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},\quad
 m_{n,N}=m^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},\\
 A_{n,k,N}=A^{\star}_{\lambda_n,k,N},\quad
 \widetilde A_{n,k,N}=\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda_n,k,N}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:ising-classes}, \({\mathcal C}_{n,N}\) is the set of
positive labels with \(|4r-3s|=n\) and \(rs\leq N\).  For every \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{n\geq0\\{\mathcal C}_{n,N}\neq\varnothing}}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M_{n,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A_{n,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k},
 \qquad
 \widetilde A_{n,k,N}=A_{n,k,N}\in\mathbb Q[d],
\end{equation}
and \(m_{n,N}\leq\widehat M_{n,N}\) is the exact multiplicity of the pole
of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda_n\).  With
\(A_{n,k}(d;q)=\sum_{N\geq1}A_{n,k,N}(d)q^N\),
\[
 H_\Delta^{(1/2)}(d\mid q)
 =1+\sum_{n\geq0}\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A_{n,k}(d;q)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k}
\]
coefficient by coefficient in \(q\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} at \(b_\star=b_0\); the
coefficient field is \(\mathbb Q(b_0^2)=\mathbb Q\).
\end{proof}

\section{Local Smith exponents and transverse collisions}\label{sec:smith}

\begin{lemma}[Pole order from local invariant factors]\label{lem:smith}
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[x]]\), and let \(G(x)\in M_m(R)\) have nonzero
determinant.  Suppose the Smith exponents of \(G\) are
\(0\leq e_1\leq\cdots\leq e_m\).  Then
\[
 \ord_x\det G=\sum_{i=1}^m e_i,\qquad
 \dim\ker G(0)=\#\{i:e_i>0\},
\]
and the pole order of \(G(x)^{-1}\) is \(e_m\).
In particular, if
\[
 \ord_x\det G=\dim\ker G(0)=k,
\]
then every positive Smith exponent equals \(1\), and \(G^{-1}\) has at
most a simple pole.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(R\) is a discrete valuation ring, there are
\(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R)\) such that
\[
 UGV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_m}).
\]
Taking determinants gives the first identity.  Reducing modulo \(x\)
gives the second.  Inverting the displayed Smith form gives pole order
\(e_m\).  If the sum of the \(k\) positive integers \(e_i\) is \(k\),
then each of them is \(1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Transverse inverse residue]\label{prop:transverse-residue}
Let \(V\) be finite dimensional, let
\(G(x)\colon V\to V^*\) be a symmetric matrix over \(\mathbb C[[x]]\)
with nonzero determinant, and put \(K=\ker G(0)\).  Let
\(\iota\colon K\hookrightarrow V\) be the inclusion and define the crossing
form
\[
 \beta(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}G'(0)v,\qquad u,v\in K.
\]
If \(\beta\) is nondegenerate, then \(G(x)^{-1}\) has at most a simple pole and
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{x=0}G(x)^{-1}
 =\iota\,\beta^{-1}\iota^*.
\]
Moreover, \(\beta\) is nondegenerate if and only if
\[
 \ord_x\det G=\dim K.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a complement \(C\) of \(K\).  Since \(K\) is the radical of the
symmetric form \(G(0)\), its restriction \(A\) to \(C\) is nondegenerate.
In the decomposition \(V=K\oplus C\), one therefore has
\[
 G(x)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  xB+O(x^2)&xD+O(x^2)\\
  xD^{\mathsf T}+O(x^2)&A+O(x)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(B\) is the matrix of \(\beta\).  The Schur complement of the
lower-right block is
\[
 S(x)=xB+O(x^2).
\]
If \(B\) is invertible, block inversion gives
\[
 G(x)^{-1}
 =\frac1x
 \begin{pmatrix}B^{-1}&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+O(1),
\]
which is the coordinate expression of the asserted residue.  Also
\[
 \det G(x)=\det(A+O(x))\det S(x).
\]
Every entry of \(S\) is divisible by \(x\), so its determinant is divisible
by \(x^{\dim K}\), with equality of orders precisely when
\(\det B\neq0\).  This proves the final equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-step inverse principal part]
\label{prop:two-step-residue}
Let
\[
 G(x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3)
\]
be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue}, and put
\[
 K_1=\ker G_0,\qquad
 \beta_1(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}G_1v,\qquad
 K_2=\ker(\beta_1\colon K_1\to K_1^*).
\]
For \(v\in K_2\), choose \(v_1\in V\) satisfying
\[
 G_0v_1=-G_1v
\]
and define
\[
 \beta_2(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}(G_2v+G_1v_1),
 \qquad u,v\in K_2.
\]
Then \(v_1\) exists, \(\beta_2\) is independent of its choice, and
\(\beta_2\) is symmetric.  Without any nondegeneracy assumption,
\[
 \#\{i:e_i\geq1\}=\dim K_1,\qquad
 \#\{i:e_i\geq2\}=\dim K_2.
\]
\(\beta_2\) is nondegenerate if and only if all Smith exponents are at most
\(2\).  In that case,
\[
 [x^{-2}]G(x)^{-1}=\iota_2\beta_2^{-1}\iota_2^*,
\]
where \(\iota_2\colon K_2\hookrightarrow V\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because \(G_0\) is symmetric,
\(\operatorname{im}G_0\) is the annihilator of \(K_1\).
For \(v\in K_2\) and \(u\in K_1\),
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}G_1v=\beta_1(u,v)=0.
\]
Thus \(G_1v\in\operatorname{im}G_0\), proving the existence of \(v_1\).
Two choices differ by an element \(k\in K_1\), and
\(u^{\mathsf T}G_1k=\beta_1(u,k)=0\) for \(u\in K_2\), proving
choice-independence.

Choose a complement to \(K_1\) as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue}, and first take the Schur
complement of its nondegenerate block.  This produces a symmetric matrix on
\(K_1\) of the form
\[
 S(x)=xB_1+x^2B_2+O(x^3),
\]
where \(B_1\) represents \(\beta_1\), and the restriction of \(B_2\) to
\(\ker B_1=K_2\) is the displayed form \(\beta_2\).  This also proves its
symmetry.  The positive Smith exponents of \(G\) are the Smith exponents of
\(S\).  Dividing \(S\) by \(x\) and reducing modulo \(x\) gives \(B_1\).
The Smith normal form, first for \(G\) and then for \(x^{-1}S\), therefore
gives
\[
 \#\{i:e_i\geq1\}=\dim K_1,\qquad
 \#\{i:e_i\geq2\}=\dim\ker B_1=\dim K_2.
\]
This count does not require the second crossing form to be nondegenerate.

Now choose a complement \(E\) to \(K_2\) in \(K_1\).
The restriction of \(B_1\) to \(E\) is nondegenerate, and a second Schur
complement gives
\[
 S(x)\sim
 \begin{pmatrix}
  x^2\beta_2+O(x^3)&0\\
  0&xB_1|_E+O(x^2)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The second block form shows that \(\beta_2\) is nondegenerate precisely when
no Smith exponent exceeds \(2\).  In that case block inversion yields the
asserted \(x^{-2}\) coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Determinant multiplicity does not determine block pole
order]\label{cor:det-overcounts}
At a collided Kac weight, the multiplicity of the corresponding factor in
\(\det G_N\) is only the sum of the positive local Smith exponents.
The inverse Shapovalov pole order is their maximum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} with \(x=\Delta-\lambda\), where \(\lambda\)
is the collided Kac weight.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Jantzen layers in the internal weight]
\label{def:jantzen-layers}
Fix \((c,h)\) and identify \(M(c,h+x)_N\) with the PBW coordinate space
\(\mathbb C^{p(N)}\).  For \(i\geq0\), let \(J^i_N(c,h)\) be the set of
\(v\in M(c,h)_N\) for which there is a polynomial family \(v(x)\) with
\(v(0)=v\) and
\[
 G_N(c,h+x)\,v(x)\in x^i\,\mathbb C[x]^{p(N)},
\]
and put \(J^i(c,h)=\bigoplus_{N\geq0}J^i_N(c,h)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Smith exponents are Jantzen dimensions]
\label{lem:jantzen-smith}
Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), with local Smith exponents
\(e_1,\ldots,e_{p(N)}\) at \(x=0\).  Then
\[
 \dim J^i_N(c,h)=\#\{j:e_j\geq i\}\qquad(i\geq1),
 \qquad
 \sum_{i\geq1}\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\ord_x\det G_N(c,h+x).
\]
Each \(J^i(c,h)\) is a submodule of \(M(c,h)\), and \(J^1(c,h)\) is the
radical of the Shapovalov form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_{p(N)}(\mathbb C[[x]])\) with
\(UG_NV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_{p(N)}})\).  Writing
\(v(x)=V(x)w(x)\), the condition \(G_Nv\in x^i\) becomes
\(w_j\in x^{\max(0,i-e_j)}\mathbb C[[x]]\) for every \(j\); truncation
modulo \(x^i\) shows that power series and polynomial families define the
same space.  Hence \(J^i_N=V(0)\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_j:e_j\geq i\}\),
where \(\epsilon_j\) are the standard basis vectors, which proves the
dimension formula.  Summing over \(i\) and using Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}
gives the second identity.  If \(v(x)\) is a family for \(v\in J^i_N\)
and \(n\in\mathbb Z\), then \(L_nv(x)\), computed in \(M(c,h+x)\), is a
polynomial family with value \(L_nv\) at \(x=0\), and contravariance gives
\(\langle y,L_nv(x)\rangle_{h+x}=\langle L_{-n}y,v(x)\rangle_{h+x}\in
x^i\mathbb C[x]\) for every PBW vector \(y\).  Thus \(J^i(c,h)\) is a
submodule.  For \(i=1\) the defining condition is \(G_N(c,h)v=0\).
\end{proof}

Thus \(J^\bullet(c,h)\) is the Jantzen filtration of \(M(c,h)\) along the
deformation \(h\mapsto h+x\) at fixed central charge.  For
\(c\neq1,25\) this is the filtration studied in
\cite[\S5.5 and equation (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011}, and it is a Jantzen
filtration along a curve in the sense of
\cite[\S4 and \S5.2]{Astashkevich1997}.  The Feigin--Fuchs structure
theorem describes it completely; at minimal-model charges it reads as
follows.

\begin{theorem}[Smith layers at minimal-model charges]
\label{thm:jantzen-smith}
Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime, let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) with \(0<r<p\) and \(0<s<p'\), labelled so that
\(n_1:=p'r+ps<pp'\).  Put \(n_0=p'r-ps\) and, for \(j\geq1\) and
\(j\geq0\) respectively,
\[
 \ell^\pm_{2j}=j\bigl(jpp'\pm n_0\bigr),\qquad
 \begin{aligned}
 \ell^+_{2j+1}&=(r+jp)(s+jp'),\\
 \ell^-_{2j+1}&=\bigl((j+1)p-r\bigr)\bigl((j+1)p'-s\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Then \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) has, for every \(k\geq1\), singular vectors
\(w^\pm_k\) of levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), and with
\(W^\pm_k=U(\Vir_-)w^\pm_k\),
\[
 J^i(c_\star,\lambda)=W^+_i+W^-_i\qquad(i\geq1).
\]
Consequently, at every level \(N\), the number of local Smith exponents of
\(G_N(c_\star,\lambda+x)\) that are at least \(i\) is
\(\dim(W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\).  The inverse Shapovalov matrix
\(G_N(c_\star,\Delta)^{-1}\) has a pole of order exactly
\[
 j_N(\lambda)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup
 \{k\geq1:\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\leq N\}\bigr)
\]
at \(\Delta=\lambda\), and \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) have poles of order at most \(j_N(\lambda)\)
there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the notation of \cite[\S5.1.4]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(c_\star=c_{p,p'}\)
and \((r,s)\) lies in the interior set \((K^+_{p,p'})^\circ\), so
\((c_\star,\lambda)\) belongs to Class~\(R^+\), Case~\(1+\).  Since
\(c_\star\neq1,25\), the deformation used there is
\(h\mapsto h+x\) at fixed \(c\) \cite[equation (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011},
so \(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)\) is the Jantzen filtration of
\cite[Theorem~6.3]{IoharaKoga2011}.  Part~(i) of that theorem, with
equation~(6.3) there, identifies the \(i\)-th layer with the sum of the
two Verma submodules of highest weights \(\xi_{\pm i}\) in the braided
embedding diagram \cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011}.
By \cite[equations (5.4) and (5.22)]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(\xi_0=\lambda\)
and, for \(k\geq1\), the weights \(\xi_{\pm k}\) correspond to the
Coulomb-gas momenta \(2jpp'\mp n_0\) when \(k=2j\), and
\(2(j+1)pp'-n_1\) and \(2jpp'+n_1\) when \(k=2j+1\).  Since
\(h_{u,v}(b_\star)=((p'u-pv)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), their levels above
\(\lambda\) are \(\ell^\mp_k\).

Independently, \cite[Theorem~B(e)]{Astashkevich1997} gives the same
layers, as sums over the same unordered pairs of generators, along every
smooth curve through \((\lambda,c_\star)\) that is not
tangent to a Kac curve.  The line \(c=c_\star\) has this property: near
\((\lambda,c_\star)\) every Kac curve is a graph
\(\Delta=h_{u,v}(b(c))\) over the \(c\)-axis, because
\(c'(b_\star)=12(b_\star+b_\star^{-1})(1-b_\star^{-2})\neq0\).
In the notation of \cite[\S5.1]{Astashkevich1997} one is in case
\(\mathrm{III}_-\): the integral points of the line
\(p'k-pl=n_0\) with \(kl>0\) are, up to sign, the labels
\((r+jp,s+jp')\) and \(((j+1)p-r,(j+1)p'-s)\), \(j\geq0\), whose
products \(\ell^+_{2j+1}<\ell^-_{2j+1}\) interlace as stated there (the
difference is \((2j+1)(n_1-pp')<0\)); the line meets neither axis at an
integral point because \(p\nmid r\) and \(p'\nmid s\); and the points of
the auxiliary line give the levels
\(rs+(jp-r)(jp'+s)=\ell^-_{2j}\) and
\(rs+(r+jp)(jp'-s)=\ell^+_{2j}\).

Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} converts the layers into Smith exponents.
The space \((W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\) is nonzero exactly when
\(\min(\ell^+_i,\ell^-_i)\leq N\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} gives the
pole order \(j_N(\lambda)\) of \(G_N^{-1}\).  The trace \(F_N\) cannot have
a higher pole.  Finally, \(j_{N-j}(\lambda)\leq j_N(\lambda)\), and
\(H_N=\sum_j\eta_jF_{N-j}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Other weights]\label{rem:jantzen-other}
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} treats the weights with an interior
label, which are the weights \(\xi_0\) of Class~\(R^+\), Case~\(1+\), in
\cite{IoharaKoga2011}.  The other weights of \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) at a
minimal-model charge are weights \(\xi_i\) with \(i\neq0\) of Case~\(1+\),
or of boundary type; for instance, at \((p,p')=(3,4)\) the weight
\(h_{1,5}(b_0)=5/2\) has no interior label, although \(3\nmid1\) and
\(4\nmid5\).  For all of them \cite[Theorem~6.3]{IoharaKoga2011}
describes the same fixed-\(c\) Jantzen filtration, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} turns its layers into Smith exponents and
pole bounds.  At \(c_\star=1,25\) the fixed-\(c\) line is tangent to some
Kac curves (not to all: \(h_{1,1}=0\) is transverse), and
\cite[equation (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011} uses a different deformation
there, so Definition~\ref{def:jantzen-layers} needs a separate analysis.
The confluence theorem uses none of these results; they explain its pole
orders.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Torus form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-factorization}
Let \(\chi\in M(c,h)\) be a singular vector of level \(\ell\), normalized
by a fixed PBW coefficient, and let
\[
 P_\chi(d)=\langle\chi|V_d(1)|\chi\rangle .
\]
For partitions \(Y,Y'\) of the same integer \(m\),
\[
 \langle\chi|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\chi\rangle
 =P_\chi(d)\,
 \rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)_{Y,Y'}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The assignment
\[
 L_{-Y}|h+\ell\rangle\longmapsto L_{-Y}\chi
\]
is a homomorphism from \(M(c,h+\ell)\) to \(M(c,h)\), because \(\chi\)
is singular and has \(L_0\)-weight \(h+\ell\).  Pull the torus
three-point form on \(M(c,h)\) back along this homomorphism in both module
arguments.  Commuting one Virasoro mode at a time through \(V_d(z)\) shows
that the pulled-back form obeys the same Ward recursion as
\(\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)\).  That recursion terminates at the highest-weight
matrix element and therefore determines every descendant matrix element
uniquely.  The pulled-back highest-weight value is \(P_\chi(d)\), whereas
the normalization
\(\langle h+\ell|V_d(1)|h+\ell\rangle=1\) gives highest-weight value
\(1\) for \(\rho_m\).  The displayed identity follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First crossing form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-crossing}
Fix \(c\) and let \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\in M(c,h_0)\) be singular of level
\(\ell\geq1\), where the PBW operator \(P\in U(\Vir_-)\) is held fixed as
\(h\) varies.  Set
\[
 \beta_\chi=
 \left.\frac{d}{dh}
 \langle h|\omega(P)P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0},
\]
where \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  For partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dh}
 \langle h|\omega(P)L_YL_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}
 =\beta_\chi\,G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the Shapovalov form of \(M(c,h)\), write
\[
 S_h(u,v)=\langle h|\omega(u)v|h\rangle,
 \qquad \widetilde u=uP\quad(u\in U(\Vir_-)).
\]
The derivative on the left defines a bilinear form
\[
 C(u,v)
 =\left.\frac{d}{dh}S_h(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)\right|_{h=h_0}
\]
on the PBW space of \(M(c,h_0+\ell)\).  At \(h=h_0\), the map
\[
 j_\chi\colon M(c,h_0+\ell)\longrightarrow M(c,h_0),
 \qquad u|h_0+\ell\rangle\longmapsto u\chi ,
\]
is a module homomorphism, and its image lies in the radical of the
Shapovalov form, because \(\chi\) has positive level and therefore
generates a proper submodule.

We claim that \(C\) is contravariant for the module structure of
\(M(c,h_0+\ell)\).  Contravariance in negative modes,
\(C(L_{-n}u,v)=C(u,\omega(L_{-n})v)\), holds at every \(h\) because the
Shapovalov form itself is contravariant and \(\widetilde{L_{-n}u}
=L_{-n}\widetilde u\).  For a positive mode, fix \(n>0\) and
\(v\in U(\Vir_-)\), and let \(w_0\in U(\Vir_-)\) be the PBW
reduction of \(L_n v\) in \(M(c,h_0+\ell)\), so that
\(L_n\,v|h_0+\ell\rangle=w_0|h_0+\ell\rangle\).  In \(M(c,h)\) set
\[
 D(h)=L_n\widetilde v\,|h\rangle-\widetilde{w_0}\,|h\rangle .
\]
Each coefficient of \(D(h)\) in the PBW basis is polynomial in \(h\),
and \(D(h_0)=0\) because \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\) is singular and
\(j_\chi\) intertwines the actions at \(h=h_0\).  Hence
\(D(h)=(h-h_0)\widetilde D(h)\) with \(\widetilde D\) polynomial in
\(h\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac{d}{dh}\Big|_{h_0}S_h(\widetilde u,L_n\widetilde v)
 =\frac{d}{dh}\Big|_{h_0}S_h(\widetilde u,\widetilde{w_0})
 +S_{h_0}\bigl(\widetilde u,\widetilde D(h_0)\bigr).
\]
The last term vanishes because \(\widetilde u\,|h_0\rangle
=j_\chi(u|h_0+\ell\rangle)\) lies in the radical of \(S_{h_0}\).  Since
\(S_h(\widetilde u,L_n\widetilde v)
=S_h(\widetilde{L_{-n}u},\widetilde v)\), this proves
\(C(L_{-n}u,v)=C(u,w_0)\), that is, contravariance in positive
modes.

A contravariant form on a Verma module is uniquely determined by its
highest-weight value, because PBW commutation recursively reduces every
matrix element to that value.  Here the highest-weight value is
\(\beta_\chi\), so \(C=\beta_\chi G_m(c,h_0+\ell)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spin-sector fixed-\(c\) recursive principal part]

\section{Generic residue data}\label{sec:generic-data}

\begin{definition}[Generic residue data used for algebraic confluence]
\label{def:generic-residue}
Use the functions \(c(b)\) and \(h_{r,s}(b)\) of
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.
Put \(\Lambda^2=(b+b^{-1})^2-4d\), and define
\[
 \begin{split}
 {\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)
 =\prod_{\substack{1\leq k\leq2r-1\\k\ {\rm odd}}}
  \prod_{\substack{1\leq l\leq2s-1\\l\ {\rm odd}}}
 &\frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}\\
 {}\times&
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4},
 \end{split}
\]
and
\[
 {\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)
 =\frac12
 \prod_{\substack{1-r\leq p\leq r,\ 1-s\leq q\leq s\\
 (p,q)\neq(0,0),(r,s)}}
 (pb+qb^{-1})^{-1},\qquad
 {\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b){\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d).
\]
For generic \(b\), \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\) is the residue coefficient in the
eta-reduced torus recursion, stated as
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} below.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Imported generic normalization identities]
\label{prop:imported-normalizations}
For positive \(r,s\) and generic \(b\), let \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) be the
singular vector whose \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient is \(1\), and let
\(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\) be its crossing scalar from
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossing-normalization}
 \beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)^{-1},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fusion-normalization}
 \langle\chi_{r,s}(b)|V_d(1)|\chi_{r,s}(b)\rangle
 ={\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d).
\end{equation}
Both identities are identities of rational functions in \(b\), and hence
continue to every specialization at which the normalized singular vector
is regular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The recursion was conjectured in \cite{Poghossian2009}.  In
\cite[Section~3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}, equation~(7) expresses the
residue through the inverse norm derivative \(A_{r,s}(c)\), the paragraph
immediately following equation~(7) gives its product for the normalization
in which the singular vector has \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\),
equation~(8) evaluates the product of the two torus fusion polynomials, and
equation~(9) is the eta-reduced recursion.

Write \(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}\) for the external momentum in that source.
Its convention is
\[
 d=\frac{(b+b^{-1})^2-\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2}{4},\qquad
 \lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2=\Lambda^2.
\]
The source defines \(A_{r,s}(c)\) as the reciprocal of
\(\partial_\Delta\langle\chi_{r,s}^{\Delta}\mid
\chi_{r,s}^{\Delta}\rangle\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}\), with the
coefficients of the normalized vector held fixed.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}, that derivative is exactly
\(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\).  The product following equation~(7) is
term-for-term \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\), proving
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization} with the stated normalization.

For every odd \(k\) and \(l\) in the ranges of
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the four factors of equation~(8)
pair as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\frac{(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}+kb+lb^{-1})
              (\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}-kb-lb^{-1})}{4}\\[-2pt]
 &\qquad{}\times
 \frac{(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}+kb-lb^{-1})
              (\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}-kb+lb^{-1})}{4}\\
 &\quad=\frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}
  \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the product proves \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization}; in particular,
the translation introduces no sign or power-of-two factor and does not
choose a square root of \(\Lambda^2\).  The normalized singular-vector
coefficients are obtained from a finite linear system over \(\mathbb Q(b)\),
so both left-hand sides are rational in \(b\).  This proves the final
continuation statement.
\end{proof}

\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Distinct weights and no shifted self-poles away from
rational \(b^2\)]
\label{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
For positive labels \((r,s)\) and \((u,v)\),
\[
 h_{r,s}(b)+rs-h_{u,v}(b)
\]
is not the zero rational function of \(b\).  Moreover, for distinct
positive labels \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\), the difference
\(h_{r,s}(b)-h_{u,v}(b)\) is not the zero rational function.  If
\(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), then
neither function vanishes at \(b_\star\), and every
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular and nonzero at \(b_\star\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Kac formula gives the identity
\[
 h_{r,s}(b)+rs=h_{r,-s}(b).
\]
It vanishes against \(h_{u,v}(b)\) exactly when
\[
 (rb-sb^{-1})^2=(ub+vb^{-1})^2,
 \quad\text{that is,}\quad
 rb-sb^{-1}=\pm(ub+vb^{-1}),
\]
since \(\mathbb C(b)\) is an integral domain.  Equivalently
\((r\mp u)b^2=s\pm v\).  As an identity in \(b\) this contradicts
\(r,s,u,v>0\), and at a point it forces \(b^2=(s\pm v)/(r\mp u)\in\mathbb Q\);
here \(r\mp u\neq0\), because \(r=u\) would force \(s+v=0\) and
\(r+u>0\).  Similarly
\(h_{r,s}=h_{u,v}\) means \((r\mp u)b^2=\pm v-s\); as an identity this
forces \((r,s)=(u,v)\), and at a point with \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\) it forces
\(b^2\in\mathbb Q\).  Finally, each factor \(pb+qb^{-1}\) of
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is finite at \(b_\star\neq0\), and it vanishes only
if \(p\neq0\) and \(b_\star^2=-q/p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic eta-reduced recursion]
\label{prop:generic-recursion}
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For all \(b\) outside a finite set of algebraic values,
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{a=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\ \ell_a=rs\leq N}}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)}{\Delta-h_{r,s}(b)}\,
 H_{N-\ell_a}\bigl(c(b),h_{r,s}(b)+rs,d\bigr)
\]
as an identity of rational functions of \(\Delta\), polynomial in
\(d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given the two imported identities of
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations}, the
proposition follows from the results of
Sections~\ref{sec:partial-fractions} and~\ref{sec:smith}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}, outside finitely many values of
\(b\) the weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with \(rs\leq N\) are pairwise
distinct, and no shifted weight \(z_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b)+rs\) is a
degenerate weight of level at most \(N\).

Concretely, for the finite set of labels of level at most \(N\), take the
exceptional set to contain \(b=0\); the zeros and poles of every nonzero
difference \(h_a-h_g\) and \(z_a-h_g\); the poles of the coefficients in
\(d\) of the finite products \({\mathcal R}_a\); the poles of the
normalized singular-vector coefficients; and the zeros and poles of
\({\mathcal A}_a\) needed for the crossing form.  These are finitely many
zeros and poles of rational functions in \(\mathbb Q(b)\), so every
excluded value is algebraic.

Fix such a \(b\), write \(c=c(b)\), and fix a label \(a=(r,s)\) with
\(rs\leq N\).  By distinctness, \(h_{r,s}(b)\) lies on no other Kac
divisor of level at most \(N\), so the level-\(N\) Kac determinant has
valuation exactly \(p(N-rs)\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)\).  The
level-\(N\) image of the injective singular embedding
\(j_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\), whose injectivity is the result stated in
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}, is a
\(p(N-rs)\)-dimensional subspace of the
kernel of the specialized Gram matrix.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} the
nullity is at most the valuation, so the kernel is exactly that image,
and every positive local Smith exponent equals \(1\); hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue} applies.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}, the crossing form on this kernel is
\(\beta_{\chi}\,G_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s})\), which is nondegenerate because
\(z_{r,s}\) is not a degenerate weight and, by
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization},
\(\beta_\chi^{-1}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\neq0\).  Taking the trace of the residue of
\(G_N^{-1}\) against \(\rho_N\) and factoring the pulled-back torus form
by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} and
\eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)}F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 ={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\,{\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)\,
 F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d)
 ={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)\,F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d).
\]
The difference between \(F_N\) and the sum of these principal parts has
no finite pole, hence is a polynomial in \(\Delta\); it is bounded as
\(\Delta\to\infty\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight}, hence equals the
constant \(p(N)\):
\[
 F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =p(N)+\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_a(b,d)}{\Delta-h_a(b)}F_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d).
\]
Finally, multiply by the Euler coefficients \(\eta_j\) of
Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced} and sum.  The
generating identity in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} gives
\(\sum_j\eta_jp(N-j)=\delta_{N,0}\), while the residue sums reassemble into
shifted \(H\)-coefficients:
\[
 H_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =\delta_{N,0}
 +\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_a(b,d)}{\Delta-h_a(b)}H_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d).
\]
For \(N\geq1\) this is the displayed recursion.
\end{proof}

\section{All-order confluent recursion}\label{sec:all-order}

\begin{definition}[Shifted Laurent recursion]\label{def:shifted-laurent}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), put
\(c_\star=c(b_\star)\), let \(t=b-b_\star\), and work in
\[
 K_\star((t)),\qquad K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,d),
\]
where \(d\) is an indeterminate.
For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), write
\[
 \ell_a=rs,\qquad h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t),\qquad
 R_a(t)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b_\star+t,d),\qquad
 z_a(t)=h_a(t)+\ell_a.
\]
Define shifted Laurent coefficients recursively by
\[
 S_{a,0}(t)=1
\]
and, for \(M\geq1\),
\[
 S_{a,M}(t)
 =\sum_{\substack{g=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_g=uv\leq M}}
 \frac{R_g(t)}
 {z_a(t)-h_g(t)}S_{g,M-\ell_g}(t).
\]
Every denominator in this formula is a nonzero rational function of
\(b\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}, so each summand has a
well-defined Laurent expansion, and the recursion terminates because its
second index strictly decreases.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A priori shifted valuation bound]
\label{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}
For positive labels \(a,g\), set
\[
 \pi_g=\max\{0,-\ord_tR_g(t)\},\qquad
 \sigma_{a,g}
 =\max\{0,\ord_t(z_a(t)-h_g(t))\}.
\]
Define nonnegative integers \(W_{a,M}\) recursively by
\(W_{a,0}=0\) and, for \(M\geq1\),
\[
 W_{a,M}
 =\max\left(
 \{0\}\cup
 \{\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}+W_{g,M-\ell_g}:\ell_g\leq M\}
 \right).
\]
Then
\[
 \ord_t S_{a,M}(t)\geq-W_{a,M}.
\]
Thus \(W_{a,M}\) is an a priori pole bound computed solely from
the finite recursion tree and the valuations of its explicit residue and
denominator factors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The assertion is clear for \(M=0\).  If it holds at lower levels, the
\(g\)-summand in Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} has valuation at least
\(-\pi_g-\sigma_{a,g}-W_{g,M-\ell_g}\).
The valuation of a finite sum is at least the minimum of the summand
valuations.  All sets involved are finite because \(\ell_g\leq M\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Collision moments]\label{def:collision-moments}
For \(N\geq1\), \(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\), and
\(a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), define
\[
 B_{a,N}(t)=R_a(t)S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t),\qquad
 \delta_a(t)=h_a(t)-\lambda\in tK_\star[[t]],
\]
let \(\varepsilon_a=\ord_t\delta_a\geq1\), with \(\varepsilon_a=\infty\)
if \(\delta_a\equiv0\), and put
\(\widehat v_{a,N}=\pi_a+W_{a,N-\ell_a}\), so that
\(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}.  Define the a priori pole bound
\[
 \widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}=
 \max_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \begin{cases}
  1+\lfloor \widehat v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor,&\varepsilon_a<\infty,\\
  1,&\varepsilon_a=\infty,
 \end{cases}
\]
and, for \(k\geq1\), the \emph{collision moments}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-moments}
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 =\operatorname{CT}_{t}
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) denotes the coefficient of \(t^0\) in a
Laurent series.  For \(k>\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) every summand has
positive valuation, since \((k-1)\varepsilon_a>\widehat v_{a,N}\), so
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Backward Laurent-precision budget]
\label{lem:precision-budget}
Suppose \(S_{a,M}\) is required through the coefficient of \(t^U\).
For its \(g\)-summand it is sufficient to know
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-propagation}
 \frac{R_g}{z_a-h_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+W_{g,M-\ell_g}},
\qquad
 S_{g,M-\ell_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}}.
\end{equation}
Iterating these requests down the strictly level-decreasing recursion tree
produces a finite a priori upper precision at every node.

In particular, to compute every collision moment at level \(N\), it is
sufficient for each \(a\) to compute \(R_a\) through \(t^{W_{a,N-\ell_a}}\),
\(S_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through \(t^{\pi_a}\), and
\(\delta_a^{\,k-1}\) through \(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\).  Thus the valuation
majorant together with \eqref{eq:precision-propagation} is an actual finite
jet budget, not only a pole-order bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first factor has valuation at least \(-\pi_g-\sigma_{a,g}\), and the
second at least \(-W_{g,M-\ell_g}\).  In a product coefficient of degree
at most \(U\), the first bound means that no coefficient of the second
factor above degree \(U+\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}\) can occur; the second means
that no coefficient of the first factor above degree
\(U+W_{g,M-\ell_g}\) can occur.  This proves
\eqref{eq:precision-propagation}.  The recursion tree is finite because the
remaining level decreases along every edge.
For \(B_{a,N}=R_aS_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through degree zero, the same argument
gives the stated precisions, and \(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\)
shows that only coefficients of \(\delta_a^{k-1}\) through degree
\(\widehat v_{a,N}\) can enter a constant term.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise pole support of a collision germ]
\label{lem:collision-pole-support}
Let \(B(t)\in K_\star((t))\) and
\(\delta(t)\in tK_\star[[t]]\).  Every coefficient of
\[
 \frac{B(t)}{x-\delta(t)}
 =\sum_{j\geq0}\frac{B(t)\delta(t)^j}{x^{j+1}}
\]
as a Laurent series in \(t\) is a finite \(K_\star\)-linear
combination of \(x^{-1},x^{-2},\ldots\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(B=0\) or \(\delta=0\) the assertion is immediate.  Otherwise choose
\(v\geq0\) with \(\ord_tB\geq-v\) and put
\(\epsilon=\ord_t\delta\geq1\).  The term \(B\delta^j\) has valuation at
least \(-v+j\epsilon\), so only the finitely many \(j\) with
\(-v+j\epsilon\leq m\) contribute to the coefficient of \(t^m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}]
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For generic \(b\), Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}
gives
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{R_a(t)}
 {\Delta-h_a(t)}
 H_{N-\ell_a}(c(b),z_a(t),d),
\]
and induction on \(M\) in the same recursion gives
\(S_{a,M}(t)=H_M(c(b),z_a(t),d)\) as rational functions of \(b\).  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-grouped}
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}{\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N},\qquad
 {\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-h_a(t)},
\end{equation}
an identity of rational functions of \((b,\Delta,d)\).  Expand both sides
in \(K_\star(\Delta)((t))\).  For the class of \(\lambda\), put
\(x=\Delta-\lambda\), which is nonzero in \(K_\star(\Delta)\); then
\(1/(\Delta-h_a(t))=1/(x-\delta_a(t))\) expands as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and Cramer's rule, the left side
of \eqref{eq:generic-grouped} is a quotient whose denominator
\(\det G_N(c(b_\star+t),\Delta)\) takes the nonzero value
\(\det G_N(c_\star,\Delta)\in K_\star[\Delta]\) at \(t=0\).  Hence the left
side lies in \(K_\star(\Delta)[[t]]\), and its constant term is
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\).  On the right, the coefficient of \(t^0\) in
\({\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support} and
Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments},
\[
 g_\lambda(\Delta)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},
\]
a polynomial in \((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\) without constant term.
Therefore
\[
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}g_\lambda(\Delta).
\]
The points \(\lambda\) are distinct, so uniqueness of partial fractions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions} gives
\(g_\lambda=\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\), that is,
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\) for every
\(k\geq1\).  In particular \(A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}=0\) for
\(k>\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}\), so
\(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\); the moments
lie in \(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}.  The finite-jet statements
are Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-budget}, and coefficientwise local finiteness
follows from the finiteness of \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).

If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
shows that every class consists of one label, that every \(R_g\) is
regular at \(t=0\), and that no shifted denominator vanishes there.
Hence \(W_{a,M}=0\), \(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}=1\), each
\(S_{a,M}\) is a power series with value
\(H_M(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\), and the only moment is
\(R_a(0)H_{N-\ell_a}(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Classwise regularity]\label{cor:class-regular}
Every \({\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) lies in \(K_\star(\Delta)[[t]]\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(m<0\), the coefficient of \(t^m\) on the left of
\eqref{eq:generic-grouped} vanishes.  On the right it is a sum over
\(\lambda\) of polynomials in \((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\) without constant
term (Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}).  Uniqueness of partial
fractions forces each of them to vanish.
\end{proof}

The two embedding diamonds of Sections~\ref{sec:energy-diamond}
and~\ref{sec:spin-diamond} are instances of the following closure
formula.  Dots denote \(d/dt\) at \(t=0\), that is, \(d/db\) at
\(b_\star\).

\begin{proposition}[Closure of a two-branch collision]
\label{prop:two-branch}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), \(N\geq1\), and
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) with
\({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}=\{\kappa_1,\kappa_2\}\).  For \(i=1,2\) put
\(M_i=N-\ell_{\kappa_i}\) and \(z_i=z_{\kappa_i}(0)\), and suppose that
there is a label \(\tau_i\) with \(\ell_{\tau_i}=M_i\) such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(\tau_i\) is the only label \(g\) with \(\ell_g\leq M_i\) and
 \(h_g(0)=z_i\);
\item[(ii)] \(R_{\kappa_i}\) and \(R_{\tau_i}\) are regular at \(t=0\);
\item[(iii)] \(\zeta_i(t):=z_{\kappa_i}(t)-h_{\tau_i}(t)\) has a simple zero
 at \(t=0\).
\end{enumerate}
Define the finite parts and derivative terms
\[
 Q_i(d)=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \bigl\{(z-z_i)H_{M_i}(c_\star,z,d)\bigr\}\right|_{z=z_i},
 \qquad
 T_i(d)=
 \frac{\dot R_{\kappa_i}R_{\tau_i}+R_{\kappa_i}\dot R_{\tau_i}}
      {\dot\zeta_i}
 -\frac{R_{\kappa_i}R_{\tau_i}\ddot\zeta_i}{2\dot\zeta_i^2},
\]
with every undotted factor evaluated at \(t=0\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-branch-cancel}
 \frac{R_{\kappa_1}R_{\tau_1}}{\dot\zeta_1}
 +\frac{R_{\kappa_2}R_{\tau_2}}{\dot\zeta_2}=0
 \quad\text{at }t=0,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-branch-pp}
 \PP_{\Delta=\lambda}H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\frac{(\dot h_{\kappa_1}-\dot h_{\kappa_2})
        R_{\kappa_1}R_{\tau_1}}
       {\dot\zeta_1\,(\Delta-\lambda)^2}
 +\frac{R_{\kappa_1}Q_1+R_{\kappa_2}Q_2+T_1+T_2}{\Delta-\lambda}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(i\).  By (i), near \((b,\Delta)=(b_\star,z_i)\) the only factor of
\(\det G_{M_i}(c(b),\Delta)\) that vanishes is
\(\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b)\), with exponent \(p(M_i-\ell_{\tau_i})=1\).
Hence \(U(b,\Delta)=(\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b))H_{M_i}(c(b),\Delta,d)\) is a
rational function regular at \((b_\star,z_i)\).  For generic \(b\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} at level \(M_i\) gives
\(U(b,h_{\tau_i}(b))=R_{\tau_i}(b)\).  Therefore
\[
 V(b,\Delta)=H_{M_i}(c(b),\Delta,d)-\frac{R_{\tau_i}(b)}{\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b)}
 =\frac{U(b,\Delta)-U(b,h_{\tau_i}(b))}{\Delta-h_{\tau_i}(b)}
\]
is regular at \((b_\star,z_i)\), and \(V(b_\star,z_i)=Q_i\) because
\((z-z_i)H_{M_i}(c_\star,z,d)=R_{\tau_i}(0)+(z-z_i)V(b_\star,z)\).
Restricting to \(\Delta=z_{\kappa_i}(t)\) gives
\[
 S_{\kappa_i,M_i}(t)=\frac{R_{\tau_i}(t)}{\zeta_i(t)}+Q_i+O(t).
\]
Expanding \(f_i=R_{\kappa_i}R_{\tau_i}=f_i(0)+\dot f_it+O(t^2)\) and
\(\zeta_i=\dot\zeta_it+\tfrac12\ddot\zeta_it^2+O(t^3)\), one finds
\[
 B_{\kappa_i,N}(t)=\frac{f_i(0)}{\dot\zeta_i\,t}
 +R_{\kappa_i}(0)Q_i+T_i+O(t).
\]
The coefficient of \(t^{-1}(\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\) in
\({\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is \(\sum_if_i(0)/\dot\zeta_i\), because
\(B_{\kappa_i,N}\delta_{\kappa_i}^j\) has nonnegative valuation for
\(j\geq1\); Corollary~\ref{cor:class-regular} gives
\eqref{eq:two-branch-cancel}.  Since
\(\delta_{\kappa_i}=\dot h_{\kappa_i}t+O(t^2)\), the moments are
\[
 \widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,1,N}=\sum_i\bigl(R_{\kappa_i}(0)Q_i+T_i\bigr),
 \qquad
 \widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,2,N}
 =\sum_i\frac{\dot h_{\kappa_i}f_i(0)}{\dot\zeta_i}
 =\frac{(\dot h_{\kappa_1}-\dot h_{\kappa_2})f_1(0)}{\dot\zeta_1},
\]
the last step by \eqref{eq:two-branch-cancel}, and
\(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}=0\) for \(k\geq3\), because
\(B_{\kappa_i,N}\delta_{\kappa_i}^{k-1}\) then has positive valuation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} identifies these moments with the
principal part.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[First nontransverse Smith layer]
\label{prop:level-seven-smith}
Let \(h_E=1/2\), and let \(U_2,U_3\) be the submodules generated by
\(\xi_2,\xi_3\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:first-collision}.  Their
intersection is zero below level \(7\), and
\((U_2\cap U_3)_7=\mathbb C\xi_7\) for a normalized singular vector
\(\xi_7\).  More explicitly, the normalized child singular vectors
are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \phi_5={}&\bigl(
 L_{-1}^5-15L_{-2}L_{-1}^3+36L_{-2}^2L_{-1}
 +\tfrac{99}{4}L_{-3}L_{-1}^2\\
 &\hspace{17mm}-45L_{-3}L_{-2}-54L_{-4}L_{-1}
 +45L_{-5}\bigr)|\tfrac52\rangle ,
 \end{split}
\]
\[
 \phi_4=\bigl(
 L_{-1}^4-\tfrac{40}{3}L_{-2}L_{-1}^2+16L_{-2}^2
 +\tfrac{88}{3}L_{-3}L_{-1}-\tfrac{152}{3}L_{-4}
 \bigr)|\tfrac72\rangle ,
\]
and
\[
 \xi_7=j_{\xi_2}(\phi_5)=j_{\xi_3}(\phi_4).
\]
The \(15\) local Smith exponents of
\(G_7(1/2,h_E+x)\) are
\[
 (0^{\,4},1^{\,10},2).
\]
For the second crossing form of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-step-residue}, normalized by the displayed
\(\xi_7\),
\[
 \gamma_7=-700700.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two child highest weights are
\(h_E+2=5/2=\lambda_{11}\) and \(h_E+3=7/2=\lambda_{13}\).  Their first
singular vectors have relative levels \(5\) and \(4\), with
labels \((1,5)\) and \((4,1)\), respectively.  Direct Virasoro commutation
checks the two displayed vectors.  Their images are nonzero singular
vectors at total level \(7\), by injectivity of nonzero homomorphisms
between Verma modules
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}; uniqueness
of a singular vector at a fixed level
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011} and their common
\(L_{-1}^7\)-coefficient \(1\) prove the displayed equality.

Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} with \((p,p')=(3,4)\) and
\((r,s)=(2,1)\) gives the levels \(\ell^\pm_1=2,3\),
\(\ell^-_2=7<\ell^+_2=17\), and \(\ell^\pm_3=25,28\).  Hence
\(J^2_7=\mathbb C\xi_7\) and \(J^3_7=0\).  A nonzero vector of
\(U_2\cap U_3\) of lowest level is singular; if that level were below
\(7\), its preimage under \(j_{\xi_2}\) would be a singular vector of
\(M(\frac12,\frac52)\) of relative level below \(5\), which the Kac
determinant of that module excludes.  So \(U_2\cap U_3\) is zero below
level \(7\); every vector of \((U_2\cap U_3)_7\) is therefore singular,
and uniqueness of singular vectors gives
\((U_2\cap U_3)_7=\mathbb C\xi_7\).  The only Kac labels of weight \(1/2\) and level at most \(7\)
are \((2,1)\) and \((1,3)\), so the determinant valuation is
\(p(5)+p(4)=12\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} gives
\(\dim J^1_7=11\).  The Smith exponents are therefore
\((0^4,1^{10},2)\).

The second crossing scalar can be obtained without an off-critical
deformation.  In the level-seven PBW basis, form
\[
 G_7(1/2,h_E+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3),
\]
solve \(G_0v_1=-G_1\xi_7\), and evaluate
\(\xi_7^{\mathsf T}(G_2\xi_7+G_1v_1)\).
Exact PBW commutation gives \(-700700\).  Choice-independence follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-step-residue}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No earlier nontransverse pole]
\label{prop:no-earlier-double-pole}
For every \(N<7\) and every finite internal weight, all local Smith
exponents of \(G_N(1/2,\Delta)\) are at most \(1\).  Consequently
\(G_N^{-1}\), \(F_N\), and \(H_N\) have at most simple poles in \(\Delta\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\lambda\) be a Kac weight represented by only one label
\(a=(r,s)\) with \(\ell_a\leq N\), and put \(k=p(N-\ell_a)\).  The
singular-vector embedding is injective
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011} and its image
lies in the radical, so \(k\leq\dim\ker G_N(1/2,\lambda)\).  The Kac
determinant has valuation \(k\) at \(\lambda\), since \(a\) is the only
label of weight \(\lambda\) and level at most \(N\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} gives
\(\dim\ker G_N(1/2,\lambda)\leq\ord_x\det G_N(1/2,\lambda+x)=k\).  Hence
equality holds, and Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} makes every positive Smith
exponent equal to \(1\).

By Corollary~\ref{cor:ising-classes}, or direct enumeration of
\(rs\leq6\), the repeated classes below level \(7\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{weight}&\text{labels}&\text{singular levels}&
 \min(\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)\\ \hline
\lambda_5=\frac12 &(2,1),(1,3)&2,3&7\\
\lambda_2=\frac1{16}&(1,2),(2,2)&2,4&10\\
\lambda_1=0&(1,1),(2,3)&1,6&11 .
\end{array}
\]
All three weights are interior for \((p,p')=(3,4)\), with
\((r,s)=(2,1),(1,2),(1,1)\), and the last column is computed from
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}.  That theorem gives
\(j_N(\lambda)=1\) below the displayed level.
\end{proof}

\]
and \(E_2{\mathcal P}_{1,5}=E_3{\mathcal P}_{4,1}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(b_0\) in the finite products of
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, and use
\(\dot h_{r,s}=i\sqrt3\,(7-16r^2+9s^2)/48\), obtained by differentiating

\begin{lemma}[Regular normalized singular vectors]
\label{lem:regular-families}
For \(m\geq2\), let \(\mathcal S_m(c,h)\) be the matrix of
\(v\mapsto(L_1v,L_2v)\) on \(M(c,h)_m\) in PBW bases, with partitions in
decreasing lexicographic order and the \(L_1\)-rows before the
\(L_2\)-rows.  Let \(B_m(c,h)\) consist of the first \(p(m)-1\) rows of
\(\mathcal S_m(c,h)\), followed by the coordinate row of \(L_{-1}^m\).
Then
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(r,s)&m=rs&h&\det B_m(\frac12,h)\\ \hline
(2,1)&2&1/2&3\\
(1,3)&3&1/2&12\\
(1,2)&2&1/16&3\\
(2,2)&4&1/16&-675/2\\
(1,5)&5&5/2&91350\\
(4,1)&4&7/2&-2730\\
(4,2)&8&33/16&-17116376343198212705625/32\\
(1,6)&6&65/16&505194765075/32
\end{array}
\]
For each displayed label, the normalized singular vector
\(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) of Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} is
regular at \(b=b_0\), and its value there is the normalized singular
vector of level \(rs\) in \(M(\frac12,h)\).  In particular, both identities
of Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} hold at \(b_0\) for these
labels.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The determinants are exact Virasoro commutations.  For generic \(b\), the
vector \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) lies in \(M(c(b),h_{r,s}(b))_m\), is annihilated by
\(L_1\) and \(L_2\), and has \(L_{-1}^m\)-coefficient \(1\), so it solves
\(B_m(c(b),h_{r,s}(b))\,v=\epsilon_{p(m)}\), where \(\epsilon_{p(m)}\) is
the last standard basis vector.  The determinant of this system is a
rational function of \(b\) that is nonzero at \(b_0\) by the table, so
Cramer's rule makes \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) regular at \(b_0\).  Its value solves
the same system at \(b_0\); since \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) generate the positive
modes, it is singular with \(L_{-1}^m\)-coefficient \(1\).  Both identities
of Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} are identities of
rational functions of \(b\) and therefore specialize.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First nontransverse fixed-\(c\) principal part]
\label{thm:level-seven-principal-part}
Put
\[
 Q_5^E(d)=
 \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \{(z-\tfrac52)H_5(1/2,z,d)\}\right|_{z=5/2},\qquad
 Q_4^E(d)=
 \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \{(z-\tfrac72)H_4(1/2,z,d)\}\right|_{z=7/2},
\]
and let \(\Theta_7=T_1+T_2\) be the derivative term of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-branch} for \(\kappa_1=(2,1)\),
\(\tau_1=(1,5)\), \(\kappa_2=(1,3)\), and \(\tau_2=(4,1)\).  Its factored
form is equation~(\ref{eq:theta7-expanded}) of
Appendix~\ref{app:expanded-data}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-seven-pp}
 \begin{split}
 \PP_{\Delta=1/2}H_7(1/2,\Delta,d)
 ={}&-\frac{E_2(d){\mathcal P}_{1,5}(b_0,d)}
 {700700\,(\Delta-\tfrac12)^2}\\
 &+\frac{
 \frac9{28}E_2(d)Q_5^E(d)
 -\frac8{105}E_3(d)Q_4^E(d)+\Theta_7(d)}
 {\Delta-\tfrac12}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
With \(d\) indeterminate, this is the earliest double pole among the
coefficients \(H_N(1/2,\Delta,d)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We check the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-branch} at
\(N=7\) and \(\lambda=1/2\).  By Corollary~\ref{cor:ising-classes},
\({\mathcal C}_{5,7}=\{(2,1),(1,3)\}\); the next label of this class,
\((5,5)\), has level \(25\).  The labels of weight
\(z_1=5/2=\lambda_{11}\) have levels \(5,15,36,\ldots\), and those of
weight \(z_2=7/2=\lambda_{13}\) have levels \(4,14,35,\ldots\), so
\(\tau_1=(1,5)\) and \(\tau_2=(4,1)\) satisfy~(i).
Lemma~\ref{lem:level-seven-constants} gives~(ii) and, since
\(\dot\zeta_1=\dot h_{2,1}-\dot h_{1,5}\neq0\) and
\(\dot\zeta_2=\dot h_{1,3}-\dot h_{4,1}\neq0\), also~(iii).  With
\(R_{2,1}(b_0)=\frac9{28}E_2\) and \(R_{1,3}(b_0)=-\frac8{105}E_3\),
\eqref{eq:two-branch-pp} becomes \eqref{eq:level-seven-pp}; its
double-pole coefficient is
\(\frac5{11}{\mathcal A}_{2,1}{\mathcal A}_{1,5}E_2{\mathcal P}_{1,5}\).
By Proposition~\ref{prop:no-earlier-double-pole} no earlier coefficient
has a double pole.

The double-pole coefficient also has an intrinsic derivation.
Propositions~\ref{prop:two-step-residue}
and~\ref{prop:level-seven-smith} give
\([x^{-2}]G_7(1/2,\tfrac12+x)^{-1}=\xi_7\xi_7^{\mathsf T}/\gamma_7\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-families}, \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives
\(\langle\phi_5|V_d(1)|\phi_5\rangle={\mathcal P}_{1,5}(b_0,d)\) and
\(\langle\phi_4|V_d(1)|\phi_4\rangle={\mathcal P}_{4,1}(b_0,d)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} applied along either branch then
gives \(\langle\xi_7|V_d(1)|\xi_7\rangle=E_2{\mathcal P}_{1,5}
=E_3{\mathcal P}_{4,1}\).  Multiplication by the Euler product does not
affect the \(x^{-2}\) coefficient at level seven, because all lower levels
have at most simple poles.  The resulting coefficient
\(E_2{\mathcal P}_{1,5}/\gamma_7\) agrees with \eqref{eq:level-seven-pp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Level-ten Smith profile at \(\Delta=1/16\)]
\label{prop:level-ten-smith}
Let \(h_0=1/16\), and let
\[
 U_2=U(\Vir_-)\chi_2,\qquad U_4=U(\Vir_-)\chi_4
\]
be the two singular submodules of \(M(1/2,h_0)\) from
Proposition~\ref{prop:spin-singular-data}.  Their intersection is zero
below level \(10\), \((U_2\cap U_4)_{10}=\mathbb C\chi_{10}\) for a
singular vector \(\chi_{10}\), and
\[
 \dim\ker G_{10}(1/2,h_0)=32.
\]
The \(42\) local Smith exponents of
\(G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)\) are
\[
 \bigl(0^{\,10},1^{\,31},2\bigr).
\]
In particular, the inverse Shapovalov matrix has a nonzero rank-one
coefficient at order \(x^{-2}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} with \((p,p')=(3,4)\) and
\((r,s)=(1,2)\) gives the levels \(\ell^\pm_1=2,4\) of \(\chi_2,\chi_4\),
\(\ell^+_2=10<\ell^-_2=14\), and \(\ell^\pm_3=24,30\).  Hence
\(J^2_{10}=\mathbb C\chi_{10}\), where \(\chi_{10}\) is the singular
vector of level \(10\), and \(J^3_{10}=0\).  As in
Proposition~\ref{prop:level-seven-smith}, a lowest vector of
\(U_2\cap U_4\) is singular and pulls back along \(j_{\chi_2}\) and
\(j_{\chi_4}\) to singular vectors of \(M(\frac12,\frac{33}{16})\) and
\(M(\frac12,\frac{65}{16})\), whose Kac determinants have no zero below
relative levels \(8\) and \(6\).  So \(U_2\cap U_4\) is zero below level
\(10\); it contains \(\chi_{10}=j_{\chi_2}(\psi_8)\) for the singular
vector \(\psi_8\) of Proposition~\ref{thm:level-ten-leading}, and since
every vector of \((U_2\cap U_4)_{10}\) is singular, uniqueness gives
\((U_2\cap U_4)_{10}=\mathbb C\chi_{10}\).  By Corollary~\ref{cor:ising-classes}, the only positive
Kac labels with \(\Delta_{r,s}=h_0\) and \(rs\leq10\) are \((1,2)\) and
\((2,2)\), so the determinant valuation is \(p(8)+p(6)=33\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} gives \(\dim J^1_{10}=32\).  Of the
\(p(10)=42\) Smith exponents, \(31\) equal \(1\), one equals \(2\), and the
remaining \(10\) vanish.  (This illustrates
Corollary~\ref{cor:det-overcounts}: the determinant valuation \(33\)
records the sum of the positive Smith exponents, while the pole order is
their maximum, \(2\).)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Representation-theoretic inputs and finite checks]
\label{rem:representation-inputs}
The embedding arguments above use three results on Virasoro Verma
modules: uniqueness, up to scalar, of a singular vector at a fixed level
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}; injectivity of a nonzero
homomorphism between Verma modules
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}; and the
Jantzen layers of Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, for which
\cite[Theorem~6.3(i)]{IoharaKoga2011} and
\cite[Theorem~B(e)]{Astashkevich1997} are independent sources.  At the
two levels used explicitly in this paper they are also checked without
the embedding diagram: exact Gram-matrix calculations give radical
dimensions \(11\) at level seven and \(32\) at level ten, while the
determinant valuations give the stated Smith profiles.  The all-order
confluence theorem does not depend on these inputs.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic leading coefficient at level ten]
\label{thm:level-ten-leading}
Normalize all singular vectors by setting their \(L_{-1}^{\ell}\)
coefficient equal to \(1\).  Let
\(\psi_8\in M(1/2,33/16)\) and
\(\psi_6\in M(1/2,65/16)\) be the singular vectors of levels \(8\) and
\(6\), respectively.  Then
\(\chi_{10}=j_{\chi_2}(\psi_8)=j_{\chi_4}(\psi_6)\) and
\[
 \langle\psi_8|V_d(1)|\psi_8\rangle={\mathcal P}_{4,2}(b_0,d),\qquad
 \langle\psi_6|V_d(1)|\psi_6\rangle={\mathcal P}_{1,6}(b_0,d).
\]
Write
\(G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3)\),
choose \(v_1\) with \(G_0v_1=-G_1\chi_{10}\), and set
\[
 \gamma_{10}
 =\chi_{10}^{\mathsf T}(G_2\chi_{10}+G_1v_1).
\]
Then \(\gamma_{10}\) is independent of the choice of \(v_1\),
\[
 \gamma_{10}=-\frac{1121229484375}{192},
\]
and
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to h_0}
 (\Delta-h_0)^2H_{10}(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =\frac{P_2(d){\mathcal P}_{4,2}(b_0,d)}{\gamma_{10}}
 =\frac{P_4(d){\mathcal P}_{1,6}(b_0,d)}{\gamma_{10}}.
\]
This is an intrinsic fixed-\(c\) expression for the spin-module
nontransverse Laurent coefficient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two compositions are nonzero singular vectors of total level \(10\)
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}, hence
proportional \cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}; their
\(L_{-1}^{10}\) coefficients are both \(1\), so they are equal.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-families}, the normalized families
\(\chi_{4,2}(b)\) and \(\chi_{1,6}(b)\) are regular at \(b_0\) with values
\(\psi_8\) and \(\psi_6\), and \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} specializes
to the displayed torus polynomials.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:level-ten-smith}, the second Smith layer
\(K_2\) of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-step-residue} is spanned by
\(\chi_{10}\) and the largest exponent is \(2\).  That proposition shows
that \(\gamma_{10}\) is choice-independent and nonzero and that
\([x^{-2}]G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)^{-1}=\chi_{10}\chi_{10}^{\mathsf T}/\gamma_{10}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization}, applied along either side of the
embedding diamond, gives
\(\langle\chi_{10}|V_d(1)|\chi_{10}\rangle=P_2{\mathcal P}_{4,2}
=P_4{\mathcal P}_{1,6}\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, all lower
levels have at most simple poles at \(h_0\), so multiplication by the
Euler product does not alter the \(x^{-2}\) coefficient at level ten.
This proves the limit formula.  Comparing it with the double-pole
coefficient of Theorem~\ref{thm:level-ten-principal-part} gives the value
of \(\gamma_{10}\).  Independently,
\path{tools/audit_grade10_direct.py}\ \path{--crossing-only} constructs
the first two Smith layers from the Gram matrix and obtains the same
scalar directly from the second crossing form, without using the
confluence calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with the pole-free expressions of Stocco]
\label{rem:stocco-comparison}
Stocco \cite[Sections~II.2--II.3 and Appendix~A]{Stocco2022} writes the

in equation~(II.11), orders three and four in Appendix~A.  Here his
deformation parameter has been written as \(b^2\); in our notation
\(\Delta_1=d\), and his
\(\Delta_{\langle r,s\rangle}\) is \(h_{r,s}(b)\).  We have verified in
exact arithmetic that his order-two and order-three numerators
reproduce the generic blocks \(H_2\) and \(H_3\) of
Definition~\ref{def:matrices} identically in \(b\), and that his
order-four numerator reproduces \(H_4\) at sample values of \(b\) and

reproduces the \(N\leq4\) list in
Appendix~\ref{app:expanded-data}.  At \(c=1/2\), collided labels
make his denominator vanish to higher order (for instance

order three), and the specialized numerators \(K_N(\Delta,d,-4/3)\)
acquire the compensating zeros, so that the actual pole multiplicities
are the \(m_{n,N}\) of Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments}.  The
present paper upgrades these closed low-order expressions to an
all-order fixed-\(c\) statement with a termination and equality proof.
\end{remark}

\section{Irreducible Ising specialization}\label{sec:irreducible}

\begin{proposition}[Identity insertion in the Verma recursion]
\label{prop:identity-verma}
For \(d=0\),
\[
 H_\Delta^{(1/2)}(0\mid q)=1
\]
as a rational function of \(\Delta\).  Thus specialization of the
meromorphic Verma block at a degenerate weight is not the irreducible
Ising trace; the latter requires taking the quotient by the radical.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the factor with \(k=l=1\)
in \({\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,0)\) is
\[
 \frac{(b+b^{-1})^2-(b+b^{-1})^2}{4}=0.
\]
Hence every generic residue \(R_a(t)\) vanishes identically at \(d=0\).
Definitions~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} and
\ref{def:collision-moments} then give
\(\widetilde A_{n,k,N}(0)=0\), hence \(A_{n,k,N}(0)=0\), for every
\(N\geq1\).
Equivalently, in the inverse-Shapovalov definition the identity insertion
has \(\rho_N=G_N\), so \(F_N=p(N)\) and eta reduction gives \(H=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced irreducible identity block]
\label{def:irreducible-identity}
For \(h\in\{0,1/2,1/16\}\), let \(L(1/2,h)\) be the irreducible quotient
of \(M(1/2,h)\), and define
\[
 \widehat H_h(q)
 =\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)
  \sum_{N\geq0}\dim L(1/2,h)_{h+N}\,q^N.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The three Ising characters]
\label{thm:ising-characters}
The quotient identity blocks of
Definition~\ref{def:irreducible-identity} are
\[
 \widehat H_0(q)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{((24j+1)^2-1)/48}
 -q^{((24j+7)^2-1)/48}
 \right),
\]
\[
 \widehat H_{1/2}(q)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{((24j+5)^2-25)/48}
 -q^{((24j+11)^2-25)/48}
 \right),
\]
and
\[
 \widehat H_{1/16}(q)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{((24j-2)^2-4)/48}
 -q^{((24j+10)^2-4)/48}
 \right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \chi_h(q)
 =q^{h-1/48}
  \frac{\widehat H_h(q)}
  {\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)}
\]
are the vacuum, energy, and spin Ising characters.  Their eta-reduced
beginnings are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat H_0&=1-q-q^6+q^{11}+q^{13}-q^{20}+\cdots,\\
 \widehat H_{1/2}&=1-q^2-q^3+q^7+q^{17}-q^{25}-q^{28}+\cdots,\\
 \widehat H_{1/16}&=1-q^2-q^4+q^{10}+q^{14}-q^{24}-q^{30}+\cdots.
 \end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Ising model is the minimal series \(c_{3,4}=1/2\).
The Class \(R^+\), Case \(1+\), BGG resolution in
\cite[Theorem~6.9]{IoharaKoga2011} is an exact complex of Verma modules.
Taking its Euler--Poincaré character gives, for an interior label
\((r,s)\),
\[
 \chi_{r,s}(q)
 =\frac1{\eta(q)}
 \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{(24j+4r-3s)^2/48}
 -q^{(24j+4r+3s)^2/48}
 \right).
\]
Use \((r,s)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)\), respectively.  Since
\[
 h_{r,s}=\frac{(4r-3s)^2-1}{48},\qquad
 \eta(q)=q^{1/24}\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m),
\]
factoring out \(q^{h_{r,s}-1/48}\) gives exactly the three displayed
\(\widehat H\)-series.  For any fixed power of \(q\), only finitely many
integers \(j\) contribute, so the Euler--Poincaré sums are
coefficientwise finite.  Direct evaluation of the exponents through
degree \(30\) gives the final three lines.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Character replay]
Through level \(30\), exact BGG numerator coefficients agree with an
independent free-fermion count: distinct positive-part partitions for the
spin module, and distinct odd-part partitions of \(2N\) and \(2N+1\), with
even and odd length respectively, for the vacuum and energy modules.  See
Remark~\ref{rem:audit-scope} for the replay.
\end{remark}

\section{Scope and outlook}\label{sec:scope}

Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} is a coefficientwise theorem for the
meromorphic Verma-module torus block at every fixed nonzero
\(b_\star\).  It has new content exactly when \(b_\star^2\) is rational;
otherwise it reduces to the generic recursion at \(b_\star\).  In
particular, it applies to every minimal-model central charge, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions} supplies the corresponding
collision classes.  The Ising specialization is developed in greater detail
because its classes, coefficient field, embedding diamonds, and exact local
computations are especially transparent.  The output is fixed at
\(c_\star\), while the construction uses the exact formal deformation
\(b=b_\star+t\) to combine resonant generic-recursion terms.  The method
should therefore be distinguished both from numerical regularization and
from a recursion formulated solely in intrinsic data of the specialized
category.

At any prescribed level the label sets and recursion trees are finite.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound} and
\ref{lem:precision-budget} give an a priori Laurent jet budget,
but do not claim a favorable complexity bound: exact rational
simplification can grow rapidly with the level.  For \(c_\star\neq1,25\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} and Remark~\ref{rem:jantzen-other}
identify every Smith layer with the embedding diagram and bound every pole
order by the Jantzen depth \(j_N(\lambda)\); the coefficientwise
confluence theorem does not depend on this identification.  The
independent level-ten checks are summarized in
Remark~\ref{rem:audit-scope}.

Open directions include uniform complexity bounds; the question of
when the block coefficient \(H_N\) attains the Jantzen bound
\(j_N(\lambda)\) for indeterminate \(d\), as it does at the two Ising
diamonds; the charges \(c_\star=1,25\), where the internal-weight line is
tangent to the Kac curves; an intrinsic fixed-central-charge recursion
that eliminates the formal deformation; and a corresponding recursion for irreducible minimal-model
traces at degenerate internal weights.  The BGG calculation of
Section~\ref{sec:irreducible} is only a quotient consistency check and is
logically separate from the confluence theorem.
