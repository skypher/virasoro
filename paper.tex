\begin{abstract}
Fix a nonzero algebraic Coulomb-gas parameter \(b_\star\).  We prove an
exact, finite confluence formula for every coefficient of the
eta-reduced torus one-point Virasoro block at
\(c_\star=1+6(b_\star+b_\star^{-1})^2\).  Generic Kac-recursion terms
are grouped by their common limiting internal weight.  Each coefficient
of the resulting fixed-central-charge principal part is a collision
moment: the constant term of a finite sum of Laurent germs in
\(t=b-b_\star\).  Separated pole sets make every collision class
individually regular, while a valuation majorant and a backward
precision lemma give an a priori finite jet budget.  Uniqueness of
partial fractions identifies the construction coefficientwise in
\(q\) with the inverse-Shapovalov definition of the meromorphic Verma
block.  The formal germ is an exact algebraic device, not a numerical
regularization or an intrinsic recursion in the specialized
representation category.  At the Ising charge we classify the collision
classes explicitly and use local Smith and crossing forms to interpret
the first two embedding diamonds, including the first double pole at
level seven and the level-ten spin example.  Ancillary scripts replay
the finite computations in exact rational arithmetic.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace Virasoro algebra; conformal blocks; torus
one-point block; Zamolodchikov recursion; minimal models; Ising model.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{MSC2020 Classification.}\enspace
17B68; 17B10; 81T40.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Residue recursions for Virasoro blocks originate with Zamolodchikov's
recursive representations of the sphere four-point block
\cite{Zamolodchikov1984,Zamolodchikov1987}; see
\cite{ChoCollierYin2017} for a modern general treatment.  For generic
central charge, the eta-reduced torus one-point Virasoro block obeys the
residue recursion conjectured by Poghossian
\cite{Poghossian2009} and derived in
\cite[Section~3, equations~(7)--(9)]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}.
Poghosyan and Poghosyan recently reviewed the Liouville and
\(\mathcal W_3\) torus one-point recursions and derived their light-limit
forms \cite{PoghosyanPoghosyan2026}.  Their regime is \(b\to0\), hence
\(c\to\infty\), with fixed light dimensions; it is complementary to the
present problem of exact specialization at a fixed algebraic \(b_\star\),
especially rational \(b_\star^2\).
At rational values of the Coulomb-gas parameter, distinct Kac labels have
the same degenerate weight and the individual generic-recursion summands
develop parameter poles.  Javerzat, Santachiara, and Foda analyzed the
cancellation of these non-physical poles in examples and in the
identity-insertion character problem
\cite[Sections~4--6, especially equations~(60)--(61)]%
{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.  Stocco subsequently constructed
central-charge-pole-free torus expressions through fourth order and proposed
an all-order Laurent-expansion procedure, whose cancellation and algebraic
form were left conjectural
\cite[Sections~II.2--II.3 and Appendix~A]{Stocco2022}.  Ribault identifies
a recursion at rational \(\beta^2\) as an open problem and singles out
torus one-point Virasoro blocks as its simplest incarnation
\cite[Section~4.3.3]{Ribault2026}.
Higher-order internal-weight poles and rational limits of sphere four-point
blocks were also analyzed by Ribault \cite[Section~3]{Ribault2019}; those
results provide important precedent for the confluence mechanism, although
they do not give a finite torus one-point recursion.

This paper solves a precise coefficientwise version of that problem at every
nonzero algebraic value \(b_\star\) of the Coulomb-gas parameter.  The object
is the meromorphic Verma-module block, and the conclusion is coefficientwise
in \(q\).  At every fixed level we partition the generic Kac summands by their
common limiting weight at \(b=b_\star\).  Each collision class has a regular
sum, and every coefficient of its internal-weight principal part is a finite
constant term of finite Laurent sums.  The shifted recursion decreases the
remaining level, while a recursive valuation majorant supplies an a priori
finite jet budget.

The construction removes the divergent, non-physical poles in the
deformation parameter; the genuine Laurent poles in the internal weight
\(\Delta\) remain.  It uses the formal germ \(b=b_\star+t\) to combine
generic summands before taking a constant term.  Thus it gives an exact
formula at fixed \(c_\star\), with no numerical \(\varepsilon\)-limit, but it
is not an intrinsic recursion formulated solely in the specialized
representation category.  Nor does it by itself construct irreducible
minimal-model traces at degenerate internal weights.

In the notation of
Sections~\ref{sec:partial-fractions}--\ref{sec:all-order}, the main
result reads: for every level \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 &=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},\\
 A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 &=\operatorname{CT}_t
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 R_a(t)\,S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\,
 \bigl(h_a(t)-\lambda\bigr)^{k-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(H_N\) is the \(N\)-th eta-reduced block coefficient,
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\) is the finite set of limiting Kac weights,
\({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is the finite class of labels
\(a=(r,s)\) with \(rs\leq N\) that collide at \(\lambda\),
\(t=b-b_\star\) is the exact Coulomb-gas germ, and \(R_a\) is the generic
residue coefficient of the label \(a\),
\(S_{a,M}\) is the shifted Laurent block of
Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}, \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) extracts
the constant term of a Laurent germ, and \(M^\star_{\lambda,N}\) is the finite
structural pole bound of Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments}.  Every
ingredient is a finite jet of an explicit rational function of \(b\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} proves this identity: the
constant-term moments on the right are exactly the partial-fraction
coefficients of the inverse-Shapovalov block, at every power of
\(q\).  At minimal-model values it applies directly, and the collision
classes are the elementary Diophantine classes of
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-recursion}
records the Ising specialization, where the coefficient field improves
from \(\mathbb Q(b_\star)\) to \(\mathbb Q\).

The local calculations exhibit how the algebraic confluence records
Virasoro embedding geometry.  The earliest collision is the transverse
energy-module collision at level three; its two branches first meet at level
seven and create the first double pole.  The fixed-\(c\) formulas are
Theorems~\ref{thm:first-recursive-residue} and
\ref{thm:level-seven-principal-part}.  The spin-module collision at level
four and its larger level-ten diamond are treated in
Theorems~\ref{thm:spin-recursive-residue} and
\ref{thm:level-ten-principal-part}.  Finally,
Theorem~\ref{thm:ising-characters} distinguishes the Verma identity
specialization from the irreducible quotient
(Proposition~\ref{prop:identity-verma}) and records the three standard
Ising characters as a separate BGG consistency check.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:blocks} defines the block and its eta reduction and
collects notation; the symbol families are indexed in
Remarks~\ref{rem:notation} and~\ref{rem:symbols}.
Section~\ref{sec:resonance} classifies the minimal-model collision classes
and records the Ising specialization.
Section~\ref{sec:partial-fractions} proves the finite coefficientwise
partial-fraction expansion and states the main theorem before the examples.
Section~\ref{sec:smith} develops the
local Smith and crossing-form toolkit and computes the transverse
collisions at levels three and four.
Section~\ref{sec:generic-data} recalls the generic residue data and

Section~\ref{sec:all-order} proves the general finite confluence theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}) and its Ising corollary.
Sections~\ref{sec:energy-diamond} and~\ref{sec:spin-diamond} work out
the two embedding diamonds---the energy diamond with its first double
pole at level seven, and the spin diamond at level ten;
Corollary~\ref{cor:recover-low-confluence} identifies these local

Section~\ref{sec:low-level-output} summarizes the output through level four

Section~\ref{sec:irreducible} treats the irreducible specialization and

\section{Blocks and the reduced series}\label{sec:blocks}

\begin{definition}[Virasoro Verma module]
The Virasoro algebra has generators \(L_n\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), and central
element \(c\), with
\[
 [L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}
 +\frac{c}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}.
\]
The Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) is generated by a vector
\(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) such that
\[
 L_0\lvert\Delta\rangle=\Delta\lvert\Delta\rangle,\qquad
 L_n\lvert\Delta\rangle=0\quad(n>0).
\]
For a partition \(Y=(y_1\geq\cdots\geq y_\ell)\), set
\(L_{-Y}=L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_\ell}\), \(|Y|=\sum_i y_i\), and
\(\ell(Y)=\ell\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shapovalov and torus matrices]\label{def:matrices}
At level \(N\), let
\[
 G_N(c,\Delta)_{Y,Y'}
 =\langle\Delta|L_YL_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle ,
 \qquad |Y|=|Y'|=N,
\]
where \(L_Y=L_{y_\ell}\cdots L_{y_1}\).
For a primary field \(V_d(z)\) normalized by
\(\langle\Delta|V_d(z)|\Delta\rangle=z^{-d}\), let
\[
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}
 =\langle\Delta|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle .
\]
Whenever \(G_N\) is invertible, define
\[
 F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{|Y|=|Y'|=N}
 \bigl(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1}\bigr)_{Y,Y'}
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'} .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced block]
Write
\[
 \mathcal F_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)
 =q^{\Delta-c/24}\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N
\]
and define
\[
 H_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)
 =\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)
   \sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N
 =\sum_{N\geq0}H_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial matrix entries]\label{lem:polynomial-entries}
For fixed \(N\), every entry of \(G_N(c,\Delta)\) belongs to
\(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta]\), and every entry of
\(\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,d]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Repeated use of the Virasoro commutator moves every positive mode in
\(L_Y\) to the right.  Positive modes annihilate
\(\lvert\Delta\rangle\), while each \(L_0\) and the central element act by
\(\Delta\) and \(c\), respectively.  This gives the first assertion.

For the second assertion use, in addition,
\[
 [L_n,V_d(z)]
 =z^n\bigl(z\partial_z+(n+1)d\bigr)V_d(z).
\]
Move positive modes through \(V_d(z)\), then move the remaining negative
modes through \(V_d(z)\) toward the bra.  The process terminates because
each commutation removes one mode from one side of the insertion.
The remaining derivatives act on \(z^{-d}\); after setting \(z=1\), their
values are polynomials in \(d\).  The other commutators contribute only
polynomials in \(c\) and \(\Delta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terminology and notation]\label{rem:notation}
The \(L_0\)-offset \(N\) of a graded component is called its
\emph{level}.  For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), we write
\(\ell_a=rs\) for the level of its singular vector.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and
Cramer's rule, each \(F_N\) and \(H_N\) is a rational function of
\((c,\Delta)\) and a polynomial in \(d\); note \(H_0=1\).  For a rational
function \(f\) of \(\Delta\) and a point \(\lambda\), we write
\(\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) for the principal part of \(f\) at \(\lambda\),
that is, the sum of the negative-power terms of its Laurent expansion,
and \(\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) for the coefficient of
\((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\).
\end{remark}

\end{remark}

\section{Algebraic specializations and Ising resonance classes}
\label{sec:resonance}

\begin{definition}[Kac weights and fixed algebraic specializations]
\label{def:fixed-specialization}
For \(b\in\mathbb C^\times\), set
\[
 c(b)=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2,
 \qquad
 h_{r,s}(b)
 =\frac{(b+b^{-1})^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2}{4}.
\]
Thus \(h_{r,s}(b)=\Delta_{r,s}(c(b))\) is the standard Kac
degenerate weight.  Fix an algebraic number
\(b_\star\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}\), and put
\[
 c_\star=c(b_\star),\qquad
 \Lambda_N(b_\star)
 =\{h_{r,s}(b_\star):r,s>0,\ rs\leq N\}.
\]
For \(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\), define the finite collision class
\[
 {\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2:
      rs\leq N,\ h_{r,s}(b_\star)=\lambda\}.
\]

For the Ising specialization, write
\[
 b_0=\frac{2i}{\sqrt3},\qquad c(b_0)=\frac12.
\]
For \(r,s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), define
\[
 \Delta_{r,s}
 =\frac{(4r-3s)^2-1}{48}.
\]
Thus \(\Delta_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b_0)\).  Set
\[
 \lambda_n=\frac{n^2-1}{48}\qquad(n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal-model collision classes]
\label{prop:minimal-collisions}
Let \(p,p'\) be coprime positive integers and choose
\(b_\star^2=-p'/p\).  Then
\[
 c_\star=1-\frac{6(p'-p)^2}{pp'},\qquad
 h_{r,s}(b_\star)
 =\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}.
\]
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\), their specialized weights
are equal if and only if, for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 (r',s')=(r+p\ell,s+p'\ell)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (r',s')=(p\ell-r,p'\ell-s).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formulas follow by substituting \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) in
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.  Equality of the two weights is
equivalent to
\[
 p'r'-ps'=\pm(p'r-ps).
\]
For the plus sign, coprimality gives
\((r'-r,s'-s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\); for the minus sign it gives
\((r'+r,s'+s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\).  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact collision classification]\label{prop:collisions}
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\),
\(\Delta_{r,s}=\Delta_{r',s'}\) if and only if one of the following holds
for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\):
\[
 (r',s')=(r+3\ell,s+4\ell),
\]
or
\[
 (r',s')=(3\ell-r,4\ell-s).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equality of the two weights is equivalent to
\[
 4r'-3s'=\pm(4r-3s).
\]
If the sign is \(+\), then
\(4(r'-r)=3(s'-s)\).  Since \(\gcd(3,4)=1\), there is an
\(\ell\in\mathbb Z\) with
\((r'-r,s'-s)=(3\ell,4\ell)\).
If the sign is \(-\), then
\(4(r'+r)=3(s'+s)\), and the same coprimality argument gives
\((r'+r,s'+s)=(3\ell,4\ell)\).
The converse follows by direct substitution.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Distinct weights and local finiteness]\label{cor:local-finite}
The set of distinct positive-label degenerate weights at \(c=1/2\) is
\[
 \{\lambda_n:n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\}.
\]
Every collision class contains infinitely many positive pairs.  For each
fixed level \(N\), only finitely many classes contain a pair with \(rs\leq N\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every positive pair, \(\Delta_{r,s}=\lambda_{|4r-3s|}\).
Conversely, given \(n\geq0\), choose a sufficiently large positive
\(r\equiv n\pmod 3\); then
\(s=(4r-n)/3\) is a positive integer and \(4r-3s=n\).
Starting with any positive pair in a class, the pairs
\((r+3\ell,s+4\ell)\), \(\ell\geq0\), are positive and remain in the
same class.  Finally, \(rs\leq N\) implies \(r\leq N\) and \(s\leq N\),
so only finitely many pairs, and hence classes, occur.
\end{proof}

\section{Coefficientwise partial fractions}\label{sec:partial-fractions}

\begin{lemma}[Large internal weight]\label{lem:large-weight}
For fixed \(c,d,N\),
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to\infty}F_N(c,\Delta,d)=p(N),
\]
where \(p(N)\) is the number of partitions of \(N\).  Consequently,
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to\infty}H_N(c,\Delta,d)=\delta_{N,0}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a partition \(Y\), put \(a_Y=\ell(Y)\), and rescale the PBW vector
\(L_{-Y}\lvert\Delta\rangle\) by \(\Delta^{-a_Y/2}\).
Commuting positive modes to the right shows that
\[
 \deg_\Delta G_N(Y,Y')\leq\min(a_Y,a_{Y'}).
\]
If \(a_Y=a_{Y'}\) and \(Y\neq Y'\), the degree is at most \(a_Y-1\).
For \(Y=Y'\), the leading term is
\[
 \left(\prod_{j\geq1}m_j(Y)!(2j)^{m_j(Y)}\right)
 \Delta^{a_Y},
\]
where \(m_j(Y)\) is the multiplicity of \(j\) in \(Y\).
Indeed, in a fully commuted matrix element every factor of \(\Delta\)
arises from an occurrence of \(L_0\), that is, from a contraction of a
positive mode of \(L_Y\) against a negative mode of \(L_{-Y'}\) of the
same index through the \(2jL_0\) term of \([L_j,L_{-j}]\).  Each
contraction consumes one mode on each side, so a single term carries at
most \(\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\) factors of \(\Delta\); this proves the degree
bound.  If \(a_Y=a_{Y'}\) and the degree is attained, every mode of
\(Y\) is contracted against a mode of \(Y'\) of equal index, which
forces \(Y=Y'\); hence off-diagonal entries of equal length have degree
at most \(a_Y-1\).  For \(Y=Y'\), the top-degree terms are the perfect
matchings of equal indices: the \(m_j(Y)!\) matchings of the modes of
index \(j\) each contribute \((2j\Delta)^{m_j(Y)}\) to leading order,
giving the displayed leading term.

Apply the same counting with one \(V_d\) insertion.  The number of
\(\Delta\)-factors of a term is again the number of positive--negative
contractions producing \(L_0\).  A term that uses no Ward commutator
\([L_n,V_d(z)]\) reproduces the corresponding Shapovalov term exactly,
because \(\langle\Delta|V_d(1)|\Delta\rangle=1\); a term that uses at
least one Ward commutator has consumed a mode without producing
\(L_0\), so its degree is strictly below \(\min(a_Y,a_{Y'})\).
It follows that, after the above rescaling, both \(G_N\) and \(\rho_N\)
converge to the same invertible diagonal matrix \(D_N\).
Therefore
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to\infty}F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =\operatorname{tr}(D_N^{-1}D_N)=p(N).
\]
If \(e_j\) are the coefficients of
\(\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)\), then
\[
 H_N=\sum_{j=0}^N e_jF_{N-j}.
\]
The generating-function identity
\[
 \left(\sum_{j\geq0}e_jq^j\right)
 \left(\sum_{k\geq0}p(k)q^k\right)=1
\]
gives the second limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite pole expansion at an algebraic specialization]
\label{thm:general-partial-fractions}
Fix \(b_\star\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}\).  Then \(H_0=1\),
and for every \(N\geq1\) there are unique coefficients
\[
 A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q(b_\star)[d],
 \qquad \lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star),\quad k\geq1,
\]
only finitely many of them nonzero, such that
\[
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}\ \sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\]
If at least one \(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\) is nonzero, the largest such
\(k\) is the multiplicity of the pole of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda\); if they
all vanish, \(\lambda\) is a removable candidate pole.  Consequently,
with
\[
 A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)
 =\sum_{N\geq1}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)q^N,
\]
one has the coefficientwise locally finite expansion
\[
 H_\Delta^{(c_\star)}(d\mid q)
 =1+\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},
\]
where at each power of \(q\) the first sum is over the corresponding
finite set \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries},
\(F_N=\operatorname{tr}(G_N^{-1}\rho_N^{\mathsf T})\) is rational in
\(\Delta\), and its poles are contained in the zero locus of
\(\det G_N\).  The Kac determinant formula
\cite{Kac1979,FeiginFuchs1982}, in the form of
\cite[Theorem~4.2 and equations (4.16)--(4.21)]{IoharaKoga2011}, gives,
up to a nonzero factor independent of \(\Delta\),
\[
 \det G_N(c,\Delta)
 =C_N\prod_{\substack{r,s>0\\rs\leq N}}
   \bigl(\Delta-\Delta_{r,s}(c)\bigr)^{p(N-rs)}.
\]
At \(c=c_\star\), the distinct zeros belong to the finite set
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).  Since \(H_N\) is a finite integer linear
combination of \(F_0,\ldots,F_N\), it has the same pole containment.
Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} shows that \(H_N\) tends to zero at infinity
for \(N\geq1\), so its partial-fraction expansion has no polynomial part
and is unique.

The matrix entries belong to \(\mathbb Q(c_\star)[\Delta,d]\), and every
pole \(\lambda\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(b_\star)\).  Laurent expansion at
each pole therefore gives
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star)[d]\).
Coefficientwise local finiteness follows because \(rs\leq N\) leaves
only finitely many labels.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite coefficientwise pole expansion]\label{thm:partial-fractions}
At \(c=1/2\), \(H_0=1\), and for every \(N\geq1\) there are unique
coefficients
\(A_{n,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q[d]\), only finitely many of them nonzero,
such that
\[
 H_N\left(\frac12,\Delta,d\right)
 =\sum_{\substack{n\geq0\\
       \exists\,r,s>0:\ rs\leq N,\ |4r-3s|=n}}
   \ \sum_{k\geq1}
   \frac{A_{n,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k}.
\]
The largest \(k\) with \(A_{n,k,N}\neq0\) is the multiplicity of the
pole of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda_n\).
In particular, setting
\[
 A_{n,k}(d;q)=\sum_{N\geq1}A_{n,k,N}(d)q^N
\]
gives a coefficientwise locally finite expansion
\[
 H_\Delta^{(1/2)}(d\mid q)
 =1+\sum_{n\geq0}\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A_{n,k}(d;q)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions} at
\(b_\star=b_0\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:collisions} identifies its pole
set with the stated \(\lambda_n\)'s.  The specialized inverse-Shapovalov
coefficient has rational coefficients, and all \(\lambda_n\) are
rational, so uniqueness of partial fractions improves the coefficient
field from \(\mathbb Q(b_0)\) to \(\mathbb Q\).  Local finiteness is
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-finite}.
\end{proof}

The conceptual result can now be stated independently of the Ising
calculations.  Its collision moments and finite precision budget are defined
in Section~\ref{sec:all-order}, after the generic residue data; the proof is
given there as well.

\begin{theorem}[Finite algebraic confluence at fixed \(b_\star\)]
\label{thm:general-confluence}
Fix \(b_\star\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}\).  For every
\(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}
        {(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\end{equation}
The sums and every constant-term calculation are finite,
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q(b_\star)[d]\),
and the level-decreasing recursion of
Definitions~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}--\ref{def:collision-moments}
terminates.  Its backward precision rule supplies a finite truncation order
for every intermediate Laurent germ.  Moreover,
\[
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}
 =A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N},\qquad
 m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 \leq M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 \leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N},
\]
where \(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) is the exact pole multiplicity.  Consequently
the resulting locally finite series agrees coefficient by coefficient in
\(q\) with the inverse-Shapovalov definition of the meromorphic Verma-module
torus one-point block.
\end{theorem}

\section{Local Smith exponents and transverse collisions}\label{sec:smith}

\begin{lemma}[Pole order from local invariant factors]\label{lem:smith}
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[x]]\), and let \(G(x)\in M_m(R)\) have nonzero
determinant.  Suppose the Smith exponents of \(G\) are
\(0\leq e_1\leq\cdots\leq e_m\).  Then
\[
 \ord_x\det G=\sum_{i=1}^m e_i,\qquad
 \dim\ker G(0)=\#\{i:e_i>0\},
\]
and the pole order of \(G(x)^{-1}\) is \(e_m\).
In particular, if
\[
 \ord_x\det G=\dim\ker G(0)=k,
\]
then every positive Smith exponent equals \(1\), and \(G^{-1}\) has at
most a simple pole.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(R\) is a discrete valuation ring, there are
\(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R)\) such that
\[
 UGV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_m}).
\]
Taking determinants gives the first identity.  Reducing modulo \(x\)
gives the second.  Inverting the displayed Smith form gives pole order
\(e_m\).  If the sum of the \(k\) positive integers \(e_i\) is \(k\),
then each of them is \(1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Transverse inverse residue]\label{prop:transverse-residue}
Let \(V\) be finite dimensional, let
\(G(x)\colon V\to V^*\) be a symmetric matrix over \(\mathbb C[[x]]\)
with nonzero determinant, and put \(K=\ker G(0)\).  Let
\(\iota\colon K\hookrightarrow V\) be the inclusion and define the crossing
form
\[
 \beta(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}G'(0)v,\qquad u,v\in K.
\]
If \(\beta\) is nondegenerate, then \(G(x)^{-1}\) has only a simple pole and
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{x=0}G(x)^{-1}
 =\iota\,\beta^{-1}\iota^*.
\]
Moreover, \(\beta\) is nondegenerate if and only if
\[
 \ord_x\det G=\dim K.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a complement \(C\) of \(K\).  Since \(K\) is the radical of the
symmetric form \(G(0)\), its restriction \(A\) to \(C\) is nondegenerate.
In the decomposition \(V=K\oplus C\), one therefore has
\[
 G(x)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  xB+O(x^2)&xD+O(x^2)\\
  xD^{\mathsf T}+O(x^2)&A+O(x)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(B\) is the matrix of \(\beta\).  The Schur complement of the
lower-right block is
\[
 S(x)=xB+O(x^2).
\]
If \(B\) is invertible, block inversion gives
\[
 G(x)^{-1}
 =\frac1x
 \begin{pmatrix}B^{-1}&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+O(1),
\]
which is the coordinate expression of the asserted residue.  Also
\[
 \det G(x)=\det(A+O(x))\det S(x).
\]
Every entry of \(S\) is divisible by \(x\), so its determinant is divisible
by \(x^{\dim K}\), with equality of orders precisely when
\(\det B\neq0\).  This proves the final equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-step inverse principal part]
\label{prop:two-step-residue}
Let
\[
 G(x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3)
\]
be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue}, and put
\[
 K_1=\ker G_0,\qquad
 \beta_1(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}G_1v,\qquad
 K_2=\ker(\beta_1\colon K_1\to K_1^*).
\]
For \(v\in K_2\), choose \(v_1\in V\) satisfying
\[
 G_0v_1=-G_1v
\]
and define
\[
 \beta_2(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}(G_2v+G_1v_1),
 \qquad u,v\in K_2.
\]
Then \(v_1\) exists, \(\beta_2\) is independent of its choice, and
\(\beta_2\) is symmetric.  Without any nondegeneracy assumption,
\[
 \#\{i:e_i\geq1\}=\dim K_1,\qquad
 \#\{i:e_i\geq2\}=\dim K_2.
\]
\(\beta_2\) is nondegenerate if and only if all Smith exponents are at most
\(2\).  In that case,
\[
 [x^{-2}]G(x)^{-1}=\iota_2\beta_2^{-1}\iota_2^*,
\]
where \(\iota_2\colon K_2\hookrightarrow V\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because \(G_0\) is symmetric,
\(\operatorname{im}G_0\) is the annihilator of \(K_1\).
For \(v\in K_2\) and \(u\in K_1\),
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}G_1v=\beta_1(u,v)=0.
\]
Thus \(G_1v\in\operatorname{im}G_0\), proving the existence of \(v_1\).
Two choices differ by an element \(k\in K_1\), and
\(u^{\mathsf T}G_1k=\beta_1(u,k)=0\) for \(u\in K_2\), proving
choice-independence.

Choose a complement to \(K_1\) as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue}, and first take the Schur
complement of its nondegenerate block.  This produces a symmetric matrix on
\(K_1\) of the form
\[
 S(x)=xB_1+x^2B_2+O(x^3),
\]
where \(B_1\) represents \(\beta_1\), and the restriction of \(B_2\) to
\(\ker B_1=K_2\) is the displayed form \(\beta_2\).  This also proves its
symmetry.  The positive Smith exponents of \(G\) are the Smith exponents of
\(S\).  Dividing \(S\) by \(x\) and reducing modulo \(x\) gives \(B_1\).
The Smith normal form, first for \(G\) and then for \(x^{-1}S\), therefore
gives
\[
 \#\{i:e_i\geq1\}=\dim K_1,\qquad
 \#\{i:e_i\geq2\}=\dim\ker B_1=\dim K_2.
\]
This count does not require the second crossing form to be nondegenerate.

Now choose a complement \(E\) to \(K_2\) in \(K_1\).
The restriction of \(B_1\) to \(E\) is nondegenerate, and a second Schur
complement gives
\[
 S(x)\sim
 \begin{pmatrix}
  x^2\beta_2+O(x^3)&0\\
  0&xB_1|_E+O(x^2)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The second block form shows that \(\beta_2\) is nondegenerate precisely when
no Smith exponent exceeds \(2\).  In that case block inversion yields the
asserted \(x^{-2}\) coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Determinant multiplicity does not determine block pole
order]\label{cor:det-overcounts}
At a collided Kac weight, the multiplicity of the corresponding factor in
\(\det G_N\) is only the sum of the positive local Smith exponents.
The inverse Shapovalov pole order is their maximum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} with \(x=\Delta-\lambda\), where \(\lambda\)
is the collided Kac weight.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Torus form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-factorization}
Let \(\chi\in M(c,h)\) be a singular vector of level \(\ell\), normalized
by a fixed PBW coefficient, and let
\[
 P_\chi(d)=\langle\chi|V_d(1)|\chi\rangle .
\]
For partitions \(Y,Y'\) of the same integer \(m\),
\[
 \langle\chi|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\chi\rangle
 =P_\chi(d)\,
 \rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)_{Y,Y'}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The assignment
\[
 L_{-Y}|h+\ell\rangle\longmapsto L_{-Y}\chi
\]
is a homomorphism from \(M(c,h+\ell)\) to \(M(c,h)\), because \(\chi\)
is singular and has \(L_0\)-weight \(h+\ell\).  Pull the torus
three-point form on \(M(c,h)\) back along this homomorphism in both module
arguments.  Commuting one Virasoro mode at a time through \(V_d(z)\) shows
that the pulled-back form obeys the same Ward recursion as
\(\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)\).  That recursion terminates at the highest-weight
matrix element and therefore determines every descendant matrix element
uniquely.  The pulled-back highest-weight value is \(P_\chi(d)\), whereas
the normalization
\(\langle h+\ell|V_d(1)|h+\ell\rangle=1\) gives highest-weight value
\(1\) for \(\rho_m\).  The displayed identity follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First crossing form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-crossing}
Fix \(c\) and let \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\in M(c,h_0)\) be singular of level
\(\ell\), where the PBW operator \(P\in U(\Vir_-)\) is held fixed as \(h\)
varies.  Set
\[
 b_\chi=
 \left.\frac{d}{dh}
 \langle h|\omega(P)P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0},
\]
where \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  For partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dh}
 \langle h|\omega(P)L_YL_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}
 =b_\chi\,G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the Shapovalov form of \(M(c,h)\), write
\[
 S_h(u,v)=\langle h|\omega(u)v|h\rangle,
 \qquad \widetilde u=uP\quad(u\in U(\Vir_-)).
\]
The derivative on the left defines a bilinear form
\[
 C(u,v)
 =\left.\frac{d}{dh}S_h(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)\right|_{h=h_0}
\]
on the PBW space of \(M(c,h_0+\ell)\).  At \(h=h_0\), the map
\[
 j_\chi\colon M(c,h_0+\ell)\longrightarrow M(c,h_0),
 \qquad u|h_0+\ell\rangle\longmapsto u\chi ,
\]
is a module homomorphism, and its image lies in the radical of the
Shapovalov form.

We claim that \(C\) is contravariant for the module structure of
\(M(c,h_0+\ell)\).  Contravariance in negative modes,
\(C(L_{-n}u,v)=C(u,\omega(L_{-n})v)\), holds at every \(h\) because the
Shapovalov form itself is contravariant and \(\widetilde{L_{-n}u}
=L_{-n}\widetilde u\).  For a positive mode, fix \(n>0\) and
\(v\in U(\Vir_-)\), and let \(w_0\in U(\Vir_-)\) be the PBW
reduction of \(L_n v\) in \(M(c,h_0+\ell)\), so that
\(L_n\,v|h_0+\ell\rangle=w_0|h_0+\ell\rangle\).  In \(M(c,h)\) set
\[
 D(h)=L_n\widetilde v\,|h\rangle-\widetilde{w_0}\,|h\rangle .
\]
Each coefficient of \(D(h)\) in the PBW basis is polynomial in \(h\),
and \(D(h_0)=0\) because \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\) is singular and
\(j_\chi\) intertwines the actions at \(h=h_0\).  Hence
\(D(h)=(h-h_0)\widetilde D(h)\) with \(\widetilde D\) polynomial in
\(h\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac{d}{dh}\Big|_{h_0}S_h(\widetilde u,L_n\widetilde v)
 =\frac{d}{dh}\Big|_{h_0}S_h(\widetilde u,\widetilde{w_0})
 +S_{h_0}\bigl(\widetilde u,\widetilde D(h_0)\bigr).
\]
The last term vanishes because \(\widetilde u\,|h_0\rangle
=j_\chi(u|h_0+\ell\rangle)\) lies in the radical of \(S_{h_0}\).  Since
\(S_h(\widetilde u,L_n\widetilde v)
=S_h(\widetilde{L_{-n}u},\widetilde v)\), this proves
\(C(L_{-n}u,v)=C(u,w_{h_0})\), that is, contravariance in positive
modes.

A contravariant form on a Verma module is uniquely determined by its
highest-weight value, because PBW commutation recursively reduces every
matrix element to that value.  Here the highest-weight value is
\(b_\chi\), so \(C=b_\chi G_m(c,h_0+\ell)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Earliest Ising collision]\label{prop:first-collision}
The first repeated Ising Kac weight occurs at level \(3\):
\[
 \Delta_{2,1}=\Delta_{1,3}=\frac12,
 \qquad \ell_{2,1}=2,\quad \ell_{1,3}=3.
\]
In \(M(1/2,1/2)\), the vectors
\[
 \xi_2=
 \left(L_{-1}^2-\frac43L_{-2}\right)|\tfrac12\rangle
\]
and
\[
 \xi_3=
 \left(L_{-1}^3-3L_{-2}L_{-1}
       +\frac34L_{-3}\right)|\tfrac12\rangle
\]
are singular.  At level \(3\),
\[
 \ker G_3(1/2,1/2)
 =\operatorname{span}\{L_{-1}\xi_2,\xi_3\},
\]
and the local Smith exponents are \((0,1,1)\).  With the
\(L_{-1}^{\ell}\)-coefficient normalized to \(1\), their crossing scalars
are
\[
 b_{\xi_2}=\frac{28}{9},\qquad
 b_{\xi_3}=-\frac{105}{8},
\]
and the level-three crossing form in the ordered basis
\((L_{-1}\xi_2,\xi_3)\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}140/9&0\\0&-105/8\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \frac{28}{9}G_1(1/2,5/2)&0\\0&-105/8
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Moreover, the diagonal torus matrix elements are
\[
 E_2(d):=\langle\xi_2|V_d(1)|\xi_2\rangle
 =\frac{d(d-1)(3d-14)(3d-5)}9,
\]
and
\[
 E_3(d):=\langle\xi_3|V_d(1)|\xi_3\rangle
 =\frac{d(d-1)(2d-15)(2d-7)(2d-5)(2d-1)}{16}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The collision statement follows immediately from
\(\Delta_{r,s}=\lambda_{|4r-3s|}\): the labels of levels at most \(2\)
have distinct values of \(|4r-3s|\), whereas both displayed labels have
value \(5\).  Direct commutation with \(L_1,L_2,L_3\) proves that the two
vectors are singular.  In the ordered basis
\((L_{-3},L_{-2}L_{-1},L_{-1}^3)|\Delta\rangle\),
\[
 \det G_3(1/2,\Delta)
 =12\Delta^2(2\Delta-1)^2(3\Delta-5)(16\Delta-1).
\]
At \(\Delta=1/2\) the matrix has rank one, and
\[
 L_{-1}\xi_2=
 \left(-\frac43L_{-3}-\frac43L_{-2}L_{-1}
 +L_{-1}^3\right)|\tfrac12\rangle
\]
together with \(\xi_3\) spans its radical.  The determinant valuation and
nullity are both two, so Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} gives the Smith profile.
Finally,
\[
 b_{\xi_j}
 =\left.
 \frac{d}{d\Delta}\langle\xi_j|\xi_j\rangle
 \right|_{\Delta=1/2}
\]
and direct multiplication by \(\partial_\Delta G_3|_{\Delta=1/2}\)
gives the displayed level-three crossing matrix.  The Ward recursion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} gives the two displayed torus
polynomials.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First fixed-\(c\) recursive residue]
\label{thm:first-recursive-residue}
At \(c=1/2\), the earliest collided coefficient has the simple principal
part
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=1/2}H_3(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =\frac9{28}E_2(d)H_1(1/2,5/2,d)
  -\frac8{105}E_3(d).
\]
Every quantity on the right is evaluated directly at \(c=1/2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The block-diagonal crossing form in
Proposition~\ref{prop:first-collision} has highest-weight inverse scalars
\(9/28\) and \(-8/105\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} therefore give
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=1/2}F_2=\frac9{28}E_2,
\qquad
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=1/2}F_3
 =\frac9{28}E_2F_1(1/2,5/2,d)-\frac8{105}E_3.
\]
The Euler product gives \(H_3=F_3-F_2-F_1\); since
\(G_1(1/2,\Delta)=(2\Delta)\), the coefficient \(F_1\) is regular at
\(\Delta=\tfrac12\), so
\(\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=1/2}H_3
=\operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=1/2}(F_3-F_2)\).  The result
follows from \(H_1=F_1-F_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[First spin-module collision]\label{prop:spin-collision}
At level \(4\), the labels \((1,2)\) and \((2,2)\) collide at

\begin{proposition}[Singular data at the first spin collision]
\label{prop:spin-singular-data}
Put \(h_0=1/16\).  The following vectors in \(M(1/2,h_0)\) are singular:
\[
 \chi_2=
 \left(L_{-1}^2-\frac34L_{-2}\right)|h_0\rangle
\]
and
\[
 \chi_4=
 \left(
 L_{-1}^4-\frac{25}{6}L_{-2}L_{-1}^2
 +\frac{49}{144}L_{-2}^2+\frac{11}{6}L_{-3}L_{-1}
 -\frac14L_{-4}
 \right)|h_0\rangle .
\]
At level \(4\),
\[
 \ker G_4(1/2,h_0)
 =\operatorname{span}
 \{L_{-2}\chi_2,L_{-1}^2\chi_2,\chi_4\}.
\]
In this ordered basis the crossing form of
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue}, with \(x=\Delta-h_0\), is
\[
 \beta_4=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -119/8&-693/32&0\\
 -693/32&-9471/128&0\\
 0&0&13475/648
 \end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\frac74G_2(1/2,33/16)&0\\
 0&13475/648
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Finally, the two highest-weight torus matrix elements are
\[
 P_2(d):=\langle\chi_2|V_d(1)|\chi_2\rangle
 =\frac{d(d-1)(2d-7)(2d-1)}4
\]
and
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_4(d)&:=\langle\chi_4|V_d(1)|\chi_4\rangle\\
 &=\frac{
 d(d-1)(2d-7)(2d-1)(3d-14)(3d-5)(6d-55)(6d-1)}
 {1296}.
 \end{split}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply \(L_1,\ldots,L_\ell\) to the displayed level-\(\ell\) vector and
use the Virasoro commutator.  Collecting coefficients in the PBW bases at
levels \(\ell-1,\ldots,0\) gives zero for \(\chi_2\) and for \(\chi_4\);
modes larger than \(\ell\) vanish by degree.  Thus both vectors are
singular.

In the level-four PBW basis of
Proposition~\ref{prop:spin-collision}, the three asserted kernel vectors
are the columns
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\-3/4\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}-3/2\\-3/2\\0\\-3/4\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}-1/4\\11/6\\49/144\\-25/6\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Multiplication by the matrix \(G_4(1/2,h_0)\) of
Appendix~\ref{app:gram} gives zero.

nullity three, proving the kernel identity.

Multiplying these columns on both sides of
\(\partial_\Delta G_4(1/2,\Delta)|_{\Delta=h_0}\) gives the displayed

\[
 G_2(1/2,33/16)=
 \begin{pmatrix}17/2&99/8\\99/8&1353/32\end{pmatrix},
\]
which verifies the second expression for \(\beta_4\).
Finally, repeated use of the primary-field Ward commutator in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}, followed by collection and

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spin-sector fixed-\(c\) recursive principal part]
\label{thm:spin-recursive-residue}
At \(c=1/2\), the level-four reduced coefficient satisfies the finite
fixed-central-charge recursion
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=1/16}H_4(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =-\frac47P_2(d)H_2(1/2,33/16,d)
  +\frac{648}{13475}P_4(d).
\]
Equivalently, this residue is
\[
 \frac{d(d-1)(2d-7)(2d-1)}{40425}
 \left(
 248d^4-5752d^3+25274d^2-33630d+5775
 \right).
\]
Every quantity on the right is evaluated directly at \(c=1/2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At levels \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), the radicals at
\(\Delta=h_0\) are respectively
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\chi_2\},\qquad
 \operatorname{span}\{L_{-1}\chi_2\},\qquad
 \operatorname{span}\{L_{-2}\chi_2,L_{-1}^2\chi_2,\chi_4\}.
\]
The crossing forms on the first two spaces are
\[
 -\frac74,\qquad -\frac74G_1(1/2,33/16),
\]
and the third is the form in
Proposition~\ref{prop:spin-singular-data}.  These identities follow by
differentiating the level-two, level-three, and level-four Gram matrices;
all three crossing forms are nondegenerate.  Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue}, then use
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} on each singular summand.  Taking
the trace against the corresponding torus matrix gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_0}F_2
   &=-\frac47P_2,\\
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_0}F_3
   &=-\frac47P_2F_1(1/2,33/16,d),\\
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_0}F_4
   &=-\frac47P_2F_2(1/2,33/16,d)
     +\frac{648}{13475}P_4.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The Euler product has coefficients \(1,-1,-1,0,0\) through degree four.
Hence \(H_4=F_4-F_3-F_2\), while
\(H_2=F_2-F_1-F_0\).  Substitution gives the recursive formula.
Inserting the explicit polynomials from
Proposition~\ref{prop:spin-singular-data} and the level-two coefficient
from the inverse Shapovalov matrix gives the second displayed expression.
\end{proof}

\section{Generic residue data}\label{sec:generic-data}

\begin{definition}[Generic residue data used for algebraic confluence]
\label{def:generic-residue}
Use the functions \(c(b)\) and \(h_{r,s}(b)\) of
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.
Put \(\Lambda^2=(b+b^{-1})^2-4d\), and define
\[
 \begin{split}
 {\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)
 =\prod_{\substack{1\leq k\leq2r-1\\k\ {\rm odd}}}
  \prod_{\substack{1\leq l\leq2s-1\\l\ {\rm odd}}}
 &\frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}\\
 {}\times&
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4},
 \end{split}
\]
and
\[
 {\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)
 =\frac12
 \prod_{\substack{1-r\leq p\leq r,\ 1-s\leq q\leq s\\
 (p,q)\neq(0,0),(r,s)}}
 (pb+qb^{-1})^{-1},\qquad
 {\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b){\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d).
\]
For generic \(b\), \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\) is the residue coefficient in the
eta-reduced torus recursion, stated as
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} below.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Source, normalization, and imported identities]
\label{rem:imported}
The recursion was conjectured in \cite{Poghossian2009}.  In
\cite[Section~3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}, equation~(7) expresses the
residue through the inverse norm derivative \(A_{r,s}(c)\), the paragraph
immediately following equation~(7) gives its product for the normalization
in which the singular vector has \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\),
equation~(8) evaluates the product of the two torus fusion polynomials, and
equation~(9) is the eta-reduced recursion.  Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}
is exactly this chain after writing the four linear factors of equation~(8)
as the two quadratic factors in \({\mathcal P}_{r,s}\), without choosing a
square root of \(\Lambda^2\).

Here is the convention check explicitly.  Write \(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}\) for
the external momentum in that source.  Its convention is
\[
 d=\frac{(b+b^{-1})^2-\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2}{4},\qquad
 \lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2=\Lambda^2.
\]
The source defines \(A_{r,s}(c)\) as the reciprocal of
\(\partial_\Delta\langle\chi_{r,s}^{\Delta}\mid
\chi_{r,s}^{\Delta}\rangle\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}\), with the
coefficients of the \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-normalized vector held fixed.  This is
exactly \(b_{\chi}^{-1}\) in Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}, and its
displayed product is term-for-term \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\).  Likewise,
for every odd \(k\) and \(l\) in the ranges of
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the four source factors pair as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\frac{(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}+kb+lb^{-1})
              (\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}-kb-lb^{-1})}{4}\\[-2pt]
 &\qquad{}\times
 \frac{(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}+kb-lb^{-1})
              (\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}-kb+lb^{-1})}{4}\\
 &\quad=\frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}
  \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the translation introduces no sign or power-of-two factor.

Two normalization identities at generic \(b\) are imported from that
derivation rather than rederived here.  First, with
\(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) the normalized singular vector and \(b_{\chi}\) the
crossing scalar of Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossing-normalization}
 b_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)^{-1};
\end{equation}
this is equation~(7) and the displayed product following it, translated
from the inverse norm derivative to the crossing-form convention of
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.  Second,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fusion-normalization}
 \langle\chi_{r,s}(b)|V_d(1)|\chi_{r,s}(b)\rangle
 ={\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d);
\end{equation}
this is the factorization displayed between equations~(7) and~(8), followed
by the explicit fusion-polynomial product in equation~(8), and is
\emph{not} a one-line consequence of the Ward recursion.  Both sides of each identity
are rational in \(b\), so the identities continue to every
specialization at which the normalized singular vector is regular.  This
fixes the normalization used below, rather than merely identifying the
polynomials up to a scalar.  Both identities are verified in exact
arithmetic by the audit scripts of
Remark~\ref{rem:audit-scope}: \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} directly
at \(b_0\) for the labels \((2,1),(1,3),(1,5),(4,1),(1,2),(2,2)\), at a
generic sample point for \((2,1),(1,2)\), and, for \((4,2),(1,6)\),
through the level-eight and level-six residues checked by
\path{tools/audit_grade10_direct.py}; and
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization} at \(b_0\) for the labels
\((2,1),(1,3),(1,2),(2,2)\) through the crossing scalars of
Section~\ref{sec:smith}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Distinct weights and no shifted self-poles at generic
\(b\)]
\label{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
For positive labels \((r,s)\) and \((u,v)\),
\[
 h_{r,s}(b)+rs-h_{u,v}(b)
\]
is not the zero rational function of \(b\).  Moreover, for distinct
positive labels \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\), the difference
\(h_{r,s}(b)-h_{u,v}(b)\) is not the zero rational function.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Kac formula gives the identity
\[
 h_{r,s}(b)+rs=h_{r,-s}(b).
\]
If this were identically equal to \(h_{u,v}(b)\), then
\[
 (rb-sb^{-1})^2=(ub+vb^{-1})^2
\]
in \(\mathbb C(b)\).  Since this is an integral domain,
\[
 rb-sb^{-1}=\pm(ub+vb^{-1}).
\]
Comparison of the coefficients of \(b\) and \(b^{-1}\) contradicts
\(r,s,u,v>0\).  For the second assertion, the same argument gives
\(rb+sb^{-1}=\pm(ub+vb^{-1})\), and positivity forces
\((r,s)=(u,v)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic eta-reduced recursion]
\label{prop:generic-recursion}
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For all \(b\) outside a finite set of algebraic values,
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{a=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\ \ell_a=rs\leq N}}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)}{\Delta-h_{r,s}(b)}\,
 H_{N-\ell_a}\bigl(c(b),h_{r,s}(b)+rs,d\bigr)
\]
as an identity of rational functions of \(\Delta\), polynomial in
\(d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given the two imported identities of Remark~\ref{rem:imported}, the
proposition follows from the results of
Sections~\ref{sec:partial-fractions} and~\ref{sec:smith}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}, outside finitely many values of
\(b\) the weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with \(rs\leq N\) are pairwise
distinct, and no shifted weight \(z_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b)+rs\) is a
degenerate weight of level at most \(N\).

Fix such a \(b\), write \(c=c(b)\), and fix a label \(a=(r,s)\) with
\(rs\leq N\).  By distinctness, \(h_{r,s}(b)\) lies on no other Kac
divisor of level at most \(N\), so the level-\(N\) Kac determinant has
valuation exactly \(p(N-rs)\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)\).  The
level-\(N\) image of the injective singular embedding
\(j_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\), whose injectivity is the result stated in
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}, is a
\(p(N-rs)\)-dimensional subspace of the
kernel of the specialized Gram matrix.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} the
nullity is at most the valuation, so the kernel is exactly that image,
and every positive local Smith exponent equals \(1\); hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:transverse-residue} applies.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}, the crossing form on this kernel is
\(b_{\chi}\,G_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s})\), which is nondegenerate because
\(z_{r,s}\) is not a degenerate weight and, by
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization},
\(b_\chi^{-1}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\neq0\).  Taking the trace of the residue of
\(G_N^{-1}\) against \(\rho_N\) and factoring the pulled-back torus form
by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} and
\eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives
\[
 \operatorname*{Res}_{\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)}F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 ={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\,{\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)\,
 F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d)
 ={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)\,F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d).
\]
The difference between \(F_N\) and the sum of these principal parts has
no finite pole, hence is a polynomial in \(\Delta\); it is bounded as
\(\Delta\to\infty\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight}, hence equals the
constant \(p(N)\):
\[
 F_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =p(N)+\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_a(b,d)}{\Delta-h_a(b)}F_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d).
\]
Finally, multiply by the Euler coefficients \(e_j\) and sum.  The
generating identity in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} gives
\(\sum_je_jp(N-j)=\delta_{N,0}\), while the residue sums reassemble into
shifted \(H\)-coefficients:
\[
 H_N(c,\Delta,d)
 =\delta_{N,0}
 +\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_a(b,d)}{\Delta-h_a(b)}H_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d).
\]
For \(N\geq1\) this is the displayed recursion.
\end{proof}

\section{All-order confluent recursion}\label{sec:all-order}

\begin{definition}[Shifted Laurent recursion]\label{def:shifted-laurent}
Fix \(b_\star\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}\), put
\(c_\star=c(b_\star)\), let \(t=b-b_\star\), and work in
\[
 K_\star((t)),\qquad K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,d),
\]
where \(d\) is transcendental.
For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), write
\[
 \ell_a=rs,\qquad h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t),\qquad
 R_a(t)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b_\star+t,d),\qquad
 z_a(t)=h_a(t)+\ell_a.
\]
Define shifted Laurent coefficients recursively by
\[
 S_{a,0}(t)=1
\]
and, for \(M\geq1\),
\[
 S_{a,M}(t)
 =\sum_{\substack{e=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_e=uv\leq M}}
 \frac{R_e(t)}
 {z_a(t)-h_e(t)}S_{e,M-\ell_e}(t).
\]
Every denominator in this formula is a nonzero rational function of
\(b\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}, so each summand has a
well-defined Laurent expansion, and the recursion terminates because its
second index strictly decreases.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A priori shifted valuation bound]
\label{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}
For a positive label \(e\), and for two positive labels \(a,e\), set
\[
 \rho_e=\max\{0,-\ord_tR_e(t)\},\qquad
 \sigma_{a,e}
 =\max\{0,\ord_t(z_a(t)-h_e(t))\}.
\]
Define nonnegative integers \(W_{a,M}\) recursively by
\[
 W_{a,0}=0,
\]
and, for \(M\geq1\),
\[
 W_{a,M}
 =\max\left(
 \{0\}\cup
 \{\rho_e+\sigma_{a,e}+W_{e,M-\ell_e}:\ell_e\leq M\}
 \right).
\]
Then
\[
 \ord_t S_{a,M}(t)\geq-W_{a,M}.
\]
In particular, \(W_{a,M}\) is an a priori pole bound computed solely from
the finite recursion tree and the valuations of its explicit residue and
denominator factors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The assertion is clear for \(M=0\).  If it holds at lower levels, the
\(e\)-summand in Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} has valuation at least
\[
 -\rho_e-\sigma_{a,e}-W_{e,M-\ell_e}.
\]
The valuation of a finite sum is at least the minimum of the summand
valuations.  Taking the displayed maximum proves the induction step.  All
sets involved are finite because \(\ell_e\leq M\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Collision moments]\label{def:collision-moments}
For \(N\geq1\), \(\lambda\in\Lambda_N(b_\star)\), and
\(a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), define
\[
 B_{a,N}(t)=R_a(t)S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t),\qquad
 \delta_a(t)=h_a(t)-\lambda.
\]
If \(\delta_a\not\equiv0\) and \(B_{a,N}\not\equiv0\), put
\[
 \varepsilon_a=\ord_t\delta_a,\qquad
 v_{a,N}=\max\{0,-\ord_tB_{a,N}\}.
\]
If \(\delta_a\not\equiv0\) and \(B_{a,N}\equiv0\), put
\(\varepsilon_a=\ord_t\delta_a\) and \(v_{a,N}=0\).  If
\(\delta_a\equiv0\), set \(\varepsilon_a=\infty\) and \(v_{a,N}=0\).
Define also the a priori numerator bound
\[
 \widehat v_{a,N}=\rho_a+W_{a,N-\ell_a}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound} gives
\[
 v_{a,N}\leq\widehat v_{a,N}.
\]
Define the exact finite structural pole bound
\[
 M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}=
 \max_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \begin{cases}
 1+\lfloor v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor,&\varepsilon_a<\infty,\\
 1,&\varepsilon_a=\infty,
 \end{cases}
\]
and its a priori majorant
\[
 \widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}=
 \max_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \begin{cases}
  1+\lfloor \widehat v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor,&\varepsilon_a<\infty,\\
  1,&\varepsilon_a=\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \(M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\).
The former can be sharpened after
the exact rational germs have been computed, while the latter bounds the
number of moments before those cancellations are simplified.
For \(1\leq k\leq M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), define the
\emph{collision moments}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-moments}
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 =\operatorname{CT}_{t}
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) denotes the coefficient of \(t^0\) in a
Laurent series.  Put
\[
 m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\max\bigl(\{k:\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\neq0\}
            \cup\{0\}\bigr).
\]
It satisfies
\(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} identifies it with the exact
multiplicity of the pole of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Backward Laurent-precision budget]
\label{lem:precision-budget}
Put
\[
 D_{a,e}(t)=\frac{R_e(t)}{z_a(t)-h_e(t)},\qquad
 p_{a,e}=\rho_e+\sigma_{a,e}.
\]
Suppose \(S_{a,M}\) is required through the coefficient of \(t^U\).
For its \(e\)-summand it is sufficient to know
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-propagation}
 D_{a,e}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+W_{e,M-\ell_e}},
\qquad
 S_{e,M-\ell_e}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+p_{a,e}}.
\end{equation}
Iterating these requests down the strictly level-decreasing recursion tree
produces a finite a priori upper precision at every node.

In particular, to compute every collision moment at level \(N\), it is
sufficient for each \(a\) to compute \(R_a\) through \(t^{W_{a,N-\ell_a}}\),
\(S_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through \(t^{\rho_a}\), and
\(\delta_a^{\,k-1}\) through \(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\).  Thus the valuation
majorant together with \eqref{eq:precision-propagation} is an actual finite
jet budget, not only a pole-order bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lower bounds are
\[
 \ord_tD_{a,e}\geq-p_{a,e},\qquad
 \ord_tS_{e,M-\ell_e}\geq-W_{e,M-\ell_e}.
\]
In a product coefficient of degree at most \(U\), the first bound means that
no coefficient of the second factor above degree \(U+p_{a,e}\) can occur;
the second means that no coefficient of the first factor above degree
\(U+W_{e,M-\ell_e}\) can occur.  This proves
\eqref{eq:precision-propagation}.  The recursion tree is finite because the
remaining level decreases along every edge.

For \(B_{a,N}=R_aS_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through degree zero, the same product
argument gives the stated precisions \(W_{a,N-\ell_a}\) and \(\rho_a\).
Its lower degree is at least \(-\widehat v_{a,N}\), so only coefficients of
\(\delta_a^{k-1}\) through degree \(\widehat v_{a,N}\) can enter a constant
term.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise pole support of a collision germ]
\label{lem:collision-pole-support}
Let \(B(t)\in K_\star((t))\) and
\(\delta(t)\in tK_\star[[t]]\).  Every coefficient of
\[
 \frac{B(t)}{x-\delta(t)}
\]
as a Laurent series in \(t\) is a finite \(K_\star\)-linear
combination of \(x^{-1},x^{-2},\ldots\).  It is therefore a proper
rational function of \(x\) whose only possible finite pole is \(x=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(B=0\) the assertion is immediate.  Otherwise choose \(v\geq0\)
with \(\ord_tB\geq-v\).  If \(\delta=0\), the quotient is
\(B/x\).  Otherwise put \(\epsilon=\ord_t\delta\geq1\).  The
formal expansion at \(x=\infty\) gives, for every integer \(m\),
\[
 [t^m]\frac{B(t)}{x-\delta(t)}
 =\sum_{\substack{j\geq0\\ -v+j\epsilon\leq m}}
   [t^m]\bigl(B(t)\delta(t)^j\bigr)x^{-j-1}.
\]
The condition under the sum bounds \(j\), so the sum is finite and has
the asserted pole support.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Separated-pole Laurent cancellation]
\label{lem:separated-pole-cancellation}
Let \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_s\) be distinct, and let
\[
 f_j(t,\Delta)\in K_\star(\Delta)((t)),
 \]
that is, a Laurent series in \(t\) whose coefficients are rational
functions of \(\Delta\).  Suppose that every \(t\)-coefficient
of \(f_j\) is a proper rational function of \(\Delta\) whose finite poles,
if any, lie only at \(\Delta=\mu_j\).  If
\(\sum_{j=1}^s f_j(t,\Delta)\) has no negative power of \(t\), then neither
does any \(f_j(t,\Delta)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each \(m<0\), the coefficient of \(t^m\) in the sum is zero.  It is a
sum of proper rational functions with pairwise disjoint pole sets.
Uniqueness of partial fractions forces every summand to vanish.  Repeating
this for the finitely many possible negative exponents proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}]
For generic \(b\), Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} gives
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{R_a(t)}
 {\Delta-h_a(t)}
 H_{N-\ell_a}(c(b),z_a(t),d).
\]
Induction on \(M\) in the same recursion gives
\[
 S_{a,M}(t)=H_M(c(b),z_a(t),d)
\]
for generic \(b\).  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-grouped}
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}
 \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-h_a(t)}.
\end{equation}
All sums are finite.

Fix \(N\), and choose \(\Delta\) outside
\(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}, the
inverse-Shapovalov expression is analytic in \(t\) near zero: its
specialized Gram determinant is nonzero at this \(\Delta\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:generic-grouped}, initially valid for generic \(b\),
is an identity of rational functions and therefore holds on the punctured
formal neighborhood of \(b_\star\).  This applies for every \(\Delta\)
in the complement of the finite exceptional set; hence each negative
\(t\)-coefficient vanishes as a rational function of \(\Delta\), not
merely after one numerical specialization.

Group the right-hand side by its distinct limiting weights:
\[
 {\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}(t,\Delta)
 =\sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-h_a(t)}.
\]
For each label in this class,
\(\delta_a=h_a-\lambda\in tK_\star[[t]]\); hence
Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}, with
\(x=\Delta-\lambda\), shows explicitly that every \(t\)-coefficient
of \({\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) is a proper rational
function of \(\Delta\) with its only possible finite pole at
\(\Delta=\lambda\).
The sum over \(\lambda\) is analytic in \(t\), as just proved.
Lemma~\ref{lem:separated-pole-cancellation} therefore shows that each
\({\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) is separately regular in \(t\).
Hence every collision class has a finite algebraic confluent value.

Put \(x=\Delta-\lambda\).  In the formal expansion at \(x=\infty\),
\[
 \frac1{\Delta-h_a(t)}
 =\frac1{x-\delta_a(t)}
 =\sum_{j\geq0}\frac{\delta_a(t)^j}{x^{j+1}}.
\]
Taking \(\operatorname{CT}_t\) after summing the collision class shows
that the coefficient of \(x^{-k}\) is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{CT}_t
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1}
 =\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d).
\]
If \(\delta_a\not\equiv0\), then the summand has positive
\(t\)-valuation whenever
\(k>1+\lfloor v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor\), so its constant term
vanishes.  If \(\delta_a\equiv0\), it vanishes identically for \(k>1\).
This proves the bound \(M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), the finiteness of the
pole sum, and the finite-jet nature of every constant-term calculation.
Independently of cancellations,
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound} replaces \(v_{a,N}\) by
\(\widehat v_{a,N}\), proving
\(M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-budget} then propagates the required upper
precision backward through the finite recursion tree.  Only finitely
many terms of the formal \(x^{-1}\)-expansion can contribute to
\(\operatorname{CT}_t\), which justifies interchanging the constant term
with that expansion.  The confluent value of each class tends to zero as
\(\Delta\to\infty\) and has no finite pole except \(\lambda\); the
moments are therefore its complete partial-fraction expansion, with no
polynomial remainder.

Every \(R_a(t)\) is polynomial in \(d\) with coefficients in
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star)((t))\), and all shifted denominators are independent
of \(d\).  Thus every collision moment belongs to
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star)[d]\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:generic-grouped} and the
analyticity argument identify the resulting rational function with the
specialized inverse-Shapovalov coefficient.  Uniqueness in
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions} gives
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}
=A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\), and coefficientwise local finiteness follows
from the finiteness of \(\Lambda_N(b_\star)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pole-free Ising recursion and all-order equality]
\label{thm:all-order-recursion}
Specialize Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} to \(b_\star=b_0\), and
write
\[
 {\mathcal C}_{n,N}={\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},\quad
 M_{n,N}=M^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},\quad
 \widehat M_{n,N}=\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},\quad
 m_{n,N}=m^{\star}_{\lambda_n,N},
\]
\[
 \widetilde A_{n,k,N}
 =\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda_n,k,N}.
\]
For every \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{n\geq0\\{\mathcal C}_{n,N}\neq\varnothing}}
   \sum_{k=1}^{M_{n,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A_{n,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k}.
\end{equation}
The sums and every constant-term calculation are finite,
\(\widetilde A_{n,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q[d]\), and the recursion in
Definitions~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}--\ref{def:collision-moments}
terminates after levels strictly below \(N\).  In particular, by the
uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm:partial-fractions}, the collision
moments are the partial-fraction coefficients of the block,
\[
 \widetilde A_{n,k,N}=A_{n,k,N},
\]
and \(m_{n,N}\) is the exact multiplicity of the pole of \(H_N\) at
\(\lambda_n\).  Moreover
\[
 m_{n,N}\leq M_{n,N}\leq\widehat M_{n,N},
\]
where \(\widehat M_{n,N}\) is computed before simplifying cancellations
inside the shifted blocks.  The backward rule of
Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-budget} supplies a finite truncation order for every
intermediate Laurent germ.  Consequently, with \(A_{n,k}(d;q)\) as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:partial-fractions},
one has the coefficientwise locally finite identity
\[
 H_\Delta^{(1/2)}(d\mid q)
 =1+\sum_{n\geq0}\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A_{n,k}(d;q)}{(\Delta-\lambda_n)^k}.
\]
This series agrees coefficient by coefficient with
Definition~\ref{def:matrices}, hence with the inverse-Shapovalov
definition of the torus one-point block.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:collisions} identifies the general collision
classes with the displayed \({\mathcal C}_{n,N}\).  The specialized
inverse-Shapovalov coefficient and all pole positions \(\lambda_n\) have
rational coefficients, so uniqueness of partial fractions improves
\(\widetilde A_{n,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_0)[d]\) to
\(\widetilde A_{n,k,N}\in\mathbb Q[d]\).  All other assertions are the
specialized assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[First nontransverse Smith layer]
\label{prop:level-seven-smith}
Let \(h_E=1/2\), and let \(U_2,U_3\) be the submodules generated by
\(\xi_2,\xi_3\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:first-collision}.  Their first
intersection is generated at total level \(7\) by a normalized singular
vector \(\xi_7\).  More explicitly, the normalized child singular vectors
are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \phi_5={}&\bigl(
 L_{-1}^5-15L_{-2}L_{-1}^3+36L_{-2}^2L_{-1}
 +\tfrac{99}{4}L_{-3}L_{-1}^2\\
 &\hspace{17mm}-45L_{-3}L_{-2}-54L_{-4}L_{-1}
 +45L_{-5}\bigr)|\tfrac52\rangle ,
 \end{split}
\]
\[
 \phi_4=\bigl(
 L_{-1}^4-\tfrac{40}{3}L_{-2}L_{-1}^2+16L_{-2}^2
 +\tfrac{88}{3}L_{-3}L_{-1}-\tfrac{152}{3}L_{-4}
 \bigr)|\tfrac72\rangle ,
\]
and
\[
 \xi_7=j_{\xi_2}(\phi_5)=j_{\xi_3}(\phi_4).
\]
The \(15\) local Smith exponents of
\(G_7(1/2,h_E+x)\) are
\[
 (0^{\,4},1^{\,10},2).
\]
For the second crossing form of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-step-residue}, normalized by the displayed
\(\xi_7\),
\[
 \gamma_7=-700700.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two child highest weights are
\[
 h_E+2=\frac52=\lambda_{11},\qquad
 h_E+3=\frac72=\lambda_{13}.
\]
Their first singular vectors have relative levels \(5\) and \(4\), with
labels \((1,5)\) and \((4,1)\), respectively.  Direct Virasoro commutation
checks the two displayed vectors.  Their images are nonzero singular
vectors at total level \(7\), by injectivity of nonzero homomorphisms
between Verma modules
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}; uniqueness
of a singular vector at a fixed level
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011} and their common
\(L_{-1}^7\)-coefficient \(1\) prove the displayed equality.  The embedding
diagram in \cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011} also shows
that there is no earlier intersection and that the radical is the sum of
the two singular submodules.

At level \(7\), the radical therefore has dimension
\[
 p(5)+p(4)-1=7+5-1=11.
\]
The only Kac labels of weight \(1/2\) and level at most \(7\) are
\((2,1)\) and \((1,3)\), so the determinant valuation is
\[
 p(5)+p(4)=12.
\]
The Smith exponents are consequently
\((0^4,1^{10},2)\).

For completeness, the second crossing scalar can be obtained without an
off-critical deformation.  In the level-seven PBW basis, form
\[
 G_7(1/2,h_E+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3),
\]
solve \(G_0v_1=-G_1\xi_7\), and evaluate
\[
 \xi_7^{\mathsf T}(G_2\xi_7+G_1v_1).
\]
Exact PBW commutation gives \(-700700\).  Choice-independence follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-step-residue}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No earlier nontransverse pole]
\label{prop:no-earlier-double-pole}
For every \(N<7\) and every finite internal weight, all local Smith
exponents of \(G_N(1/2,\Delta)\) are at most \(1\).  Consequently
\(G_N^{-1}\), \(F_N\), and \(H_N\) have at most simple poles in \(\Delta\).
Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:level-seven-smith}, this proves that the
double pole at \(\Delta=1/2\) and level \(7\) is the first double pole of the
Ising Verma block.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a Kac weight represented by only one label \(a=(r,s)\) with
\(\ell_a\leq N\), injectivity of the singular-vector embedding
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011} gives a
radical of dimension \(p(N-\ell_a)\).  This equals the valuation contributed
by that label to the Kac determinant.  Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} therefore makes
all corresponding positive Smith exponents equal to \(1\).

It remains to inspect repeated labels.  Proposition~\ref{prop:collisions},
or direct enumeration of \(rs\leq6\), shows that the complete list of
repeated classes below level \(7\) is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{weight}&\text{labels}&\text{singular levels}\\ \hline
\lambda_5=\frac12 &(2,1),(1,3)&2,3\\
\lambda_2=\frac1{16}&(1,2),(2,2)&2,4\\
\lambda_1=0&(1,1),(2,3)&1,6 .
\end{array}
\]
The two energy branches have no intersection below level \(7\) by
Proposition~\ref{prop:level-seven-smith}.

For the spin class, the child highest weights along the level-two and
level-four branches are \(33/16\) and \(65/16\).  Their Kac determinants
have no zero below relative levels \(8\) and \(6\), respectively.  A first
intersection below level \(7\) would be singular; injectivity would pull it
back along the level-two branch to a singular vector of relative level at
most \(4\), which is impossible.

For the vacuum class, the level-one branch is generated by
\(L_{-1}|0\rangle\) and has child highest weight \(1\).  The Kac determinant
of \(M(1/2,1)\) has no zero through relative level \(5\).  Hence the
level-six singular vector cannot lie in the level-one branch, since its
preimage would be singular at relative level \(5\).

Thus, at every repeated weight and every \(N<7\), the relevant singular
submodules have zero intersection.  The radical dimension is therefore the
sum \(\sum_a p(N-\ell_a)\), exactly the Kac-determinant valuation.  A final
application of Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} proves the Smith and pole bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First nontransverse fixed-\(c\) principal part]
\label{thm:level-seven-principal-part}
Define
\[
 E_5(d)={\mathcal P}_{1,5}(b_0,d),\qquad
 E_4(d)={\mathcal P}_{4,1}(b_0,d).
\]
Explicitly,
\[
 E_5(d)=\frac1{16}
 d(d-20)(d-13)(d-11)(d-6)(d-1)
 (2d-15)(2d-7)(2d-5)(2d-1),
\]
\[
 E_4(d)=\frac1{9}
 d(d-20)(d-13)(d-11)(d-6)(d-1)
 (3d-14)(3d-5),
\]
and \(E_2E_5=E_3E_4\).  Put
\[
 Q_5^E(d)=
 \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \{(z-\tfrac52)H_5(1/2,z,d)\}\right|_{z=5/2},
\]
\[
 Q_4^E(d)=
 \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \{(z-\tfrac72)H_4(1/2,z,d)\}\right|_{z=7/2}.
\]
For
\[
 \alpha=(2,1),\quad\beta=(1,3),\quad
 \mu=(1,5),\quad\nu=(4,1),
\]
let
\[
 \delta_\alpha=h_\alpha+2-h_\mu,\qquad
 \delta_\beta=h_\beta+3-h_\nu,
\]
and, with dots denoting \(d/db\) at \(b_0\), define the explicit finite
polynomial
\[
 \Theta_7(d)=
 \sum_{(\kappa,\tau)=(\alpha,\mu),(\beta,\nu)}
 \left(
 \frac{\dot{\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau+
       {\mathcal R}_\kappa\dot{\mathcal R}_\tau}{\dot\delta_\kappa}
 -\frac{{\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau\ddot\delta_\kappa}
 {2\dot\delta_\kappa^2}
 \right)_{b=b_0}.
\]
Its factored expansion is recorded in
equation~(\ref{eq:theta7-expanded}) of Appendix~\ref{app:expanded-data}.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-seven-pp}
 \begin{split}
 \PP_{\Delta=1/2}H_7(1/2,\Delta,d)
 ={}&-\frac{E_2(d)E_5(d)}
 {700700\,(\Delta-\tfrac12)^2}\\
 &+\frac{
 \frac9{28}E_2(d)Q_5^E(d)
 -\frac8{105}E_3(d)Q_4^E(d)+\Theta_7(d)}
 {\Delta-\tfrac12}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
This is the earliest double pole of the Ising block.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-recursion}, the confluent Laurent
expansion computed below coincides with the fixed-\(c\) Laurent
expansion of \(H_7\) at \(\Delta=\tfrac12\).
The double-pole coefficient also has an intrinsic derivation
that avoids the confluence altogether:
Propositions~\ref{prop:two-step-residue}
and~\ref{prop:level-seven-smith} give
\([x^{-2}]G_7(1/2,\tfrac12+x)^{-1}=\xi_7\xi_7^{\mathsf T}/\gamma_7\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} applied along either branch of
the diamond gives
\(\langle\xi_7|V_d(1)|\xi_7\rangle=E_2E_5=E_3E_4\), and
multiplication by the Euler product does not affect the
\(x^{-2}\) coefficient at level seven because all lower levels have at
most simple poles by Proposition~\ref{prop:no-earlier-double-pole}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives the four fusion polynomials.
Direct evaluation of the residue products and Kac slopes gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 (r,s)&(2,1)&(1,3)&(1,5)&(4,1)\\ \hline
 {\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b_0)&
 9/28&-8/105&-1/102375&3/61600
 \end{array}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\dot h_{2,1}-\dot h_{1,3}}
 {\dot h_{2,1}-\dot h_{1,5}}=\frac5{11},\qquad
 \frac{\dot h_{1,3}-\dot h_{2,1}}
 {\dot h_{1,3}-\dot h_{4,1}}=\frac5{13}.
\]
Either path therefore expresses the coefficient produced jointly by their
cancelled \(t^{-1}\) parts:
\[
 \frac5{11}{\mathcal A}_{2,1}{\mathcal A}_{1,5}
 =\frac5{13}{\mathcal A}_{1,3}{\mathcal A}_{4,1}
 =-\frac1{700700}.
\]
This agrees with the intrinsic inverse second crossing scalar
\(\gamma_7^{-1}\) from Proposition~\ref{prop:level-seven-smith}.

Expanding the two outer recursion terms at
\(\Delta=1/2\), their cancelled \(t^{-1}\) numerators give the displayed
double pole.  Their finite parts are respectively
\[
 \frac9{28}E_2Q_5^E+T_{\alpha,\mu},\qquad
 -\frac8{105}E_3Q_4^E+T_{\beta,\nu},
\]
where the two \(T\)'s are precisely the summands defining \(\Theta_7\).
Differentiating the finite products of
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} and factoring over \(\mathbb Q[d]\)
gives the displayed explicit polynomial form of \(\Theta_7\).
This proves the complete principal part.  Its global minimality in level is
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-earlier-double-pole}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Computing the finite parts]\label{rem:finite-parts}
The lower-level finite parts \(Q_5^E,Q_4^E\) here, and \(Q_8,Q_6\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:level-ten-principal-part} below, are defined by
fixed-\(c\) data only: they can be computed directly from the
inverse-Shapovalov matrices or recursively from
Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-recursion}, and no off-critical numerical
limit is involved.  Closed forms (of degrees \(16\) and \(12\) in

are recorded in machine-readable form.  The confluence-side polynomial
identities are checked exactly, while the independent inverse-Shapovalov
scripts check the closed forms at the sample external weights specified in
Remark~\ref{rem:audit-scope}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Level-ten Smith profile at \(\Delta=1/16\)]
\label{prop:level-ten-smith}
Let \(h_0=1/16\), and let
\[
 U_2=U(\Vir_-)\chi_2,\qquad U_4=U(\Vir_-)\chi_4
\]
be the two singular submodules of \(M(1/2,h_0)\) from
Proposition~\ref{prop:spin-singular-data}.  Their first intersection is
generated at total level \(10\) by a singular vector \(\chi_{10}\), and
\[
 \dim\ker G_{10}(1/2,h_0)=32.
\]
The \(42\) local Smith exponents of
\(G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)\) are
\[
 \bigl(0^{\,10},1^{\,31},2\bigr).
\]
In particular, the inverse Shapovalov matrix has a nonzero rank-one
coefficient at order \(x^{-2}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The weight \(h_0\) is the interior Ising weight labelled by \((1,2)\), so
it is in Class \(R^+\), Case \(1+\), in the classification of
\cite[Section~5.1.4]{IoharaKoga2011}: both Coulomb-gas parameters are
rational and the weight lies in the interior of the Kac table, so the
embedding diagram is the standard braided pattern of two infinite
strings of singular vectors.
The commutative embedding diagram in
\cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011} describes the two
branches and their successive intersections.  More precisely, the
hypotheses of Class~\(R^+\), Case~\(1+\), in
\cite[Theorem~6.3(i)]{IoharaKoga2011} apply to this interior rational
weight, and equation~(6.3) there identifies the first Jantzen term with
the sum of the two immediately embedded Verma submodules.  In the present
notation those submodules are \(U_2\) and \(U_4\); their sum is the maximal
proper submodule.

The two highest weights are
\[
 h_0+2=\frac{33}{16}=\lambda_{10},\qquad
 h_0+4=\frac{65}{16}=\lambda_{14}.
\]
The first singular vector inside \(U_2\) has relative level \(8\), with
Kac label \((4,2)\), and the first singular vector inside \(U_4\) has
relative level \(6\), with Kac label \((1,6)\).  Both therefore have total
level \(10\) and weight
\[
 h_0+10=\frac{161}{16}=\lambda_{22}.
\]
The embeddings are injective by
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.
Their two images are proportional by uniqueness of a singular vector at a
fixed level, \cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}.  Hence the
level-ten intersection is one dimensional.  There is no earlier
intersection: a vector at the first intersection level would be singular,
whereas the Kac determinant for \(M(1/2,33/16)\), respectively
\(M(1/2,65/16)\), has no zero below relative level \(8\), respectively
\(6\).

The level-ten radical is the level-ten part of \(U_2+U_4\).  Since
\[
 p(8)=22,\qquad p(6)=11,
\]
and the intersection has dimension one at this level,
\[
 \dim\ker G_{10}(1/2,h_0)=22+11-1=32.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:collisions}, the only positive Kac labels with
\(\Delta_{r,s}=h_0\) and \(rs\leq10\) are \((1,2)\) and \((2,2)\).
The Kac determinant formula therefore gives
\[
 \ord_x\det G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)
 =p(8)+p(6)=33.
\]
There are \(p(10)=42\) Smith exponents, exactly \(32\) of them are
positive, and their sum is \(33\).  Thus \(31\) positive exponents equal
\(1\), one equals \(2\), and the remaining \(10\) vanish.
Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} gives the final assertion.
(This illustrates Corollary~\ref{cor:det-overcounts}: the determinant
valuation \(33\) records the sum of the positive Smith exponents,
while the pole order is their maximum, \(2\).)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Representation-theoretic inputs and finite checks]
\label{rem:representation-inputs}
The embedding argument above uses four specific results of
\cite{IoharaKoga2011}: Proposition~5.1 gives uniqueness, up to scalar, of
a singular vector at a fixed positive level; the paragraph preceding
Corollary~5.2 gives injectivity of a nonzero homomorphism between Verma
modules; Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4 give the braided Class~\(R^+\)
embedding diagram; and Theorem~6.3(i), with equation~(6.3), identifies the
first Jantzen layer used here with the sum of the two branches.  These inputs
imply the first-intersection and radical descriptions at all levels.  At the
two levels used explicitly in this paper, they are also checked without the
embedding diagram: exact Gram-matrix calculations give radical dimensions
\(11\) at level seven and \(32\) at level ten, while the determinant
valuations give the stated Smith profiles.  The all-order confluence theorem
does not depend on the embedding-diagram interpretation.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Exact constants of the level-ten confluence]
\label{lem:level-ten-constants}

 {\dot h_{1,2}-\dot h_{4,2}}=\frac15,\qquad
 \frac{\dot h_{2,2}-\dot h_{1,2}}
 {\dot h_{2,2}-\dot h_{1,6}}=\frac17,

and

 \frac15{\mathcal A}_{1,2}{\mathcal A}_{4,2}
 =\frac17{\mathcal A}_{2,2}{\mathcal A}_{1,6}

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Leading double-pole coefficient at level ten]
\label{thm:level-ten-leading}
Normalize all singular vectors by setting their \(L_{-1}^{\ell}\)
coefficient equal to \(1\).  Let
\(\psi_8\in M(1/2,33/16)\) and
\(\psi_6\in M(1/2,65/16)\) be the singular vectors of levels \(8\) and
\(6\), respectively.  Then
\[
 \chi_{10}=j_{\chi_2}(\psi_8)=j_{\chi_4}(\psi_6).
\]
Define
\[
 P_8^{(33/16)}(d)=\langle\psi_8|V_d(1)|\psi_8\rangle,\qquad
 P_6^{(65/16)}(d)=\langle\psi_6|V_d(1)|\psi_6\rangle .
\]
They are the explicit polynomials
\[
 P_8^{(33/16)}(d)={\mathcal P}_{4,2}(b_0,d),\qquad
 P_6^{(65/16)}(d)={\mathcal P}_{1,6}(b_0,d)
\]
listed in Lemma~\ref{lem:level-ten-constants}.
Write
\[
 G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3),
\]
choose \(v_1\) with
\[
 G_0v_1=-G_1\chi_{10},
\]
and set
\[
 \gamma_{10}
 =\chi_{10}^{\mathsf T}(G_2\chi_{10}+G_1v_1).
\]
Then it is independent of the choice of \(v_1\), and
\[
 \gamma_{10}=-\frac{1121229484375}{192}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lim_{\Delta\to h_0}
 (\Delta-h_0)^2H_{10}(1/2,\Delta,d)
 =\frac{P_2(d)P_8^{(33/16)}(d)}{\gamma_{10}}
 =\frac{P_4(d)P_6^{(65/16)}(d)}{\gamma_{10}}.
\]
This is an intrinsic fixed-\(c\) expression for the spin-module
nontransverse Laurent coefficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two compositions in the first display are nonzero singular vectors of
total level \(10\).  Proposition~5.1 of \cite{IoharaKoga2011} makes them
proportional.  Their \(L_{-1}^{10}\) coefficients are both \(1\), so they
are equal.
The Kac determinants of the child modules of highest weights \(33/16\) and
\(65/16\) have no zero below levels \(8\) and \(6\), respectively.
Consequently the standard \(L_{-1}^{\ell}\)-normalized singular-vector
recursion has a nonsingular coefficient system at every preceding level.
Cramer's rule then shows that the normalized rational families
\(\psi_8(b)\) and \(\psi_6(b)\) are regular at \(b_0\).  Hence the rational
identity
\eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} specializes to
\[
 P_8^{(33/16)}={\mathcal P}_{4,2}(b_0,d),\qquad
 P_6^{(65/16)}={\mathcal P}_{1,6}(b_0,d).
\]

Let \(K_1=\ker G_0=(U_2+U_4)_{10}\), and let \(\beta_1\) be its first
crossing form.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}, the restriction of
\(\beta_1\) to \(U_2\) is the scalar \(b_{\chi_2}\) times the
Shapovalov form on \(M(1/2,33/16)\).  Since \(\psi_8\) is singular,
\(\beta_1(\chi_{10},U_2)=0\).  The same argument through \(U_4\) gives
\(\beta_1(\chi_{10},U_4)=0\).  Therefore
\(\chi_{10}\in K_2=\ker\beta_1\).

Proposition~\ref{prop:level-ten-smith} shows that exactly one Smith
exponent is at least \(2\).  The unconditional Smith-layer count in
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-step-residue} therefore shows that \(K_2\) is
spanned by \(\chi_{10}\).  Since that exponent is exactly \(2\), the same
proposition shows that its second crossing form is the nonzero scalar
\(\gamma_{10}\), and gives
\[
 [x^{-2}]G_{10}(1/2,h_0+x)^{-1}
 =\frac{\chi_{10}\chi_{10}^{\mathsf T}}{\gamma_{10}}.
\]
Taking the trace against the specialized torus matrix yields
\[
 [x^{-2}]F_{10}(1/2,h_0+x,d)
 =\frac{\langle\chi_{10}|V_d(1)|\chi_{10}\rangle}{\gamma_{10}}.
\]
For \(N<10\), the two singular submodules have zero intersection at level
\(N\).  Thus the radical dimension is
\[
 p(N-2)+p(N-4),
\]
with \(p(k)=0\) for \(k<0\), and this equals the Kac-determinant valuation.
Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} therefore shows that all lower-level Smith exponents
at \(h_0\) are at most one.  Multiplication by the Euler product consequently
does not alter the \(x^{-2}\) coefficient at level \(10\).  Finally,
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization}, applied along either side of the
embedding diamond, gives
\[
 \langle\chi_{10}|V_d(1)|\chi_{10}\rangle
 =P_2(d)P_8^{(33/16)}(d)
 =P_4(d)P_6^{(65/16)}(d).
\]
The Gram matrices and the normalized singular-vector coefficients are
rational at \(c=1/2\), so the defining linear system gives
\(\gamma_{10}\in\mathbb Q\).  It remains to evaluate this intrinsic scalar.
Apply the generic recursion of Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}
algebraically near \(b_0\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-recursion},
once the resonant paths are combined, their constant term equals the
specialized fixed-\(c\) coefficient as a rational function of
\(\Delta\), including the coefficient of \((\Delta-h_0)^{-2}\).

The two nested paths have opposite
\(t^{-1}\) numerator coefficients.  After their cancellation, the resulting
double-pole coefficient can be written using the
\((1,2)\to(4,2)\) path as
\[
 \frac{\dot h_{1,2}-\dot h_{2,2}}
 {\dot h_{1,2}-\dot h_{4,2}}\,
 {\mathcal A}_{1,2}(b_0){\mathcal A}_{4,2}(b_0)
 P_2(d)P_8^{(33/16)}(d).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:level-ten-constants} evaluates this scalar coefficient as
\(-192/1121229484375\).  Rewriting the same combined coefficient using the
other path gives the equal expression with slope ratio \(1/7\); the two
expressions are alternatives, not additive contributions.  Comparison
with the already proved intrinsic coefficient
\(P_2P_8/\gamma_{10}\) yields the displayed value of \(\gamma_{10}\).
Independently, the Gram-matrix calculation in
\path{tools/audit_grade10_direct.py --crossing-only} constructs the first
two Smith layers and obtains the same scalar directly from the second
crossing form, without using the confluence calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite recursive level-ten principal part]
\label{thm:level-ten-principal-part}
Define the finite shifted-block parts
\[
 Q_8(d)=
 \left.
 \frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \left\{(z-\tfrac{33}{16})H_8(1/2,z,d)\right\}
 \right|_{z=33/16}
\]
and
\[
 Q_6(d)=
 \left.
 \frac{\partial}{\partial z}
 \left\{(z-\tfrac{65}{16})H_6(1/2,z,d)\right\}
 \right|_{z=65/16}.
\]
For
\[
 \alpha=(1,2),\quad\beta=(2,2),\quad
 \mu=(4,2),\quad\nu=(1,6),
\]
set
\[
 \delta_\alpha=h_\alpha+2-h_\mu,qquad
 \delta_\beta=h_\beta+4-h_\nu,
\]
and, with dots denoting \(d/db\) at \(b_0\), put
\[
 \Theta_{10}(d)=
 \sum_{(\kappa,\tau)=(\alpha,\mu),(\beta,\nu)}
 \left(
 \frac{\dot{\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau+
       {\mathcal R}_\kappa\dot{\mathcal R}_\tau}{\dot\delta_\kappa}
 -\frac{{\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau\ddot\delta_\kappa}
 {2\dot\delta_\kappa^2}
 \right)_{b=b_0}.
\]
Its factored expansion is equation~(\ref{eq:theta10-expanded}) of
Appendix~\ref{app:expanded-data}.
Then the entire principal part of the level-ten reduced coefficient at
\(\Delta=h_0=1/16\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-ten-pp}
 \begin{split}
 \PP_{\Delta=h_0}H_{10}(1/2,\Delta,d)
 ={}&
 -\frac{192\,P_2(d)P_8^{(33/16)}(d)}
 {1121229484375\,(\Delta-h_0)^2}\\
 &+
 \frac{
 -\frac47P_2(d)Q_8(d)
 +\frac{648}{13475}P_4(d)Q_6(d)
 +\Theta_{10}(d)}
 {\Delta-h_0}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Every term on the right is finite and is evaluated at \(c=1/2\).  Thus
this is a complete finite recursive formula for the principal part.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(t=b-b_0\), and abbreviate the four labels
\[
 \alpha=(1,2),\quad \beta=(2,2),\quad
 \mu=(4,2),\quad \nu=(1,6).
\]
Set
\[
 z_\alpha=h_\alpha+2,\qquad z_\beta=h_\beta+4,\qquad
 \delta_\alpha=z_\alpha-h_\mu,\qquad
 \delta_\beta=z_\beta-h_\nu.
\]
Only the \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-terms of the generic recursion can
develop a pole at \(\Delta=h_0\).  Their numerators at level ten are
\[
 C_\alpha(b)
 ={\mathcal R}_\alpha(b,d)
 H_8(c(b),z_\alpha(b),d),\qquad
 C_\beta(b)
 ={\mathcal R}_\beta(b,d)
 H_6(c(b),z_\beta(b),d).
\]
At \((b_0,z_\alpha(b_0))\), the level-eight shifted block has one
transverse pole, labelled by \(\mu\), and no other Kac divisor through
that point.  The analogous statement holds for the level-six block and
\(\nu\).  Therefore the definitions of \(Q_8,Q_6\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_8(c(b),z_\alpha(b),d)
 &=\frac{{\mathcal R}_\mu(b,d)}{\delta_\alpha(b)}
   +Q_8(d)+O(t),\\
 H_6(c(b),z_\beta(b),d)
 &=\frac{{\mathcal R}_\nu(b,d)}{\delta_\beta(b)}
   +Q_6(d)+O(t).
 \end{aligned}
\]

For either nested pair \((\kappa,\tau)\), expansion of
\({\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau/\delta_\kappa\) gives finite term
\[
 T_{\kappa,\tau}
 =
 \frac{\dot{\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau
       +{\mathcal R}_\kappa\dot{\mathcal R}_\tau}
      {\dot\delta_\kappa}
 -
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_\kappa{\mathcal R}_\tau\ddot\delta_\kappa}
      {2\dot\delta_\kappa^2},
\]
where every undotted factor on the right is evaluated at \(b_0\).
The two \(t^{-1}\) terms cancel because
\[
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_\alpha{\mathcal R}_\mu}{\dot\delta_\alpha}
 +\frac{{\mathcal R}_\beta{\mathcal R}_\nu}{\dot\delta_\beta}=0;
\]
this is Lemma~\ref{lem:level-ten-constants} together with
\(P_2P_8=P_4P_6\).

Write \(y=\Delta-h_0\).  Expanding
\[
 \frac{C_\alpha(b)}{\Delta-h_\alpha(b)}
 +\frac{C_\beta(b)}{\Delta-h_\beta(b)}
\]
at \(t=0\), the cancelled \(t^{-1}\) numerator produces the \(y^{-2}\)
coefficient
\[
 \frac{\dot h_\alpha-\dot h_\beta}{\dot\delta_\alpha}
 {\mathcal R}_\alpha(b_0,d){\mathcal R}_\mu(b_0,d)
 =
 -\frac{192}{1121229484375}P_2(d)P_8^{(33/16)}(d).
\]
The \(y^{-1}\) coefficient is
\[
 {\mathcal R}_\alpha(b_0,d)Q_8(d)
 +{\mathcal R}_\beta(b_0,d)Q_6(d)
 +T_{\alpha,\mu}+T_{\beta,\nu}.
\]
The first two residue constants are
\(-4P_2/7\) and \(648P_4/13475\).
Differentiating the finite products in
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} and factoring over
\(\mathbb Q[d]\) gives
\[
 T_{\alpha,\mu}+T_{\beta,\nu}=\Theta_{10}(d).
\]
Substitution proves the stated principal part.
\end{proof}

\section{Irreducible Ising specialization}\label{sec:irreducible}

\begin{proposition}[Identity insertion in the Verma recursion]
\label{prop:identity-verma}
For \(d=0\),
\[
 H_\Delta^{(1/2)}(0\mid q)=1
\]
as a rational function of \(\Delta\).  Thus specialization of the
meromorphic Verma block at a degenerate weight is not the irreducible
Ising trace; the latter requires taking the quotient by the radical.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the factor with \(k=l=1\)
in \({\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,0)\) is
\[
 \frac{(b+b^{-1})^2-(b+b^{-1})^2}{4}=0.
\]
Hence every generic residue \(R_a(t)\) vanishes identically at \(d=0\).
Definitions~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} and
\ref{def:collision-moments} then give
\(\widetilde A_{n,k,N}(0)=0\), hence \(A_{n,k,N}(0)=0\), for every
\(N\geq1\).
Equivalently, in the inverse-Shapovalov definition the identity insertion
has \(\rho_N=G_N\), so \(F_N=p(N)\) and eta reduction gives \(H=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced irreducible identity block]
\label{def:irreducible-identity}
For \(h\in\{0,1/2,1/16\}\), let \(L(1/2,h)\) be the irreducible quotient
of \(M(1/2,h)\), and define
\[
 \widehat H_h(q)
 =\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)
  \sum_{N\geq0}\dim L(1/2,h)_{h+N}\,q^N.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The three Ising characters]
\label{thm:ising-characters}
The quotient identity blocks of
Definition~\ref{def:irreducible-identity} are
\[
 \widehat H_0(q)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{((24j+1)^2-1)/48}
 -q^{((24j+7)^2-1)/48}
 \right),
\]
\[
 \widehat H_{1/2}(q)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{((24j+5)^2-25)/48}
 -q^{((24j+11)^2-25)/48}
 \right),
\]
and
\[
 \widehat H_{1/16}(q)
 =\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{((24j-2)^2-4)/48}
 -q^{((24j+10)^2-4)/48}
 \right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \chi_h(q)
 =q^{h-1/48}
  \frac{\widehat H_h(q)}
  {\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)}
\]
are the vacuum, energy, and spin Ising characters.  Their eta-reduced
beginnings are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat H_0&=1-q-q^6+q^{11}+q^{13}-q^{20}+\cdots,\\
 \widehat H_{1/2}&=1-q^2-q^3+q^7+q^{17}-q^{25}-q^{28}+\cdots,\\
 \widehat H_{1/16}&=1-q^2-q^4+q^{10}+q^{14}-q^{24}-q^{30}+\cdots.
 \end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Ising model is the minimal series \(c_{3,4}=1/2\).
The Class \(R^+\), Case \(1+\), BGG resolution in
\cite[Theorem~6.9]{IoharaKoga2011} is an exact complex of Verma modules.
Taking its Euler--Poincaré character gives, for an interior label
\((r,s)\),
\[
 \chi_{r,s}(q)
 =\frac1{\eta(q)}
 \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 \left(
 q^{(24j+4r-3s)^2/48}
 -q^{(24j+4r+3s)^2/48}
 \right).
\]
Use \((r,s)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)\), respectively.  Since
\[
 h_{r,s}=\frac{(4r-3s)^2-1}{48},\qquad
 \eta(q)=q^{1/24}\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m),
\]
factoring out \(q^{h_{r,s}-1/48}\) gives exactly the three displayed
\(\widehat H\)-series.  For any fixed power of \(q\), only finitely many
integers \(j\) contribute, so the Euler--Poincaré sums are
coefficientwise finite.  Direct evaluation of the exponents through
degree \(30\) gives the final three lines.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Character replay]
The three BGG numerators and the resulting quotient dimensions through
level \(30\) are replayed by
\path{tools/audit_ising_characters.py} using exact integer arithmetic,
and are checked there against an independent free-fermion count: the
spin module is counted by partitions into distinct positive integers,
and the vacuum and energy modules by partitions of \(2N\)
(respectively \(2N+1\)) into distinct odd parts with an even
(respectively odd) number of parts.
\end{remark}

\section{Scope and outlook}\label{sec:scope}

Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} is a coefficientwise theorem for the
meromorphic Verma-module torus block at every fixed nonzero algebraic
\(b_\star\).  In particular, it applies to every minimal-model central
charge; Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions} supplies the corresponding
collision classes.  The Ising specialization is developed in greater detail
because its classes, coefficient field, embedding diamonds, and exact local
computations are especially transparent.  The output is fixed at
\(c_\star\), while the construction uses the exact formal deformation
\(b=b_\star+t\) to combine resonant generic-recursion terms.  The method
should therefore be distinguished both from numerical regularization and
from a recursion formulated solely in intrinsic data of the specialized
category.

At any prescribed level the label sets and recursion trees are finite.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound} and
\ref{lem:precision-budget} give an a priori Laurent jet budget,
but do not claim a favorable complexity bound: exact rational
simplification can grow rapidly with the level.  The local Smith analysis
explains the first simple and double poles, but an all-depth identification
of every Smith layer with the Ising embedding diagram is not required for
the coefficientwise confluence theorem.  The level-ten principal part has
been verified directly against the inverse-Shapovalov definition, and the
level-ten second crossing form is now also reconstructed intrinsically from
the \(42\times42\) Gram matrix (Remark~\ref{rem:audit-scope}).

Open directions include uniform complexity bounds and a representation-
theoretic description of the resulting pole layers as \(p,p'\) vary,
an intrinsic fixed-central-charge recursion that eliminates the formal
deformation, and a corresponding recursion for irreducible minimal-model
traces at degenerate internal weights.  The BGG calculation of
Section~\ref{sec:irreducible} is only a quotient consistency check and is
logically separate from the confluence theorem.
