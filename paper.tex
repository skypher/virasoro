\begin{abstract}
For every fixed \(b_\star\ne0\) we give a finite formula for each coefficient
\(H_N\) of the torus one-point Virasoro block, with the eta-function factor
removed, at central charge \(c_\star=1+6(b_\star+b_\star^{-1})^2\).  This
includes rational \(b_\star^2\), where Kac weights coincide and the residues
of the generic recursion of Poghossian and of Hadasz, Jask\'olski and Suchanek
can have poles.  The principal part of \(H_N\) at each Kac weight is a sum of
constant terms of finite Laurent expansions in \(b-b_\star\), with an explicit
bound on the precision needed.

At an interior Kac weight \(\lambda\) of a minimal model, the pole order of
\(H_N\) at \(\lambda\) is at most the number of Jantzen layers of the Verma
module that have reached level \(N\).  When this number is positive, the
coefficient at that order is a sum of blocks at shifted weights, weighted by
torus matrix elements of singular vectors divided by their Jantzen norms.  For
an indeterminate external weight \(d\) the bound is attained at every interior
weight and every \(N\geq1\).  In the Ising model the first double poles at the
energy, spin and identity weights occur at levels 7, 10 and 11.

At an interior weight \(\lambda\), the normalized torus pairing descends to the
irreducible module exactly when \(d\) lies in the minimal-model fusion set, and
all Verma coefficients \(F_N\) are regular at \(\lambda\) exactly when \(d\)
lies in a larger reflected set.  For \(d\) in the fusion set, a generalized
Rocha--Caridi identity writes the irreducible trace as an alternating sum of
Verma blocks over the braided embedding diagram, weighted by products of
explicit rational first-layer ratios.  With the confluence formula, it
computes every coefficient of the trace.

\vskip5pt
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}\enspace Virasoro algebra; conformal blocks; torus
one-point block; Zamolodchikov recursion; minimal models; Jantzen
filtration; Ising model; irreducible modules; Rocha--Caridi formula.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The torus one-point Virasoro block with internal weight \(\Delta\) and
external weight \(d\) is a power series in \(q\).  Its coefficient \(F_N\)
pairs the level-\(N\) subspace of the Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) with itself
through the inverse Shapovalov matrix and the matrix elements of a primary
field of weight \(d\) (Section~\ref{sec:blocks}).  Multiplying the series by
\(\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)\) gives the eta-reduced coefficients \(H_N\)
(Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced}).  Write \(c=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2\).  For
generic \(b\), each \(H_N\) has only simple poles in \(\Delta\), at the Kac
weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with \(rs\leq N\), and the residues satisfy
Poghossian's recursion~\cite[\S5, (5.3)--(5.7)]{Poghossian2009}, derived
in~\cite[\S3, (7)--(9)]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}; see
\cite[\S2.2]{PoghosyanPoghosyan2026} for the light limit \(b\to0\) at fixed
weights.  At rational \(b^2\), which includes every minimal-model charge, two
things go wrong in this recursion: different Kac labels give the same weight,
and individual residues have poles in \(b\).  The coefficients \(H_N\) are
still well defined there.  By \emph{confluence} we mean passing to the limit
\(b\to b_\star\) in the generic recursion after grouping the terms whose poles
collide.

Zamolodchikov introduced residue recursions for sphere four-point
blocks~\cite{Zamolodchikov1984,Zamolodchikov1987}. Cho, Collier and Yin give
central-charge recursions in the plumbing frame at arbitrary genus and
internal-weight recursions in the sphere linear and torus necklace
channels~\cite{ChoCollierYin2017}.  At rational \(b^2\), Javerzat,
Santachiara and Foda analyze the cancellation of the colliding poles in
examples and for the identity
insertion~\cite[\S\S4--6; the resonant-term limits are (60)--(61)]{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.
For \(d=0\) they also obtain the minimal-model characters by a
three-parameter limit of the generic torus recursion, and check the
coefficient identity following their (83) numerically up to
\(N\approx300\)~\cite[\S5]{JaverzatSantachiaraFoda2018}.
Stocco gives central-charge-pole-free expressions through fourth order,
conjectures their all-order form and proposes a way to compute
them~\cite[\S\S II.B--II.C and Appendix~A]{Stocco2022}.  In our comparison
(see the code availability section) his expressions reproduce \(H_2\) and
\(H_3\) identically and \(H_4\) at sample values of \(b\).  Ribault analyzes
higher-order poles and rational limits of sphere
blocks~\cite[\S3]{Ribault2019}, and calls a recursion at rational \(b^2\) an
open problem whose simplest case is the torus one-point
block~\cite[\S4.3.3]{Ribault2026}.  For irreducible minimal-model modules,
Coulomb-gas screening integrals in Felder's Fock-space BRST
complex~\cite{Felder1989,FelderSilvotti1989} give integral representations
of torus traces with a primary insertion.

At every fixed \(b_\star\neq0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} gives the
coefficients of the Verma block at internal weight \(\Delta\), for \(N\geq1\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 &=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k},\\
 A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 &=\CT_t
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}}
 R_a(t)\,S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\,
 \bigl(h_a(t)-\lambda\bigr)^{k-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) consists of the Kac weights with
\(rs\leq N\), and \({\mathcal C}^\star_{\lambda,N}\) contains the labels
attaining \(\lambda\).  For \(a=(r,s)\), set \(t=b-b_\star\),
\(h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\) and \(\ell_a=rs\).  \(R_a\) is the generic
residue, \(S_{a,M}\) the shifted Laurent block
(Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent}), \(\CT_t\) the constant term, and
\(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}\) an explicit bound on the pole order.  The
formula uses finite jets of explicit rational functions of \(b\), and
uniqueness of partial fractions identifies it with the coefficient defined
through the inverse Shapovalov matrix.  It is obtained by expanding the generic
recursion in \(b-b_\star\); a recursion that uses only data at \(c=c_\star\),
as asked for in~\cite[\S4.3.3]{Ribault2026}, is not given here
(Section~\ref{sec:scope}).

At a minimal-model charge and an interior Kac weight \(\lambda\), the
Feigin--Fuchs embedding diagram describes the Jantzen filtration
\(J^\bullet\) of the specialized Verma module
(Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} and Figure~\ref{fig:braided}).  Its dimensions
determine the multiplicities of the local Smith exponents \(e_j\) of the
Shapovalov matrix: \(\dim J^i_N=\#\{j:e_j\geq i\}\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}).  Thus the pole order of \(H_N\) at
\(\lambda\) is at most the Jantzen depth \(j_N(\lambda)\), the number of
layers that have reached level \(N\).  Let \(w^\pm_k\) be the \(k\)-th
layer's singular vectors, at levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), and put
\(\ell_k=\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\).  For \(k\geq1\) and
\(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\), Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-top}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^kH_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}
 \frac{\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}\bigl(c_\star,\lambda+\ell^\pm_k,d\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(\gamma^\pm_k\), nonzero for each sign in the sum, is the \(k\)-th
Jantzen norm of \(w^\pm_k\).  This is a fixed-\(c\) analogue of
Zamolodchikov's residue formula for the coefficient at the Jantzen bound.  The
Jantzen form uses the deformation in \(\Delta\) at fixed charge, and the
coefficient can vanish at specialized \(d\).

For indeterminate \(d\), Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp} shows that the
bound \(j_N(\lambda)\) is attained at every interior weight and every
\(N\geq1\).  On the levels where both layer vectors contribute, the proof
compares \(\ell\)-adic valuations of the two terms of~\eqref{eq:intro-top} at
the external weights \(d=h_{1,\pm3}(b_\star)\)
(Propositions~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing} and~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}).
At specialized \(d\) the pole can disappear: for \(d=0\), \(H_N=0\) for
\(N\geq1\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}).
Proposition~\ref{prop:ising-double-poles} gives the first Ising double poles.

For fixed \(d\) and an interior minimal-model weight,
Theorem~\ref{thm:descent-regularity} identifies the fusion set for descent to
\(L(c_\star,\lambda)\times L(c_\star,\lambda)\) and the larger reflected set for
regularity of all \(F_N\).  For fusion insertions,
Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-rocha-caridi} gives the irreducible trace as a weighted
alternating sum over the braided diagram; Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio}
gives the generic vertex ratio, and Proposition~\ref{prop:bgg-cocycle} its
finite nonzero specialization and path-independent products.

Part~I (Sections~\ref{sec:blocks}--\ref{sec:ising}) concerns Verma blocks.
Section~\ref{sec:blocks} fixes notation; Table~\ref{tab:notation} lists the
main symbols.  Section~\ref{sec:resonance} describes which Kac labels give the
same weight at fixed \(b_\star\), and Section~\ref{sec:partial-fractions}
proves the finite partial-fraction expansion.  Section~\ref{sec:smith} relates
the pole order at an interior minimal-model weight to the Jantzen filtration
and computes the top coefficient.  Section~\ref{sec:generic-data} collects the
generic residue data and the generic recursion and proves that the pole-order
bound is sharp.  Section~\ref{sec:all-order} proves the confluence formula, and
Section~\ref{sec:ising} treats the Ising model.  Part~II
(Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:examples}) concerns irreducible traces:
Section~\ref{sec:setup} sets up two-weight Ward identities and the braided
orbit, Section~\ref{sec:irr-results} states the results,
Sections~\ref{sec:descent}--\ref{sec:telescoping} prove them, and
Section~\ref{sec:examples} gives examples.  Section~\ref{sec:scope} lists what
the paper leaves open.  Appendix~\ref{app:h13} contains the integrality and
level-polynomiality proofs used in Section~\ref{sec:generic-data}.

\section{Blocks and the reduced series}\label{sec:blocks}

\begin{definition}[Virasoro Verma module]
The Virasoro algebra has generators \(L_n\) (\(n\in\mathbb Z\)) and central
element \(C\), with
\([L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{C}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}\).
The Verma module \(M(c,\Delta)\) is generated by \(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) with
\(L_0\lvert\Delta\rangle=\Delta\lvert\Delta\rangle\),
\(L_n\lvert\Delta\rangle=0\) (\(n>0\)), on which \(C\) acts by the central
charge \(c\in\mathbb C\).
For a partition \(Y=(y_1\geq\cdots\geq y_\ell)\), set
\(L_{-Y}=L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_\ell}\), \(|Y|=\sum_i y_i\), and
\(\ell(Y)=\ell\).  Write \(\langle\Delta|\) for the linear functional on
\(M(c,\Delta)\) that maps \(\lvert\Delta\rangle\) to \(1\) and vanishes on
all positive levels, and \(\omega\) for the anti-involution of the enveloping
algebra with \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  The \emph{Shapovalov form} is the
symmetric bilinear form
\(\langle x\lvert\Delta\rangle,y\lvert\Delta\rangle\rangle
=\langle\Delta|\omega(x)y|\Delta\rangle\) on \(M(c,\Delta)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shapovalov and torus matrices]\label{def:matrices}
At level \(N\), for partitions \(|Y|=|Y'|=N\) and \(L_Y=L_{y_\ell}\cdots L_{y_1}\), let
\[
 G_N(c,\Delta)_{Y,Y'}=\langle\Delta|L_YL_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle,
 \qquad
 \rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}=\langle\Delta|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\Delta\rangle,
\]
where \(V_d(z)\) is primary with \(\langle\Delta|V_d(z)|\Delta\rangle=z^{-d}\).
Thus \(G_N\) is the Gram matrix of the Shapovalov form at level \(N\).  The
matrix \(\rho_N\) is computed from the primary Ward identity, as in the proof
of Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}; Lemma~\ref{lem:two-weight-ward} gives
the defining property of the underlying bilinear form, which we call the
\emph{torus form}.  Whenever \(G_N\) is invertible, put
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=\sum_{|Y|=|Y'|=N}(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1})_{Y,Y'}\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Eta-reduced block]\label{def:eta-reduced}
The torus one-point block is
\(q^{\Delta-c/24}\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)\), where
\(\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)=\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N\).
Set \(\phi(q)=\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)=\sum_{j\geq0}\eta_jq^j\) and define the
eta-reduced series
\(H_\Delta^{(c)}(d\mid q)=\phi(q)\,\mathcal F^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)\).
The coefficient of \(q^N\) in \(H_\Delta^{(c)}\) is \(H_N=\sum_{j=0}^N\eta_jF_{N-j}\),
with \(\eta_0=1\) and \(\eta_1=\eta_2=-1\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial matrix entries]\label{lem:polynomial-entries}
For fixed \(N\), every entry of \(G_N(c,\Delta)\) belongs to
\(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta]\), and every entry of
\(\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q[c,\Delta,d]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Commute positive modes to the ket, where they annihilate
\(|\Delta\rangle\). The surviving \(L_0\) and central terms act by
\(\Delta\) and \(c\), giving polynomial Gram entries. For the torus entries,
also commute through the insertion using
\([L_n,V_d(z)]=z^n(z\partial_z+(n+1)d)V_d(z)\), and move the remaining
negative modes to the bra. Each Ward commutator removes a mode, and the
remaining derivatives of \(z^{-d}\) at \(z=1\) are polynomial in \(d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terminology and notation]\label{rem:notation}
We call \(\Delta\) the \emph{internal weight} and \(d\) the \emph{external
weight}.  The \(L_0\)-offset \(N\) of a graded component is called its
\emph{level}.  For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), we write
\(\ell_a=rs\) for the level of its singular vector.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries} and
Cramer's rule, each \(F_N\) and \(H_N\) is a rational function of
\((c,\Delta)\) and a polynomial in \(d\), and \(H_0=1\).  For a rational
function \(f\) of \(\Delta\) and a point \(\lambda\),
\(\PP_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) denotes the principal part of \(f\) at \(\lambda\),
the sum of the negative-power terms of its Laurent expansion.
\(\Res_{\Delta=\lambda}f\) denotes the coefficient of
\((\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\).
Unless \(d\) is specialized, it is an indeterminate and pole
orders in \(\Delta\) are understood over \(\mathbb Q(c,d)\), or over
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\) after fixing \(b=b_\star\).  We write \(\Vir_-\) for the span
of the \(L_{-n}\), \(n\geq1\), call \(\{L_{-Y}\lvert\Delta\rangle\}\) the PBW
(Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt) basis, and \(\ord_x\) is the order at \(x=0\).
\end{remark}

\section{Fixed specializations and collision classes}
\label{sec:resonance}

\begin{definition}[Kac weights and fixed specializations]
\label{def:fixed-specialization}
For \(b\in\mathbb C^\times\), set
\(c(b)=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2\) and
\(h_{r,s}(b)=\frac14((b+b^{-1})^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2)\),
which depend on \(b\) only through \(b^2\).  Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\),
put \(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and
\(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)=\{h_{r,s}(b_\star):r,s>0,\ rs\leq N\}\), and for
\(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) define the finite collision class
\[
 {\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2:
      rs\leq N,\ h_{r,s}(b_\star)=\lambda\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal-model collision classes]
\label{prop:minimal-collisions}
Let \(p,p'\) be coprime positive integers and choose
\(b_\star^2=-p'/p\).  Then
\[
 c_\star=1-\frac{6(p'-p)^2}{pp'},\qquad
 h_{r,s}(b_\star)
 =\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}.
\]
For positive pairs \((r,s)\) and \((r',s')\), their specialized weights
are equal if and only if, for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 (r',s')=(r+p\ell,s+p'\ell)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (r',s')=(p\ell-r,p'\ell-s).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formulas follow by substituting \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) in
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}.  Equality of the two weights is
equivalent to \(p'r'-ps'=\pm(p'r-ps)\).  For the plus sign, coprimality gives
\((r'-r,s'-s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\); for the minus sign it gives
\((r'+r,s'+s)=(p\ell,p'\ell)\).  Conversely, both operations preserve
\((p'r-ps)^2\) and hence the weight.
\end{proof}


\section{Coefficientwise partial fractions}\label{sec:partial-fractions}

\begin{lemma}[Large internal weight]\label{lem:large-weight}
For fixed \(c,d,N\), \(F_N(c,\Delta,d)\to p(N)\), the number of partitions
of \(N\), and \(H_N(c,\Delta,d)\to\delta_{N,0}\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rescale \(L_{-Y}|\Delta\rangle\) by \(\Delta^{-\ell(Y)/2}\). Each factor \(\Delta\) in a Gram entry comes from a
commutator producing \(L_0\) and uses at least one original mode on each
side, and each original mode is used once. The degree is at most \(\min(\ell(Y),\ell(Y'))\), so a rescaled entry
vanishes in the limit if \(\ell(Y)\ne\ell(Y')\). At equal lengths, a term of top
degree pairs equal positive and negative indices via \(2jL_0\) in
\([L_j,L_{-j}]\). Thus it occurs only on the diagonal, where the leading
coefficient is \(\prod_j m_j(Y)!\,(2j)^{m_j(Y)}\), with \(m_j(Y)\) the
multiplicity of \(j\) in \(Y\).

For \(\rho_N\), the terms with no Ward commutator reproduce the Gram
terms since \(\langle\Delta|V_d(1)|\Delta\rangle=1\). Each Ward
commutator spends a mode without producing a factor \(\Delta\), lowering
the degree below the top degree at equal lengths. The two rescaled matrices
therefore tend to the same invertible diagonal matrix \(D_N\).
Then \(F_N\to\Tr(D_N^{-1}D_N)=p(N)\), and
\((\sum_j\eta_jq^j)(\sum_kp(k)q^k)=1\) gives the limit of \(H_N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite pole expansion at a fixed specialization]
\label{thm:general-partial-fractions}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\).  Then \(H_0=1\),
and for every \(N\geq1\) there are unique coefficients
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\)
(\(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\), \(k\geq1\)), only finitely many nonzero,
such that
\[
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}\ \sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\]
The largest \(k\) with \(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\neq0\) (or \(0\) if all
vanish) is the pole multiplicity \(m^\star_{\lambda,N}\) of \(H_N\) at \(\lambda\).
Consequently
\(H_\Delta^{(c_\star)}(d\mid q)=1+\sum_{\lambda,k}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)
(\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\) with
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k}(d;q)=\sum_{N\geq1}A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)q^N\),
locally finite coefficientwise in \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries},
\(F_N=\Tr(G_N^{-1}\rho_N^{\mathsf T})\) is rational in
\(\Delta\), and its poles are contained in the zero locus of
\(\det G_N\).  The Kac determinant formula
\cite{Kac1979}, \cite[\S3.2, Theorem~3.1]{FeiginFuchs1982}, in the form of
\cite[Theorem~4.2, (4.16) and (4.21)]{IoharaKoga2011}, gives
\(\det G_N(c(b),\Delta)=C_N\prod_{rs\leq N}(\Delta-h_{r,s}(b))^{p(N-rs)}\)
with \(C_N\neq0\) independent of \(\Delta\).  At \(c=c_\star\), the determinant
roots lie in \(\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\), and the finite sum defining \(H_N\)
introduces no other poles.  For \(N\geq1\), Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight} rules
out a polynomial part, so the partial fractions are unique.

The field \(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)\) contains \(c_\star\) and every pole
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\).  The matrix entries belong to
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[\Delta,d]\), and \(\det G_N\) does not involve
\(d\).  Laurent expansion at each pole therefore gives
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\).
Coefficientwise local finiteness follows because \(rs\leq N\) leaves
only finitely many labels.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} in Section~\ref{sec:all-order}
computes the coefficients \(A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\) as finite constant-term
expressions in the generic residue data.


\section{Smith exponents, Jantzen layers, and the top pole}\label{sec:smith}

\begin{lemma}[Pole order from local invariant factors]\label{lem:smith}
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[x]]\), and let \(G(x)\in M_m(R)\) have nonzero determinant
and Smith exponents \(0\leq e_1\leq\cdots\leq e_m\).  Then
\(\ord_x\det G=\sum_{i=1}^m e_i\), \(\dim\ker G(0)=\#\{i:e_i>0\}\), and
\(G(x)^{-1}\) has pole order \(e_m\).  In particular, if
\(\ord_x\det G=\dim\ker G(0)\), every positive Smith exponent equals \(1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(R\) is a discrete valuation ring, there are
\(U,V\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R)\) with
\(UGV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_1},\ldots,x^{e_m})\).  Taking determinants,
reducing modulo \(x\), and inverting give the three assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Jantzen layers in the internal weight]
\label{def:jantzen-layers}
Fix \((c,h)\) and identify \(M(c,h+x)_N\) with \(\mathbb C^{p(N)}\) in the PBW
basis.  For \(i\geq0\), let \(J^i_N(c,h)\) be the set of \(v\in M(c,h)_N\)
admitting a polynomial family \(v(x)\) with \(v(0)=v\) and
\(G_N(c,h+x)\,v(x)\in x^i\,\mathbb C[x]^{p(N)}\), and put
\(J^i(c,h)=\bigoplus_{N\geq0}J^i_N(c,h)\).
\end{definition}

For \(c\neq1,25\), this fixed-charge filtration agrees with the Jantzen
filtration of~\cite[\S5.5, (5.49)]{IoharaKoga2011}, and it is a Jantzen
filtration along a curve in the sense of~\cite[\S4 and \S5.2]{Astashkevich1997}.

\begin{lemma}[Smith exponents and the Jantzen filtration]
\label{lem:jantzen-smith}
Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), with local Smith exponents
\(e_1,\ldots,e_{p(N)}\) at \(x=0\).  Then
\(\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\#\{j:e_j\geq i\}\) (\(i\geq1\)) and
\(\sum_{i\geq1}\dim J^i_N(c,h)=\ord_x\det G_N(c,h+x)\).
Each \(J^i(c,h)\) is a submodule of \(M(c,h)\), and \(J^1(c,h)\) is the
radical of the Shapovalov form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(UG_NV=\operatorname{diag}(x^{e_j})\) over \(\mathbb C[[x]]\).
With \(v(x)=V(x)w(x)\), the layer condition is
\(w_j\in x^{\max(0,i-e_j)}\mathbb C[[x]]\). Truncation modulo \(x^i\)
gives polynomial families, so \(J^i_N=V(0)\operatorname{span}\{\epsilon_j:e_j\geq i\}\)
for the coordinate vectors \(\epsilon_j\). Counting and summing gives
both dimension identities by Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}.

If \(v(x)\) is a family for \(v\), then \(L_nv(x)\) specializes to
\(L_nv\). It is a polynomial family, and for every PBW vector \(y\),
\(\langle y,L_nv(x)\rangle_{h+x}=\langle L_{-n}y,v(x)\rangle_{h+x}\in
x^i\mathbb C[x]\). Thus the layers are submodules. At \(i=1\) the
condition is \(G_N(c,h)v=0\).
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Jantzen forms and the top coefficient]
\label{lem:top-coefficient}
Fix \((c,h)\).  For \(i\geq1\) and \(u,v\in J^i_N(c,h)\), choose families
\(u(x),v(x)\) as in Definition~\ref{def:jantzen-layers} and put
\(B^i(u,v)=[x^i]\,v(x)^{\mathsf T}G_N(c,h+x)\,u(x)\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(B^i\) is a well-defined symmetric form, and
\(B^i(L_{-n}u,v)=B^i(u,L_nv)\) for \(n>0\), \(u\in J^i_{N-n}(c,h)\), and
\(v\in J^i_N(c,h)\).
\item[(ii)] Suppose \(\det G_N(c,h+x)\not\equiv0\), and let \(k\geq1\) be
the largest local Smith exponent at \(x=0\).  Then \(B^k\) is
nondegenerate on \(J^k_N(c,h)\), and
\([x^{-k}]\,G_N(c,h+x)^{-1}=\iota\,(B^k)^{-1}\iota^*\),
where \(\iota\colon J^k_N(c,h)\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^{p(N)}\) is the
inclusion.
\item[(iii)] Write \(G_N(c,h+x)=G_0+xG_1+x^2G_2+O(x^3)\).  Then
\(B^1(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}G_1u\) for \(u,v\in J^1_N(c,h)\), and
\(B^2(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}(G_2u+G_1u_1)\) for \(u,v\in J^2_N(c,h)\) and every
\(u_1\) with \(G_0u_1=-G_1u\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Changing \(u(x)\) by \(xf(x)\) changes the pairing by
\(x(G_Nv(x))^{\mathsf T}f(x)\in x^{i+1}\mathbb C[x]\), since \(G_N\) is symmetric;
by symmetry the same holds for \(v(x)\). As in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}, applying \(L_{-n}\) or \(L_n\) gives families
for the descendants. Contravariance gives the stated identity at order \(x^i\).

(ii) In the Smith bases, \(G_N^{-1}=V\operatorname{diag}(x^{-e_j})U\).
Its coefficient \(S=[x^{-k}]G_N^{-1}=V(0)EU(0)\), with \(E\) projecting to
the indices \(e_j=k\), is symmetric with column space \(J_N^k(c,h)\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}.
Thus \(S=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}u_au_b^{\mathsf T}\) for a basis \((u_a)\) of
\(J_N^k\). For a family \(u(x)\) write \(G_Nu(x)=x^kg(x)\).
At \(x=0\), \(u=Sg(0)\) and \(B^k(u,v)=v^{\mathsf T}g(0)\).
For \(u=u_c\) this gives \(u_c=\sum_{a,b}u_aC_{ab}B^k(u_c,u_b)\), so
\(C\) is the inverse matrix of \(B^k\).

(iii) For \(J^1_N\) use constant families. For \(J^2_N\) use
\(u+xu_1\) and \(v+xv_1\) with \(G_0u_1=-G_1u\), \(G_0v_1=-G_1v\).
The \(x^2\) coefficient is
\(v^{\mathsf T}(G_2u+G_1u_1)+v_1^{\mathsf T}(G_1u+G_0u_1)\), with the
last term zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Torus form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-factorization}
Let \(\chi\in M(c,h)\) be a singular vector of level \(\ell\), normalized
by a fixed PBW coefficient, and put \(P_\chi(d)=\langle\chi|V_d(1)|\chi\rangle\).
Then for all partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\(\langle\chi|L_YV_d(1)L_{-Y'}|\chi\rangle=P_\chi(d)\,\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\chi\) is singular of weight \(h+\ell\), the map
\(L_{-Y}|h+\ell\rangle\mapsto L_{-Y}\chi\) is a module homomorphism.
The torus form pulled back along it in both arguments obeys the Ward
recursion of \(\rho_m(c,h+\ell,d)\), which determines every entry from the
highest-weight value: \(P_\chi(d)\) for the pullback and \(1\) for \(\rho_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First crossing form on a singular submodule]
\label{lem:singular-crossing}
Fix \(c\) and let \(\chi=P|h_0\rangle\in M(c,h_0)\) be singular of level
\(\ell\geq1\), with \(P\in U(\Vir_-)\) held fixed as \(h\) varies.  Set
\(\beta_\chi=\left.\frac{d}{dh}\langle h|\omega(P)P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}\),
where \(\omega(L_n)=L_{-n}\).  Then for all partitions \(Y,Y'\) of \(m\),
\(\left.\frac{d}{dh}\langle h|\omega(P)L_YL_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\right|_{h=h_0}
=\beta_\chi\,G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The vectors \(u=L_{-Y'}P|h\rangle\) and \(v=L_{-Y}P|h\rangle\) have
constant PBW coordinates and specialize at \(h_0\) into the submodule
generated by \(\chi\), hence into the radical.
Using them as constant families identifies the derivative with \(B^1(u,v)\)
and gives \(\beta_\chi=B^1(\chi,\chi)\). By contravariance of \(B^1\) and
singularity of \(\chi\),
\[
 B^1(L_{-Y'}\chi,L_{-Y}\chi)=B^1(\chi,L_YL_{-Y'}\chi)
 =G_m(c,h_0+\ell)_{Y,Y'}\,\beta_\chi .
\]
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Torus norm of a singular vector]\label{lem:singular-degree}
Let \(w\in M(c,h)\) be a nonzero singular vector of level \(\ell\geq1\).
Then the coefficient \(a_w\) of \(L_{-1}^\ell\lvert h\rangle\) in \(w\) is
nonzero, and \(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle\) is a polynomial in \(d\) of
degree exactly \(2\ell\) with leading coefficient \(a_w^2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Call \(\ell(Y)\) the length of the PBW vector \(L_{-Y}\lvert h\rangle\).
The commutator of two modes is a single mode or a scalar, so a product of
\(K\) negative modes applied to \(\lvert h\rangle\) equals its ordered PBW
monomial plus vectors of length less than \(K\), and a positive mode does
not increase the length.  Let \(K\) be the largest length occurring in
\(w\), and encode its length-\(K\) part
\(\sum_{\ell(Y)=K}a_YL_{-Y}\lvert h\rangle\) as the nonzero polynomial
\(\sigma=\sum_{\ell(Y)=K}a_Yx_{y_1}\cdots x_{y_K}\) in commuting variables
\(x_1,x_2,\ldots\).  Let \(j\geq1\).  Moving \(L_j\) to the right through
\(L_{-y_1}\cdots L_{-y_K}\lvert h\rangle\), a factor \(L_{-m}\) with \(m>j\)
is replaced by \((m+j)L_{-(m-j)}\); for \(m=j\) the commutator acts by a
scalar, and for \(m<j\) it is the positive mode \(L_{j-m}\); both lower the
length.  Hence the length-\(K\) part of \(L_jw\) is encoded by
\(D_j\sigma\), where
\(D_j=\sum_{m>j}(m+j)\,x_{m-j}\,\partial/\partial x_m\), and singularity of
\(w\) gives \(D_j\sigma=0\) for all \(j\geq1\).  If a variable \(x_m\) with
\(m\geq2\) occurs in \(\sigma\), let \(m\) be the largest one and
\(j=m-1\); then \(D_j\sigma=(2m-1)\,x_1\,\partial\sigma/\partial x_m\neq0\),
a contradiction.  So \(\sigma\) is a multiple of \(x_1^K\), and comparing
levels gives \(K=\ell\) and \(a_w\neq0\).

For the second assertion, evaluate \(\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\) by the Ward
recursion in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:polynomial-entries}.  When a mode
\(L_{\pm n}\), \(n>0\), is commuted through \(V_d(z)\) inside a matrix
element between vectors of levels \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), the commutator
\([L_{\pm n},V_d(z)]=z^{\pm n}(z\partial_z+(1\pm n)d)V_d(z)\) acts on
\(z^{m_1-m_2-d}\) and contributes, with the sign of the commutation, the
factor \(nd\pm(m_1-m_2)\), of degree one in \(d\) with leading coefficient
\(n\).  Every other step (a commutator of two modes, the action of \(L_0\)
or of the central element, or an annihilation at \(\lvert h\rangle\) or
\(\langle h|\)) removes at least one mode without producing a factor
\(d\).  Since \(\langle h|V_d(1)|h\rangle=1\), the entry
\(\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\) has degree at most \(\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')\) in
\(d\), and the coefficient of \(d^{\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')}\) is
\(\prod_iy_i\prod_jy'_j\).  In
\(\langle w|V_d(1)|w\rangle=\sum_{Y,Y'}a_Ya_{Y'}\rho_\ell(c,h,d)_{Y,Y'}\)
the only pair with \(\ell(Y)+\ell(Y')=2\ell\) is \(Y=Y'=(1^\ell)\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interior weights and layer levels.}  Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be
coprime, let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) with
\(0<r<p\) and \(0<s<p'\).  The labels \((r,s)\) and \((p-r,p'-s)\) give the
same weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions}); we choose the one with
\(n_1:=p'r+ps<pp'\), and put \(n_0:=p'r-ps\).  Define the layer levels
\begin{gather*}
 \ell^\pm_{2j}=j(jpp'\pm n_0)\quad(j\geq1),\\
 \ell^+_{2j+1}=(r+jp)(s+jp'),\qquad
 \ell^-_{2j+1}=((j+1)p-r)((j+1)p'-s)\quad(j\geq0),
\end{gather*}
put \(\ell_k:=\min(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), and define the \emph{Jantzen depth}
at level \(N\) as
\(j_N(\lambda):=\max(\{0\}\cup\{k\geq1:\ell_k\leq N\})\), the number of layers
that have reached level \(N\).  Figure~\ref{fig:braided} shows the embedding
diagram that these levels describe.

\begin{theorem}[Smith layers at minimal-model charges]
\label{thm:jantzen-smith}
In this setting, \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) has, for every \(k\geq1\), singular
vectors \(w^\pm_k\) of levels \(\ell^\pm_k\), and with
\(W^\pm_k=U(\Vir_-)w^\pm_k\), one has
\(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)=W^+_i+W^-_i\) for \(i\geq1\).  Consequently, at every
level \(N\), the number of local Smith exponents of \(G_N(c_\star,\lambda+x)\)
that are at least \(i\) is \(\dim(W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\).  The inverse Shapovalov
matrix \(G_N(c_\star,\Delta)^{-1}\) has a pole of order exactly
\(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\), and \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) and
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) have poles of order at most \(j_N(\lambda)\)
there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the notation of~\cite[\S5.1.4]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(c_\star=c_{p,p'}\), and
\(p'r+ps<pp'\) puts \((r,s)\) in the interior set, so \((c_\star,\lambda)\) is in
Class~\(R^+\), Case~\(1+\).  Since \(c_\star\neq1,25\),
\cite[Theorem~6.3(i), (6.3)]{IoharaKoga2011} identifies \(J^i(c_\star,\lambda)\)
with the sum of the two Verma submodules of highest weights \(\xi_{\pm i}\) in
the braided diagram of~\cite[Proposition~5.4 and Figure~5.4]{IoharaKoga2011}.
By \cite[(5.4) and (5.22)]{IoharaKoga2011}, \(\xi_0=\lambda\)
and, for \(k\geq1\), the weights \(\xi_{\pm k}\) have squared Coulomb-gas
momenta \((2jpp'\mp n_0)^2\) when \(k=2j\), and \((2(j+1)pp'-n_1)^2\) and
\((2jpp'+n_1)^2\) when \(k=2j+1\).  Since
\(h_{u,v}(b_\star)=((p'u-pv)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\), their levels above
\(\lambda\) are \(\ell^\mp_k\); note that the sign of \(\xi_{\pm k}\) is
opposite to that of the level.

The same layers follow from \cite[Theorem~B(e), case \(\mathrm{III}_-\)]{Astashkevich1997},
which applies because the line \(c=c_\star\) is transverse to every Kac
curve through \((\lambda,c_\star)\) (as \(c'(b_\star)\neq0\)).

By Lemmas~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith} and~\ref{lem:smith}, the pole order of
\(G_N^{-1}\) is the largest \(i\geq1\) with \((W^+_i+W^-_i)_N\neq0\), or zero if
there is none, and this space is
nonzero exactly when \(\min(\ell^+_i,\ell^-_i)\leq N\). The trace \(F_N\) has at most
this order, and so does \(H_N=\sum_j\eta_jF_{N-j}\) since \(j_{N-j}\leq j_N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Top Jantzen layer]\label{thm:top-layer}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, put
\(\mu^\pm_k=\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\),
\(\gamma^\pm_k=B^k(w^\pm_k,w^\pm_k)\), and
\(\tau^\pm_k(d)=\langle w^\pm_k|V_d(1)|w^\pm_k\rangle\).
Let \(k\geq1\) and \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\).  Then \(j_N(\lambda)=k\);
for each sign with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N\), one has \(\gamma^\pm_k\neq0\),
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\neq0\) for \(M\leq N-\ell^\pm_k\),
\(\deg_d\tau^\pm_k=2\ell^\pm_k\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-layer}
 \lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^kH_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\pm:\ \ell^\pm_k\leq N}\frac{\tau^\pm_k(d)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 H_{N-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\ell^\pm_0=0\).  For \(j\geq0\), expanding the layer levels gives
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
  & \text{upper sign} & \text{lower sign}\\[2pt]
  \ell^+_{2j+1}-\ell^\pm_{2j} & s(r+2jp) & r(s+2jp')\\
  \ell^-_{2j+1}-\ell^\pm_{2j} & (p-r)((2j+1)p'-s) & (p'-s)((2j+1)p-r)\\
  \ell^\pm_{2j+2}-\ell^+_{2j+1} & (p'-s)((2j+1)p+r) & (p-r)((2j+1)p'+s)\\
  \ell^\pm_{2j+2}-\ell^-_{2j+1} & r(2(j+1)p'-s) & s(2(j+1)p-r)
 \end{array}
\]
and all these differences are positive.  Hence \(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<\ell_{k+1}\), so
\(j_N(\lambda)=k\) and \(J^{k+1}_N(c_\star,\lambda)=0\).  Moreover,
for \(j\geq1\), \(\ell^+_{2j}-\ell^-_{2j}=2jn_0\neq0\) (since \(p\nmid r\)), and
for \(j\geq0\), \(\ell^+_{2j+1}-\ell^-_{2j+1}=(2j+1)(n_1-pp')\neq0\).

Fix \(N'\) with \(\ell_k\leq N'\leq N\).  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}, \(k\) is the largest local Smith exponent
of \(G_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x)\), and
\(J^k_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda)=(W^+_k)_{N'}+(W^-_k)_{N'}\).  For each sign with
\(\ell^\pm_k\leq N'\), the vectors \(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k\), with \(Z\) a partition
of \(N'-\ell^\pm_k\), form a basis of \((W^\pm_k)_{N'}\), because the
embedding \(M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\to M(c_\star,\lambda)\) that sends the
highest-weight vector to \(w^\pm_k\) is injective
\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(i) and singularity of \(w^\pm_k\),
\[
 B^k(L_{-Z}w^\pm_k,L_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)=B^k(w^\pm_k,L_ZL_{-Z'}w^\pm_k)
 =\gamma^\pm_k\,G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)_{Z,Z'} .
\]
If both signs occur, let \(w\) and \(w'\) be the singular vectors of the
lower and the higher level.  Then \(L_ZL_{-Z'}w\) is a combination of
vectors \(L_{-Y}w\) with \(|Y|\) equal to the level of \(w'\) minus the level
of \(w\), which is positive, and
\(B^k(w',L_{-Y}w)=B^k(L_Yw',w)=0\); so the two summands are
\(B^k\)-orthogonal.  As \(B^k\) is nondegenerate on \(J^k_{N'}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii), the sum is direct and
\(\gamma^\pm_kG_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)\) is invertible.  Letting
\(N'\) range from \(\ell^\pm_k\) to \(N\) proves the nonvanishing
assertions.

Since \(k\) is the pole order of \(G_{N'}^{-1}\) at \(\lambda\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}), Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(ii) in the basis
above and Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [x^{-k}]F_{N'}(c_\star,\lambda+x,d)
 &=\sum_{\pm}\frac1{\gamma^\pm_k}\sum_{Z,Z'}
 \bigl(G_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k)^{-1}\bigr)_{Z,Z'}
 \langle w^\pm_k|L_ZV_d(1)L_{-Z'}|w^\pm_k\rangle\\
 &=\sum_{\pm}\frac{\tau^\pm_k(d)}{\gamma^\pm_k}\,
 F_{N'-\ell^\pm_k}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the sums run over signs with \(\ell^\pm_k\leq N'\).  For
\(N'<\ell_k\), \(F_{N'}\) has a pole of order at most
\(j_{N'}(\lambda)<k\) at \(\lambda\).  Multiplying by the Euler
coefficients and summing as in Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced} gives
\eqref{eq:top-layer}.  The degree of \(\tau^\pm_k\) is
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}.
\end{proof}

\section{Generic residue data and sharpness of the Jantzen bound}\label{sec:generic-data}

This section collects the residue data of the generic recursion
(Section~\ref{sec:generic-recursion}) and uses them to prove that the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} is attained for indeterminate \(d\)
(Section~\ref{sec:sharpness}).

\subsection{Generic residue data and the generic recursion}\label{sec:generic-recursion}

\begin{definition}[Generic residue data]
\label{def:generic-residue}
With \(c(b)\) and \(h_{r,s}(b)\) from Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization}
and \(\Lambda^2=(b+b^{-1})^2-4d\), define
\[
 {\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)
 =\prod_{\substack{1\leq k\leq2r-1\\k\ {\rm odd}}}
  \prod_{\substack{1\leq l\leq2s-1\\l\ {\rm odd}}}
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2}{4}\,
 \frac{\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2}{4},
\]
and \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b){\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d)\),
where
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)=\frac12\prod_{(a,b')\in[1-r,r]\times[1-s,s]\setminus\{(0,0),(r,s)\}}(ab+b'b^{-1})^{-1}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Crossing scalar and torus norm of singular vectors]
\label{prop:imported-normalizations}
For positive \(r,s\) and generic \(b\), let \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) be the
singular vector of level \(rs\) in \(M(c(b),h_{r,s}(b))\) whose
\(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient is \(1\); it exists and is unique by
\cite[Propositions~5.1, 5.2 and Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}
(with \(t=-b^2\) in their (5.4)).  Let
\(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\) be its crossing scalar from
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.  Then
\begin{align}
 \beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}&={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)^{-1},\label{eq:crossing-normalization}\\
 \langle\chi_{r,s}(b)|V_d(1)|\chi_{r,s}(b)\rangle&={\mathcal P}_{r,s}(b,d).\label{eq:fusion-normalization}
\end{align}
The identities specialize wherever the normalized singular vector is regular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation (7) of~\cite[\S3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010} uses the reciprocal
norm derivative at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}\) with the PBW coefficients fixed,
which is \(\beta_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}^{-1}\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.
The product following their (7), for \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\), is
term for term \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\), which proves~\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization}.

With \(d=((b+b^{-1})^2-\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2)/4\), we have
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}^2=\Lambda^2\). For each odd \(k,l\) in
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the four factors
\((\lambda_{\mathrm{HJS}}\pm(kb\pm lb^{-1}))/2\) of their (8) multiply to
\(\frac14(\Lambda^2-(kb+lb^{-1})^2)\cdot\frac14(\Lambda^2-(kb-lb^{-1})^2)\); the
product over \(k,l\) proves~\eqref{eq:fusion-normalization}.  Their (9) is the
recursion of Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} below.
Both identities are rational in \(b\) since the normalized singular-vector
coefficients solve a finite linear system over \(\mathbb Q(b)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Distinct weights and no shifted self-poles away from
rational \(b^2\)]
\label{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
For positive labels \((r,s)\) and \((u,v)\), \(h_{r,s}(b)+rs-h_{u,v}(b)\)
is not the zero rational function of \(b\), nor is
\(h_{r,s}(b)-h_{u,v}(b)\) when \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\).  If
\(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), then
neither function vanishes at \(b_\star\), and every
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular and nonzero at \(b_\star\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Kac formula gives \(h_{r,s}(b)+rs=h_{r,-s}(b)\), which equals
\(h_{u,v}(b)\) exactly when \((rb-sb^{-1})^2=(ub+vb^{-1})^2\), that is,
\(rb-sb^{-1}=\pm(ub+vb^{-1})\), since \(\mathbb C(b)\) is an integral
domain.  Equivalently
\((r\mp u)b^2=s\pm v\).  As an identity in \(b\) this contradicts
\(r,s,u,v>0\), and at a point it forces \(b^2=(s\pm v)/(r\mp u)\in\mathbb Q\);
here \(r\mp u\neq0\), because \(r=u\) would force \(s+v=0\) and
\(r+u>0\).  Similarly
\(h_{r,s}=h_{u,v}\) means \((r\mp u)b^2=\pm v-s\); as an identity this
forces \((r,s)=(u,v)\), and at a point with \((r,s)\neq(u,v)\) it forces
\(b^2\in\mathbb Q\).  Each factor \(ab+b'b^{-1}\) of
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is finite at \(b_\star\neq0\), and it vanishes only
if \(a\neq0\) and \(b_\star^2=-b'/a\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic eta-reduced recursion]
\label{prop:generic-recursion}
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For all \(b\) outside a finite set of algebraic values,
\[
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\substack{a=(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\ \ell_a=rs\leq N}}
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)}{\Delta-h_{r,s}(b)}\,
 H_{N-\ell_a}\bigl(c(b),h_{r,s}(b)+rs,d\bigr)
\]
as an identity of rational functions of \(\Delta\), polynomial in
\(d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exclude \(b=0\).  For all labels of level at most \(N\), exclude also the
zeros and poles in \(\mathbb Q(b)\) of the nonzero differences \(h_a-h_g\)
and \(z_a-h_g\), where \(z_a=h_a+\ell_a\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}), of the nonzero
coefficients of \({\mathcal R}_a\) and \({\mathcal A}_a\), and of the
nonzero normalized singular-vector coefficients.  These are finitely many
algebraic values.  For the remaining \(b\) the weights \(h_{r,s}(b)\) with
\(rs\leq N\) are pairwise distinct, and no shifted weight
\(z_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(b)+rs\) is a degenerate weight of level at most \(N\).

Fix such a \(b\), write \(c=c(b)\), and take \(a=(r,s)\) with \(rs\leq N\).
By distinctness, the Kac determinant has valuation \(p(N-rs)\) at \(\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)\).
The level-\(N\) image of the singular embedding \(j_{\chi_{r,s}(b)}\) is a
\(p(N-rs)\)-dimensional subspace of the specialized Gram kernel by
injectivity~\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:smith} the nullity is at most the valuation, so the kernel
is that image and every positive local Smith exponent equals \(1\).
With \(k=1\), Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient} gives the inverse Gram residue
\(\iota(B^1)^{-1}\iota^*\) on this kernel, and by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}(iii) and~\ref{lem:singular-crossing},
\(B^1=\beta_{\chi}\,G_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s})\) in the basis
\(L_{-Z}\chi_{r,s}(b)\), where
\(\beta_\chi^{-1}={\mathcal A}_{r,s}(b)\) by
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization}.  Taking the trace of the residue of \(G_N^{-1}\) against \(\rho_N\) and
factoring the pulled-back torus form by Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-factorization}
and \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} gives
\(\Res_{\Delta=h_{r,s}(b)}F_N(c,\Delta,d)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b,d)\,F_{N-rs}(c,z_{r,s},d)\).
The difference between \(F_N\) and the sum of these principal parts has no
finite pole and tends to \(p(N)\) as \(\Delta\to\infty\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:large-weight}), so
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=p(N)+\sum_{\ell_a\leq N}({\mathcal R}_a(b,d)/(\Delta-h_a(b)))F_{N-\ell_a}(c,z_a,d)\).
Convolving with \(\eta_j\) gives the stated recursion because
\(\sum_j\eta_jp(N-j)=\delta_{N,0}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing at \(d=0\) and the first \(d\)-derivative]
\label{lem:d-derivatives}
For \(N\geq1\), \(H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)\) vanishes at \(d=0\), and, as
rational functions of \((b,\Delta)\),
\[
 \partial_dH_N\big|_{d=0}
 =-\sum_{rs=N}\frac{rb^2+s}{2(b^2+1)(\Delta-h_{r,s}(b))}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x,y)=xb+yb^{-1}\) and \(B=[1-r,r]\times[1-s,s]\).  Since
\(\Lambda^2=\varphi(1,1)^2-4d\), at \(d=0\) the factors of
\({\mathcal P}_{r,s}\) in Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} are
\(\frac14(\varphi(1,1)^2-\varphi(k,\pm l)^2)
=\frac14\varphi(1-k,1\mp l)\varphi(1+k,1\pm l)\), and over the odd \(k,l\) and
both signs these products run once over \(\varphi(2a,2b')\),
\((a,b')\in B\).  The factor with \((k,\pm l)=(1,1)\) equals \(-d\).  Hence
every \({\mathcal R}_{r,s}\) vanishes at \(d=0\), and comparison with
\({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\), whose product runs over
\(B\setminus\{(0,0),(r,s)\}\), gives
\(\partial_d{\mathcal R}_{r,s}|_{d=0}=-\varphi(r,s)/(2\varphi(1,1))\), the
displayed coefficient.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} and
induction on \(N\), \(H_N\) vanishes at \(d=0\) for \(N\geq1\), and
differentiating the recursion there leaves only the labels with \(rs=N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Regular normalized singular vectors]
\label{lem:regular-everywhere}
For every positive label \((r,s)\) and every \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\),
the normalized singular vector \(\chi_{r,s}(b)\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} is regular at \(b_\star\),
and its value is a singular vector of level \(rs\) in
\(M(c_\star,h_{r,s}(b_\star))\) with \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient \(1\).
Hence \eqref{eq:fusion-normalization} holds at \(b_\star\), and so does
\eqref{eq:crossing-normalization} when \({\mathcal A}_{r,s}\) is regular
there.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e\geq0\) be minimal such that \((b-b_\star)^e\chi_{r,s}(b)\) is
regular at \(b_\star\), with value \(w\neq0\).  The conditions
\(L_1v=L_2v=0\) are linear with coefficients Laurent-polynomial in \(b\), and
\(L_1,L_2\) generate the positive modes, so \(w\) is singular in
\(M(c_\star,h_{r,s}(b_\star))\).  The \(L_{-1}^{rs}\)-coefficient of \(w\) is
\((b-b_\star)^e\) evaluated at \(b_\star\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree} it
cannot vanish; hence \(e=0\).  The rational identities of
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} then specialize as stated.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Sharpness of the Jantzen bound}\label{sec:sharpness}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, the pole order of \(H_N\) at an interior
weight equals \(j_N(\lambda)\) as soon as the right side
of~\eqref{eq:top-layer} is a nonzero polynomial in \(d\).  On a level where
only one layer vector contributes, this follows from the nonvanishing of the
shifted block (Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}).  Where both
contribute, the two terms could cancel.  Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}
rules this out by comparing their \(\ell\)-adic valuations at the two external
weights \(d=h_{1,\pm3}(b_\star)\), where the generic recursion keeps only the
labels \((u,1)\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} supplies the
leading behaviour of the torus norms at \(d=0\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing} the integrality and the dependence on
the level that the comparison needs.

\begin{proposition}[Layer coefficients at \(d=0\)]
\label{prop:layer-coefficients}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, let \(m^\pm_k\) be the
Coulomb momentum of \(\lambda+\ell^\pm_k\) (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}), \(\varrho=-n_0/(2(p'-p))\), and
\(c_k=(-1)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-1}{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\).  Then
\(\tau^\pm_k/\gamma^\pm_k=c_k(\pm\varrho)^kd^k+O(d^{k+1})\); in particular
\(\tau^\pm_k\) vanishes to order exactly \(k\) at \(d=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\nu=b^2\), \(\nu_\star=-p'/p\), \(N_0=2pp'\); dots denote \(d/d\nu\) at
\(\nu_\star\).  As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, write
\(\varphi(x,y)=xb+yb^{-1}\), which vanishes at \(\nu_\star\) iff
\((x,y)\in\mathbb Z(p,p')\), and put \(B_g=[1-u,u]\times[1-v,v]\) for a label
\(g=(u,v)\).

\emph{Step 1: the multipliers along a blow-up.}  By the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, the factors of \({\mathcal P}_g(b,d)\) vanishing
at \((\nu,d)=(\nu_\star,0)\) are \(-d\) and the factors whose restrictions to
\(d=0\) are \(\frac14\varphi(2jp,2jp')\varphi(2-2jp,2-2jp')\) with \(j\neq0\)
and \((jp,jp')\in B_g\).  At \(d=0\) their \(\nu\)-derivatives are
\(j\kappa_0\), where \(\kappa_0=p(p'-p)/p'\).  The pole of \({\mathcal A}_g\)
has order at most their number minus one, with equality when
\(\varrho_g:=\partial_d{\mathcal R}_g|_{d=0}=-(p'u-pv)/(2(p'-p))\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}) is nonzero.  So along the curve
\[
 (\nu,\Delta,d)=(\nu_\star+\delta,\ \lambda+\delta\omega,\ \delta y)
 \qquad(\delta\to0)
\]
with \(\omega\) and \(y\) fixed,
\[
 \frac{{\mathcal R}_g}{\delta}\to\varrho_g\,y\prod_j\Bigl(1-\frac{y}{j\kappa_0}\Bigr).
\]

\emph{Step 2: the limit along the curve.}  Expand \(H_N\) by
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} into chains \(g_1,\dots,g_n\) with
denominators \(z_{i-1}-h_{g_i}\), where \(z_0=\Delta\) and
\(z_i=h_{g_i}+\ell_{g_i}\).  For generic \(\omega,y\), such a denominator is of
order \(\delta\) if \(h_{g_i}(\nu_\star)=z_{i-1}(\nu_\star)\), and a unit
otherwise; consecutive slopes differ, as Step~4 shows.  Only the chains whose
denominators are all of order \(\delta\) contribute to the limit, and \(H_N\)
tends to
\[
 T_N(\omega,y)=\sum_{\text{colliding chains}}\ \prod_i
 \frac{\varrho_{g_i}\,y\prod_j(1-y/(j\kappa_0))}{\zeta_{i-1}-\dot h_{g_i}},
 \qquad \zeta_0=\omega,\quad\zeta_i=\dot h_{g_i}.
\]

\emph{Step 3: reduction to a limit in \(\omega\).}  The denominator of
\(H_N\) is a product of powers of \(\Delta-h_g\), whose leading forms are
units or \(\Delta-\lambda-\dot h_g(\nu-\nu_\star)\).  So
\(T_N(\omega,y)=\Phi(1,\omega,y)\) for the lowest homogeneous part \(\Phi\) of
\(H_N\) in \((\nu-\nu_\star,\Delta-\lambda,d)\), which has degree \(0\) and is
regular at \((0,1,\cdot)\).  Hence \([y^k]T_N=O(\omega^{-k})\) as
\(\omega\to\infty\), and
\(H_N(c_\star,\lambda+\varepsilon,\varepsilon y)\to\Phi(0,1,y)\) as
\(\varepsilon\to0\).  With Theorems~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith}
and~\ref{thm:top-layer} and Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives} this gives
\[
 [d^k]\frac{\tau^\pm_k}{\gamma^\pm_k}
 =\lim_{\omega\to\infty}\omega^k[y^k]T_{\ell^\pm_k}.
\]

\emph{Step 4: the colliding chains.}  By integrality of \(u,v\), the colliding
chains from \(\lambda\) are the sequences \(n_0=x_0,x_1,\dots\) with
\(|x_i|\) increasing and \(x_i\) alternately in \(n_1+N_0\mathbb Z\) and
\(n_0+N_0\mathbb Z\), via
\[
 g_i=\Bigl(\frac{\epsilon_i(x_{i-1}+x_i)}{2p'},\
          \frac{\epsilon_i(x_i-x_{i-1})}{2p}\Bigr),
 \qquad \epsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}x_i.
\]
Then
\[
 \varrho_{g_i}=-\frac{\epsilon_ix_{i-1}}{2(p'-p)},\qquad
 4p'^2\dot h_{g_i}=p'^2-p^2-x_{i-1}x_i,
\]
so \(4p'^2(\dot h_{g_{i-1}}-\dot h_{g_i})=x_{i-1}(x_i-x_{i-2})\neq0\), and
\(|x_i|\) is the momentum reached.  The endpoints \(\pm m^\pm_k\) are
\(n_1+jN_0\) and \(n_1-(j+1)N_0\) for \(k=2j+1\), and \(n_0\pm jN_0\) for
\(k=2j\).  The first steps of the chains ending there are \(x_1=n_1+iN_0\)
with \(-j\leq i\leq j\), \(-j-1\leq i\leq j-1\) and \(-j\leq i\leq j-1\) in
these three cases; these are \(k\) distinct slopes.

\emph{Step 5: the moments.}  As
\([y^k]T_N=\sum_\theta C_\theta/(\omega-\dot h_\theta)\) is
\(O(\omega^{-k})\), its moments of order \(\leq k-2\) vanish, and for each
\(\kappa\)
\[
 \lim_{\omega\to\infty}\omega^k[y^k]T_N
 =C_\kappa\prod_{\theta\neq\kappa}\frac{n_0(x_\theta-x_\kappa)}{4p'^2}.
\]
For \(k=2j+1\) take \(\kappa\) the endpoint, \((r+jp,s+jp')\) or
\(((j+1)p-r,(j+1)p'-s)\), whose box meets \(\mathbb Z(p,p')\) for
\(|j'|\leq j\) (as \(0<r<p\), \(0<s<p'\)).  Then
\(C_\kappa=\pm\varrho(-1)^j/(j!^2\kappa_0^{2j})\), the product is
\((n_0N_0/4p'^2)^{2j}(2j)!\), and \(n_0N_0/(4p'^2\kappa_0)=-\varrho\).  For
\(k=2j\) take \(\kappa=n_1+(j-1)N_0\) and then the step \(g\) to the endpoint,
\((r+(2j-1)p,p'-s)\) or \((p-r,s+(2j-1)p')\), whose box meets
\(\mathbb Z(p,p')\) only at \(0\).  Then
\(\varrho_g/(\dot h_\kappa-\dot h_g)=-2p'^2/((p'-p)jN_0)\), and the product is
\(-(n_0N_0/4p'^2)^{2j-1}(2j-1)!\).  Both cases give \(c_k(\pm\varrho)^k\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integrality, valuation, and level polynomiality at \(d=h_{1,\pm3}(b)\)]
\label{prop:h13-nonvanishing}
Put \(t=b^2\), \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b)=-1-2/t\), \(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b)=2-2/t\),
\(P_0^{(\nu)}=1\), and for \(M\geq1\) and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\),
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y):=M!\,t^{2M}\prod_{u=1}^M(2\Delta-2h_{u,1}(b))\,
H_M(c(b),\Delta,d_\nu)|_{\Delta=Y/t^2}\).
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\topsep}{3pt}
\item[(i)] For \(M\geq1\) and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\),
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\),
\(P_M^{(2)}(0,Y)=P_M^{(1)}(0,Y)\), and \(P_M^{(\nu)}(0,0)=4^M\).
\item[(ii)] For every \(i,j\geq0\) and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\),
\(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M):=[t^iY^j](P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)/4^M)\) is a polynomial in
\(M\) of degree at most \(2i+2j\), valid for all integers \(M\geq0\).
\item[(iii)] Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime, \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and let
\(\ell\mid p'\) be an odd prime with \(a=v_\ell(p')\geq1\).  If
\(M\geq1\) and \(\mu=(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4pp')\) (\(m\in\mathbb Z\)) satisfy
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\), then \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\) (\(\nu=1,2\))
is regular and nonzero with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h13-valuation}
 v_\ell\bigl(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\bigr)
 =-v_\ell(M!)-Ma-\sum_{u=1}^M v_\ell\bigl(m^2-(p'u-p)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, for every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\) (using the symmetry
\(p\leftrightarrow p'\) when \(p'\) has no odd prime factor), every \(M\geq1\)
and every integer momentum \(m\) with
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\), one has \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\) in
\(\mathbb Q[d]\).
\item[(iv)] In the setting of~(iii), if \(t_0\in\ell^a\mathbb Z_\ell\),
\(Y_1,Y_2\in\ell^{a+1}\mathbb Z_\ell\), and integers \(M_1,M_2\geq1\),
\(e\geq0\) satisfy \(v_\ell(M_1-M_2)\geq e\) and \(v_\ell(Y_1-Y_2)\geq e+a\),
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-screening-lip}
 v_\ell\!\left(
 \frac{P_{M_1}^{(1)}(t_0,Y_1)\,P_{M_2}^{(2)}(t_0,Y_2)}
      {P_{M_1}^{(2)}(t_0,Y_1)\,P_{M_2}^{(1)}(t_0,Y_2)}-1\right)
 \geq e+a.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Parts (i) and (ii) are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:h13}.

(iii) Specialize to \(t_\star=-p'/p\) and \(\Delta=\mu\).  By the Kac
determinant formula, \(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) gives
\(\det G_{M'}(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) (\(M'\leq M\)) and
\(\mu\neq h_{u,1}(b_\star)\) (\(1\leq u\leq M\)), so
\(H_M(c(b),\Delta,d_\nu)\) is regular at \((b_\star,\mu)\).  Both \(t_\star\)
and \(Y_\star=\mu t_\star^2=p'(m^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4p^3)\) lie in
\(\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\), so~(i) gives
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t_\star,Y_\star)\equiv4^M\not\equiv0\pmod{\ell\mathbb Z_\ell}\),
whence \(v_\ell(P_M^{(\nu)}(t_\star,Y_\star))=0\) and
\(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d_\nu)\neq0\); since
\(2\mu-2h_{u,1}(b_\star)=(m^2-(p'u-p)^2)/(2pp')\), taking \(v_\ell\) gives
\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}.  Every coprime pair \(p,p'\geq2\) has an odd prime
factor, so \(b\mapsto b^{-1}\) gives \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\) in
general.

(iv) Fix \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\).  By~(i)
and~(ii), each \(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M)\) has degree \(D\leq2i+2j\) and lies in
\(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]\) for all \(M\geq0\), so
\(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M)=\sum_{u=0}^D\beta_u\binom{M}{u}\)
(\(\beta_u\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]\)).  For \(D=0\),
\(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_1)=c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_2)\).  For \(i+j\geq1\) and
\(1\leq u\leq D\), \((M_1-M_2)\mid u!(\binom{M_1}{u}-\binom{M_2}{u})\), so
\(v_\ell(\binom{M_1}{u}-\binom{M_2}{u})\geq e-v_\ell(D!)\); since \(\ell\geq3\),
Legendre's formula gives \(v_\ell(D!)\leq(D-1)/(\ell-1)\leq(D-1)/2\), hence
\(v_\ell(D!)\leq i+j-1\).  Since \(v_\ell(t_0)\geq a\) and
\(v_\ell(Y_1)\geq a+1\), each term with \(i+j\geq1\) in
\(P_{M_1}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_1}-P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_2}
=\sum_{i+j\geq1}(c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_1)-c_{i,j}^{(\nu)}(M_2))t_0^iY_1^j\)
has valuation at least \(e-(i+j-1)+ia+j(a+1)=e+(i+j)(a-1)+j+1\geq e+a\).
As \(P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y)/4^{M_2}\in\mathbb Z_\ell[Y]\),
\(v_\ell(P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_2}-P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_2)/4^{M_2})
\geq v_\ell(Y_1-Y_2)\geq e+a\).  Combining gives
\(v_\ell(P_{M_1}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_1)/4^{M_1}-P_{M_2}^{(\nu)}(t_0,Y_2)/4^{M_2})\geq e+a\)
(\(\nu=1,2\)), and since all four values are \(\ell\)-adic units,
\eqref{eq:second-screening-lip} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Nonvanishing on all two-vector levels]
\label{prop:two-term-criteria}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, let
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\), and let \(P_N(d)\) denote the
right side of \eqref{eq:top-layer}.  Then \(P_N(d)\not\equiv0\) in
\(\mathbb Q[d]\), and \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) has a pole of order exactly
\(k\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \(b\mapsto b^{-1}\) (swapping \(p\leftrightarrow p'\),
\(r\leftrightarrow s\)), assume an odd prime \(\ell\mid p'\) with
\(a=v_\ell(p')\geq1\).  Write \(M^\pm=N-\ell^\pm_k\geq1\) and
\[
 d_x=\frac{x^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'},\qquad x_0=|p'-p|,\quad x_1=|p'-3p|,\quad
 x_2=p'+3p,
\]
so that \(d_{x_0}=0\), \(d_{x_1}=d_1=h_{1,3}(b_\star)\) and
\(d_{x_2}=d_2=h_{1,-3}(b_\star)\).  Normalize each \(w^\pm_k\) to
\(L_{-1}^{\ell^\pm_k}\)-coefficient \(1\), and write
\(\tau^\pm_k(d)=\prod_x(d-d_x)^{o^\pm_k(x)}\), so that
\(o^\pm_k(x)=\ord_{d=d_x}\tau^\pm_k\).  By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere} and~\ref{lem:singular-factorization} and
\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}, at \(b=b_\star\),
\[
 \tau^+_{2j+1}={\mathcal P}_{r+jp,\,s+jp'},\qquad
 \tau^-_{2j+1}={\mathcal P}_{(j+1)p-r,\,(j+1)p'-s},
\]
and for \(k=2j\),
\[
 \tau^+_{2j}={\mathcal P}_{r+(j-1)p,\,s+(j-1)p'}\,{\mathcal P}_{(2j-1)p+r,\,p'-s},\qquad
 \tau^-_{2j}={\mathcal P}_{r+(j-1)p,\,s+(j-1)p'}\,{\mathcal P}_{p-r,\,(2j-1)p'+s},
\]
where, by Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue},
\[
 {\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d)=\prod_{a',l}(d-d_{p'a'-pl})(d-d_{p'a'+pl})
 \qquad(\text{odd }1\leq a'<2R,\ \text{odd }1\leq l<2S).
\]
Moreover \(M^+-M^-=\ell^-_k-\ell^+_k\) equals \(k(pp'-n_1)>0\) for odd \(k\)
and \(-kn_0\neq0\) for even \(k\).  Put \(e_\pm:=o^\pm_k(x_1)\), the
multiplicity of \(d_1\) in \(\tau^\pm_k\).

\emph{Case 1: \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\).}  Solve \(|p'a'\pm pl|=|p'-3p|\) in odd
positive integers \((a',l)\).  The plus sign gives \(p'(a'+1)=p(3-l)\), which
forces \(l=1\) and \(p'\mid2\), impossible as \(\ell\mid p'\), or
\(p'(a'-1)=-p(l+3)\), which is impossible.  The minus sign gives
\(p'(a'-1)=p(l-3)\) or \(p'(a'+1)=p(l+3)\), with solutions
\((1+2ip,3+2ip')\) (\(i\geq0\)) and \((2ip-1,2ip'-3)\) (\(i\geq1\)).  Thus the
multiplicity of \(d_1\) in \({\mathcal P}_{R,S}\) is
\[
 \#\{i\geq0:ip\leq R-1,\ ip'\leq S-2\}+\#\{i\geq1:ip\leq R,\ ip'\leq S+1\}.
\]
Evaluating on \(\tau^\pm_k\) gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
  & e_+ & e_-\\\hline
  k=2j+1 & 2j+\mathbf 1_{s\geq2} & 2j+\mathbf 1_{s\leq p'-2}\\
  k=2j & 2j-1+\mathbf 1_{s\leq p'-2} & 2j-\mathbf 1_{s=1}
 \end{array}
\]
Hence \(e_+\neq e_-\) if and only if \(s\in\{1,p'-1\}\), and \(e_+=e_-=k\)
otherwise.  When \(e_+\neq e_-\), extracting
\([(d-d_1)^{\min(e_+,e_-)}]P_N(d)\) removes the branch of higher order and
leaves a nonzero multiple of \(H_{M^{\min}}(c_\star,\mu^{\min}_k,d_1)\), which
is nonzero by Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iii).

\emph{Case 2: \(2\leq s\leq p'-2\) and \(s\not\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell\).}  Now
\(e_+=e_-=k\).  By Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients},
\(\gamma^\pm_k=[d^k]\tau^\pm_k/(c_k(\pm\varrho)^k)\).  We have
\begin{align*}
 [(d-d_1)^k]\tau^\pm_k&=(4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}\prod_{x\neq x_1}(x_1^2-x^2)^{o^\pm_k(x)},\\
 [d^k]\tau^\pm_k&=(4pp')^{k-2\ell^\pm_k}\prod_{x\neq x_0}(x_0^2-x^2)^{o^\pm_k(x)}.
\end{align*}
Combined with~\eqref{eq:h13-valuation}, this shows that the two summands of
\([(d-d_1)^k]P_N(d)\) have \(\ell\)-adic valuations differing by
\(V_\ell^+(N)-V_\ell^-(N)\), where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_\ell^\pm(N):={}&\sum_{x\neq x_1}o^\pm_k(x)\,v_\ell(x_1^2-x^2)
 -\sum_{x\neq x_0}o^\pm_k(x)\,v_\ell(x_0^2-x^2)\\
 &-v_\ell(M^\pm!)-M^\pm a-\sum_{u=1}^{M^\pm}v_\ell\bigl((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'u-p)^2\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
We show that the first two sums give the same total for both signs.  In
every factor \({\mathcal P}_{R,S}\) of \(\tau^\pm_k\), one has
\(S\equiv\pm s\not\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell\).

If \(\ell\geq5\), the odd indices \(1\leq l<2S\) with
\(l\equiv\pm3\pmod\ell\) pair bijectively with those with
\(l\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell\) via \((2i\ell+3\epsilon,\,2i\ell+\epsilon)\), with
\(\epsilon=+1\), \(i\geq0\) and with \(\epsilon=-1\), \(i\geq1\).  For
\(\beta\in\{1,3\}\), \(\sigma\in\{\pm1\}\) and odd \(a'\), put
\(A=p'-\beta p\) and \(B=p'a'+\sigma p(2i\ell+\beta\epsilon)\).  Then
\[
 A^2-B^2=(A+\epsilon\sigma B)(A-\epsilon\sigma B),\qquad
 A+\epsilon\sigma B=p'(1+\epsilon\sigma a')+2\epsilon i\ell p,
\]
where \(A+\epsilon\sigma B\) does not depend on \(\beta\), and
\(A-\epsilon\sigma B\equiv-2\beta p\not\equiv0\pmod\ell\) (as \(\ell\mid p'\)).
So the paired factors either both vanish or have the same \(\ell\)-adic
valuation.

If \(\ell=3\), then \(3\mid s\) and \(3\mid S\), so the odd indices
\(1\leq l<2S\) partition into triples \(\{6i+1,6i+3,6i+5\}\)
(\(0\leq i<S/3\)).  Fix an odd \(a'\) and a triple.  At \(l=6i+3\) one has
\(v_3(x_0^2-x^2)=0\), while \((x_1^2-(p'a'+pl)^2)(x_1^2-(p'a'-pl)^2)\) has the
\(3\)-divisible factors
\[
 p'(1+a')+6ip,\quad p'(1-a')-6(i+1)p,\quad p'(1+a')-6(i+1)p,\quad p'(1-a')+6ip.
\]
At \(l=6i+1\) and \(l=6i+5\) one has \(v_3(x_1^2-x^2)=0\), and the
\(3\)-divisible factors of \((x_0^2-(p'a'+pl)^2)(x_0^2-(p'a'-pl)^2)\) are the
first and fourth of these (at \(6i+1\)) and the second and third (at
\(6i+5\)).  Whenever one of them vanishes (at \(x=x_1\)), the \(x_1\)-sum skips
both it and its partner \(2(p'-3p)\), whereas the \(x_0\)-sum skips the
matching zero, whose partner \(\pm2(p'-p)\) is a \(3\)-adic unit, and keeps
\(2(p'-3p)\).  Thus the first two sums in \(V_3^\pm(N)\) total
\(-e_\pm v_3(p'-3p)=-kv_3(p'-3p)\), independent of the sign.

In both cases these sums cancel in \(V_\ell^+(N)-V_\ell^-(N)\).  Since
\(m^\pm_k\equiv\pm ps\pmod{p'}\) gives
\((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'u-p)^2\equiv p^2(s^2-1)\not\equiv0\pmod\ell\), the last sum
also vanishes, leaving
\[
 V_\ell^+(N)-V_\ell^-(N)=-\bigl(v_\ell(M^+!)-v_\ell(M^-!)\bigr)-(M^+-M^-)a\neq0,
\]
so \([(d-d_1)^k]P_N(d)\neq0\).

\emph{Case 3: \(2\leq s\leq p'-2\) and \(s\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell\).}  Solving
\(|p'a'\pm pl|=p'+3p\) in odd positive \((a',l)\) yields \((1,3)\),
\((1+2ip,2ip'-3)\) and \((2ip-1,2ip'+3)\) (\(i\geq1\)); counting on
\(\tau^\pm_k\) as in Case~1 gives
\(\ord_{d=d_2}\tau^+_k=\ord_{d=d_2}\tau^-_k=k\).  Put
\(x_1(\varepsilon)=p'-3p+\varepsilon\) and
\(x_2(\varepsilon)=p'+3p-\varepsilon\).  As \(a'\) runs over odd
\(1\leq a'<2R\), \(A\in\{1-a',1+a'\}\) runs once over
\(\{0,\pm2,\ldots,\pm2(R-1),2R\}\), so
\[
 \frac{{\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_1(\varepsilon)})}
      {{\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_2(\varepsilon)})}
 =\prod_{A}\prod_{\text{odd }1\leq l<2S}
 \frac{(p'A-p(3+l)+\varepsilon)(p'A-p(3-l)+\varepsilon)}
      {(p'A+p(3+l)-\varepsilon)(p'A+p(3-l)-\varepsilon)}.
\]
Sending \(A\mapsto-A\) in the denominator cancels every
\(A\in\{0,\pm2,\ldots,\pm2(R-1)\}\) (with \((-1)^{2S}=+1\)), leaving
\(A=2R\).  For each factor \({\mathcal P}_{R,S}\) of \(\tau^\pm_k\) that is not
common to both signs, \(p\nmid R\), so no factor \(2Rp'\pm2ip\) vanishes at
\(\varepsilon=0\).  The values \(3\pm l\) form \(\{4-2S,6-2S,\dots,2S+2\}\);
cancelling \(\{4-2S,\dots,2S-4\}\) with its negation leaves
\(\{2S-2,2S,2S+2\}\), so
\[
 \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \frac{{\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_1(\varepsilon)})}
      {{\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d_{x_2(\varepsilon)})}
 =\prod_{\epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}}\frac{Rp'-Sp-\epsilon p}{Rp'+Sp+\epsilon p}.
\]
Since
\[
 d_{x_1(\varepsilon)}-d_1=\tfrac{p'-3p}{2pp'}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 d_{x_2(\varepsilon)}-d_2=-\tfrac{p'+3p}{2pp'}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)
\]
(with \(p'\neq3p\) as \(\gcd(p,p')=1\) and \(p\geq2\)), and
\(\ord_{d_1}\tau^\pm_k=\ord_{d_2}\tau^\pm_k=k\), the prefactors
\((-(p'-3p)/(p'+3p))^k\) cancel in the ratio of the two branches.  For both
parities of \(k\), the factors \((R^\pm,S^\pm)\) not common to both signs
satisfy \(R^+p'-S^+p=-(R^-p'-S^-p)\notin\{0,\pm p\}\) (since \(p\nmid r\),
hence \(p\nmid(R^+p'-S^+p)\)) and \(R^\pm p'+S^\pm p=m^\pm_k\).  So the
numerator triple has ratio \((-1)^3=-1\), and
\[
 T_k:=\frac{[(d-d_1)^k]\tau^+_k\big/[(d-d_1)^k]\tau^-_k}
          {[(d-d_2)^k]\tau^+_k\big/[(d-d_2)^k]\tau^-_k}
 =-\frac{m^-_k\bigl((m^-_k)^2-p^2\bigr)}{m^+_k\bigl((m^+_k)^2-p^2\bigr)}.
\]
Using \(m^+_k+m^-_k=2kpp'\) and factoring \((m^+_k)^3+(m^-_k)^3\) gives
\[
 T_k-1=\frac{-2kpp'\bigl(3(m^+_k)^2-p^2-6kpp'm^+_k+4k^2p^2p'^2\bigr)}
            {m^+_k\bigl((m^+_k)^2-p^2\bigr)}.
\]
Because \(m^+_k\equiv\pm ps\equiv\pm p\pmod\ell\) and \(\ell\nmid2p\), both
\(m^+_k\) and the parenthesized factor (\(\equiv2p^2\pmod\ell\)) are
\(\ell\)-adic units, while \(v_\ell((m^+_k)^2-p^2)\geq1\).  Hence either
\(v_\ell(T_k)\neq0\) or
\[
 v_\ell(T_k-1)=v_\ell(k)+a-v_\ell\bigl((m^+_k)^2-p^2\bigr)\leq v_\ell(k)+a-1.
\]
Put \(t_0=-p'/p\) and \(Y^\pm=p'((m^\pm_k)^2-(p'-p)^2)/(4p^3)\).  Then
\(v_\ell(t_0)=a\), \(v_\ell(Y^\pm)\geq a+1\) (as
\((m^\pm_k)^2\equiv p^2\pmod\ell\)), \(v_\ell(M^+-M^-)\geq v_\ell(k)\), and
\(Y^+-Y^-=p'(2kpp')(m^+_k-m^-_k)/(4p^3)\) has
\(v_\ell(Y^+-Y^-)\geq v_\ell(k)+2a\).  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iii),
\(H_{M^\pm}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_k,d_\nu)\) is regular and nonzero for \(\nu=1,2\).
Put
\[
 H_{\mathrm{rat}}:=\frac{H_{M^+}(d_1)/H_{M^+}(d_2)}{H_{M^-}(d_1)/H_{M^-}(d_2)};
\]
the common denominators \(\prod_u(2\mu^\pm_k-2h_{u,1})\) cancel in this ratio.
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iii) gives
\(v_\ell(H_{\mathrm{rat}})=0\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(iv) with \(e=v_\ell(k)\) gives
\(v_\ell(H_{\mathrm{rat}}-1)\geq v_\ell(k)+a\).  Therefore
\(T_kH_{\mathrm{rat}}\neq1\), so \([(d-d_1)^k]P_N(d)\) and
\([(d-d_2)^k]P_N(d)\) cannot both vanish, proving \(P_N\not\equiv0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp pole order for \(H_N\)]
\label{cor:top-layer-sharp}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer}, for every coprime pair
\(p,p'\geq2\), every interior Kac weight \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\), and
every level \(N\geq1\), the coefficient \(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\), viewed as
a rational function of \(\Delta\) over \(\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\) with \(d\)
indeterminate, has pole order exactly \(j_N(\lambda)\) at \(\Delta=\lambda\),
where order zero means regularity.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(k=j_N(\lambda)\).  If \(k=0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} gives
regularity.  Suppose \(k\geq1\), so \(\ell_k\leq N<\ell_{k+1}\).  On a single-vector level
\(\ell_k\leq N<\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), only the sign with
\(\ell^\pm_k=\ell_k\) contributes to \eqref{eq:top-layer}, giving
\((\tau/\gamma)H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\) with \(M=N-\ell_k\),
\(\mu=\lambda+\ell_k\), and \(\tau\neq0\).  For \(M=0\) one has \(H_0=1\);
for \(M\geq1\), Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu)\neq0\) and
Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing} gives \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)\not\equiv0\).
At \(N=\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)\), \eqref{eq:top-layer} reads
\((\tau/\gamma)H_M(c_\star,\mu,d)+\tau'/\gamma'\) with \(M\geq1\); by
Lemma~\ref{lem:d-derivatives}, \(H_M(c_\star,\mu,0)=0\), so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} the first term vanishes at \(d=0\) to
order at least \(k+1\) and the second to order exactly \(k\), whence the sum is
nonzero.  Every two-vector level
\(\max(\ell^+_k,\ell^-_k)<N<\ell_{k+1}\) is settled by
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria}.
\end{proof}

\section{All-order confluent recursion}\label{sec:all-order}

\begin{definition}[Shifted Laurent recursion]\label{def:shifted-laurent}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\), put \(c_\star=c(b_\star)\) and
\(t=b-b_\star\), and work in \(K_\star((t))\) with
\(K_\star=\mathbb Q(b_\star,d)\), \(d\) indeterminate.  (The working field
contains \(b_\star\) because \(t=b-b_\star\); the final coefficients lie in
\(\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}.)
For a positive Kac label \(a=(r,s)\), write \(\ell_a=rs\),
\(h_a(t)=h_{r,s}(b_\star+t)\), \(R_a(t)={\mathcal R}_{r,s}(b_\star+t,d)\), and
\(z_a(t)=h_a(t)+\ell_a\).
Define the shifted Laurent blocks recursively by \(S_{a,0}(t)=1\) and, for
\(M\geq1\),
\[
 S_{a,M}(t)
 =\sum_{\substack{g=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\\\ell_g=uv\leq M}}
 \frac{R_g(t)}
 {z_a(t)-h_g(t)}S_{g,M-\ell_g}(t).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole} ensures each denominator is nonzero.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A priori shifted valuation bound]
\label{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}
For positive labels \(a,g\), set \(\pi_g=\max\{0,-\ord_tR_g(t)\}\) and
\(\sigma_{a,g}=\max\{0,\ord_t(z_a(t)-h_g(t))\}\).  Define nonnegative integers
\(W_{a,M}\) recursively by \(W_{a,0}=0\) and, for \(M\geq1\),
\(W_{a,M}=\max(\{0\}\cup\{\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}+W_{g,M-\ell_g}:\ell_g\leq M\})\).
Then \(\ord_t S_{a,M}(t)\geq-W_{a,M}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The claim holds at \(M=0\). By induction the \(g\)-summand has valuation at
least \(-\pi_g-\sigma_{a,g}-W_{g,M-\ell_g}\geq-W_{a,M}\), as does their sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Collision moments]\label{def:collision-moments}
For \(N\geq1\), \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\), and
\(a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\), put
\(B_{a,N}(t)=R_a(t)S_{a,N-\ell_a}(t)\),
\(\delta_a(t)=h_a(t)-\lambda\in tK_\star[[t]]\),
\(\varepsilon_a=\ord_t\delta_a\geq1\) (\(\varepsilon_a=\infty\) if
\(\delta_a\equiv0\)), and \(\widehat v_{a,N}=\pi_a+W_{a,N-\ell_a}\), so that
\(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted-valuation-bound}.  Define the a priori pole bound
\(\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}=\max_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
(1+\lfloor\widehat v_{a,N}/\varepsilon_a\rfloor)\) (with \(1\) when
\(\varepsilon_a=\infty\)) and, for \(k\geq1\), the \emph{collision moments}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-moments}
 \widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)
 =\CT_{t}
 \sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 B_{a,N}(t)\delta_a(t)^{k-1},
\end{equation}
where \(\CT_t\) extracts the coefficient of \(t^0\).  For
\(k>\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) every summand has positive valuation,
since \((k-1)\varepsilon_a>\widehat v_{a,N}\), so
\(\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Laurent precision for the recursion]
\label{lem:precision-budget}
Suppose \(S_{a,M}\) is needed modulo \(t^{U+1}\).  For its \(g\)-summand it
suffices to know
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-propagation}
 \frac{R_g}{z_a-h_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+W_{g,M-\ell_g}},
 \qquad
 S_{g,M-\ell_g}\quad\text{through }t^{\,U+\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}}.
\end{equation}
The level decreases at each request. For the collision moments it suffices to compute
\(R_a\) through \(t^{W_{a,N-\ell_a}}\), \(S_{a,N-\ell_a}\) through \(t^{\pi_a}\),
and \(\delta_a^{\,k-1}\) through \(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For factors of valuations at least \(-\alpha\) and \(-\beta\), the product
through \(t^U\) uses the first factor through \(t^{U+\beta}\) and the second
through \(t^{U+\alpha}\).  With \(\alpha=\pi_g+\sigma_{a,g}\) and
\(\beta=W_{g,M-\ell_g}\) this gives~\eqref{eq:precision-propagation}; applied to
\(B_{a,N}=R_aS_{a,N-\ell_a}\) with \(U=0\), \(\alpha=\pi_a\) and
\(\beta=W_{a,N-\ell_a}\), it gives the stated bounds.  Since
\(\ord_tB_{a,N}\geq-\widehat v_{a,N}\), only terms of \(\delta_a^{k-1}\) through
\(t^{\widehat v_{a,N}}\) can contribute to the constant term.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise pole support of a collision germ]
\label{lem:collision-pole-support}
Let \(B(t)\in K_\star((t))\) and
\(\delta(t)\in tK_\star[[t]]\).  Every coefficient of
\(B(t)/(x-\delta(t))=\sum_{j\geq0}B(t)\delta(t)^jx^{-j-1}\), as a Laurent
series in \(t\), is a finite \(K_\star\)-linear combination of
\(x^{-1},x^{-2},\ldots\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\ord_tB\geq-v\), the term \(B\delta^j\) has valuation at least
\(j-v\), so only \(j\leq m+v\) contribute to the coefficient of \(t^m\).
\end{proof}

We can now state the main result of Part~I.

\begin{theorem}[Finite algebraic confluence at fixed \(b_\star\)]
\label{thm:general-confluence}
Fix \(b_\star\in\mathbb C^\times\) and \(N\geq1\).  For every
\(\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)\) and \(k\geq1\), the
partial-fraction coefficients of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions}
are the collision moments~\eqref{eq:collision-moments}:
\(A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}=\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}\).  Hence
\(m^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) for every
\(\lambda\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-pole-free-recursion}
 H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}
   \sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
   \frac{\widetilde A^{\star}_{\lambda,k,N}(d)}{(\Delta-\lambda)^k}.
\end{equation}
If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), every collision class is a singleton, every
\(\widehat M^{\star}_{\lambda,N}\) equals \(1\), and
\eqref{eq:general-pole-free-recursion} is
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} evaluated at \(b=b_\star\).
\end{theorem}

The computation of the right side is finite: the recursion of
Definition~\ref{def:shifted-laurent} lowers the level at each step, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-budget} gives a truncation order for every Laurent
germ that occurs.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}]
Fix \(N\geq1\).  For generic \(b\), Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}
and induction on \(M\) give \(S_{a,M}(t)=H_M(c(b),z_a(t),d)\) as rational
functions of \(b\), and grouping by limiting weights yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-grouped}
 H_N(c(b),\Delta,d)
 =\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Kac}_N(b_\star)}{\mathcal G}^\star_{\lambda,N},\qquad
 {\mathcal G}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}
 =\sum_{a\in{\mathcal C}^{\star}_{\lambda,N}}
 \frac{B_{a,N}(t)}{\Delta-h_a(t)},
\end{equation}
as rational functions of \((b,\Delta,d)\). Work in
\(K_\star(\Delta)((t))\) and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support}
with \(x=\Delta-\lambda\).

By Cramer's rule the denominator of each \(F_m\), \(m\leq N\), divides
\(\det G_m\). The Kac exponents \(p(m-rs)\) are nondecreasing in \(m\), so
\(\det G_N\) is a common denominator for the left side of
\eqref{eq:generic-grouped}. It is nonzero in \(K_\star(\Delta)\) at
\(t=0\), so the left side is regular there with constant term
\(H_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\). On the right each group has constant term
\(\sum_{k=1}^{\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}}
\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}(d)(\Delta-\lambda)^{-k}\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:collision-pole-support} and Definition~\ref{def:collision-moments}.
By uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm:general-partial-fractions} these are its
principal parts. This gives \(\widetilde A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}
=A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\in\mathbb Q(b_\star^2)[d]\) and
\(m^\star_{\lambda,N}\leq\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}\).

If \(b_\star^2\notin\mathbb Q\), Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}
shows that every class is a singleton, every \(R_g\) is regular at \(t=0\), and
no shifted denominator vanishes there; hence \(W_{a,M}=0\),
\(\widehat M^\star_{\lambda,N}=1\), \(S_{a,M}(0)=H_M(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\),
and the sole moment is \(R_a(0)H_{N-\ell_a}(c_\star,z_a(b_\star),d)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Ising double poles]\label{prop:ising-double-poles}
For \(N<7\) every pole of \(H_N(\tfrac12,\Delta,d)\) in \(\Delta\) is

\(\lim_{\Delta\to\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda)^2H_N(\tfrac12,\Delta,d)=\tau(d)/\gamma\):

\begin{array}{cclr}
\lambda&N&\tau(d)&\gamma\\ \hline
1/2&7&{\mathcal P}_{2,1}{\mathcal P}_{1,5}&-700700\\
1/16&10&{\mathcal P}_{1,2}{\mathcal P}_{4,2}&-1121229484375/192\\
0&11&{\mathcal P}_{1,1}{\mathcal P}_{2,5}&-2477917160468750/6561
\end{array}
\]
Here \({\mathcal P}_{R,S}={\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d)\) is the polynomial of

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(rs\leq6\), the only repeated values of \(|4r-3s|\) are \(1,2,5\), giving
the three classes in the table. Consider a
weight with a single label \(a\) of level \(\ell_a\leq N\).  The injective

puts \(p(N-\ell_a)\) independent vectors in the radical.  This number is the
valuation of the Kac determinant, so all positive Smith exponents are \(1\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:smith}).  At the three interior weights,
Theorem~\ref{thm:jantzen-smith} gives \(j_N(\lambda)\leq1\) below the

Proposition~\ref{prop:two-term-criteria} with \(j=1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:layer-coefficients} with \(c_2=-1\) gives
\(\gamma=-[d^2]\tau/\varrho^2\), where \(\varrho=-n_0/2\).
\end{proof}

The ancillary scripts compare the norms \(\gamma\) at \(N=7\) and \(N=10\)

and, in the deep suite, at \(N=10\) (see the code availability section).

\section{Two-weight Ward identities and braided orbits}\label{sec:setup}

Part~II studies traces over irreducible minimal-model modules.  This section
sets up the two tools it uses: the two-weight form of the torus pairing, and
the braided orbit of an interior weight.  Section~\ref{sec:irr-results} states
the results, and Sections~\ref{sec:descent}--\ref{sec:telescoping} prove them.

Write \(v_\Delta=\lvert\Delta\rangle\) and \(B_\Delta=B_{c,\Delta}\) for the
Shapovalov form on \(M(c,\Delta)\) (Definition~\ref{def:matrices}), whose
Gram matrices are \(G_N(c,\Delta)\).  Its radical
\(J(c,\Delta)=\rad B_\Delta\) is the unique maximal proper submodule; it equals
the first Jantzen layer \(J^1(c,\Delta)\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}.  The
induced form \(\bar B\) on the irreducible quotient
\(L(c,\Delta)=M(c,\Delta)/J(c,\Delta)\) is nondegenerate.  We use \(\mathcal{A}_{r,s}\), \(\mathcal{P}_{r,s}\) and
\(\mathcal{R}_{r,s}=\mathcal{A}_{r,s}\mathcal{P}_{r,s}\) from
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}: \(\mathcal{P}_{r,s}\) is the torus norm of
the normalized singular vector and \(\mathcal{A}_{r,s}^{-1}\) its crossing scalar
(Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations}).

Throughout Part~II, \(t=b^2\), and \(h_{R,S}(t)\) denotes the Kac weight in
this coordinate; this differs from Section~\ref{sec:all-order}, where
\(t=b-b_\star\).  The charge deformation in Section~\ref{sec:telescoping} is
\(b^2=b_\star^2+\tau\).  For \(b\in\mathbb{C}^\times\) we set \(Q=b+b^{-1}\),
\(c(b)=1+6Q^2=13+6t+6t^{-1}\), and we extend
Definition~\ref{def:fixed-specialization} to all \(r,s\in\mathbb{Z}\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kac-weight}
 h_{r,s}(b)
 :=\frac{Q^2-(rb+sb^{-1})^2}{4}
  =\frac{(t+1)^2-(rt+s)^2}{4t},
\end{equation}
so that \(h_{r,-s}(b)=h_{r,s}(b)+rs\).  When \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) with
coprime integers \(p,p'\geq2\), we have \(c_\star=c(b_\star)=1-6(p'-p)^2/(pp')\)
and, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-collisions},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mm-weight}
 h_{r,s}(b_\star)
 =\frac{(p'r-ps)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Two-weight three-point pairings and fusion polynomials}

\begin{lemma}[Two-weight primary Ward identity]\label{lem:two-weight-ward}
Fix \(c,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,d\in\mathbb{C}\).  There is a unique bilinear form
\(\rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}:M(c,\Delta_1)\times M(c,\Delta_2)\to\mathbb{C}\)
such that \(\rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}(v_{\Delta_1},v_{\Delta_2})=1\) and,
for every \(m\in\mathbb{Z}\) and homogeneous vectors
\(u\in M(c,\Delta_1)_{N_1}\), \(v\in M(c,\Delta_2)_{N_2}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-weight-ward}
 \rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}(L_{-m}u,v)
 -\rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}(u,L_mv)
 =\bigl((\Delta_1+N_1)-(\Delta_2+N_2)+md\bigr)\,
  \rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}(u,v).
\end{equation}
Its matrix entries in the PBW bases belong to
\(\mathbb{Q}[c,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,d]\).  It satisfies the symmetry
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-weight-sym}
 \rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}(u,v)=\rho_{\Delta_2,\Delta_1;d}(v,u).
\end{equation}
On the diagonal \(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=\Delta\) at \(d=0\) it coincides with the
Shapovalov form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d0-shapovalov}
 \rho_{\Delta,\Delta;0}(u,v)=B_{c,\Delta}(u,v)
 \qquad\text{for all }u,v\in M(c,\Delta).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The relation \eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} is the \(z=1\) specialization of the
primary Ward identity
\[
 [L_m,V_d(z)]=z^m(z\partial_z+(m+1)d)V_d(z)=z^m([L_0,V_d(z)]+mdV_d(z))
\]
acting on \(\langle u|V_d(z)|v\rangle=z^{\wt(u)-\wt(v)-d}\rho(u,v)\), where \(\wt(u)\)
is the \(L_0\)-weight of \(u\).  Induction
on \(N_1+N_2\) computes \(\rho_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2;d}(L_{-Y}v_{\Delta_1},
L_{-Y'}v_{\Delta_2})\) uniquely.  It moves positive modes from left to right,
using \(m>0\), and then negative modes from right to left, using \(m<0\) and
\(L_{-m}v_{\Delta_1}=0\) for \(m<0\); the results are polynomials in
\(\mathbb{Q}[c,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,d]\).  Existence of this coefficientwise
formal bilinear pairing is standard: it is the normalized three-Verma
three-point block \cite[\S3, p.~4]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010},
equivalently the \(z=1\) evaluation of the unique \(\Vir\)-covariant formal
pairing into \(z^{\Delta_1-\Delta_2-d}\mathbb{C}[z,z^{-1}]\).

Swapping \((\Delta_1,u)\leftrightarrow(\Delta_2,v)\) and replacing \(m\) by
\(-m\) in \eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} negates both sides, so the defining
identity is unchanged.  Since the value on \((v_{\Delta_1},v_{\Delta_2})\) is
\(1\), uniqueness gives \eqref{eq:two-weight-sym}.  When
\(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=\Delta\) and \(d=0\), the Shapovalov form \(B_{c,\Delta}\)
satisfies \(B_{c,\Delta}(v_\Delta,v_\Delta)=1\), \(B_{c,\Delta}(u,v)=0\) for
\(N_1\neq N_2\), and \(B_{c,\Delta}(L_{-m}u,v)=B_{c,\Delta}(u,L_mv)\).  Hence
\eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} holds for \(B_{c,\Delta}\), and uniqueness gives
\eqref{eq:d0-shapovalov}.
\end{proof}

On the diagonal put \(\rho_{\Delta,d}:=\rho_{\Delta,\Delta;d}\); when the
charge matters we also write \(\rho_{c,\Delta,d}\) or \(\rho(c,\Delta,d)\).  This
is the torus form of Definition~\ref{def:matrices}: by Ward uniqueness
(Lemma~\ref{lem:two-weight-ward}) its symmetric PBW matrix is
\(\rho_N(c,\Delta,d)\), so
\(F_N(c,\Delta,d)=\Tr(G_N(c,\Delta)^{-1}\rho_N(c,\Delta,d))\).

\begin{proposition}[Singular vectors and fusion polynomials]\label{prop:fusion-poly}
Fix integers \(R,S\geq1\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] There is a unique family of singular vectors
   \(\chi_{R,S}(b)\in M(c(b),h_{R,S}(b))_{RS}\) whose coefficient on
   \(L_{-1}^{RS}v\) equals \(1\); its PBW coefficients belong to
   \(\mathbb{Q}[b^2,b^{-2}]\), so \(\chi_{R,S}(b_\star)\) is well-defined and
   nonzero at every \(b_\star\in\mathbb{C}^\times\).
 \item[(ii)] For every \(b\in\mathbb{C}^\times\) and
   \(\Delta_1,\Delta_2\in\mathbb{C}\), writing
   \(\Delta_i=(Q^2-\Lambda_i^2)/4\) (\(i=1,2\)),
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:fusion-poly}
    \rho_{h_{R,S}(b),\Delta_1;\Delta_2}\bigl(\chi_{R,S}(b),v_{\Delta_1}\bigr)
    =C_{R,S}(b)\,P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,\Delta_2),
   \end{equation}
   where \(C_{R,S}(b)\in\mathbb{C}^\times\) is independent of
   \((\Delta_1,\Delta_2)\) and
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:P-RS-def}
    P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,\Delta_2)
    :=\prod_{j,l}
      \tfrac14\bigl(\Lambda_1+\Lambda_2+p'b+q'b^{-1}\bigr)
      \bigl(\Lambda_1-\Lambda_2+p'b+q'b^{-1}\bigr),
   \end{equation}
   with \(j\in\{1-R,3-R,\ldots,R-1\}\) and \(l\in\{1-S,3-S,\ldots,S-1\}\).
   In particular, at \(\Delta_1=h_{R,S}(b)\) (where \(\Lambda_1=Rb+Sb^{-1}\)),
   the roots of \(P_{R,S}(b;h_{R,S}(b),d)=0\) in \(d\) are precisely
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:P-RS-roots}
    d=h_{u,v}(b), \qquad
    u\in\{1,3,\ldots,2R-1\},\quad v\in\{1,3,\ldots,2S-1\}.
   \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The fusion polynomial \(P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,\Delta_2)\), a function of two
weights, should not be confused with the torus norm
\({\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b,d)\) of Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, a
polynomial in the external weight alone.  By
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}, the roots of
\({\mathcal P}_{R,S}(b,d)\) in \(d\) are the weights \(h_{u,\pm v}(b)\) with
\(u,v\) as in~\eqref{eq:P-RS-roots}.

\begin{proof}
By \cite[Theorem~3.1]{Astashkevich1997}, the normalized singular vector has
PBW coefficients polynomial in \((c,h)\); see also
\cite[Propositions~5.1--5.2 and Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011},
Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere}.  Substituting the Laurent expressions of
\(c(b)\) and \(h_{R,S}(b)\) in \(t=b^2\) gives Laurent polynomials in \(t\).
Their coefficients are rational: the normalized singular-vector equations
have coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(t)\) and a unique solution, so the PBW
coefficients lie in \(\mathbb Q(t)\cap\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}]=\mathbb Q[t,t^{-1}]\), which is part~(i).
Part~(ii) is Zamolodchikov's fusion polynomial
\cite[\S5, (5.9)--(5.12)]{Zamolodchikov2004}; see also the
fusion-polynomial display preceding (8) in
\cite[\S3]{HadaszJaskolskiSuchanek2010}.  For the
root list \eqref{eq:P-RS-roots}, set \(\Lambda_1=Rb+Sb^{-1}\).  Then
\(\Lambda_2=\pm(\Lambda_1+p'b+q'b^{-1})\) ranges over \(\pm(ub+vb^{-1})\) with
\(u=R+j\in\{1,3,\ldots,2R-1\}\) and \(v=S+l\in\{1,3,\ldots,2S-1\}\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The braided BGG orbit at minimal-model charges}

The embedding diagram of Figure~\ref{fig:braided} is the braided form of the
Bernstein--Gelfand--Gelfand (BGG) resolution; we use the abbreviation BGG for
its nodes and arrows.

Fix coprime \(p,p'\geq2\), \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and an interior Kac weight
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\), labeled as in Section~\ref{sec:smith}, so that
\(n_1=p'r+ps<pp'\) and \(n_0=p'r-ps\neq0\).  Define the sequence of
integers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bgg-momenta}
 \begin{aligned}
  n^+_{2j}&:=2jpp'+n_0, &\qquad n^-_{2j}&:=2jpp'-n_0 \qquad (j\geq0,\;n^\pm_0=\pm n_0),\\
  n^+_{2j+1}&:=2jpp'+n_1, &\qquad n^-_{2j+1}&:=2(j+1)pp'-n_1 \qquad (j\geq0),
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and the associated BGG orbit weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bgg-weights}
 \mu^\pm_k:=\frac{(n^\pm_k)^2-(p'-p)^2}{4pp'}, \qquad
 \mu^\pm_k-\lambda=\frac{(n^\pm_k)^2-n_0^2}{4pp'}=\ell^\pm_k \qquad (k\geq1),
\end{equation}
where \(\ell^\pm_k\) are the layer levels of Section~\ref{sec:smith}.  Put
\(\mu_0:=\lambda\) and \(\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s):=\{\mu_0\}\cup
\{\mu^\pm_k:k\geq1\}\), the \emph{braided orbit} of \(\lambda\); these are the
nodes of Figure~\ref{fig:braided}, and we call that figure the braided BGG
diagram.

\begin{lemma}[Structure and arithmetic of primitive BGG arrows]\label{lem:bgg-arrows}
Let \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) lie at layer \(k\geq0\).  Thus
\(\mu=\mu_0\) if \(k=0\), and \(\mu=\mu^\epsilon_k\) with
\(\epsilon\in\{+,-\}\) if \(k\geq1\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] The Verma module \(M(c_\star,\mu)\) belongs to Feigin--Fuchs
   Class~\(\mathrm{III}_-\): for every \(i\geq1\), with
   \(A_\pm:=M(c_\star,\mu^\pm_{k+i})\) and the Jantzen filtration under
   \(\Delta\mapsto\Delta+x\) at fixed \(c_\star\),
   \begin{gather*}
    J^i(c_\star,\mu)=A_++A_-,\qquad J^{i+1}(c_\star,\mu)=A_+\cap A_-,\\
    J^i(c_\star,\mu)/J^{i+1}(c_\star,\mu)\cong L(c_\star,\mu^+_{k+i})\oplus L(c_\star,\mu^-_{k+i}).
   \end{gather*}
 \item[(ii)] The two primitive singular vectors of \(M(c_\star,\mu)\) generate
   \(M(c_\star,\mu^+_{k+1})\) and \(M(c_\star,\mu^-_{k+1})\).  Each primitive
   arrow \(\mu\to\nu\) (\(\nu\in\{\mu^+_{k+1},\mu^-_{k+1}\}\)) has a unique
   positive Kac label \((R,S)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq1}^2\) with
   \(h_{R,S}(b_\star)=\mu\) and \(h_{R,-S}(b_\star)=\nu\), determined by
   \(p'R+pS=n_\nu\) and \(p'R-pS=\pm n_\mu\) (with \(n_\mu:=n^\epsilon_k\)).
 \item[(iii)] Every primitive BGG arrow \((R,S)\) satisfies:
   \begin{align}
    &R\equiv r\text{ or }p-r\pmod p, \qquad S\equiv s\text{ or }p'-s\pmod{p'},
      \label{eq:RS-mod}\\
    &R\geq\min(r,p-r), \qquad S\geq\min(s,p'-s),
      \label{eq:RS-lower}\\
    &\min(R/p,\;S/p')<1.
      \label{eq:RS-small}
   \end{align}
   In particular, no integer pair \((qp,qp')\) with \(q\neq0\) lies in
   \([1-R,R]\times[1-S,S]\), so the first-layer Jantzen norm
   \(\gamma_{R,S}(b_\star):=\left.\partial_\Delta\right|_{\Delta=\mu}
   B_{c_\star,\Delta}(\chi_{R,S}(b_\star),\chi_{R,S}(b_\star))\) is nonzero.
 \item[(iv)] The two primitive arrows \((R_1,S_1)\) and \((R_2,S_2)\) out of
   \(\mu\) have distinct levels \(R_1S_1\neq R_2S_2\).  Setting
   \(\kappa_i:=p'R_i-pS_i\in\{\pm n_\mu\}\) and \(\theta_i:=p'R_i+pS_i>0\) (\(i=1,2\)),
   we have
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:transverse-slope}
    \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=t_\star}
    \bigl(h_{R_1,S_1}(t)-h_{R_2,S_2}(t)\bigr)
    =\frac{\kappa_2\theta_2-\kappa_1\theta_1}{4p'^2}\neq0,
    \qquad t_\star=-\frac{p'}{p}.
   \end{equation}
   Moreover, \(\max(\ell^+_1,\ell^-_1)<\min(\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part~(i) follows from \cite[Theorem~A(a),(b)(iii),(c) and
Theorem~B(e)]{Astashkevich1997}; see also \cite[Proposition~5.4, Figure~5.4,
and Theorem~6.3]{IoharaKoga2011}.  Since \(n_\mu\equiv\pm n_0\) or
\(\pm n_1\pmod{2pp'}\), \(n_\mu\) is divisible by neither \(p\) nor \(p'\).
Hence the line \(p'x-py=n_\mu\) meets neither axis at an integer point, which
places \(M(c_\star,\mu)\) in Class~\(\mathrm{III}_-\).  The fixed-\(c\) line
\(c=c_\star\) is transverse to every Kac curve through \((c_\star,\mu)\),
because \(dc/dt=6(1-t^{-2})\neq0\) at \(t_\star=-p'/p<0\).
For part~(ii), \(h_{R,S}(b_\star)=\mu\) and \(h_{R,-S}(b_\star)=\nu\) are
equivalent by \eqref{eq:mm-weight} to \(p'R+pS=n_\nu\) and
\(p'R-pS=\sigma n_\mu\) for some \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\), i.e.\
\(2p'R=n_\nu+\sigma n_\mu\) and \(2pS=n_\nu-\sigma n_\mu\).  Because
\(p\nmid n_\mu\) and \(p'\nmid n_\mu\), at most one sign \(\sigma\) can make
\(n_\nu+\sigma n_\mu\) divisible by \(2p'\), giving uniqueness; the
consecutive-layer formulas in \eqref{eq:bgg-momenta} supply existence with
\(R,S\geq1\).

For part~(iii), reducing \(p'R-pS=\sigma n_\mu\equiv\pm(p'r\pm ps)\pmod{2pp'}\)
modulo \(p\) and modulo \(p'\) yields \(p'R\equiv\pm p'r\pmod p\) and
\(-pS\equiv\pm ps\pmod{p'}\).  Since \(\gcd(p,p')=1\) and \(R,S\geq1\), this
gives \eqref{eq:RS-mod} and \eqref{eq:RS-lower}.  Furthermore,
\eqref{eq:bgg-momenta} gives \(0<n_\nu-|n_\mu|<2pp'\) for every consecutive
pair \((\mu,\nu)\): indeed, for \(k=2j\) even,
\(n^+_{2j+1}-n^\pm_{2j}=n_1\mp n_0\in\{2ps,2p'r\}\subset(0,2pp')\) and
\(n^-_{2j+1}-n^\pm_{2j}=2pp'-n_1\mp n_0\in\{2p(p'-s),2p'(p-r)\}\subset(0,2pp')\);
for \(k=2j+1\) odd, the four differences are likewise
\(\{2p(p'-s),2p'(p-r),2ps,2p'r\}\subset(0,2pp')\).  Whichever sign
\(\sigma=\pm1\) satisfies \(\sigma n_\mu=-|n_\mu|\) or \(+|n_\mu|\), one of
\(2p'R=n_\nu-|n_\mu|\) or \(2pS=n_\nu-|n_\mu|\) is strictly less than \(2pp'\),
proving \eqref{eq:RS-small}.  For the normalized singular vector, Proposition~\ref{prop:imported-normalizations}
gives
\(\gamma_{R,S}(b)^{-1}=\mathcal{A}_{R,S}(b)
=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{(a,b')\in[1-R,R]\times[1-S,S]\setminus\{(0,0),(R,S)\}}(ab+b'b^{-1})^{-1}\),
and Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-everywhere} gives its specialization at
\(b_\star\).  By \eqref{eq:RS-small}, no factor has
\((a,b')=(qp,qp')\) with \(q\neq0\).  Hence every factor is nonzero at
\(b_\star\), and \(\gamma_{R,S}(b_\star)\neq0\).

For part~(iv), \(R_1S_1=R_2S_2\) would mean \(n^+_{k+1}=n^-_{k+1}\), which
requires \(n_0=0\) (for \(k+1\) even) or \(n_1=pp'\) (for \(k+1\) odd), both
impossible since \(0<r<p\), \(0<s<p'\), and \(\gcd(p,p')=1\).  Differentiating
\(h_{R,S}(t)=\frac{1-R^2}{4}t+\frac{1-RS}{2}+\frac{1-S^2}{4t}\) gives
\(h'_{R,S}(t)=\frac{1-R^2}{4}-\frac{1-S^2}{4t^2}\), so at \(t_\star=-p'/p\),
\[
 4p'^2\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=t_\star}
 \bigl(h_{R_1,S_1}(t)-h_{R_2,S_2}(t)\bigr)
 =p'^2(R_2^2-R_1^2)-p^2(S_2^2-S_1^2)
 =\kappa_2\theta_2-\kappa_1\theta_1.
\]
Here \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2\in\{\pm n_\mu\}\) with \(n_\mu\neq0\), and
\(\theta_1,\theta_2\) are distinct positive integers.  If
\(\kappa_1=\kappa_2\), then
\(\kappa_2\theta_2-\kappa_1\theta_1=\pm n_\mu(\theta_2-\theta_1)\neq0\).  If
\(\kappa_1=-\kappa_2\), then
\(\kappa_2\theta_2-\kappa_1\theta_1=\pm n_\mu(\theta_2+\theta_1)\neq0\), which
gives~\eqref{eq:transverse-slope}.  For the first two layers,
\(\ell^+_1=rs<\min(p'r,ps)<pp'-|p'r-ps|=\min(\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)\), and
similarly \(\ell^-_1=(p-r)(p'-s)<\min(p'(p-r),p(p'-s))<\min(\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)\).
\end{proof}

\section{Irreducible minimal-model traces}\label{sec:irr-results}

In a Virasoro minimal model \(\mathcal{M}(p,p')\) with coprime integers
\(p,p'\geq2\) and central charge \(c_\star=1-6(p'-p)^2/(pp')\), the physical
highest-weight representation at an interior Kac weight
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) (\(0<r<p\), \(0<s<p'\), \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\)) is the
irreducible quotient \(L(c_\star,\lambda)=M(c_\star,\lambda)/J(c_\star,\lambda)\)
of Section~\ref{sec:setup}.  When the external operator is the identity (\(d=0\)),
the torus trace is the minimal-model character, expressed by Rocha--Caridi
\cite{RochaCaridi1985} as an alternating sum of Verma characters over the
braided BGG diagram of \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) (Figure~\ref{fig:braided}).  For a
primary insertion \(V_d(1)\) whose pairing descends to \(L(c_\star,\lambda)\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:descent-regularity}), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:irr-trace-def}
 \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\lambda,d}(q)
 =\Tr_{L(c_\star,\lambda)}\bigl(q^{L_0-\lambda}V_d(1)\bigr)
 =\sum_{N=0}^\infty T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N(c_\star,\lambda,d)\,q^N.
\end{equation}
We compare it with the Verma series
\(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c,\Delta,d}(q)=\sum_{N\geq0}F_N(c,\Delta,d)q^N\)
of Definition~\ref{def:eta-reduced}, and set \(p(n)=0\) and
\(F_n=T^{\mathrm{irr}}_n=0\) for \(n<0\).

The Verma coefficients at \(\lambda\) differ from the trace coefficients
even when they are regular there.  At the Ising point \((p,p')=(3,4)\) with
\(\lambda=h_{1,2}=1/16\) and \(d=d_\star=h_{1,3}=1/2\), the coefficients
\(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d_\star)\) are regular at \(\Delta=\lambda\), and at
level \(2\) one finds \(F_2=8/7\) and \(T^{\mathrm{irr}}_2=-1\), a difference
of \(15/7\).  Approaching \(\lambda\) along a ray does not remove the
difference: in a direct computation of the limit of \(F_N\) along the
fixed-\(c_\star\) ray \((\Delta,d)=(\lambda+x,d_\star+\alpha x)\), the slope
\(\alpha=5\) gives \(T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N\) at \(N=2,3\), while \(N=4\) needs
\(\alpha=17/19\).  The difference comes from the first Jantzen layer
\(J^1/J^2\), on which the torus and Shapovalov forms both vanish at \(x=0\).
Their first-order terms give the ratio \(\sigma^\pm_1/\gamma^\pm_1\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:jantzen-vertex}) in the layer
decomposition~\eqref{eq:jantzen-trace-decomp}, which accounts for the
difference.

The results are as follows.  Theorem~\ref{thm:descent-regularity} decides when
the torus form descends to the irreducible module and when the Verma
coefficients are regular; it is proved in Section~\ref{sec:descent}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio} computes the first-layer ratio at generic
charge (Section~\ref{sec:cocycle}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-rocha-caridi}, the
generalized Rocha--Caridi identity, is proved in
Section~\ref{sec:telescoping}: each quotient
\(M(c_\star,\mu)/M(c_\star,\nu_1)\) is deformed along
\(b^2=b_\star^2+\tau\), which gives a two-term trace identity, and these
identities telescope along the braided diagram.

\begin{theorem}[Fusion descent and Verma regularity]\label{thm:descent-regularity}
Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime, \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\), and
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) with \(0<r<p\) and \(0<s<p'\).  Let
\(\mathrm{Box}_{r,s}\) be the set of \((k,m)\in\mathbb Z^2\) with
\(0\leq k<\min(r,p-r)\) and \(0\leq m<\min(s,p'-s)\), and define the
minimal-model fusion set and its reflected enlargement
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)
 &:=\bigl\{h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star):(k,m)\in\mathrm{Box}_{r,s}\bigr\},
   \label{eq:fusion-set}\\
 \mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)
 &:=\bigl\{h_{2k+1,\pm(2m+1)}(b_\star):(k,m)\in\mathrm{Box}_{r,s}\bigr\}.
   \label{eq:fusion-set-pm}
\end{align}
The map \((k,m)\mapsto h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star)\) is injective on
\(\mathrm{Box}_{r,s}\).  For each \(d\in\mathbb{C}\):
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] the torus form
   \(\rho(c_\star,\lambda,d):M(c_\star,\lambda)\times M(c_\star,\lambda)\to\mathbb{C}\)
   descends to a well-defined bilinear form on
   \(L(c_\star,\lambda)\times L(c_\star,\lambda)\) if and only if
   \(d\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\);
 \item[(ii)] the Verma torus one-point block coefficient
   \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) is regular (pole order zero) at \(\Delta=\lambda\)
   for every \(N\geq0\) if and only if \(d\in\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(iii)] if \(d\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\), then \(V_d(1)\) descends to
   a primary operator on \(L(c_\star,\lambda)\), and
   \(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\lambda,d}(q)\) is well defined;
 \item[(iv)] if \(d=h_{2k+1,\epsilon(2m+1)}(b_\star)\in\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\)
   (\(\epsilon\in\{+,-\}\)), then for every \(N\geq0\) the coefficient
   \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) is regular at every weight \(\mu\) of the braided
   orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) of \(\lambda\)
   (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), and
   \(F_N(c(b),\Delta,h_{2k+1,\epsilon(2m+1)}(b))\) is jointly regular in
   \((b,\Delta)\) at \((b_\star,\mu)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Generic first-layer Jantzen vertex ratio]\label{thm:first-layer-ratio}
Let \(b\in\mathbb{C}^\times\) be generic, put \(t=b^2\), fix integers
\(R,S\geq1\), and let \(0\leq k\leq R-1\) and \(0\leq m\leq S-1\) with
\(d=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b)\).  Let \(\chi_{R,S}(b)\in M(c(b),h_{R,S}(b))_{RS}\) be
the nonzero singular vector.  In~\eqref{eq:box-formula} below, the
\(\Delta\)-derivatives are taken with the PBW coefficients of \(\chi_{R,S}(b)\)
held fixed, so that \(\chi_{R,S}(b)\) is regarded as a vector of
\(M(c(b),\Delta)\) for \(\Delta\) near \(h_{R,S}(b)\), as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-crossing}.  Let
\(\mathrm{Rect}_{R,S}=\{(a,b')\in\mathbb Z^2:1-R\leq a\leq R,\ 1-S\leq b'\leq S\}\).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:box-formula}
 \begin{aligned}
 c_{R,S}(t;2k+1,2m+1)
 &:=\frac{\left.\partial_\Delta\right|_{\Delta=h_{R,S}(b)}
         \rho_{\Delta,d}(\chi_{R,S},\chi_{R,S})}
        {\left.\partial_\Delta\right|_{\Delta=h_{R,S}(b)}
         B_\Delta(\chi_{R,S},\chi_{R,S})}\\
 &=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{(a,b')\in\mathrm{Rect}_{R,S}\setminus\{(-k,-m)\}}
        \bigl((a+k)t+(b'+m)\bigr)}
       {\displaystyle\prod_{(a,b')\in\mathrm{Rect}_{R,S}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}
        (at+b')}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Moreover, setting \(\varpi_{R,S}=(Rb+Sb^{-1})/2\) and
\(\varpi_{R,-S}=(Rb-Sb^{-1})/2\), this rational function factorizes as
\(c_{R,S}(t;2k+1,2m+1)=\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_{R,-S};b)/\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_{R,S};b)\),
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-potential}
 \begin{aligned}
  \Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi;b)
  :=\prod_{j=1}^m&\frac{1}{\Gamma\bigl(1+\tfrac{2\varpi}{b}+\tfrac{j}{b^2}\bigr)
                           \Gamma\bigl(1-\tfrac{2\varpi}{b}+\tfrac{j}{b^2}\bigr)}\\
   \times\prod_{i=1}^k&\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+2\varpi b+ib^2+m)\,\Gamma(1-2\varpi b+ib^2+m)}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Let \(\{\mu^\pm_j\}_{j\geq1}\) be the BGG orbit weights of
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) at levels \(\ell^\pm_j=\mu^\pm_j-\lambda\)
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}).  By Proposition~\ref{prop:bgg-cocycle}, the
ratios~\eqref{eq:box-formula} specialize at \(b_\star\) to nonzero rationals,
and their product along a BGG path from \(\lambda\) to \(\mu^\pm_j\) does not
depend on the path.  We denote this product by
\(\Omega_{2k+1,2m+1}(\lambda\to\mu^\pm_j)\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\) and call it the
\emph{cocycle value}.

\begin{theorem}[Generalized Rocha--Caridi identity and confluence for traces]\label{thm:gen-rocha-caridi}
Let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) (\(p,p'\geq2\) coprime), let
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) be an interior Kac weight, and let
\(d=h_{u,v}(b_\star)\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) with
\((u,v)=(2k+1,2m+1)\).  Then as formal power series in \(q\) (with finite sums
at every coefficient \(q^N\)),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-rocha-caridi}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\lambda,d}(q)
 =\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\lambda,d}(q)
  +\sum_{j=1}^\infty(-1)^j\Bigl(
    &\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu^+_j)\,q^{\ell^+_j}\,
    \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\mu^+_j,d}(q)\\
   +&\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu^-_j)\,q^{\ell^-_j}\,
    \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\mu^-_j,d}(q)\Bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(a)] At \(d=0\) (\(k=m=0\)), \(\Omega_{1,1}\equiv1\) and
   \(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\mu,0}(q)=\phi(q)^{-1}\),
   recovering the Rocha--Caridi character formula \cite{RochaCaridi1985}.
 \item[(b)] For every \(N\geq0\), \eqref{eq:gen-rocha-caridi} and
   fixed-\(b_\star\) Verma confluence (Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence})
   compute \(T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N(c_\star,\lambda,d)\in\mathbb{Q}\) by a finite
   algebraic calculation from the generic recursion
   (Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}).
 \item[(c)] For every layer \(j\geq1\), the \(j\)-th Jantzen vertex ratio on
   \(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) satisfies
   \[
    \sigma^\pm_j(\lambda)/\gamma^\pm_j(\lambda)
    =\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu^\pm_j),
   \]
   with \(\sigma^\pm_j\) and \(\gamma^\pm_j\) as in
   Proposition~\ref{prop:jantzen-vertex}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

We call a fusion weight \(d=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star)\) \emph{single-index} if
\(k=0\) or \(m=0\), and \emph{two-index} otherwise.  The single-index weights
include every fusion weight when \(p\leq3\) (Lee--Yang, Ising, \((2,7)\),
\((3,5)\)) and \(d=3/5,3/2\) in Tricritical Ising.  For
\(d(b)=h_{1,2m+1}(b)\), the multiplier \(\mathcal{R}_{R,S}(b,d(b))\) vanishes
identically when \(S>m\) (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-holomorphicity}), so
the generic recursion of Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} for
\(H_N(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\) keeps only the labels with \(S\leq m\); we call it
the \emph{truncated recursion}, and similarly with \(R\leq k\) for
\(d(b)=h_{2k+1,1}(b)\).

\begin{corollary}[Collision-free single-index recursion]\label{cor:collision-free}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-rocha-caridi}, suppose \(d=h_{1,2m+1}(b_\star)\) with
\(0\leq m<\min(s,p'-s)\) (or dually \(d=h_{2k+1,1}(b_\star)\) with
\(0\leq k<\min(r,p-r)\)).  Then in the truncated recursion for
\(H_N(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\), every recursion multiplier \(\mathcal{R}_{R,S}(b,d(b))\) is regular at
\(b=b_\star\), no two active poles \(h_{R_1,S_1}(b_\star)\) and
\(h_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\) collide, and no shifted weight
\(h_{R_1,-S_1}(b_\star)\) hits any active pole \(h_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\).  Thus
each \(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\mu^\pm_j,d}(q)\) in
\eqref{eq:gen-rocha-caridi} can be evaluated at \(b=b_\star\) by the
unmodified truncated recursion, without expanding in \(b-b_\star\).
\end{corollary}

\section{Fusion descent, the Jantzen vertex filtration, and regularity}\label{sec:descent}

\begin{lemma}[Intersection of primitive fusion and residue roots]\label{lem:fusion-injective}
Let \(p,p'\geq2\) be coprime and \(0<r<p\), \(0<s<p'\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] The common roots \(d\in\mathbb{C}\) of
   \(P_{r,s}(b_\star;h_{r,s}(b_\star),d)=0\) and
   \(P_{p-r,p'-s}(b_\star;h_{r,s}(b_\star),d)=0\) are precisely the elements of
   \(\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) in \eqref{eq:fusion-set}.
 \item[(ii)] The map \((k,m)\mapsto h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star)\) is injective on
   \(0\leq k<\min(r,p-r)\), \(0\leq m<\min(s,p'-s)\).
 \item[(iii)] The common roots \(d\in\mathbb{C}\) of
   \(\mathcal{P}_{r,s}(b_\star,d)=0\) and \(\mathcal{P}_{p-r,p'-s}(b_\star,d)=0\) are
   precisely the elements of \(\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\) in
   \eqref{eq:fusion-set-pm}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:P-RS-roots}, a common root \(d\) in~(i) has representations
\(d=h_{u_1,v_1}(b_\star)=h_{u_2,v_2}(b_\star)\) with odd integers
\(1\leq u_1\leq 2r-1\), \(1\leq v_1\leq 2s-1\) and
\(1\leq u_2\leq 2(p-r)-1\), \(1\leq v_2\leq 2(p'-s)-1\).  By
\eqref{eq:mm-weight}, \(p'u_1-pv_1=\pm(p'u_2-pv_2)\).
If the sign is \(+\), then \(p'(u_1-u_2)=p(v_1-v_2)\); since \(\gcd(p,p')=1\)
and \(u_1-u_2,v_1-v_2\) are even, \(2p\mid(u_1-u_2)\).  Because
\(|u_1-u_2|\leq\max(2r-2,2(p-r)-2)<2p\), we get \(u_1=u_2\) and \(v_1=v_2\),
so \((u_1,v_1)=(2k+1,2m+1)\) lies in the intersection box
\eqref{eq:fusion-set}.
If the sign is \(-\), then \(p'(u_1+u_2)=p(v_1+v_2)\).  Since \(u_1+u_2\) and
\(v_1+v_2\) are positive even integers and \(\gcd(p,p')=1\), this forces
\(u_1+u_2\geq 2p\), which contradicts
\(u_1+u_2\leq (2r-1)+(2(p-r)-1)=2p-2\), proving~(i).
For~(ii), if two pairs \((u_1,v_1),(u_2,v_2)\) in the intersection box have the
same weight, the same argument applies since
\(u_1+u_2\leq 4\min(r,p-r)-2\leq 2p-2<2p\) and \(|u_1-u_2|<p\).

For~(iii), by Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} the roots of
\(\mathcal{P}_{R,S}(b_\star,d)=0\) are \(d=h_{u,\pm v}(b_\star)\) with
\(u\in\{1,3,\ldots,2R-1\}\), \(v\in\{1,3,\ldots,2S-1\}\).  A common root of
\(\mathcal{P}_{r,s}\) and \(\mathcal{P}_{p-r,p'-s}\) therefore has
\(p'u_1-\epsilon_1pv_1=\pm(p'u_2-\epsilon_2pv_2)\) with
\(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{+1,-1\}\).  If \(\epsilon_1=\epsilon_2=+1\),
part~(i) gives \(d\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).  If
\(\epsilon_1=\epsilon_2=-1\), the minus sign is impossible because both sides
are positive.  The plus sign gives \(p'(u_1-u_2)=p(v_2-v_1)\), which forces
\(u_1=u_2\) and \(v_1=v_2\) as above, so \(d\in\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\).
If \(\epsilon_1=-\epsilon_2\) (say \(\epsilon_1=+1,\epsilon_2=-1\)), then
either \(p'(u_1-u_2)=p(v_1+v_2)\) (requiring \(2p'\mid(v_1+v_2)\), impossible
since \(0<v_1+v_2\leq 2p'-2<2p'\)) or \(p'(u_1+u_2)=p(v_1-v_2)\) (requiring
\(2p\mid(u_1+u_2)\), impossible since \(0<u_1+u_2\leq 2p-2<2p\)).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ward induction]\label{lem:ward-induction}
Let \(\psi\) be a symmetric bilinear form on \(M(c_\star,\nu)\) satisfying
the \(V_d(1)\) Ward identity~\eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} with
\(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=\nu\), and let \(w\in M(c_\star,\nu)_\ell\) be a singular
vector with \(\psi(w,v_\nu)=0\).  Then \(U(\Vir_-)w\subset\rad\psi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(n>0\) and homogeneous \(u\), \(L_nw=0\) and
\eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} give \(\psi(w,L_{-n}u)=-(\ell-|u|-nd)\psi(w,u)\).
Induction on \(|u|\) yields \(\psi(w,M(c_\star,\nu))=0\).  A second induction,
on PBW length, using
\[
 \psi(L_{-n}a,u)=\psi(a,L_nu)+(|a|-|u|+nd)\,\psi(a,u),
\]
gives \(\psi(U(\Vir_-)w,M(c_\star,\nu))=0\).  Symmetry yields the other slot.
\end{proof}

Fix a fusion weight \(d\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) and an orbit weight
\(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) at layer \(k_0\geq0\).  Identify \(M(c_\star,\mu+x)_N[[x]]\) with
\(\mathbb{C}[[x]]^{p(N)}\) via the PBW basis.  For \(i\geq0\), define the
\(x\)-adic Jantzen lattice
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x-adic-jantzen}
 \mathcal{J}^{(i)}_N
 :=\bigl\{\,a(x)\in M(c_\star,\mu+x)_N[[x]]
   : B_x(a(x),v)\in x^i\mathbb{C}[[x]]\ \text{for all}\ v\bigr\},
\end{equation}
with \(v\) ranging over \(M(c_\star,\mu+x)_N[[x]]\),
where \(B_x:=B_{c_\star,\mu+x}\) and \(\rho_x:=\rho_{c_\star,\mu+x,d}\), and
set \(\mathcal{J}^{(i)}=\bigoplus_{N\geq0}\mathcal{J}^{(i)}_N\).  Since
\(B_x(L_ma(x),v)=B_x(a(x),L_{-m}v)\), each \(\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\) is a
\(\Vir\)-submodule of \(M(c_\star,\mu+x)[[x]]\).  Evaluating at \(x=0\) maps
\(\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\) onto \(J^i(c_\star,\mu)\): Definition~\ref{def:jantzen-layers}
uses polynomial families, and truncating a power-series family modulo \(x^i\)
gives one, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:jantzen-smith}.  From
\eqref{eq:x-adic-jantzen},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x-shift-lattice}
 \mathcal{J}^{(i)}\cap x\,M(c_\star,\mu+x)[[x]] = x\,\mathcal{J}^{(i-1)}
 \qquad (i\geq1).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Jantzen vertex filtration and all-order pole cancellation]\label{prop:jantzen-vertex}
Let \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) (at layer \(k_0\geq0\)) and let
\(d=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star)\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] For every \(i\geq0\),
   \(\rho_x(\mathcal{J}^{(i)},\mathcal{J}^{(i)})\subset x^i\mathbb{C}[[x]]\),
   and the \(i\)-th Jantzen vertex form
   \(\rho^{(i)}(a(0),b(0)):=[x^i]\rho_x(a(x),b(x))\) is well-defined on
   \(J^i(c_\star,\mu)\times J^i(c_\star,\mu)\), satisfies the \(V_d(1)\)
   Ward identity \eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} (with \(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=\mu\)),
   and satisfies \(J^{i+1}(c_\star,\mu)\subset\rad\rho^{(i)}\).
 \item[(ii)] For every \(N\geq0\), \(F_N(c_\star,\mu+x,d)\) is holomorphic at
   \(x=0\), and its value at \(x=0\)
   decomposes over the Jantzen layers as
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:jantzen-trace-decomp}
    F_N(c_\star,\mu,d)
    =T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N(c_\star,\mu,d)
     +\sum_{i=1}^{j_N(\mu)}\sum_{\pm}
       \frac{\sigma^\pm_i(\mu)}{\gamma^\pm_i(\mu)}\,
       T^{\mathrm{irr}}_{N-(\mu^\pm_{k_0+i}-\mu)}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_{k_0+i},d),
   \end{equation}
   where \(\gamma^\pm_i(\mu)=B^i(w^\pm_{k_0+i},w^\pm_{k_0+i})\neq0\), with
   \(B^i\) the Jantzen form of Lemma~\ref{lem:top-coefficient}, and
   \(\sigma^\pm_i(\mu)=\rho^{(i)}(w^\pm_{k_0+i},w^\pm_{k_0+i})\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We prove~(i) by induction on \(i\geq0\).  For \(i=0\),
\(\rho^{(0)}=\rho_{c_\star,\mu,d}\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(i)--(iii),
\(J^1(c_\star,\mu)=U(\Vir_-)w^+_{k_0+1}+U(\Vir_-)w^-_{k_0+1}\), where each
primitive singular vector has Kac label \((R,S)\) with
\(R\geq\min(r,p-r)>k\) and \(S\geq\min(s,p'-s)>m\).  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(ii),
\(P_{R,S}(b_\star;\mu,h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star))=0\), so
\(\rho^{(0)}(w^\pm_{k_0+1},v_\mu)=0\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:ward-induction} gives
\(J^1(c_\star,\mu)\subset\rad\rho^{(0)}\).

Now assume~(i) holds for \(i-1\geq0\).  If \(a(x),b(x)\in\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\),
then \(a(x),b(x)\in\mathcal{J}^{(i-1)}\) and
\(a(0),b(0)\in J^i\subset\rad\rho^{(i-1)}\), so
\[
 [x^{i-1}]\rho_x(a(x),b(x))=\rho^{(i-1)}(a(0),b(0))=0,
\]
whence \(\rho_x(a(x),b(x))\in x^i\mathbb{C}[[x]]\).  Let
\(a_1(x),a_2(x)\in\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\) with \(a_1(0)=a_2(0)\).  Then
\(a_1(x)-a_2(x)=xw(x)\) with \(w(x)\in\mathcal{J}^{(i-1)}\),
by
\eqref{eq:x-shift-lattice}.  Since \(b(0)\in J^i\subset\rad\rho^{(i-1)}\),
\([x^{i-1}]\rho_x(w(x),b(x))=\rho^{(i-1)}(w(0),b(0))=0\), and so
\(\rho_x(xw(x),b(x))\in x^{i+1}\mathbb{C}[[x]]\).  By symmetry of \(\rho_x\)
the same holds in the second slot, so \(\rho^{(i)}\) is independent of the
lifts in \(\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\).  For homogeneous
\(a(x),b(x)\in\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\) of levels \(N_a,N_b\), both \(L_{-n}a(x)\)
and \(L_nb(x)\) lie in the \(\Vir\)-submodule \(\mathcal{J}^{(i)}\), and in
\eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} the weight difference
\((\mu+x+N_a)-(\mu+x+N_b)=N_a-N_b\) is independent of \(x\); taking \([x^i]\)
gives the \(V_d(1)\) Ward identity for \(\rho^{(i)}\) on \(J^i\).

For \(i\geq1\) we show \(J^{i+1}\subset\rad\rho^{(i)}\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(i), \(J^i=M(\mu^+_{k_0+i})+M(\mu^-_{k_0+i})\), and the
next layer is the intersection of these two Verma submodules,
\(J^{i+1}=M(\mu^+_{k_0+i})\cap M(\mu^-_{k_0+i})
=U(\Vir_-)w^+_{k_0+i+1}+U(\Vir_-)w^-_{k_0+i+1}\), generated by the two
primitive singular vectors of layer \(k_0+i+1\).  On each Verma submodule \(M(\mu^\epsilon_{k_0+i})\subset J^i\)
(\(\epsilon\in\{+,-\}\)), the Ward identity and
Lemma~\ref{lem:two-weight-ward} force the restriction of \(\rho^{(i)}\) to
\(M(\mu^\epsilon_{k_0+i})\times M(\mu^\epsilon_{k_0+i})\) to equal
\(\sigma^\epsilon_i(\mu)\,\rho_{c_\star,\mu^\epsilon_{k_0+i},d}\).  Each
\(w^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1}\) (\(\epsilon'\in\{+,-\}\)) is a primitive singular vector in
\emph{both} \(M(\mu^+_{k_0+i})\) and \(M(\mu^-_{k_0+i})\).  Its Kac label
\((R,S)\) satisfies \(R>k\) and \(S>m\) by \eqref{eq:RS-lower}, so, as for
\(i=0\), Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(ii) gives
\(\rho^{(i)}(w^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1},w^+_{k_0+i})=0\), viewing
\(w^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1}\in M(\mu^+_{k_0+i})\), and
\(\rho^{(i)}(w^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1},w^-_{k_0+i})=0\), viewing
\(w^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1}\in M(\mu^-_{k_0+i})\).  Since
\(L_nw^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1}=0\) for all \(n>0\), the first induction in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ward-induction}, applied to the PBW descendants of
\(w^+_{k_0+i}\) and \(w^-_{k_0+i}\), gives
\(\rho^{(i)}(w^{\epsilon'}_{k_0+i+1},J^i)=0\).  The second induction there gives
\(\rho^{(i)}(J^{i+1},J^i)=0\), the inductive step for~(i).

For~(ii), fix \(N\geq0\).  Since \(2\) is a unit in the discrete valuation
ring \(\mathbb{C}[[x]]\), the nondegenerate symmetric matrix
\(G_N(c_\star,\mu+x)\) is congruent over
\(\mathrm{GL}_{p(N)}(\mathbb{C}[[x]])\) to a diagonal matrix.  Let
\(U(x)\in\mathrm{GL}_{p(N)}(\mathbb{C}[[x]])\) be such a congruence, with
columns \(v_1(x),\ldots,v_{p(N)}(x)\).  Then
\(B_x(v_a(x),v_b(x))=\delta_{ab}x^{e_a}u_a(x)\), where \(e_a\geq0\) are the
local Smith exponents and \(u_a(0)\neq0\).  Thus
\(v_a(x)\in\mathcal{J}^{(e_a)}_N\), and for each \(i\geq0\) the vectors
\(\{v_a(0):e_a=i\}\) project to a basis of \(J^i_N/J^{i+1}_N\) diagonalizing
the nondegenerate \(i\)-th Jantzen form \(\bar B^i_N\) with diagonal
entries \(u_a(0)\).  Computing the trace in this basis yields
\[
 F_N(c_\star,\mu+x,d)
 =\Tr\bigl((U^{\mathsf{T}}G_NU)^{-1}(U^{\mathsf{T}}\rho_NU)\bigr)
 =\sum_{a=1}^{p(N)}\frac{\rho_x(v_a(x),v_a(x))}{x^{e_a}u_a(x)}.
\]
By part~(i), \(\rho_x(v_a(x),v_a(x))=x^{e_a}\rho^{(e_a)}(v_a(0),v_a(0))+O(x^{e_a+1})\).
Every factor \(x^{e_a}\) cancels, so \(F_N(c_\star,\mu+x,d)\) is regular at
\(x=0\) with value
\[
 F_N(c_\star,\mu,d)
 =\sum_{i=0}^{j_N(\mu)}\Tr_{J^i_N/J^{i+1}_N}
   \bigl((\bar B^i_N)^{-1}\bar\rho^{(i)}_N\bigr).
\]
For \(i=0\), \(J^0/J^1=L(c_\star,\mu)\) gives \(T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N(c_\star,\mu,d)\).
For \(i\geq1\), \(J^i/J^{i+1}\cong L(c_\star,\mu^+_{k_0+i})\oplus
L(c_\star,\mu^-_{k_0+i})\) is a direct sum of non-isomorphic irreducibles.
They are orthogonal under the contravariant form \(\bar B^i\), so
\((\bar B^i_N)^{-1}\) is block-diagonal, and the off-diagonal blocks of
\(\bar\rho^{(i)}_N\) drop out of the trace.  On each summand
\(L(c_\star,\mu^\pm_{k_0+i})\), uniqueness of the contravariant and primary
forms gives \(\bar B^i=\gamma^\pm_i(\mu)\bar B_{L(\mu^\pm_{k_0+i})}\) and
\(\bar\rho^{(i)}=\sigma^\pm_i(\mu)\bar\rho_{L(\mu^\pm_{k_0+i}),d}\), with
\(\gamma^\pm_i(\mu)\neq0\) because \(\bar B^i\) is nondegenerate.  This
yields \eqref{eq:jantzen-trace-decomp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Joint regularity in \((b,\Delta)\)]\label{lem:joint-holomorphicity}
Let \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) be an interior Kac weight, let
\(d_\star=h_{2k+1,\epsilon(2m+1)}(b_\star)\in\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\)
(\(\epsilon\in\{+,-\}\)), and set \(d(b):=h_{2k+1,\epsilon(2m+1)}(b)\).  Then
for every \(N\geq0\) and every \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\), the rational
function \(F_N(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\in\mathbb{Q}(b,\Delta)\) is regular at
\((b_\star,\mu)\in\mathbb{C}^2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in the Laurent-polynomial ring \(\mathbb{Q}[b,b^{-1},\Delta]\).  By the
Kac determinant formula,
\[
 \det G_N(c(b),\Delta)
 =C_N\prod_{RS\leq N}\bigl(\Delta-h_{R,S}(b)\bigr)^{p(N-RS)}
\]
with \(C_N\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\) independent of \((b,\Delta)\), so
\(\det G_N(c(b),\Delta)\) has unit \(\Delta\)-leading coefficient and no
vertical prime factor in \(\mathbb{Q}[b,b^{-1}]\).  Since the entries of
\(G_N(c(b),\Delta)\) and \(\rho_N(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\) lie in
\(\mathbb{Q}[b,b^{-1},\Delta]\), the trace \(F_N(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\) lies in
the localization of \(\mathbb{Q}[b,b^{-1},\Delta]\) at the Kac factors
\(\Delta-h_{R,S}(b)\) (\(RS\leq N\)).

At generic \(b\), the generic recursion
(Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion}) writes \(H_M(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\) as a
sum of simple poles at \(\Delta=h_{R,S}(b)\) (\(RS\leq M\)).  The residue at
\(h_{R,S}(b)\) is
\[
 \mathcal{R}_{R,S}(b,d(b))\,H_{M-RS}\bigl(c(b),h_{R,S}(b)+RS,d(b)\bigr),
\]
with the recursion multiplier
\(\mathcal{R}_{R,S}=\mathcal{A}_{R,S}\mathcal{P}_{R,S}\)
(Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue}).  The shifted coefficient
\(H_{M-RS}(c(b),h_{R,S}(b)+RS,d(b))\) is finite for generic \(b\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-generic-shifted-pole}.  The explicit product for
\(\mathcal{P}_{R,S}(b,d)\) vanishes at \(d=h_{u,\pm v}(b)\) for all odd
\(1\leq u\leq 2R-1\) and \(1\leq v\leq 2S-1\).  Hence, whenever \(R\geq k+1\)
and \(S\geq m+1\), the multiplier \(\mathcal{R}_{R,S}(b,d(b))\) vanishes
identically in \(b\), and so does the residue.  Over \(\mathbb{Q}(b)\), every
inactive linear Kac factor \(\Delta-h_{R,S}(b)\) with \(R\geq k+1\) and
\(S\geq m+1\) therefore cancels from the denominator of
\(H_M(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\), and also from
\(F_N(c(b),\Delta,d(b))=\sum_{j=0}^Np(j)\,H_{N-j}(c(b),\Delta,d(b))\).  The
coefficient \(F_N\) already had no vertical prime factor in
\(\mathbb{Q}[b,b^{-1}]\), and each \(\Delta-h_{R,S}(b)\) is monic in
\(\Delta\).  So the denominator of \(F_N\) lies in
\(\mathbb{Q}[b,b^{-1},\Delta]\) and divides
\(\prod_{(R,S)\in\mathcal{K}_N}(\Delta-h_{R,S}(b))\), where
\(\mathcal{K}_N:=\{(R,S):RS\leq N,\; R\leq k\text{ or }S\leq m\}\).

For any \((R,S)\) with
\(h_{R,S}(b_\star)=\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\), \eqref{eq:mm-weight} gives
\(p'R-pS=\pm n_\mu\equiv\pm(p'r\pm ps)\pmod{2pp'}\); reducing modulo \(p\) and
\(p'\) forces \(R\geq\min(r,p-r)\geq k+1\) and \(S\geq\min(s,p'-s)\geq m+1\),
so \((R,S)\notin\mathcal{K}_N\).  Thus every active factor
\(\Delta-h_{R,S}(b)\) is nonzero at \((b_\star,\mu)\), so the denominator is a
unit in the local ring at \((b_\star,\mu)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:descent-regularity}]
Injectivity on \(\mathrm{Box}_{r,s}\) is
Lemma~\ref{lem:fusion-injective}(ii).

\emph{Part~(i):} By symmetry \eqref{eq:two-weight-sym},
\(\rho(c_\star,\lambda,d)\) descends to
\(L(c_\star,\lambda)\times L(c_\star,\lambda)\) if and only if
\(J^1(c_\star,\lambda)\subset\rad\rho(c_\star,\lambda,d)\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(i) and Lemma~\ref{lem:ward-induction}, this holds if and only if
\(\rho(w^+_1,v_\lambda)=\rho(w^-_1,v_\lambda)=0\).  A singular vector of
\(M(c_\star,\lambda)\) of a given level is unique up to a
scalar~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Astashkevich1997}; see
also~\cite[Proposition~5.1]{IoharaKoga2011}.  So \(w^+_1\) and \(w^-_1\) are
nonzero multiples of the normalized vectors \(\chi_{r,s}(b_\star)\) and
\(\chi_{p-r,p'-s}(b_\star)\) of levels \(rs\) and \((p-r)(p'-s)\).  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(ii), the two vanishing conditions are
equivalent to \(P_{r,s}(b_\star;\lambda,d)=0\) and
\(P_{p-r,p'-s}(b_\star;\lambda,d)=0\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:fusion-injective}(i), these two conditions together are
equivalent to \(d\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).

\emph{Part~(iii)} follows from~(i): the induced forms \(\bar B\) and
\(\bar\rho\) on \(L(c_\star,\lambda)\) define \(V_d(1)\) on the quotient, and
the trace~\eqref{eq:irr-trace-def} is
\(\sum_N\Tr(\bar B_N^{-1}\bar\rho_N)q^N\).

\emph{Parts~(iv) and~(ii), regularity.}  If
\(d\in\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\), then Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-holomorphicity}
gives joint regularity of \(F_N(c(b),\Delta,h_{2k+1,\epsilon(2m+1)}(b))\) at
\((b_\star,\mu)\), and hence regularity of \(F_N(c_\star,\Delta,d)\) at
\(\Delta=\mu\), for every \(N\geq0\) and every
\(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).  This is~(iv), and with \(\mu=\lambda\) it
is the regularity in~(ii).  When \(d\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:jantzen-vertex}(ii) gives a second proof that uses only
the specialized module,
together with the Jantzen trace decomposition \eqref{eq:jantzen-trace-decomp}.

\emph{Part~(ii), converse.}  Suppose \(d\notin\mathcal{F}^\pm_{p,p'}(r,s)\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:fusion-injective}(iii), at least one of
\(\tau^+_1(d)=\mathcal{P}_{r,s}(b_\star,d)\) and
\(\tau^-_1(d)=\mathcal{P}_{p-r,p'-s}(b_\star,d)\) is nonzero.
Let \(\ell_{\min}:=\min(\ell^+_1,\ell^-_1)\) and
\(\ell_{\max}:=\max(\ell^+_1,\ell^-_1)\), with corresponding branch labels
\(\mathrm{small}\) and \(\mathrm{big}\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(iv),
\(\ell_{\min}<\ell_{\max}<\min(\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)\), so both levels lie in
the range of levels \(N\) with \(j_N(\lambda)=1\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer},
\(\det G_M(c_\star,\mu^{\mathrm{small}}_1)\neq0\) for
\(M<\min(\ell^+_2,\ell^-_2)-\ell_{\min}\), so
\(F_{\ell_{\max}-\ell_{\min}}(c_\star,\mu^{\mathrm{small}}_1,d)\) is finite.
If \(\tau^{\mathrm{small}}_1(d)\neq0\), the top-layer residue formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} gives
\[
 \Res_{\Delta=\lambda}F_{\ell_{\min}}(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\tau^{\mathrm{small}}_1(d)/\gamma^{\mathrm{small}}_1\neq0.
\]
If
\(\tau^{\mathrm{small}}_1(d)=0\) and \(\tau^{\mathrm{big}}_1(d)\neq0\), it
gives
\[
 \Res_{\Delta=\lambda}F_{\ell_{\max}}(c_\star,\Delta,d)
 =\frac{\tau^{\mathrm{small}}_1(d)}{\gamma^{\mathrm{small}}_1}
  F_{\ell_{\max}-\ell_{\min}}(c_\star,\mu^{\mathrm{small}}_1,d)
 +\frac{\tau^{\mathrm{big}}_1(d)}{\gamma^{\mathrm{big}}_1}
 =\frac{\tau^{\mathrm{big}}_1(d)}{\gamma^{\mathrm{big}}_1}\neq0.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{First-layer vertex ratio and the BGG cocycle}\label{sec:cocycle}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio}, and then shows that
the first-layer ratios specialize at \(b_\star\) to nonzero rationals whose
products along the braided BGG diagram do not depend on the path
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bgg-cocycle}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio}]
Fix generic \(b\in\mathbb{C}^\times\), \(t=b^2\), \(R,S\geq1\), and
\(0\leq k\leq R-1\), \(0\leq m\leq S-1\).  Let \(\mathcal{X}_{R,S}\in U(\Vir_-)_{RS}\)
be the constant-coefficient PBW operator such that
\(\chi_{R,S}(b)=\mathcal{X}_{R,S}v_{h_{R,S}(b)}\).  For any \(d\in\mathbb{C}\),
consider the two-variable polynomial
\[
 f_d(x_1,x_2)
 :=\rho_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,\;h_{R,S}(b)+x_2;\;d}
   \bigl(\mathcal{X}_{R,S}v_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1},\;
         \mathcal{X}_{R,S}v_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_2}\bigr).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:two-weight-ward}, \(f_d(x_1,x_2)=f_d(x_2,x_1)\).

Set \(x_2=0\).  The singular vector
\(\chi_{R,S}(b)=\mathcal{X}_{R,S}v_{h_{R,S}(b)}\) generates the Verma submodule of
\(M(c(b),h_{R,S}(b))\) of highest weight \(h_{R,-S}(b)=h_{R,S}(b)+RS\).
Restricting the second slot of \(\rho_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,\,h_{R,S}(b);\,d}\) to it
gives a form satisfying~\eqref{eq:two-weight-ward} for the weights
\(h_{R,S}(b)+x_1\) and \(h_{R,-S}(b)\), so uniqueness in
Lemma~\ref{lem:two-weight-ward} gives, for every
\(u\in M(c(b),h_{R,S}(b)+x_1)\),
\begin{multline*}
 \rho_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,\,h_{R,S}(b);\,d}\bigl(u,\chi_{R,S}(b)\bigr)\\
 =\rho_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,\,h_{R,S}(b);\,d}\bigl(v_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1},\chi_{R,S}(b)\bigr)\,
  \rho_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,\,h_{R,-S}(b);\,d}\bigl(u,v_{h_{R,-S}(b)}\bigr).
\end{multline*}
Apply \eqref{eq:two-weight-sym} and Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(ii)
to the first factor, and substitute
\(u=\mathcal{X}_{R,S}v_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1}\).  This yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 f_d(x_1,0)
 &=C_{R,S}(b)\,P_{R,S}\bigl(b;h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,d\bigr)\\
 &\quad\times\rho_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1,\,h_{R,-S}(b);\,d}
  \bigl(\mathcal{X}_{R,S}v_{h_{R,S}(b)+x_1},v_{h_{R,-S}(b)}\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Specialize to \(d=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b)\) with \(0\leq k<R\) and
\(0\leq m<S\); this includes \(d=h_{1,1}(b)=0\) for \(k=m=0\).  At
\(x_1=0\), \(P_{R,S}(b;h_{R,S}(b),d)=0\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(ii) evaluates the second factor as
\(C_{R,S}(b)P_{R,S}(b;h_{R,-S}(b),d)\).  By the symmetry
\(f_d(x_1,x_2)=f_d(x_2,x_1)\), the diagonal derivative is
\(2\partial_{x_1}f_d(0,0)\), and the product rule evaluates it:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f-diag-deriv}
 \begin{aligned}
 \left.\frac{d}{dx}\right|_{x=0}f_d(x,x)
 &=2\,\partial_{x_1}f_d(0,0)\\
 &=2\,C_{R,S}(b)^2\,
  \left(\left.\partial_{\Delta_1}\right|_{\Delta_1=h_{R,S}(b)}
        P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,d)\right)
  P_{R,S}\bigl(b;h_{R,-S}(b),d\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:d0-shapovalov}, \(f_0(x,x)=B_{c(b),h_{R,S}(b)+x}(\chi_{R,S},\chi_{R,S})\).
Dividing \eqref{eq:f-diag-deriv} for \(d=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b)\) by
\eqref{eq:f-diag-deriv} for \(d=0\) cancels \(2C_{R,S}(b)^2\) identically:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-from-P}
 \begin{aligned}
 c_{R,S}(t;2k+1,2m+1)
 &=\frac{\left.\partial_{\Delta_1}\right|_{\Delta_1=h_{R,S}(b)}
        P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b))}
       {\left.\partial_{\Delta_1}\right|_{\Delta_1=h_{R,S}(b)}
        P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,0)}\\
 &\quad\cdot
  \frac{P_{R,S}(b;h_{R,-S}(b),h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b))}
       {P_{R,S}(b;h_{R,-S}(b),0)}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

To evaluate \eqref{eq:ratio-from-P}, rescale the momenta in
\eqref{eq:P-RS-def} by \(L_i:=b\Lambda_i\).  Then
\(\Delta_1=(Q^2-L_1^2/t)/4\), and the chain rule gives
\(\partial_{\Delta_1}=(-2t/L_1)\partial_{L_1}\).  Put \(L_{\mathrm{in}}:=Rt+S\).
The prefactor \(-2t/L_{\mathrm{in}}\) cancels between the numerator and the
denominator of \eqref{eq:ratio-from-P}.  Put
\(L_{\mathrm{out}}:=Rt-S\) and \(L_d:=(2k+1)t+(2m+1)\).  At generic \(t\), the
factorized product \(P_{R,S}(b;\Delta_1,d)\) in \eqref{eq:P-RS-def} has a
unique vanishing linear factor at \(L_1=L_{\mathrm{in}}\), namely
\(L_1-L_d+j_0t+l_0\) with \((j_0,l_0)=(2k+1-R,2m+1-S)\), whose
\(L_1\)-derivative is \(1\).  The remaining \(4RS-1\) linear factors of
\(\partial_{L_1}P_{R,S}|_{L_{\mathrm{in}}}\cdot P_{R,S}|_{L_{\mathrm{out}}}\),
apart from a power of \(2b\) independent of \((k,m)\), fall into four
quadrants indexed by
\((j,l)\in\{1-R,3-R,\ldots,R-1\}\times\{1-S,3-S,\ldots,S-1\}\):
\begin{align*}
 \tfrac{1}{2}(L_{\mathrm{in}}+L_d+p't+l)
 &=(a+k)t+(b'+m),
 \quad (a,b')\in[1,R]\times[1,S],\\
 -\tfrac{1}{2}(L_{\mathrm{in}}-L_d+p't+l)
 &=(a+k)t+(b'+m),
 \quad (a,b')\in[1-R,0]\times[1-S,0],\\
 \tfrac{1}{2}(L_{\mathrm{out}}+L_d+p't+l)
 &=(a+k)t+(b'+m),
 \quad (a,b')\in[1,R]\times[1-S,0],\\
 -\tfrac{1}{2}(L_{\mathrm{out}}-L_d+p't+l)
 &=(a+k)t+(b'+m),
 \quad (a,b')\in[1-R,0]\times[1,S],
\end{align*}
where the four bijections are, respectively,
\begin{gather*}
 (a,b')=\Bigl(\tfrac{R+1+j}{2},\tfrac{S+1+l}{2}\Bigr),\qquad
 \Bigl(-\tfrac{R-1+j}{2},-\tfrac{S-1+l}{2}\Bigr),\\
 \Bigl(\tfrac{R+1+j}{2},\tfrac{-S+1+l}{2}\Bigr),\qquad
 \Bigl(-\tfrac{R-1+j}{2},\tfrac{S+1-l}{2}\Bigr),
\end{gather*}
with the unique omitted factor at \((a,b')=(-k,-m)\) in the second line.  The
four quadrants partition \([1-R,R]\times[1-S,S]\setminus\{(-k,-m)\}\), and the
total sign \((-1)^{2RS-1}\) and power of \(2b\) are independent of \((k,m)\).
Dividing by the case \(k=m=0\) gives \eqref{eq:box-formula}.

For the Gamma factorization \eqref{eq:gamma-potential}, cancel the common
intersection between the two boxes in \eqref{eq:box-formula} in two steps:
first shift \(b'\) by \(m\) at fixed \(a\in[1-R,R]\), then shift \(a\) by \(k\)
at fixed \(b'\in[1-S+m,S+m]\).  This writes \eqref{eq:box-formula} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-step-box}
 c_{R,S}(t;2k+1,2m+1)
 =\left(\prod_{j=1}^m\prod_{a=1-R}^R\frac{at+S+j}{at-S+j}\right)
  \left(\prod_{i=1}^k\prod_{b'=1-S+m}^{S+m}\frac{(R+i)t+b'}{(-R+i)t+b'}\right).
\end{equation}
Set \(x_{\mathrm{in}}:=2\varpi_{R,S}/b=R+S/t\), \(x_{\mathrm{out}}:=2\varpi_{R,-S}/b=R-S/t\),
\(y_{\mathrm{in}}:=2\varpi_{R,S}b=Rt+S\), and \(y_{\mathrm{out}}:=2\varpi_{R,-S}b=Rt-S\).
The paired Gamma arguments differ by positive integers:
\begin{align*}
 (1+x_{\mathrm{in}}+j/t)-(1-x_{\mathrm{out}}+j/t)
 &=(1+x_{\mathrm{out}}+j/t)-(1-x_{\mathrm{in}}+j/t)=2R,\\
 (1+y_{\mathrm{in}}+it+m)-(1+y_{\mathrm{out}}+it+m)
 &=(1-y_{\mathrm{out}}+it+m)-(1-y_{\mathrm{in}}+it+m)=2S.
\end{align*}
Iterating \(\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)\) therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 \prod_{a=1-R}^R\frac{a+(S+j)/t}{a+(-S+j)/t}
 &=\frac{\Gamma(1+x_{\mathrm{in}}+j/t)}{\Gamma(1-x_{\mathrm{out}}+j/t)}
   \frac{\Gamma(1-x_{\mathrm{in}}+j/t)}{\Gamma(1+x_{\mathrm{out}}+j/t)},\\
 \prod_{b'=1-S+m}^{S+m}\frac{(R+i)t+b'}{(-R+i)t+b'}
 &=\frac{\Gamma(1+y_{\mathrm{in}}+it+m)}{\Gamma(1+y_{\mathrm{out}}+it+m)}
   \frac{\Gamma(1-y_{\mathrm{in}}+it+m)}{\Gamma(1-y_{\mathrm{out}}+it+m)}.
\end{align*}
Multiplying over \(j=1,\ldots,m\) and \(i=1,\ldots,k\) gives
\[
 c_{R,S}(t;2k+1,2m+1)
 =\frac{\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_{R,-S};b)}{\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_{R,S};b)}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finiteness, nonvanishing, and path-independence of the BGG cocycle]\label{prop:bgg-cocycle}
Let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) (\(p,p'\geq2\) coprime), \(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\)
interior, and \(d=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b_\star)\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] For every primitive BGG arrow \(\mu\to\nu\) in
   \(\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) with Kac label \((R,S)\), setting
   \(B_0:=[1-R,R]\times[1-S,S]\) and \(B_1:=[1-R+k,R+k]\times[1-S+m,S+m]\),
   every factor \(pb'-p'a\) with \((a,b')\in(B_1\setminus B_0)\cup(B_0\setminus B_1)\)
   is nonzero.  Consequently,
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-box-mm}
    c_{R,S}\Bigl(-\frac{p'}{p};2k+1,2m+1\Bigr)
    =\frac{\prod_{(a,b')\in B_1\setminus B_0}(pb'-p'a)}
          {\prod_{(a,b')\in B_0\setminus B_1}(pb'-p'a)}
    \;\in\;\mathbb{Q}^\times
   \end{equation}
   is finite and nonzero.
 \item[(ii)] For every diamond
   \(\mu\to\nu_1\to\xi\), \(\mu\to\nu_2\to\xi\) in the braided BGG diagram of
   \(\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\),
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:diamond-cocycle}
    c(\mu\to\nu_1)\,c(\nu_1\to\xi)
    =c(\mu\to\nu_2)\,c(\nu_2\to\xi),
   \end{equation}
   where \(c(\alpha\to\beta):=c_{R,S}(-p'/p;2k+1,2m+1)\) for the primitive
   arrow \(\alpha\to\beta\) of label \((R,S)\).  Hence the product of
   \(c(\alpha\to\beta)\) along any directed BGG path from \(\mu\) to \(\xi\)
   depends only on \((\mu,\xi)\), defining a canonical rational cocycle
   \(\Omega_{2k+1,2m+1}(\mu\to\xi)\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For~(i), since \(\gcd(p,p')=1\), \(pb'-p'a=0\) requires \(p\mid a\) and
\(p'\mid b'\).  If \((a,b')\in B_1\setminus B_0\), then either
\(a=R+i\) with \(1\leq i\leq k\) or \(b'=S+j\) with \(1\leq j\leq m\).
By \eqref{eq:RS-mod} and \(k<\min(r,p-r)\), if \(a=R+i\) (\(1\leq i\leq k\))
then \(a\equiv r+i\in\{r+1,\ldots,p-1\}\) (when \(R\equiv r\pmod p\)) or
\(a\equiv p-r+i\in\{p-r+1,\ldots,p-1\}\) (when \(R\equiv p-r\pmod p\)), so
\(p\nmid a\).  By the same check on \(b'=S+j\) (\(1\leq j\leq m\)) using
\(m<\min(s,p'-s)\), we have \(p'\nmid b'\).  If
\((a,b')\in B_0\setminus B_1\), then either \(a=i-R\) (\(1\leq i\leq k\)) or
\(b'=j-S\) (\(1\leq j\leq m\)), and the same congruences give
\(a\equiv i-r\) or \(i-(p-r)\in\{1-p,\ldots,-1\}\) (so \(p\nmid a\)) or
\(p'\nmid b'\).  Thus \(pb'-p'a\neq0\) throughout both set differences.  Since
\(|B_1\setminus B_0|=|B_0\setminus B_1|\), the common powers of \(p^{-1}\) in
\(a(-p'/p)+b'=(pb'-p'a)/p\) cancel between numerator and denominator, giving
\eqref{eq:reduced-box-mm}.

For~(ii), set \(z_\mu:=n_\mu/p'\) for each \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\)
(so \(2\varpi_\mu/b_\star=\pm z_\mu\) and \(2\varpi_\mu b_\star=\pm t_\star z_\mu=\mp n_\mu/p\)),
and evaluate the Gamma potential \eqref{eq:gamma-potential} at
\(b=b_\star\):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\mu;b_\star)
 &=\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{\Gamma\bigl(1+\tfrac{n_\mu-jp}{p'}\bigr)
                         \Gamma\bigl(1+\tfrac{-n_\mu-jp}{p'}\bigr)}\\
 &\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{1}{\Gamma\bigl(1+m+\tfrac{-n_\mu-ip'}{p}\bigr)
                         \Gamma\bigl(1+m+\tfrac{n_\mu-ip'}{p}\bigr)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
None of the Gamma arguments is an integer.  By \eqref{eq:bgg-momenta},
\(n_\mu\equiv\pm ps\pmod{p'}\) and \(n_\mu\equiv\pm p'r\pmod{p}\).  Hence
\((n_\mu\pm jp)/p'\in\mathbb{Z}\) would require \(p'\mid(\pm s-j)\) for some
\(1\leq j\leq m<\min(s,p'-s)\); this is impossible because
\(1\leq s-j\leq s-1<p'\) and \(2\leq s+j\leq p'-1<p'\).  Similarly,
\((\pm n_\mu-ip')/p\in\mathbb{Z}\) would require \(p\mid(\pm r-i)\) for some
\(1\leq i\leq k<\min(r,p-r)\), which is impossible because
\(1\leq r-i\leq r-1<p\) and \(2\leq r+i\leq p-1<p\).  Since \(\Gamma\) has no
zeros and has poles only at \(\mathbb{Z}_{\leq0}\), every factor in
\(\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\mu;b_\star)\) is finite and nonzero, so
\(\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\mu;b_\star)\in\mathbb{C}^\times\).  Since
\eqref{eq:gamma-potential} is even in \(\varpi\), this value depends only on
\(\mu\).

For any primitive BGG arrow \(\alpha\to\beta\) of label \((R,S)\) with
\(p'R-pS=\sigma n_\alpha\) (\(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\)) and \(p'R+pS=n_\beta\),
we have \(R+S/t_\star=\sigma n_\alpha/p'\), \(R-S/t_\star=n_\beta/p'\),
\(Rt_\star+S=-\sigma n_\alpha/p\), and \(Rt_\star-S=-n_\beta/p\).  Since
every Gamma value is finite and nonzero, the \(\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)\)
telescoping in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio} holds pointwise
at \(t=t_\star=-p'/p\), giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arrow-is-psi-ratio}
 c_{R,S}\Bigl(-\frac{p'}{p};2k+1,2m+1\Bigr)
 =\frac{\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\beta;b_\star)}{\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\alpha;b_\star)}.
\end{equation}
On either path around a BGG diamond, these factors telescope to
\[
 \Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\xi;b_\star)/\Psi_{2k+1,2m+1}(\varpi_\mu;b_\star).
\]
This gives~\eqref{eq:diamond-cocycle}; the product lies in \(\mathbb Q^\times\)
by~(i).
\end{proof}

\section{Two-layer \texorpdfstring{\(\tau\)}{tau}-Jantzen telescoping and the Rocha--Caridi identity}\label{sec:telescoping}

\begin{lemma}[Two-layer \(\tau\)-Jantzen identity on the generic degenerate quotient]\label{lem:degenerate-quotient-t}
Let \(b_\star^2=-p'/p\) (\(p,p'\geq2\) coprime), let
\(\lambda=h_{r,s}(b_\star)\) be an interior Kac weight, and let
\(d_\star=h_{u,v}(b_\star)\in\mathcal{F}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) with
\((u,v)=(2k+1,2m+1)\).  Let \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) have primitive
BGG arrows \(\mu\to\nu_1\) (label \((R_1,S_1)\)) and \(\mu\to\nu_2\) (label
\((R_2,S_2)\)).  Then as formal power series in \(q\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-term-id}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\mu,d_\star}(q)
 +\Omega_{u,v}(\mu\to\nu_2)\,q^{\nu_2-\mu}\,
  \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\nu_2,d_\star}(q)\\
 &\qquad=\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\mu,d_\star}(q)
 -\Omega_{u,v}(\mu\to\nu_1)\,q^{\nu_1-\mu}\,
  \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\nu_1,d_\star}(q).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(t_0:=b_\star^2=-p'/p\).  Let \(\tau\) be a formal deformation
parameter, with \(t(\tau):=t_0+\tau\) and \(b(\tau):=\sqrt{t(\tau)}\) near
\(b_\star\).  Put \(\Delta_1(\tau):=h_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))\),
\(\Delta_2(\tau):=h_{R_2,S_2}(b(\tau))\), and
\(d(\tau):=h_{2k+1,2m+1}(b(\tau))\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(iii),
\(0\leq k<R_i\) and \(0\leq m<S_i\) for \(i=1,2\).

\emph{Step 1: Generic-\(\tau\) splitting.}
Fix generic \(\tau\neq0\) so that \(t(\tau)\) is irrational.  Then the module
\(M\bigl(c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau)\bigr)\) has a unique proper nonzero
submodule, generated by the singular vector \(\chi_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))\), which
has weight \(h_{R_1,-S_1}(b(\tau))=\Delta_1(\tau)+R_1S_1\).  Denote its
irreducible quotient by
\[
 V_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))
 :=M\bigl(c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau)\bigr)\big/
   U(\Vir_-)\chi_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau)).
\]
Since \(0\leq k<R_1\) and \(0\leq m<S_1\), Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(ii)
and Lemma~\ref{lem:ward-induction} show that
\(U(\Vir_-)\chi_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))\) lies in the radical of both
\(B_{c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau)}\) and
\(\rho_{c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau),d(\tau)}\).  So both forms descend to
\(V_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))\), and \(\bar B_{V,\tau}\) is nondegenerate.  The
two-layer \(\Delta\)-Jantzen decomposition of the generic Verma module
\(M(c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau))\), proved as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:jantzen-vertex}(ii), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio} give, at every level \(N\geq0\) and
generic \(\tau\neq0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generic-tau-split}
 \begin{aligned}
  F_N\bigl(c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau),d(\tau)\bigr)
  &=\Tr_{V_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))_N}\bigl(V_{d(\tau)}(1)\bigr)\\
  &\quad+c_{R_1,S_1}(t(\tau);u,v)\,
    F_{N-R_1S_1}\bigl(c(b(\tau)),h_{R_1,-S_1}(b(\tau)),d(\tau)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: Free \(\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]\)-lattice and length-two specialization.}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:fusion-poly}(i), \(\chi_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))\) specializes
regularly to the nonzero singular vector \(\chi_{R_1,S_1}(b_\star)\), whose
embedding is injective~\cite[the paragraph preceding Corollary~5.2]{IoharaKoga2011}.
Complete its specialized descendants at level \(N\) to a basis of
\(M(c_\star,\mu)_N\) and lift the complement as constant PBW families.  The
full matrix is invertible modulo \(\tau\), hence over \(\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]\).
Thus \(\mathcal{V}_N:=V_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))_N\) is free over \(\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]\) of
rank \(p(N)-p(N-R_1S_1)\), with special fiber the level-\(N\) part of
\(V_0:=M(c_\star,\mu)/M(c_\star,\nu_1)\).

By Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(i), \(J^1(c_\star,\mu)=M(c_\star,\nu_1)+M(c_\star,\nu_2)\)
and \(M(c_\star,\nu_1)\cap M(c_\star,\nu_2)=J^2(c_\star,\mu)=\rad M(c_\star,\nu_2)\).
By the second isomorphism theorem,
\[
 \frac{J^1(c_\star,\mu)}{M(c_\star,\nu_1)}
 =\frac{M(c_\star,\nu_1)+M(c_\star,\nu_2)}{M(c_\star,\nu_1)}
 \cong\frac{M(c_\star,\nu_2)}{\rad M(c_\star,\nu_2)}
 =L(c_\star,\nu_2),
\]
and \(V_0/\bigl(J^1(c_\star,\mu)/M(c_\star,\nu_1)\bigr)
=M(c_\star,\mu)/J^1(c_\star,\mu)=L(c_\star,\mu)\).  The resulting short exact
sequence is \(0\to L(c_\star,\nu_2)\to V_0\to L(c_\star,\mu)\to0\).  A
submodule that surjects onto the head \(L(c_\star,\mu)\) contains the
highest-weight line by the \(L_0\)-grading and hence generates \(V_0\); a proper
submodule therefore lies in the simple socle \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\).  So \(V_0\)
has length two, and its only submodules are \(V_0\), \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\)
and \(0\).

\emph{Step 3: The \(\tau\)-Jantzen filtration on \(\mathcal{V}_N\) has depth one.}
Define the \(\tau\)-Jantzen filtration
\(V_0=J_\tau^0\supseteq J_\tau^1\supseteq J_\tau^2\supseteq\cdots\) via
\(\mathcal{J}_\tau^{(i)}
:=\{a(\tau)\in\mathcal{V}:\bar B_{V,\tau}(a(\tau),v)\in\tau^i\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]
\;\forall\,v\in\mathcal{V}\}\).
Each \(J_\tau^i\) is a \(\Vir\)-submodule of \(V_0\), with
\(J_\tau^1=\rad\bar B_{V,0}=L(c_\star,\nu_2)\).  Since \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\) is
irreducible, \(J_\tau^2\) is either \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\) or \(0\).
Let \(w_2(\tau)\in\mathcal{V}_{R_2S_2}\) be the image of
\(\chi_{R_2,S_2}(b(\tau))\in M(c(b(\tau)),\Delta_1(\tau))_{R_2S_2}\)
(written with PBW coefficients polynomial in \(t(\tau),t(\tau)^{-1}\)).
Consider the two-variable regular functions
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 g_B(t,\Delta)&:=B_{c(b),\Delta}\bigl(\chi_{R_2,S_2}(b),\chi_{R_2,S_2}(b)\bigr),\\
 g_\rho(t,\Delta)&:=\rho_{c(b),\Delta,h_{u,v}(b)}\bigl(\chi_{R_2,S_2}(b),\chi_{R_2,S_2}(b)\bigr),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where \(b=\sqrt{t}\).  On \(\Delta=h_{R_2,S_2}(t)\), singularity of
\(\chi_{R_2,S_2}(b)\) gives \(g_B=0\), and the ranges \(0\leq k<R_2\),
\(0\leq m<S_2\) give \(g_\rho=0\).  Hence
\(g_B(t,\Delta)=(\Delta-h_{R_2,S_2}(t))\tilde g_B(t,\Delta)\) and
\(g_\rho(t,\Delta)=(\Delta-h_{R_2,S_2}(t))\tilde g_\rho(t,\Delta)\), with
\(\tilde g_B\) and \(\tilde g_\rho\) regular at \((t_0,\mu)\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(iii),
\(\tilde g_B(t_0,\mu)=\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\neq0\).  By
\eqref{eq:transverse-slope},
\(\Delta_1(\tau)-\Delta_2(\tau)=\alpha\tau+O(\tau^2)\) with
\(\alpha=(\kappa_2\theta_2-\kappa_1\theta_1)/(4p'^2)\neq0\), in the notation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}.  Evaluating along
\((t,\Delta)=(t_0+\tau,\Delta_1(\tau))\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-deriv-chain}
 \begin{aligned}
  \bar B_{V,\tau}(w_2(\tau),w_2(\tau))
  &=g_B(t_0+\tau,\Delta_1(\tau))
   =\alpha\,\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\,\tau+O(\tau^2),\\
  \bar\rho_{V,\tau}(w_2(\tau),w_2(\tau))
  &=g_\rho(t_0+\tau,\Delta_1(\tau))
   =\alpha\,\tilde g_\rho(t_0,\mu)\,\tau+O(\tau^2).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Any other lift of \(w_2(0)\) to \(\mathcal{V}_{R_2S_2}\) has the form
\(w_2(\tau)+\tau u(\tau)\).  Since \(w_2(0)\in J_\tau^1=\rad\bar B_{V,0}\), its
norm has the same linear coefficient in \(\tau\).  That coefficient,
\(\alpha\,\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\), is nonzero, so \(w_2(0)\) admits no lift
in \(\mathcal{J}_\tau^{(2)}\) and \(w_2(0)\notin J_\tau^2\).  Since the only
submodules of \(V_0\) are \(V_0\), \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\) and \(0\), this forces
\(J_\tau^2=0\).

\emph{Step 4: Taking \(\tau\to0\).}
Diagonalize \(\bar B_{V,\tau}\) by congruence on \(\mathcal{V}_N\) over
\(\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]\):
\[
 U(\tau)^{\mathsf{T}}\bar B_{V,\tau}U(\tau)
 =\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\tau^{e_1}\upsilon_1(\tau),\ldots,\tau^{e_n}\upsilon_n(\tau)\bigr),
\]
where \(n\) is the rank of \(\mathcal V_N\) and each \(\upsilon_a\) is a unit.  Since \(J_\tau^2=0\), every exponent \(e_a\) lies in
\(\{0,1\}\).  The vectors \(\{v_a(0):e_a=0\}\) descend to a basis of
\(L(c_\star,\mu)_N\), and \(\{v_a(0):e_a=1\}\) form a basis of
\(L(c_\star,\nu_2)_{N-R_2S_2}\).  Let \(a(\tau),b(\tau)\in\mathcal{J}_\tau^{(1)}\)
be homogeneous of levels \(N_a,N_b\).  Since
\(J_\tau^1=L(c_\star,\nu_2)\subset\rad\bar\rho_{V,0}\), we have
\(\bar\rho_{V,\tau}(a(\tau),b(\tau))=O(\tau)\), and shifting either lift by
\(\tau u(\tau)\) changes this value by \(O(\tau^2)\).  Hence
\(\bar\rho^{(1)}_\tau(a(0),b(0)):=[\tau^1]\bar\rho_{V,\tau}(a(\tau),b(\tau))\)
is well-defined on \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\times L(c_\star,\nu_2)\).  Extract the
coefficient of \(\tau\) in the Ward identity
\(\bar\rho_{V,\tau}(L_{-n}a(\tau),b(\tau))-\bar\rho_{V,\tau}(a(\tau),L_nb(\tau))
=(N_a-N_b+nd(\tau))\bar\rho_{V,\tau}(a(\tau),b(\tau))\).  The term
\(nd'(0)\tau\) multiplies a pairing already of order \(\tau\), so
\(\bar\rho^{(1)}_\tau\) satisfies the \(V_{d_\star}(1)\) Ward identity
on \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\).  By uniqueness of the contravariant and primary forms
on the irreducible quotient \(L(c_\star,\nu_2)\), we have
\(\bar B^{(1)}_\tau=\alpha\,\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\,\bar B_{L(\nu_2)}\) and
\(\bar\rho^{(1)}_\tau=\alpha\,\tilde g_\rho(t_0,\mu)\,\bar\rho_{L(\nu_2),d_\star}\).
Both \(\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\) and the reduced box denominator of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bgg-cocycle}(i) are nonzero at \(t=t_0\), so the
rational identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:first-layer-ratio} specializes there,
and the ratio of the two forms is
\[
 \frac{\alpha\,\tilde g_\rho(t_0,\mu)}{\alpha\,\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)}
 =\frac{\tilde g_\rho(t_0,\mu)}{\gamma_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)}
 =c_{R_2,S_2}(t_0;u,v)
 =\Omega_{u,v}(\mu\to\nu_2).
\]
Therefore the \(\tau^1\) factors cancel in the diagonalized trace on
\(\mathcal{V}_N\), giving
\[
 \lim_{\tau\to0}\Tr_{V_{R_1,S_1}(b(\tau))_N}\bigl(V_{d(\tau)}(1)\bigr)
 =T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N(c_\star,\mu,d_\star)
  +\Omega_{u,v}(\mu\to\nu_2)\,T^{\mathrm{irr}}_{N-R_2S_2}(c_\star,\nu_2,d_\star).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-holomorphicity} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:bgg-cocycle}(i), the remaining terms in
\eqref{eq:generic-tau-split} are regular at \(\tau=0\) and converge to
\(F_N(c_\star,\mu,d_\star)\) and
\(\Omega_{u,v}(\mu\to\nu_1)F_{N-R_1S_1}(c_\star,\nu_1,d_\star)\).  Taking
\(\tau\to0\) in \eqref{eq:generic-tau-split} yields \eqref{eq:two-term-id}.
\end{proof}

For comparison, Astashkevich~\cite[Theorem~C(e)]{Astashkevich1997} studies
the same deformation on the Verma module \(M(c_\star,\mu)\) before the
quotient is taken: there the filtration terms eventually equal the branch
\(M(c_\star,\nu_1)\) generated by the singular vector of the deforming label,
and this branch is removed in \(V_0\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-rocha-caridi}]
For each \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\), define the rescaled formal power
series
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)
 :=\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu)\,q^{\mu-\lambda}\,
   \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\mu,d}(q),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu)
 :=\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu)\,q^{\mu-\lambda}\,
   \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Verma}}_{c_\star,\mu,d}(q),
\]
with \(\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\lambda):=1\).  By the cocycle property
\(\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu)\Omega_{u,v}(\mu\to\nu)=\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\nu)\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bgg-cocycle}(ii)), multiplying \eqref{eq:two-term-id}
by \(\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu)q^{\mu-\lambda}\) yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-two-term}
 \widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)+\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\nu_2)
 =\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu)-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\nu_1)
\end{equation}
for every \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\) with primitive children
\(\{\nu_1,\nu_2\}\).  Apply \eqref{eq:scaled-two-term} along the alternating
chain \(\mu_0:=\lambda\), \(\mu_1:=\mu^-_1\), \(\mu_2:=\mu^+_2\),
\(\mu_3:=\mu^-_3\), \(\ldots\).  At step \(j\geq0\) choose
\(\nu_2=\mu_{j+1}\); the other child is \(\nu_1=\mu^{\epsilon_j}_{j+1}\) with
\(\epsilon_j=(-1)^j\):
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_j)+\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{j+1})
 =\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu_j)-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu^{(-1)^j}_{j+1})
 \qquad (j\geq0).
\]
Fix \(N\) and take \(K\) with \(\min(\ell^+_{K+1},\ell^-_{K+1})>N\).  The
alternating sum \(\sum_{j=0}^K(-1)^j\) leaves
\(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\lambda)+(-1)^K\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{K+1})\equiv
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{c_\star,\lambda,d}(q)\) modulo \(q^{N+1}\) on the left
and, on the right,
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda)
 +\sum_{j=1}^K(-1)^j\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu^+_j)
                         +\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu^-_j)\bigr)
 +(-1)^{K+1}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu^{(-1)^K}_{K+1}).
\]
The last term also vanishes modulo \(q^{N+1}\).  Since \(N\) was arbitrary,
\eqref{eq:gen-rocha-caridi} holds as a formal series.

For part~(a), \(k=m=0\) gives \(d=0\), \(\Omega_{1,1}\equiv1\) and
\(F_N(c_\star,\mu,0)=p(N)\) for all \(\mu\).

For part~(b), Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} gives \(H_M(c_\star,\Delta,d)\)
in partial fractions from the generic recursion.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:descent-regularity} the principal part vanishes at each orbit
weight \(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).  Evaluate the remaining terms at \(\mu\),
recover \(F_M(c_\star,\mu,d)=\sum_{i=0}^Mp(i)H_{M-i}(c_\star,\mu,d)\), and keep the
terms of~\eqref{eq:gen-rocha-caridi} with \(\ell^\pm_j\leq N\).

For part~(c), the same telescoping argument, started from any
\(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\), gives \eqref{eq:gen-rocha-caridi} with
\(\lambda\) replaced by \(\mu\).  Let \(U_0\) be the unitriangular zeta matrix
of the braided poset on the ordered orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\), with
\((U_0)_{\mu,\nu}=1\) for \(\nu\succeq\mu\); the matrix identities below are
taken coefficientwise in \(q\), where each orbit sum is finite.  Let
\(\mathrm{dist}(\mu,\nu)\) be the difference of the layers of \(\nu\) and
\(\mu\).  The inverse has entries
\((U_0^{-1})_{\mu,\nu}=(-1)^{\mathrm{dist}(\mu,\nu)}\), since an interval
\([\mu,\nu]\) with \(\mathrm{dist}(\mu,\nu)=n>0\) contains one bottom vertex
\(\mu\), two vertices at each intermediate distance \(1\leq i\leq n-1\), and
one top vertex \(\nu\), and \(1+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^i+(-1)^n=0\).  In matrix
form, the identities for all \(\mu\) read
\((\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\mu))_\mu=U_0^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mu))_\mu\).
Multiplying by \(U_0\) recovers
\(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda)=\sum_{\nu\succeq\lambda}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\nu)\),
i.e.
\[
 F_N(c_\star,\lambda,d)
 =T^{\mathrm{irr}}_N(c_\star,\lambda,d)
  +\sum_{j=1}^{j_N(\lambda)}\sum_{\pm}
    \Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu^\pm_j)\,
    T^{\mathrm{irr}}_{N-\ell^\pm_j}(c_\star,\mu^\pm_j,d).
\]
The orbit levels \(\ell^\pm_j\) (\(j\geq1\)) are pairwise distinct.  Each
layer-\((j+1)\) weight exceeds both layer-\(j\) weights by positive primitive
arrow levels, and within each layer \(\ell^+_j\neq\ell^-_j\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:bgg-arrows}(iv).  Compare this expansion with
\eqref{eq:jantzen-trace-decomp} in increasing order of \(\ell^\pm_j\).  Since
\(T^{\mathrm{irr}}_0=1\), each step gives
\(\sigma^\pm_j(\lambda)/\gamma^\pm_j(\lambda)=\Omega_{u,v}(\lambda\to\mu^\pm_j)\)
for that \(j\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:collision-free}]
By duality take \(d(b)=h_{1,2m+1}(b)\) with \(0\leq m<\min(s,p'-s)\).  The
residue multiplier \(\mathcal{R}_{R,S}(b,h_{1,2m+1}(b))\) vanishes identically
unless \(S\leq m\) (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-holomorphicity}, with \(k=0\)),
and \(2m\leq2\min(s,p'-s)-2\leq p'-2\).  We check
the three obstructions for the active labels \((R,S)\) with \(1\leq S\leq m\):
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{No \(b\)-poles in \(\mathcal{R}_{R,S}(b,d(b))\) at \(b=b_\star\):}
   the denominator factors of \(\mathcal{A}_{R,S}(b)\) are \(ab+b'b^{-1}=(at+b')/b\) with
   \(1-S\leq b'\leq S\) and \((a,b')\neq(0,0)\).  At \(t_\star=-p'/p\),
   \(pb'-p'a=0\) requires \(p'\mid b'\).  Since \(|b'|\leq S\leq m<p'/2\), this
   forces \(b'=0\) and hence \(a=0\), which is excluded.
 \item \emph{No primary collisions:}
   if \(h_{R_1,S_1}(b_\star)=h_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\) with
   \(1\leq S_1,S_2\leq m\), then \(p'R_1-pS_1=\pm(p'R_2-pS_2)\) implies
   \(p'\mid(S_1\mp S_2)\).  Since \(|S_1-S_2|<p'/2\) and
   \(0<S_1+S_2\leq 2m<p'\), we must have \(S_1=S_2\) and the \(+\) sign, hence
   \(R_1=R_2\).
 \item \emph{No secondary collisions:}
   if \(h_{R_1,-S_1}(b_\star)=h_{R_2,S_2}(b_\star)\) with
   \(1\leq S_1,S_2\leq m\), then \(p'R_1+pS_1=\pm(p'R_2-pS_2)\).  The \(-\)
   sign gives \(p'(R_1+R_2)=p(S_2-S_1)\), forcing \(p'\mid(S_2-S_1)\), hence
   \(S_1=S_2\) and \(R_1+R_2=0\), impossible.  The \(+\) sign gives
   \(p'(R_2-R_1)=p(S_1+S_2)\), forcing \(p'\mid(S_1+S_2)\), impossible since
   \(0<S_1+S_2\leq 2m<p'\).
\end{enumerate}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-holomorphicity}, no active pole
\(h_{R,S}(b_\star)\) coincides with any BGG weight
\(\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{p,p'}(r,s)\).  Therefore every denominator in the
truncated recursion for \(H_N(c_\star,\mu,d_\star)\) is nonzero at
\(b=b_\star\).
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and outlook}\label{sec:scope}

The confluence theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence}) holds at every
\(b_\star\neq0\).  The sharp pole orders (Corollary~\ref{cor:top-layer-sharp})
and the results on irreducible traces concern interior minimal-model weights.

Several questions remain open.  At the boundary weights of Class \(R^+\),
whose Jantzen filtrations are given
by~\cite[Theorem~6.3(ii)--(iv) and (6.4)]{IoharaKoga2011}, and at the charges
\(c_\star=1,25\), the confluence formula still gives every coefficient, but
the pole order and the top coefficient are not determined here.  The
coefficients below the top one, \(A^\star_{\lambda,k,N}\) with
\(k<m^\star_{\lambda,N}\), are computed here only as collision moments; a
formula for them in terms of the specialized module, like
Theorem~\ref{thm:top-layer} for the top one, is not known.  Finally, the
formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-confluence} passes through generic \(b\);
a recursion that uses only data at \(c=c_\star\), as asked for
in~\cite[\S4.3.3]{Ribault2026}, would be the natural next step.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(i)--(ii)}\label{app:h13}

We keep the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}; in
particular \(t=b^2\), \(\Delta=Y/t^2\), and \(\nu\in\{1,2\}\) indexes the two
external weights \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b)\) and \(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b)\).  Partitions are
denoted \(Z,Z'\), and \(\delta i\), \(\delta j\) denote increments of the
exponents \(i\) and \(j\) in \([t^iY^j]\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:h13-nonvanishing}(i)--(ii)]
(i) At \(d_1=h_{1,3}(b)\) and \(d_2=h_{1,-3}(b)\) one has
\(\Lambda^2=(b\pm3b^{-1})^2\), so the factor \((k,l)=(1,3)\) in
Definition~\ref{def:generic-residue} vanishes for every \(v\geq2\) and
\({\mathcal R}_{u,v}(b,d_\nu)=0\) (\(v\geq2\)).  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-recursion} and induction on \(M\),
\(H_M(c(b),\Delta,d_\nu)\) has at most simple poles in \(\Delta\), all at
\(h_{u,1}(b)\) (\(1\leq u\leq M\)), so \(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in\mathbb Q(t)[Y]\)
has \(Y\)-degree at most \(M-1\).
Write \(c(b)=6/t+13+6t\), \(\Delta=Y/t^2\), and \(d=-2/t+\zeta\).  Since
\(6\mid(m^3-m)\) and \(c(b)/2\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1}]\), the entries of
\(G_M\) and \(\rho_M\) lie in \(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1},\Delta]\) and
\(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1},\Delta,\zeta]\).  For \(Z,Z'\vdash M\),
put \(\widetilde G_{Z,Z'}=t^{\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')}G_{M,Z,Z'}\)
and \(\widetilde\rho_{Z,Z'}=t^{\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')}\rho_{M,Z,Z'}\).
In \(G_{M,Z,Z'}\), every \(\Delta\) or \(c\) consumes at least two
modes, so \(2(d_\Delta+d_c)\leq\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')\); since
\(\Delta=Y/t^2\) and \(c=O(t^{-1})\), a \(t^0\)-term requires
\(2d_\Delta+d_c=\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')\), forcing \(d_c=0\) and direct
opposite-mode contractions only, hence \(Z=Z'\).  Thus
\(\widetilde G(t,Y)=D_0(Y)+tG_1(t,Y)\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y])\)
with \(D_0(Y)=\operatorname{diag}(K_Z Y^{\ell(Z)})\),
\(K_Z=\prod_{n\geq1}m_n!(2n)^{m_n}\) for \(Z=(1^{m_1}2^{m_2}\cdots)\).
In \(\rho_{M,Z,Z'}\), each \(d\) consumes at least one mode and each
\(\Delta\) or \(c\) at least two, so
\(d_d+2d_\Delta+d_c\leq\ell(Z)+\ell(Z')\), giving
\(\widetilde\rho\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y,\zeta])\)
with \(\widetilde\rho(0,Y;\zeta)\) independent of \(\zeta\).  Thus
\(F_M=\Tr(\widetilde G^{-1}\widetilde\rho)\) and
\(H_M=\sum_s\eta_sF_{M-s}\) lie in \(\mathbb Q(Y)[[t]]\) with \(t=0\) value
independent of \(\zeta\).  Since
\(t^{2M}\prod_{u=1}^M(2\Delta-2h_{u,1})=\prod_{u=1}^M(2Y+(u-1)t^2+\tfrac{u^2-1}{2}t^3)\),
\(P_M^{(\nu)}\) is regular at \(t=0\), \(P_M^{(2)}(0,Y)=P_M^{(1)}(0,Y)\), and
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(0,Y)=M!(2Y)^M\sum_{s=0}^M\eta_s\sum_{Z\vdash M-s}
(K_Z Y^{\ell(Z)})^{-1}\widetilde\rho^{(M-s)}(0,Y)_{Z,Z}\).
Only \((s,Z)=(0,(1^M))\) has \(M-\ell(Z)=0\), where
\(M!2^M/K_{(1^M)}=1\) and \(\widetilde\rho^{(M)}(0,0)_{(1^M),(1^M)}=(-2)^{2M}=4^M\)
(proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:singular-degree}), so \(P_M^{(\nu)}(0,0)=4^M\).
Also, \(\det G_M=K_M\prod_{uv\leq M}(\Delta-h_{u,v})^{p(M-uv)}\)
(\(K_M=\prod_{Z\vdash M}K_Z\)); Cramer's rule and monic division
by \(\Delta-h_{u,v}\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1}][\Delta]\) give
\(K_MP_M^{(\nu)}\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,t^{-1},Y]\), hence
\(P_M^{(\nu)}\in\mathbb Q[t,Y]\).  To clear odd primes, adjoin \(q_0\) with
\(q_0^2=-(b+b^{-1})^2/2\) (\(c(b)=1-12q_0^2\)) and \(\alpha\) with
\(\Delta=\frac12\alpha(\alpha-2q_0)\).  On the Fock module
\({\mathcal H}(\alpha)\) (\([a_m,a_n]=m\delta_{m+n,0}\),
\(a_0|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle\)),
\(L_n=\frac12\sum_k{:}a_ka_{n-k}{:}-q_0(n+1)a_n\) gives a Virasoro action of
charge \(c(b)\) and weight \(\Delta\).  Under the contravariant pairing between
\({\mathcal H}(2q_0-\alpha)\) and \({\mathcal H}(\alpha)\)
(\(a_n^\dagger=-a_{-n}\) for \(n\neq0\), \(a_0^\dagger=2q_0-a_0\)),
\(L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}\) and
\(\langle2q_0-\alpha|(a_{-Z})^\dagger a_{-Z'}|\alpha\rangle
=\delta_{Z,Z'}(-1)^{\ell(Z)}\prod_n m_n!n^{m_n}\).  Hence
\(G_M=B_M(q_0,2q_0-\alpha)^TD_MB_M(q_0,\alpha)\), where
\(B_M(q_0,\alpha)\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][q_0,\alpha])\)
expresses \(L_{-Z}|\alpha\rangle\) in \(a_{-Z'}|\alpha\rangle\) and
\(D_M=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^{\ell(Z)}\prod_n m_n!n^{m_n})\).  Since
\(|\det D_M|=2^{-\sum_Z\ell(Z)}K_M\), taking determinants gives
\(\det B_M(q_0,2q_0-\alpha)\det B_M(q_0,\alpha)
=\pm2^{\sum\ell(Z)}\prod_{uv\leq M}(\Delta-h_{u,v})^{p(M-uv)}\), whose
leading \(\alpha\)-coefficient is a power of \(2\).  Fix an odd prime \(\ell\),
put \(R=\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}[b,b^{-1},\alpha]_{(\ell)}\) (a DVR where
\(b+b^{-1}\) is a unit), and let \(\mathfrak p\) be a prime above \((\ell)\) in
\({\mathcal S}=R[q_0]\) (finite \'etale over \(R\) as \(u=q_0/(b+b^{-1})\)
satisfies \(u^2=-\tfrac12\)).  Then \({\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}\) is a DVR
with uniformizer \(\ell\) in which \(\det B_M(q_0,\alpha)\) and
\(\det B_M(q_0,2q_0-\alpha)\) are units.  By Lagrange's theorem for the
centralizer of cycle type \(Z\) in \(S_M\),
\(\prod_n m_n!n^{m_n}\mid M!\), so
\(M!D_M^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M)}(\mathbb Z)\) and
\(M!G_{M-s}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{p(M-s)}({\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p})\)
for all \(0\leq s\leq M\) (as \((M-s)!\mid M!\)).  Hence
\(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in{\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}\cap\mathbb Q[t,Y]
=\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}[t,Y]\) (since \(t=b^2\) and
\(Y=\frac12b^4\alpha(\alpha-2q_0)\) are algebraically independent over
\(\mathbb F_\ell\) in \({\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p{\mathcal S}_{\mathfrak p}\));
as \(\ell\) was arbitrary, \(P_M^{(\nu)}(t,Y)\in\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][t,Y]\).

(ii) Factor
\(\prod_{u=1}^M(2Y+(u-1)t^2+\tfrac{u^2-1}{2}t^3)
=(2Y)^M\prod_{u=1}^M(1+\tfrac{u-1}{2}\tfrac{t^2}{Y}+\tfrac{u^2-1}{4}\tfrac{t^3}{Y})\).
Each \([t^aY^{-b}]\) of the normalized product is a polynomial in \(M\) of
degree at most \(2a-2b\) for all \(M\geq0\) (since \(\sum_{u=1}^M(u-1)\) and
\(\sum_{u=1}^M(u^2-1)\) have degrees \(2\) and \(3\leq4\) and vanish at
\(M=0\)).

Put
\[
 R_M(t,Y):=(M!/2^M)Y^MF_M(c(b),Y/t^2,-2/t+\zeta)\qquad(\zeta\in\{-1,2\}).
\]
Then
\((M!/2^M)Y^MH_M=\sum_{s\geq0}\eta_s\,M(M-1)\cdots(M-s+1)(Y/2)^sR_{M-s}\),
where \(M(M-1)\cdots(M-s+1)\) has degree \(s\leq2s\) and vanishes for
\(0\leq M<s\), so it suffices to show that each \([t^iY^j]R_M\) (\(i\geq0\),
\(j\in\mathbb Z\)) is a polynomial in \(M\) of degree at most \(2i+2j\) for all
\(M\geq0\).

Expand
\[
 R_M=(M!/2^M)Y^M\sum_{r\geq0}(-t)^r\Tr\bigl(D_0^{-1}(G_1D_0^{-1})^r\widetilde\rho\bigr);
\]
only \(0\leq r\leq i\) contribute to \([t^iY^j]\).  Write each
\(Z_m\vdash M\) (\(0\leq m\leq r\)) as \(Z_m=(1^{a_m},\bar Z_m)\)
with all parts of \(\bar Z_m\) at least \(2\), \(w_m=|\bar Z_m|\geq2\ell(\bar Z_m)\),
and \(a_m=M-w_m\), so
\(D_0(Z_m)=a_m!\,2^{a_m}K_{\bar Z_m}Y^{a_m+\ell(\bar Z_m)}\).  In the
\(m\)-th factor
\((tG_1D_0^{-1})_{Z_m,Z_{m+1}}=\widetilde G_{Z_m,Z_{m+1}}/D_0(Z_{m+1})-\delta_{Z_m,Z_{m+1}}\)
(\(0\leq m<r\)), the subtracted diagonal (\(Z_m=Z_{m+1}\),
\(p_m=q_m=0\)) has degree \(0\); for the \(\widetilde G\)-term, applying
\(L_1^{a_m}X=\sum_{p_m}\binom{a_m}{p_m}(\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_m}X)L_1^{a_m-p_m}\)
and \(L_1^kL_{-1}^b|\Delta\rangle=(b!/q!)(\prod_{s=q}^{b-1}(2\Delta+s))L_{-1}^q|\Delta\rangle\)
(\(k=a_m-p_m\), \(q=b-k\)) yields a sum over \(p_m,q_m\geq0\) with
\(p_m-q_m=w_{m+1}-w_m\) of
\begin{multline*}
 \frac{\binom{a_m}{p_m}}{q_m!\,2^{q_m}K_{\bar Z_{m+1}}}
 \Bigl(\prod_{s=q_m}^{a_{m+1}-1}\bigl(1+\tfrac{s\,t^2}{2Y}\bigr)\Bigr)
 \frac{t^{p_m+q_m+\ell(\bar Z_m)+\ell(\bar Z_{m+1})}}{Y^{q_m+\ell(\bar Z_{m+1})}}\\
 \times\langle\Delta|\bigl(\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_m}(L_{\bar Z_m}L_{-\bar Z_{m+1}})\bigr)L_{-1}^{q_m}|\Delta\rangle.
\end{multline*}
Since every part of \(\bar Z_{m+1}\) is at least \(2\) and \([L_1,L_n]\)
introduces no central charge, each term of
\(\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_m}(L_{\bar Z_m}L_{-\bar Z_{m+1}})L_{-1}^{q_m}\) has at
most \(\ell(\bar Z_m)+\lfloor p_m/2\rfloor\) non-negative modes; moving the
rightmost non-negative mode to the right shows by induction that every
monomial in the bracket satisfies
\(d_\Delta^{(m)}+d_c^{(m)}\leq\ell(\bar Z_m)+p_m/2\).  In
\(\widetilde\rho_{Z_r,Z_0}\), commute \(L_1^{a_r}\) past
\(L_{\bar Z_r}\) (\(p_1\) times) and \(L_{-1}^{a_0}\) past \(L_{-\bar Z_0}\)
(\(q_1\) times), and apply the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) identities below, which
are proved by induction on the exponent; the contravariant adjoint of the
first one moves \(L_{-1}^{b'-k_r}\) past \(V_d(1)\):
\begin{align*}
 \langle v|L_1^{a'}V_d(1)|W\rangle
 &=\sum_{m_L=0}^{a'}\tbinom{a'}{m_L}\!\Bigl(\prod_{u=0}^{m_L-1}(d+h_v-h_W+a'-1-u)\Bigr)
   \langle v|V_d(1)L_1^{a'-m_L}|W\rangle,\\
 L_1^{\tilde r}L_{-1}^{b'}
 &=\sum_{k_r=0}^{\min(\tilde r,b')}\!\tbinom{\tilde r}{k_r}\tfrac{b'!}{(b'-k_r)!}\,
   L_{-1}^{b'-k_r}\!\Bigl(\prod_{u=0}^{k_r-1}(2L_0+b'+\tilde r-k_r-1-u)\Bigr)L_1^{\tilde r-k_r}.
\end{align*}
This yields a sum over \(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2,k_r\geq0\).  Put
\(p_r^*=p_1+p_2\), \(q_r^*=q_1+q_2\), \(s_L=a_r-k_r-p_r^*\) and
\(s_R=a_0-k_r-q_r^*\).  Each summand is the product of
\[
 \frac{M!}{s_R!}\frac{a_r!}{s_L!}\frac{1}{k_r!\,p_1!\,p_2!\,q_1!\,q_2!},\quad
 \prod_{u=0}^{k_r-1}(2Y+(M+c_u)t^2),\quad
 (-2)^{s_L+s_R}\prod_{u=0}^{s_L-1}(1+c'_ut)\prod_{u=0}^{s_R-1}(1+c''_ut)
\]
and the local bracket
\(\langle\Delta|M_{p_1,q_2}V_d(1)N_{p_2,q_1}|\Delta\rangle\), where
\[
 M_{p_1,q_2}=(-\operatorname{ad}(L_{-1}))^{q_2}\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_1}(L_{\bar Z_r}),\qquad
 N_{p_2,q_1}=\operatorname{ad}(L_1)^{p_2}(-\operatorname{ad}(L_{-1}))^{q_1}(L_{-\bar Z_0}).
\]
The coefficients \(c'_u,c''_u\) are affine-linear in \(M\) and \(u\), and
\((-1)^{s_L+s_R}\) and \(2^{s_L+s_R}/(2^M2^{a_0})\) are independent of \(M\).
The bracket has \(\ell(\bar Z_r)+\ell(\bar Z_0)\) modes and hence satisfies
\(d_d^{(r)}+d_\Delta^{(r)}+d_c^{(r)}\leq\ell(\bar Z_r)+\ell(\bar Z_0)\).
Expanding \(k\) correction factors, each a factor \(t^2/Y\), a factor \(t\)
from the \(s_L\) or \(s_R\) product, or a factor \((M+c_u)t^2\) from the
\(k_r\) product, increases \(i+j\) by \(k\) and \(2i+2j\) by \(2k\).  The
result is an elementary symmetric polynomial in \(M\), of degree at most
\(2k\) by Faulhaber's formula and Newton's identities.

On the remaining base exponents, for each \(0\leq m<r\) one has
\(\delta i_m+\delta j_m\geq p_m+\ell(\bar Z_m)-(d_\Delta^{(m)}+d_c^{(m)})\geq p_m/2\),
whence \(2\delta i_m+2\delta j_m-p_m\geq0\); on the \(\widetilde\rho\) factor,
\(w_0\geq2\ell(\bar Z_0)\) and
\(d_d^{(r)}+d_\Delta^{(r)}+d_c^{(r)}\leq\ell(\bar Z_r)+\ell(\bar Z_0)\) give
\(\delta i_r+\delta j_r\geq w_0/2+k_r+p_r^*+q_r^*\) and
\(2\delta i_r+2\delta j_r-(w_0+p_r^*+q_r^*+2k_r)
\geq w_0+2\ell(\bar Z_r)+p_r^*+q_r^*-2(d_d^{(r)}+d_\Delta^{(r)}+d_c^{(r)})\geq0\).
Summing yields
\(i+j\geq w_0/2+k_r+p_r^*+q_r^*+\frac12\sum_{m=0}^{r-1}p_m\) and
\(\deg_M\leq2i+2j\).  Since \(0\leq r\leq i\) and \(w_{m+1}-w_m=p_m-q_m\),
\(w_0\leq2(i+j)\), \(w_m\leq4(i+j)\), \(p_m\leq2(i+j)\),
\(q_m\leq6(i+j)\) and \(k_r,p_r^*,q_r^*\leq i+j\).  Hence only finitely many
tuples contribute to \((i,j)\).  Each such tuple yields a polynomial in \(M\)
that vanishes on
\[
 [0,w_0+q_r^*+k_r-1]\cup[w_r,w_r+p_r^*+k_r-1]\cup\bigcup_{m=0}^{r-1}[w_m,w_m+p_m-1].
\]
Since \(w_m+p_m=w_{m+1}+q_m\geq w_{m+1}\), induction on \(m\) shows that this
union contains every level
\(0\leq M<\max(w_m+p_m,w_r+p_r^*+k_r,w_0+q_r^*+k_r)\) at which the tuple is
absent.  So the finite sum is a single polynomial in \(M\) for all
\(M\geq0\).
\end{proof}
